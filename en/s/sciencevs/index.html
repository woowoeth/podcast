Sunscreen Toxicity Myth Debunked: Entering Blood Isn't Harmful, but Vitamin D Risk Is Real

The most-cited evidence for 'sunscreen is toxic/causes cancer' mostly involves absurd doses or reversed causality; chemical UV filters entering the blood doesn't equal toxicity. But daily application does increase the risk of winter vitamin D deficiency, and evidence for melanoma prevention in darker skin is especially weak.

Yo-yo dieting doesn't wreck your metabolism; habits, not weight, decide lifespan

Weight cycling — yo-yo dieting — does not permanently break your metabolism, and in ordinary people the weight that comes back rarely costs you muscle. What actually shapes health and lifespan is the healthy habits themselves, not the swings in weight.

Brain Signals Show Up Seven Seconds Early, and the Researcher Still Says Free Will Survives

The Libet and Haynes experiments show brain signals appearing half a second to seven seconds before ‘you decide’, but the researcher behind them denies this amounts to the absence of free will; compatibilism holds that will may be a macro-level emergent property of microscopic brain processes, the way temperature emerges from atoms.

The narcissism epidemic is media hype: the data doesn't support it, and narcissists themselves suffer more

The much-hyped ‘narcissism epidemic’ has little data behind it; narcissism as measured by the NPI has actually been falling in recent years. True NPD affects about 1%, patients often carry depression and anxiety alongside it, and treatment works but takes years.
