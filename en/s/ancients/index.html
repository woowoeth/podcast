The Vandals Were Not Barbarians: Cutting Off Tax Revenue Was What Killed the Western Roman Empire

Early on, the Vandals were a small tribe that lost nine out of ten battles in Roman records, yet by seizing North Africa and cutting off Rome's tax revenue, they sacked Rome in 455; the word "vandalism" was a political metaphor invented during the French Revolution.

Terror birds weren't killed by humans — the Isthmus of Panama closed first

Terror birds were the apex avian predators that emerged on the island continent of South America after the dinosaurs died out, and they ruled for tens of millions of years; their eventual disappearance is tied to the great species interchange that followed the joining of the Isthmus of Panama, earlier than the arrival of humans in South America.

The Maya ballgame was ritual, not sport: the sacrificed-winner story is invented

The Mesoamerican ballgame is humanity's earliest rubber-ball team sport, but it was religious ritual first: the court re-staged the creation myth, and the people who could be killed were captives who lost a ritual re-enactment. The idea that the winners were sacrificed was made up later.

The Roman Empire ran on enslaved people, not on its legions

Rome's administration, its army, its domestic work, its workshops and its mines ran almost entirely on enslaved people; after Carthage fell in 146 BC slavery became the engine of the economy, and once Augustus stopped expanding, that population reproduced itself by birth.
