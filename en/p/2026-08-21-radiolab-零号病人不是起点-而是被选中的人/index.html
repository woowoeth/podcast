Patient Zero Is Not the Source, It's the Scapegoat Society Needs

From Typhoid Mary to HIV to the high five, every so-called source is a story humans needed; the truth is a jigsaw puzzle, and all we want is a focal point.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:06

Disease doesn't come from filth, it comes from healthy people

In 1906, 6 of 11 members of a wealthy household in Oyster Bay, Long Island, were infected with typhoid, and sanitary engineer George Soper traced it to the cook, Mary Mallon. She had no symptoms at all, yet live typhoid bacilli were cultured from her feces and urine, making her the first documented healthy carrier in North America. The discovery overturned the era's public-health notion that disease came from dirty surroundings — a person could spread disease without symptoms.

14:43

Locking Mary up for life wasn't epidemic control, it was reassuring the public

Mary was forcibly quarantined on North Brother Island twice, but she was not the only healthy carrier: New York had already identified several hundred healthy carriers at the time, most of them male cooks, and some even kept working illegally in kitchens. Only she was imprisoned for life. The line "more important than actually keeping people safe was making the public feel safe" nails the essence of Patient Zero: society needs an identifiable sinner, not the most scientific explanation.

20:00

The named Patient Zero arrived later than the epidemic itself

In the 1980s the CDC mapped a sexual contact network among 30 gay male patients, and the node at the center, numbered 0, was later identified by And the Band Played On as Gaëtan Dugas, a French-Canadian flight attendant, whom the media cast as a demon deliberately spreading AIDS. But scientists used viral mutation rates as a molecular clock, compared the diversity of US HIV samples, and found HIV entered the US long before Dugas became sexually active. He was not the starting point, and he should not be called Patient Zero.

29:18

The real Patient Zero is a hunter who cut himself

By comparing two HIV-positive samples from Kinshasa, from 1959 and 1960, the most recent common ancestor was calculated to date to about 1908. Tracing the animal reservoir showed that the virus closest to HIV-1 group M lives in chimpanzee populations in a small area bounded by three rivers in southeastern Cameroon. The most credible "cut hunter" hypothesis is this: a hunter butchering a chimpanzee cut himself, virus-laden blood entered his wound, and cross-species transmission was completed — that nameless hunter is the biological Patient Zero.

34:10

Cross-species transmission happened 12 times, and only one became a catastrophe

HIV is known to have at least 12 major lineages, meaning at least 12 independent cross-species transmission events occurred (8 from monkeys, 3 from chimpanzees, 1 from gorillas), and only one of them — group M — caused a global pandemic, infecting 60 million people and killing 30 million. Most spillover events never formed a sustained transmission chain. In other words, the "source" is not the rare event; the "pandemic" is what needs explaining.

45:57

The high five's origin story was invented to test the media

In 2007 National High Five Day put out a press release claiming the high five was invented by basketball player Lamont Sleets: his father belonged to the 1st Battalion, 5th Infantry in Vietnam, and the family slapped hands with a "High five." Reporter John Moe dug in and found it was entirely fabricated — founder Greg Caroldge admitted they made up the story just to see whether the media would report it. When they learned Glen Burke's story, they felt guilty.

50:00

There is never just one origin, we just pick the best-sounding one

The high five's real origins are disputed: Glen Burke and Dusty Baker slapped hands in the 1977 MLB playoffs, Louisville's Derek Smith did it in 1980 too, 1960s women's volleyball players say they were already using it, and the gesture appears in the 1955 French film Breathless. The show ultimately admits: trace the origin of anything and you will find more than one. When the "first" can't be determined, we choose the better story — and so the invention of the high five still goes to Glen Burke.

Glossary

healthy carrier
Someone infected with a pathogen who shows no symptoms but can still transmit it to others.
spillover
An event in which a pathogen crosses from one species to another, often the starting point of an emerging infectious disease.
molecular clock
A method that uses the rate of genetic mutation to estimate when evolutionary events occurred.
cut hunter
The hypothesis that a pathogen entered humans through a wound a hunter suffered while butchering prey.
SIV
Simian Immunodeficiency Virus, an HIV-like virus carried by monkeys.
