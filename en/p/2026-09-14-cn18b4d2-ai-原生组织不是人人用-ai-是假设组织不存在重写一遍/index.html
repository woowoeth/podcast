An AI-native org isn't everyone using AI, it's rewriting the org as if it didn't exist

Lockin merged HR and IT into HARO, put a technical founder-type in charge of people and AI; circle leads get the bonus pool and the right to divide it, and to hire you first have to prove AI can't do the job.

The founder of a 12-year hardware company explains the concrete mechanics of an org rewrite: HARO, circles, AI management trainees, how FDEs actually land — with details and with the pitfalls.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:01

AI-native starts by assuming the org doesn't exist

Chen Bin's definition of an AI-native organization is not ‘everyone has to use AI well’ but starting from AI and asking again: can AI do this thing, and as far as possible don't let a person do it. The harsher step is to assume the old organization and processes don't exist — ‘they shouldn't even exist’ — and go back to the state of one person starting a company to redesign the roles and the process. So this is not accelerating the existing process, it's tearing the process down first and rebuilding it. He admits this is mainly aimed at the core org builders, not a slogan shouted at everyone.

— Chen Bin
6:04

The HARO lead comes from tech, not from HR

Lockin merged HR and IT into HARO (Human AI Resource Office), but Chen Bin stresses it is not an ordinary HR+IT: the lead has to understand the business, and the person chosen is an old partner who started the company with him and has done technology, product, CEO, R&D, brand and IT. The logic is inverted — the old HR lead leaned toward people and was insensitive to AI, so now a technology-oriented person runs it, with HR BPs who are sensitive to people assigned to him. Under this department sit the AIO (the former IT team, which will do Agent Builder work) on a solid line, and FDEs reporting on a dotted line.

— Chen Bin
10:04

Agent Builder and FDE are two kinds of people in the short term

Chen Bin draws the line clearly: an Agent Builder leans technical and can build an Agent; an FDE has to go into the business front line, sort out supply, planning and procurement processes, and is best a business expert. Long term one person might cover it; short term two kinds of people must work together. They did not copy Anker's approach of pulling people out of work and locking them away for a month, because they don't have that much slack; instead people from the business put 30% to 50% of their energy into AI-ification while still doing the business. The company also set up AI levels L1 to L4, with L2 and above called ‘AI navigators’ and above that ‘pilots’.

— Chen Bin
16:06

Circles erase functional power; the circle lead gets the bonus pool

Lockin's circles differ from project teams and from Huawei's matrix: a matrix is product line plus function on two axes, while a circle is a small team around an end-to-end value loop, deliberately erasing functional power and boundaries so the circle lead's power grows. The company gives the circle a bonus pool, the circle lead decides how to divide it, and can even decide how much to give himself; HR and the functional departments only have the right to advise. Chen Bin says at this stage the ‘fighting’ line dominates, and the functional line may in future become something like a technology committee, but within six months to a year of the change the front line gets to decide first.

— Chen Bin
20:36

Experience can turn into a burden

Chen Bin says AI management trainees naturally started using AI from university in 2023, have no fear about doing things, and use it more than the old hands. His words are ‘sometimes experience instead becomes a burden’, and the most troublesome are the half-baked — whose experience hasn't arrived but who feel they are very experienced and can't change. Old hands have framework thinking and systematic thinking as their foundation, but which processes and which roles can be removed is easier for a clear-headed person to judge. The threshold for an AI management trainee is internal AI level L2 or above, requiring the ability to use skills and build Agents; only L3 can write workflows.

— Chen Bin
23:23

R&D turns fast, marketing turns slow

Chen Bin admits the speed of transformation varies a lot by department: technical R&D tied to software and coding is easiest, because writing code every day gives positive feedback; marketing and branding are slow, because after publishing more content they ask ‘so what’ — did it bring revenue growth and a bigger bonus. He also mentions a real conflict: people doing design and content feel AI can only accelerate quantity, not raise quality, and that 60-point output doesn't stick around. His response is to give everyone an operating mindset: whether the content can bring conversion is the standard.

— Chen Bin
35:32

Three rounds of sign-ups: stand up again, then sit down again

Lockin ran three rounds of internal sign-ups: the first for incremental roles, such as the company-level twenty-odd Agents and the corresponding Agent Builders and FDEs; the second for roles missing after the circles were formed, such as an Agent Builder for content marketing; the third to keep filling gaps. Applicants first do a self-assessment to find what they are good at, then give a three-to-five-minute defense, and after the judges score, AI is used to analyze the meeting minutes and the talk for matching. Of a hundred applicants, twenty or thirty may finally be chosen. Chen Bin says both the mechanism and the results are very good.

— Chen Bin
42:16

To hire, you first have to prove AI can't do it

After a circle picks off people from the original department, if the boss wants to add headcount there are logically only two channels: find an AI management trainee, or use more AI tokens. If you really must hire again, it goes to Chen Bin for special approval, and you have to explain clearly why a management trainee won't do and why AI won't do. Chen Bin says ‘logically he has no channel left’. They will hire 50 AI management trainees by year-end and are already close to 20. Xiaofei's role at Lockin is like a strategic HR, a CHO as a service, doing the AI-native transformation together with Chen Bin.

— Chen Bin
51:22

IPD is not overturned, only accelerated

Chen Bin says inside the company he is in fact the most radical one, and people often pull him back and ask him to be more cautious. On deposits like Huawei's IPD, his judgment is: AI can only remake processes that were already unreasonable and especially redundant; IPD itself is very reasonable and can only be accelerated. For example, from strategy to execution, SP to BP, used to take three months to half a year, now two months, doubling efficiency, but the nodes like the five looks and three settings are still there, just three weeks become three days. Training a product line manager used to take 10 to 15 years, now three to five years, but the rotations that should happen cannot be skipped.

— Chen Bin
1:00:24

Not every company should run this fast

Chen Bin's advice to three kinds of bosses — milk tea shops, PCB factories, call centers — is to look at the real value AI brings in that industry: the more traditional the industry, the lower the value, and you can wait until platforms like Doubao and Volcano Engine mature and then choose, with lower trial-and-error cost. But the more frontier the tech company, the earlier it should embrace AI, because technology changes a lot and the market changes fast, and decision quality matters more. He specifically mentions that before physical AI takes off, PCB factories and the like should be cautious and not move first; milk tea shops are driven more by channels and operations, and AI can quickly build a feedback loop from the user end to the supply chain end, so it matters more there.

— Chen Bin

Glossary

HARO / Human AI Resource Office
The department Lockin formed by merging HR and IT, responsible for AI transformation and org restructuring.
FDE / Forward Deployed Engineer
A role that goes into the business front line to sort out processes and land AI in the business; Lockin requires it to be a business expert.
Agent Builder
A more technical role responsible for building Agents; short term it splits work with the FDE, long term the two may merge.
AI Buff
What Lockin uses to quantify the efficiency gain AI brings inside a circle or a role.
IPD / Integrated Product Development
Huawei's product development process system, which Chen Bin believes AI can only accelerate, not overturn.
SP / BP / Strategy Plan / Business Plan
The two stages from strategy to execution in Huawei's DSTE process, whose cycle Lockin compresses with AI.
