An AI-native org isn't everyone using AI — it's assuming the org doesn't exist and rebuilding it

Lockin merged HR and IT into HARO, put a technical founder-type in charge of people and AI, gave circle leads the power to split the bonus pool, and now requires proof that AI can't do the job before anyone gets hired.

A 12-year hardware company's founder lays out the concrete mechanics of rewriting an organization: HARO, circles, AI management trainees, how FDEs actually land — with details and the potholes he hit.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:01

AI-native starts by assuming the organization doesn't exist

Chen Bin's definition of an AI-native organization is not ‘everyone should use AI well’ but starting from AI and asking again: can AI do this thing, and as far as possible don't have a person do it. The harsher step is to assume the original organization and processes don't exist — ‘they shouldn't even exist’ — and go back to the state of a one-person startup to redesign roles and processes. So this is not accelerating existing processes; it's tearing the processes down first and rebuilding. He concedes this is mainly aimed at the core org builders, not a slogan shouted at everyone.

— Chen Bin
6:04

The HARO lead comes from tech, not HR

Lockin merged HR and IT into HARO (Human AI Resource Office), but Chen Bin stresses it is not an ordinary HR+IT combo: the lead must understand the business, and the person chosen is an old partner who co-founded with him and has done technology, product, CEO, R&D, brand and IT. The logic is inverted — the old HR lead leaned toward people and was insensitive to AI, so now a tech-oriented person leads, with HR BPs who are sensitive to people assigned to him. Under this department sit the AIO (the former IT team, which will do Agent Builder work) on a solid line, and FDEs reporting on a dotted line.

— Chen Bin
10:04

Agent Builder and FDE are two different people in the short term

Chen Bin draws the line clearly: an Agent Builder leans technical and can build an Agent; an FDE has to go into the business front line and map out supply, planning and procurement processes, and is best a business expert. Long term one person might cover it; short term it must be two kinds of people working together. They did not copy Anker's approach of pulling people out of work and locking them in a room for a month, because they don't have that much slack; instead people from the business spend 30% to 50% of their energy doing AI-ification alongside their day jobs. The company also set up an AI proficiency ladder from L1 to L4, with L2 and above called ‘AI 起航员’ (AI navigators) and above that ‘领航员’ (pilots).

— Chen Bin
16:06

Circles erase functional power; circle leads get the bonus pool

Lockin's circles differ from project teams and Huawei's matrix: a matrix is product line plus function on two axes, while a circle is a small team around an end-to-end value loop, deliberately erasing functional power and boundaries so the circle lead's power grows. The company gives a circle a bonus pool, and the circle lead decides how to split it — even how much to give himself — while HR and functional departments only have an advisory voice. Chen Bin says at this stage the ‘fighting’ line dominates; the functional line may in future become something like a technology committee, but within the first half year to a year of the transformation the front line gets to decide.

— Chen Bin
20:36

Experience can become a liability

Chen Bin says AI management trainees naturally started using AI from their university years in 2023, have no fear about doing things, and use it more than the veterans. His exact words: ‘sometimes experience instead becomes a burden’, and the most troublesome are the half-baked — whose experience hasn't arrived but who feel they are very experienced and can't change. Veterans have framework thinking and systematized thinking as their foundation, but the clear-eyed ones find it easier to judge which processes and which roles can be removed. The bar for AI management trainees is internal AI proficiency L2 or above, requiring skills and Agent-building ability; only L3 can author workflows.

— Chen Bin
23:23

R&D turns fast, marketing turns slow

Chen Bin admits transformation speed varies a lot by department: technical R&D tied to software and coding is easiest, because writing code every day gives positive feedback; marketing and branding are slow, because after publishing more content they ask ‘so what’ — did it bring revenue growth and bigger bonuses. He also mentions a real conflict: people doing design and content feel AI can only accelerate volume, not raise quality, and that 60-point output doesn't stick around. His response is to give everyone an operating mindset: whether content can drive conversion is the standard.

— Chen Bin
35:32

Three rounds of sign-ups: stand up again, then sit down again

Lockin ran three internal rounds of sign-ups: the first for incremental roles, such as a dozen or two company-level Agents and the corresponding Agent Builders and FDEs; the second for roles missing after circles formed, such as a content marketing Agent Builder; the third to keep filling gaps. Applicants first do a self-assessment to find what they're good at, then give a three-to-five-minute defense, and after the judges score, AI is used to analyze the meeting minutes and the talks for matching. Of a hundred applicants, twenty or thirty may be chosen in the end. Chen Bin says both the mechanism and the results were very good.

— Chen Bin
42:16

To hire, you must first prove AI can't do the job

After a circle picks over the people in an original department, if the boss wants to add headcount there are logically only two channels: find an AI management trainee, or use more AI tokens. If you really must hire again, it goes to Chen Bin for special approval, and you have to explain clearly why a management trainee won't work and why AI won't work. Chen Bin says ‘logically he has no channel left’. They plan to hire 50 AI management trainees by year-end and are already close to 20. Xiaofei's role at Lockin is like a strategic HR or CHO as a service, working with Chen Bin on the AI-native transformation.

— Chen Bin
51:22

IPD isn't overturned, only accelerated

Chen Bin says inside the company he is in fact the most radical one, and people often pull him back to be more cautious. On Huawei IPD and similar accumulated practice, his judgment is: AI can only rework processes that were already unreasonable and especially redundant; IPD itself is very reasonable and can only be accelerated. For example, going from strategy to execution, SP to BP, used to take three months to half a year, now two months — double the efficiency — but the nodes like Five Looks Three Sets are still there, just three days instead of three weeks. Training a product line manager used to take 10 to 15 years, now three to five, but the rotation posts that must be done cannot be skipped.

— Chen Bin
1:00:24

Not every company should run this fast

Chen Bin's advice to three kinds of bosses — milk tea shops, PCB factories and call centers — is to look at the real value AI brings in that industry: the more traditional the industry, the lower the value, so you can wait until platforms like Doubao and Volcano Engine mature and then choose, with lower trial-and-error cost. But the more frontier the tech company, the earlier it should embrace AI, because technology changes a lot and the market changes fast, so decision quality matters more. He specifically notes that before physical AI takes off, PCB factories and the like should be cautious and not move first; milk tea shops are more channel- and operations-driven, and AI can quickly build a feedback loop from the user end to the supply chain end, so it matters more there.

— Chen Bin

Glossary

HARO / Human AI Resource Office
The department Lockin formed by merging HR and IT, responsible for AI transformation and org restructuring.
FDE / Forward Deployed Engineer
A role that goes into the business front line to map processes and land AI in the business; Lockin requires it to be a business expert.
Agent Builder
A technical-leaning role responsible for building Agents; short term it splits from the FDE, long term the two may merge.
AI Buff
Lockin's measure of the efficiency gain AI brings inside a circle or role, expressed as a percentage.
IPD / Integrated Product Development
Huawei's product development process system, which Chen Bin believes AI can only accelerate, not overturn.
SP / BP / Strategy Plan / Business Plan
The two stages from strategy to execution in Huawei's DSTE process, whose cycle Lockin compressed with AI.
