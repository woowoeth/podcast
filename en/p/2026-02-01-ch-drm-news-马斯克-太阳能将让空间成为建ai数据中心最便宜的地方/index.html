Musk: Solar power will make space the cheapest place to build AI data centers

Musk predicts AI could exceed individual human intelligence this year, and the constraint limiting AI expansion is not chips but power—this is the fundamental reason he is betting on space-based solar data centers.

21:17

More robots means more evenly distributed economic output

Musk defines future economic output as average robot productivity per unit multiplied by total robot count. He predicts that in the best case, humanity will manufacture robots and AI so abundantly that they saturate all human demand—so many that you couldn't imagine additional uses. Abundance, he argues, requires robot labor that everyone can do; if only some people can perform a job, economic growth stays narrow, not broad.

— Elon Musk
27:37

The constraint on AI is power, not chips

Musk identifies power as the fundamental limiting factor for AI deployment. Chip production is growing exponentially, but new electrical capacity grows only about 4% annually. The result: 'Soon, perhaps later this year, we'll produce more chips than we can power.' He contrasts this with China's power growth, which he calls 'staggering'—it is simultaneously building 100 gigawatts of nuclear capacity, and solar manufacturing is China's greatest energy asset.

— Elon Musk
28:46

China's annual solar output equals half U.S. total electricity consumption

Musk provides concrete numbers: China's solar production capacity is roughly 1,500 gigawatts annually, deployed at over 1,000 gigawatts per year. Converting to steady-state supply—dividing by 4 to 5—yields about 250 gigawatts, roughly half of America's average electricity consumption of 500 gigawatts. He concludes that the sun is essentially 100% of the solar system's energy source; even if you burned Jupiter as nuclear fuel, solar energy would round to 100%.

— Elon Musk
31:52

A small desert patch can power the entire United States

Musk provides concrete scale: a 160-by-160-kilometer solar field would supply all U.S. electricity, equivalent to a small corner of Utah, Nevada, or New Mexico. Europe likewise could be powered by sparse regions of Spain and Sicily. But he is blunt: the U.S. lacks large-scale deployment because 'U.S. solar tariffs are extremely high.' The tariffs artificially raise deployment costs, since China manufactures nearly all solar cells.

— Elon Musk
37:25

Fully reusable rockets cut space costs by 99 percent

Musk calls Starship's full reusability the key breakthrough SpaceX aims for this year. Currently, Falcon 9 recovers only the first stage—now over 500 times—while the second stage burns up on reentry, equivalent to discarding a small to medium jet each flight. Full reusability drops space access costs by 100 times. Comparing disposable versus reusable aircraft, he projects costs below $100 per pound—lower than air cargo rates.

— Elon Musk
39:34

Space power generation is five times as efficient as ground-based

Musk explains the economics of space-based solar data centers. Without day-night and weather cycles, with no atmospheric loss, the same solar panel generates five times the power in space as on Earth. The shaded side reaches only 3 Kelvin, making heat rejection extremely efficient—panels face the sun, radiators face away. His conclusion: 'The lowest-cost location to build AI will be space,' and this will happen within two to three years. SpaceX will launch solar-powered AI satellites within years.

— Elon Musk
40:35

Musk predicts AI may surpass the smartest humans this year

Asked about ten to twenty years ahead, Musk says he doesn't know what will happen in a decade, but offers a specific near-term timeline. At current AI progress rates, 'We may have AI smarter than any person by year-end,' or at the latest next year. By 2030 or 2031—five years out—AI will be 'smarter than all humanity combined.'

— Elon Musk
