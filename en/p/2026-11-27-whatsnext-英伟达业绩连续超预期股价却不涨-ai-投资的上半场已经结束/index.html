9:35

Nvidia beats three straight quarters; stock flat on priced-in gains

Nvidia has beaten expectations three straight quarters with no revenue decline or demand falloff, yet the stock price hasn't responded positively. This isn't a bottleneck but a "plateau of expectations"—investors have already priced in 2027-28 good news, so even better earnings can't create new upside. This marks the apex of the AI infrastructure foundation stage that Nvidia represents, with the next phase about application penetration rates.

— Aaron
12:05

Market prices Huang's value at $1 trillion: entrepreneurship as a line item

In the 11 days Huang stepped down, Nvidia's market cap fell nearly $1 trillion, then rose back nearly $1 trillion when he returned, though not to the pre-departure peak. This trillion-dollar swing is the market's specific price tag on "entrepreneurship"—when he bet on the CUDA ecosystem and acquired Mellanox for GPU interconnect against board pressure, only a founder could make those calls; without him, the ecosystem loses value.

— Aaron
18:07

The food-delivery war burned through Chinese companies' chance to grab compute

Before and around Spring Festival, Alibaba and JD.com threw massive funds into the food-delivery battle, then had no seat at the compute table when that course came. When Chinese companies realized they needed to ramp compute, the US had already tightened export and cloud-compute rental controls; the supply window post-H200 had closed, they couldn't buy even if they wanted to, missing a full year's strategic window.

— Aaron
24:13

Anthropic IPO breaks on bad luck meeting an election-scale rout

Anthropic popped on IPO day but soon broke offer price. The main cause isn't its own capital drain but the black swan of the governing party's "worst defeat in 50 years" after the midterm elections, compounded by Huang's scandals and Korean storage-chip sentiment reversal; three things hit at once and punched through the entire AI hardware-sector mood.

— Aaron
33:19

Circular financing has no unbreakable contracts; real risk is on demand

On Anthropic's long-term compute purchases and Neo Cloud circular financing concerns, Aaron rebuts that rigid multi-decade contracts don't actually exist—you sign them so the downstream can use them as collateral, but once demand softens (GPU rental prices keep falling, large-model UX gets worse), that chain breaks for real, not because the contract structure is complex.

— Aaron
44:25

After coding, the next frontier is far from ordinary life

Everyone asks "what after coding?"—short-form video, personal agents, AI for Science are candidates, but none iterates as fast or gets feedback as cheap as coding. AI for Science bumps into the physical world; new drugs need real clinical trials, new materials need tape-out; verification cycles and costs are both high; the real winners may be those touching the physical world (like CROs running clinical trials), not AI itself.

— Aaron
55:27

Alibaba and JD bet $200-300 billion on instant retail at the crucial window

During the AI race's crucial window, Alibaba and JD threw strategy resources into instant retail, an unrelated battlefield, burning nearly a year and 200-300 billion yuan in capital; instant retail never propped up core business, and ByteDance and Huawei grabbed the first-mover advantage in compute.

— Aaron
1:10:35

Tech is the only sector that beats the index consistently

Aaron back-tested 12 industries in the US market over the past 10, 20, 30 years; conclusion: only tech continuously outperforms the S&P 500; other sectors just benefit for a phase from their own cycle. So for 2027 opportunities, he still looks to tech first, not consumer or energy.

— Aaron
