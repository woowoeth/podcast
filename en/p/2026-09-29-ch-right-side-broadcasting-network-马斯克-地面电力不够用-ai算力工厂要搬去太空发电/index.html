Musk: Ground Power Is Insufficient; AI Compute Must Move to Space

Musk and Huang have shifted AI competition's decisive point from chips to power—ground-based power is insufficient, so they want to put compute factories into orbit where it is ‘always sunny’, feeding superintelligence with space solar.

Not vague AI optimism, but a roadmap with concrete gigawatt numbers, economic calculations, and named security products—information is tightly focused on power and orbital computing.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:01

Without power, the entire AI stack collapses

Jensen breaks the computing stack into chip design, algorithm training, applications, and power, emphasizing that power is the foundational layer without which nothing runs. He directly points out that without Trump's ‘pro-energy’ policies, data centers cannot be built at all; at the same time, data centers are rapidly spreading into communities, and the industry must proactively include these communities in prosperity distribution rather than merely imposing infrastructure unilaterally.

— Jensen Huang
3:12

Every 1% electricity growth corresponds to roughly 1% GDP growth

Musk quantifies the power problem as arithmetic: US average electricity consumption is roughly 500 gigawatts; every 5 gigawatts of new stable capacity corresponds to 1% electricity growth, and he will ‘bet against anyone’ that this 1% electricity growth roughly corresponds to 1% GDP growth—by this calculation, each gigawatt annually enables hundreds of billions in economic output. He simultaneously identifies China as the greatest competitor, reasoning that China's power generation is roughly three times that of the US; the chip problem ‘will be solved’, but the true long-term chokepoint is power generation capacity.

— Elon Musk
6:20

Ground power is insufficient; compute must move into orbit

Musk's answer is not to build more ground-based power plants, but to move computing to orbit: ground-based solar panels receive only one-fifth to one-eighth of rated power and require massive battery systems, but in space ‘it is almost always sunny’. The joint goal of SpaceX and Tesla is 200 gigawatts of solar capacity annually; Google is already planning to launch TPUs into orbit, and last year an H100 was already validated in orbit. He believes now is the window, because these computing systems' economic returns are high enough to cover the still-expensive launch costs.

— Elon Musk
8:25

These facilities produce intelligence, not just store data

Jensen wants to rename ‘data center’ to something else—these facilities don't store data, they produce economically valuable output, so they should be called ‘SI factories’. He provides specific scale: assuming the US adds 10 to 20 gigawatts annually, building these facilities alone creates roughly one million jobs, spanning power plant construction, cooling systems, pipe work, and other blue-collar work; he calls this America's first genuine reindustrialization in 50 years, intended to reorient economic structure to accommodate blue-collar jobs, not just white-collar ones.

— Jensen Huang
12:27

AI agents need sandboxes and monitoring, not just rules

Regarding safety for AI agents, NVIDIA provides two specific products rather than principle-based commitments: OpenShell is a containerized system, essentially a browser for agents, strictly limiting their permissions to appropriate ranges; BlueField is an independent monitoring chip that watches agents' every move out-of-band and escalates according to preset procedures if they violate boundaries. Jensen compares this logic to hiring a new employee—grant permissions, provide tools, then monitor continuously, rather than trusting it is inherently safe.

— Jensen Huang
15:36

Starship can now carry payloads larger than any since Skylab

Musk confirms Starship completed its first orbital flight; the Starlink B3 satellites launched this time have a wingspan equivalent to a 737, making them the largest single payload sent to orbit since Skylab. He projects Starship will reach one to two launches per week next year, putting at least 300 gigawatts of AI computing into orbit annually—equivalent to a 40% increase in annual US energy consumption—and this number ‘may one day become a terawatt’. Starship's other mission is to deliver roughly a million tons of cargo to the Moon and Mars, supporting self-expanding cities there.

— Elon Musk
22:49

AI systems with judgment may refuse necessary work

When discussing whether AI systems should have values and judgment, Jensen uses a steak knife as an analogy: the same knife cuts medium-rare steak smoothly, but if presented with rare beef that looks like living tissue, the knife might ‘refuse to cut’. His concern is that once tools develop their own judgment, usage effectiveness becomes subject to that judgment—for instance, in cybersecurity, defensive actions often look identical to attack patterns, and you need AI to execute defensive moves even if those actions appear aggressive. He admits he hasn't fully thought this through.

— Jensen Huang
24:57

AI regulation needs external and mutual oversight

Both reveal that the core mechanism of the White House lunch statement is industry mutual oversight—joint monitoring, board special committees, and ‘mutual peer review’ rather than self-grading. Specific provisions include strict internal controls, clear documentation of product intent, hierarchical internal audits, third-party external audits, and cross-industry sharing of best practices. Jensen assesses this statement as ‘having real teeth’ and believes it should become the standard for the entire industry going forward, not just the commitment of the participating companies.

— Jensen Huang
