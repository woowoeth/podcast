Going Against the Mainstream Is Also Mimetic: AI Will Actually Weaken Social Contagion

In an age of contagion, escape is not the cure; Burgis bets that AI will actually weaken social contagion, and drawing on Catholicism and the experience of Cluny, argues that what's really needed is to rebuild intermediate institutions.

2:18

A greater tragedy may be what forces people to rebuild

Burgis doesn't think pessimism and negative contagion can last forever. Society has release valves akin to the scapegoat mechanism, and sometimes it overcorrects; even a trauma or tragedy more severe than the current wars might actually force people to rebuild. He admits this sounds contradictory, but optimism often grows out of tragedy. This is his mechanistic answer to "how the world turns around."

9:47

What can be measured is often not the most important thing in life

Burgis received a consulting invitation from Anthropic, which wanted to use Christian virtue ethics to vet Claude's constitution. After asking "what does it mean to make Claude good," he felt it was philosophically off, and he refused to describe his participation as a moral obligation. He then criticised effective altruism: it hasn't proven it can achieve its goals, it is essentially too materialist, and what can be measured is often not the most important thing in life — such as the quality of relationships, depth and culture.

13:40

Silicon Valley contrarianism is also a form of imitation

If Girard were alive, he would be embarrassed that mimetic theory itself became so popular; Silicon Valley-style contrarianism is also a fake rebellion, because "anti-mainstream" long ago became a new mimetic identity. Burgis stresses that The One and the 99 is not advocating that everyone become that "one," but a reminder: when all attention is concentrated on the "one," rethink the "99," and how the self and the group shape each other.

16:08

Porn is too easy to get; friction must be reintroduced

Burgis lists "getting young men to lose interest in porn" as the norm he most wants to change. He cites a Free Press report: a 13-year-old girl came across porn unintentionally and said it had become a kind of rite of passage — not actively sought but suddenly descending on you. He believes porn is too easy to get and friction must be reintroduced; by the data, about 90% of the boys in his class watch it, and the share of girls is also high.

22:47

Whether a church is alive or dead shows in the confession queue

Italian Catholicism and the American high liturgy revival are very different: old churches have no heating or air conditioning, bad sound systems, and people come and go as in an Orthodox church. Burgis judges a parish's first signal by confession frequency. If confession is heard several times a week or even daily, a queue naturally forms; if it's only by appointment or only on Saturdays, it's decline. He says this is like church politics — one signal predicts ten other things.

— Luke Burgis
27:11

The best evangelism is not arguing truth but showing beauty

In his second year at the Roman seminary, Burgis saw a talk on the street by Pixar storyboard artist Matthew Luhn, skipped class to listen, was completely lit up, and realised he inwardly longed for something creative and aesthetic. He says that class ruined that year of theology for him. He calls himself a von Balthasar-style Catholic: truth can be reached through beauty, so the best evangelism is not arguing why Christianity is true, but taking people to Rome to see beauty, drink good wine and talk late into the night.

— Luke Burgis
36:14

Contemporary people prefer the lost sheep that won't return to the flock

Tyler asks Burgis whether he feels an affinity with Shia Islam; Burgis says he doesn't know enough, but it shares certain elements with Catholicism: the world is enchanted, truth is hidden, intermediaries are needed. He then turns to Gnosticism: in the version of the lost sheep parable in the Gospel of Thomas, the lost sheep is not a sinner but the smartest sheep, and after Jesus finds it he does not bring it back to the flock but praises it for not being like the other stupid sheep. He thinks people today are more easily drawn to this version.

— Luke Burgis
47:12

What this talent network should produce is not companies but culture

The Cluny Institute, which Burgis founded, aims to be a talent network and artists' workshop, seeking people who are religiously minded, smart, care about serious thought and want to truly make things, working at the intersection of three things: Athens, Jerusalem, Silicon Valley. He is inspired by the original Cluny: the original Cluny was a wholly new institution, a network of more than 1200 monasteries, and became Europe's centre of innovation for three hundred years. He hopes that in 10 or 20 years this place will produce culture, aesthetics and community forms, not companies.

Glossary

mimetic desire
Desire is not spontaneous; it comes from imitating the desires of others.
scapegoat mechanism
A group deflects internal tension and restores order by blaming and expelling a victim.
achievement subject
Byung-Chul Han's concept: modern people internalise "must constantly achieve" and turn it into self-exploitation.
sprezzatura
The Italian style of being carefully dressed yet appearing effortless.
via negativa
Knowing an object through what it is not; Burgis uses it to describe how AI reveals human nature in reverse.
memento mori
A reminder that one must die; monks meditate on death daily.
