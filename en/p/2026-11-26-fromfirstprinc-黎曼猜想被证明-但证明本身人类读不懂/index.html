Euler's algebraic sieving permanently bound primes to the zeta function

Krishna reconstructs how Euler transformed the zeta function—the sum of reciprocals of integers—into an infinite product over all primes. Each time you multiply by ‘1 − 1/p^s’, it is equivalent to sieving out multiples of 2, 3, 5 in turn until only primes remain. The derivation itself is heuristic, not rigorous, but it was the first to weld primes and the zeta function together—the true starting point for more than two centuries of research that followed.

1:12:50

All nontrivial zeros must have equal influence on prime distribution

If some nontrivial zero of the zeta function had real part not equal to one-half—say, three-quarters—its amplitude would dwarf all others and overwhelm the rest in a sufficiently large range, causing prime distribution to show long gaps followed by sudden clustering, rather than the near-random distribution we see today. The Riemann Hypothesis amounts to saying that every nontrivial zero contributes equally to prime randomness; no single zero can dominate the trend.

Using primes to constrain zeros instead of vice versa broke the circular logic

Conventionally, one uses zeta zeros to calculate prime distribution; this proof reverses it. First, it uses Chebyshev-style combinatorial tricks—for example, the binomial coefficient C(20,10), where dividing numerator by denominator leaves only primes between 10 and 20—to independently bound the error in prime counting without relying on the zeta function. Then it backsolves from that bound to constrain where nontrivial zeros must fall, thereby avoiding circular reasoning.

Formal proofs that only machines can verify still count as proofs

Alpöge's proof is not a traditional paper but a collection of Lean files that even he admits he cannot fully read. Stanford mathematician Brian Conrad publicly criticized it; University of Chicago's Frank Caligari countered with an analogy to Borges's Library of Babel: Alpöge merely found the book on the shelf that contains the proof, and now everyone can try to read it together. The two camps have not converged, and Conrad happens to be the doctoral advisor of the scholar who spent twelve years on a hand-written proof.

Glossary

Euler product formula
Transforms the zeta function's integer sum into an infinite product over all primes, the first connection between primes and the zeta function.
analytic continuation
The method of uniquely extending a complex function defined on only part of its domain to a larger domain.
critical line / critical strip
The region in the complex plane where nontrivial zeros of the zeta function lie; the Riemann Hypothesis asserts all zeros fall exactly on the critical line (real part 1/2).
Lean
A programming language for formal proof that can be automatically verified by computers for logical correctness, commonly used to write machine-readable mathematical proofs.
quasi-Riemann Hypothesis
A weakened version of the Riemann Hypothesis that only requires narrowing the interval where nontrivial zeros can appear, rather than pinpointing them exactly to the critical line.
