A lifetime mitochondria researcher says it may not drive normal aging

Raising mitochondrial DNA mutations to 10 times normal aging didn't trigger aging in mice—suggesting that in healthy people, mitochondrial decline may be coincidental rather than causal.

Mitochondria's third function: signaling across the cell

Beyond producing ATP and synthesis precursors, mitochondria continuously signal the cell: leaked electrons generate H2O2 for immune cell differentiation; acetyl-CoA and α-ketoglutarate control histone acetylation and DNA demethylation; released mtDNA/RNA trigger interferon pathways. These three signaling pathways are independent of ATP production—the part rediscovered over the past 30 years.

32:26

Want to measure your mitochondrial function? No reliable method exists

Chandel says that aside from muscle biopsy, there's no way to assess mitochondrial function. Market tests using metabolomics or acylcarnitine ratios only measure steady-state concentrations at one moment, not flux—you can't distinguish whether elevated levels reflect a fast system, metabolite surplus, or blockage downstream.

— Nav Chandel
34:33

With 70% redundancy, mitochondrial problems don't arise easily

Genetic evidence shows patients carrying mitochondrial mutations need 70% loss of mitochondrial protein function before cells malfunction—meaning mitochondria have enormous redundancy. Chandel's analogy: a car reaching 217 mph instead of 140 mph is irrelevant when the speed limit is 50 mph.

— Nav Chandel
48:01

Mutating mitochondrial DNA doesn't replicate normal aging

Artificially mutating nuclear DNA in mice reproduces aging to some degree; but mutating mtDNA—even at 10 times normal aging rates—doesn't cause deterioration or aging-like phenotypes. This suggests mitochondrial dysfunction is *sufficient* to cause aging-like damage, but hasn't been proven *necessary* for normal aging.

— Nav Chandel
56:15

Metformin works in the gut, not the liver

After oral ingestion, metformin reaches ~1 millimolar in intestinal cells, then drops to 50-100 micromolar entering the liver via the portal vein, and only ~10 micromolar in blood—a 20-fold drop. PET imaging with glucose shows signal concentrates in the gut, not liver; NDI1 protein experiments confirm that intestinal Complex I inhibition is the key mechanism.

— Nav Chandel
1:08:28

Metformin's cancer trials failed because cells can't absorb it

Phase 3 trials for breast cancer and prostate cancer failed. In the lab, researchers identified a metformin-sensitive cancer cell line; once they knocked out the organic cation transporter (OCT) with CRISPR, that line became completely resistant. Most tumor tissues don't highly express these transporters—the drug can't reach them, likely explaining the trial failures.

— Nav Chandel
1:23:03

Metformin fails against cancer but suppresses clonal hematopoiesis

Metformin doesn't target cancer, but suppresses clonal hematopoiesis. UK Biobank data on ~450,000 people shows lower rates of CHIP (age-related blood stem cell mutations that drive cardiovascular inflammation) in metformin users; three independent papers (two in Nature, one in Nature Communications) confirm that inhibiting Complex I blocks these clones, with metformin being the safest drug.

— Nav Chandel
1:51:56

Hyperbaric oxygen has no evidence besides wound healing

Chandel advises against buying a hyperbaric oxygen chamber. ICU doctors want patients off high-oxygen as soon as possible—animal studies show prolonged hyperoxia causes lung epithelial cells to age and die. No benefit evidence exists. He sees the anti-aging popularity as largely sourced from an Israeli study measuring telomere length, but telomere length itself has weak evidence linking to lifespan.

Glossary

TCA cycle / Krebs cycle
A metabolic pathway in mitochondria that generates energy and produces precursors for synthesizing lipids, nucleic acids, and amino acids.
OCT (organic cation transporter)
A cell membrane protein that transports positively charged molecules like metformin into the cell.
Clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP)
Age-related expansion of blood-forming stem cell mutations that contribute to cardiovascular inflammation.
NDI1
A yeast-derived protein that substitutes for mammalian Complex I, used experimentally to verify whether a drug targets Complex I.
Warburg effect
The phenomenon of tumor cells producing large amounts of lactate even with adequate oxygen, driven by growth needs rather than mitochondrial dysfunction.
