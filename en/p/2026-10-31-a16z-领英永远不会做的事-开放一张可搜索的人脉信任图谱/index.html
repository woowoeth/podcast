What LinkedIn Will Never Do: Open a Searchable Web of Professional Trust

Cosign, a16z's bet, argues that the next chapter in hiring isn't mass-applying for jobs—it's having someone you trust, or eventually your own AI agent, run the first round of screening for you.

2:07

Talent data gets trapped inside corporate silos, invisible across companies

Sophia observes that knowledge of who's a growth expert or top engineer usually stays confined to each company or small circle, never crossing organizational boundaries. Dhruv responds that Cosign Awards aims to break down these silos—transforming what companies normally keep quiet through internal gossip (like ‘which company has the highest talent density’ or ‘which product had the biggest impact this year’) into searchable public information, not just awards for famous founders and CEOs.

— Sophia
5:15

Niche award categories surface far more talented people than expected

David cites specific numbers: the public intellectual category has 65 nominations, podcaster of the year has 76, and Rising Stars—his personal favorite—already has 135 nominations, currently the largest category. None of these align with traditional job titles, revealing a massive population of people with strong internal company reputations but no public visibility, who need a window to be seen.

— David Booth
8:15

The real product isn't the network—it's your surface area for serendipity

David quotes Jobs: you can't plan to meet the person who changes your life, but you can place yourself on the path where luck travels and grab the opportunity when it appears. He applies this to Cosign—building profiles, populating the graph, browsing relationships—is fundamentally increasing the odds that future partners or the right person will spot you. He calls this ‘coincidence of intent’: when two people with complementary skills wanting to build the same thing can actually be matched by the system.

— David Booth
14:27

Three layers build the complete graph: profiles, lists, then co-signs

David breaks Cosign into three layers. The bottom is a Wikipedia-like profile system for 40,000 to 50,000 people, clickable through companies and investors. The middle layer is ‘lists’—about 400 people have published lists so far, like Packy McCormick's 36 companies he thinks are worth joining. The top layer is ‘co-sign’ itself, where someone formally tells the platform ‘I'd work with this person anywhere.’ All three together form the complete graph.

— David Booth
17:39

LinkedIn's search limits are a choice, not a technical constraint

Dhruv believes LinkedIn will never open an MCP that enables full-text search across its entire relationship graph—even with hundreds of thousands of accounts, ordinary users still can't find the people they need. He recounts his own experience: hunting for startups worth joining in New York, he asked Gemini, then ChatGPT, and both gave poor answers. His conclusion: factual ‘who's doing what’ questions fail because the underlying data simply doesn't exist. Cosign's graph, fed by real professional relationships, fills that gap.

— Dhruv Gupta
19:41

The next frontier: agents that negotiate on your behalf, not just write emails

Dhruv notes the personal-agent wave lasted only a month or two; Muse hit 10 million users, but even the most tailored agent today can only draft an email in your inbox, send it to theirs, and wait for their agent to pick it up and reply—essentially the old ‘send message, wait for response’ loop. He points to early examples like Instagon where agent-to-agent protocols are already emerging (such as car-rental customer-service integrations), imagining the future: when job hunting, your agent could negotiate a round with the company's agent, assess whether your intentions align, and only then decide whether to surface you to the actual hiring manager.

— Dhruv Gupta
21:42

Everyone's inbox treats all signals equally—and that's the core problem to solve

Dhruv points out that in today's social networks, Sam Altman's inbox and a regular person's inbox—even his 14-year-old sister's—work exactly the same way, with no signal-strength differentiation. This is exactly what Cosign wants to fix: ensuring you only spend time with people you should actually meet, rather than sifting through mountains of connection requests yourself.

— Dhruv Gupta

Glossary

MCP / Model Context Protocol
An open protocol enabling AI agents to access external data and tools.
coincidence of intent
The moment when two people's goals and needs align perfectly enough to warrant an introduction.
golden handcuffs
Salary and benefits structured to keep talented people from leaving their current employer.
agent-to-agent protocol
Direct negotiation between two AI agents acting on behalf of their respective users.
