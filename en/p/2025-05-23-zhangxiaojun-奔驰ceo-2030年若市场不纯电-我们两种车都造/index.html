Mercedes CEO: If the Market Isn't Fully Electric by 2030, We'll Build Both

Ola Källenius won't bet on a single technology path: he insists on launching a full EV lineup before 2030 while keeping both combustion and electric options. He judges China to be the fastest-moving, most competitive market — win there, and the odds of winning globally go up.

7:11

Don't bet on pure electric — build both

Källenius concedes that EV penetration in Europe is only about 13%, far below the 25% he expected five years ago, and under 10% in North America. His response is not to double down on EVs but to keep both options: stick to launching a full EV lineup before 2030, but if the global market hasn't reached 100% electric by 2030, offer both combustion and electric choices. He states plainly that the choice preserves brand value for customers rather than chasing numbers on the sales sheet. It is a textbook posture for a CEO refusing to bet one way when the technology path is uncertain.

— Ola Källenius
8:11

China's EV war isn't lost — the win is in high-end

The host presses with data: China's EV penetration rose from 4.7% in 2019 to 47.6% in 2024 — has Mercedes already lost? Källenius answers "absolutely not," reasoning that electrification is climbing from the bottom up, from entry-level city cars to mid-size sedans, while Mercedes dominates the S-Class segment and holds the highest share in the high-end sedan market. He admits the overall volume share is small but stresses that the high-end segment is Mercedes' battlefield, and the next fight is in entry-level SUVs and C-Class cars.

— Ola Källenius
16:19

China R&D shifts from for-China to for-the-world

Mercedes' old model was decisions made at German headquarters and executed elsewhere. Källenius says that has changed: many initiatives start in China, and China's work serves the whole world. He gives two concrete examples — the latest intelligent parking system was developed by the China R&D team and rolled out to global markets, and the all-new rear-seat entertainment system launching next year is also a China team development. He states plainly that more and more decisions are now not just in China for China, but in China for the world.

— Ola Källenius
20:23

MBOS is Mercedes' digital lifeline

Källenius uses a cooking metaphor: you can source ingredients from different places, but you must be the chef in charge. MBOS is that chef's tool — a holistic operating system that can integrate partners' software or be developed in-house. He stresses that software for core vehicle functions like the battery management system is written by Mercedes itself, with over 20 years of experience, the first EV being the Smart launched in 2007. His judgment: automakers need to be the architect of intelligent driving and control it, but they don't have to write every line of code — they can partner with Qualcomm, Tencent, Amap — the key is to be the conductor of the band.

— Ola Källenius
26:25

Battery efficiency is the new currency of EVs

Källenius says battery efficiency is like a new currency for electric vehicles. Mercedes has deep vertically integrated R&D capability in battery chemistry, while also choosing to develop batteries adapted to Mercedes with the best partners in the field. He reveals they are working with two or three partners on different versions of solid-state batteries, researching high-silicon-content anode batteries, and one day possibly achieving 100% silicon anode batteries. Recently they also worked with a company on new technology that let an EQS sedan drive over 1,000 km on a single charge. These are future technologies in R&D, to be used for customer needs only after testing and engineering are complete.

— Ola Källenius
29:42

AI drives safer than humans — the zero-accident vision

Källenius says AI-driven computers will make better decisions than human drivers; many accidents worldwide are caused to some degree by human error, and eliminating that brings us closer to the zero-accident vision Mercedes has long pursued. He gives a concrete fact: Mercedes is the world's first company to truly bring a fully certified L3-level car to market, starting in Germany with the US following, raising assisted driving speed from 60 km/h to 95 km/h. He calls this the most precious gift — getting some time back.

— Ola Källenius
33:53

DeepSeek proves innovation can happen overnight

Källenius recounts his reaction when he first read about DeepSeek: he called Gao Xu, the Chinese strategy chief working in both Beijing and Stuttgart, about the article; the reply was "let me take a look," and finally "it seems to be true." His judgment is that the company used original thinking to raise technology to a new level without spending much — this is not blind development. He says the company's success shows that with smarter methods, innovation can happen overnight, meaning in AI and generative AI development and how to use large language models, you can move faster and more economically.

— Ola Källenius
45:00

51 vs 49 decisions — if wrong, change

Källenius describes his decision-making framework: usually you know clearly what to do, either do it or don't, like choosing between A and B or 80 and 20. But sometimes due to uncertainty you don't know how the market will develop — that's like 51 and 49; normally you might pick 51, and that's very important, don't consider face. But if you find the situation is the opposite, that you originally picked 49, you must be prepared to change, because sooner or later you'll have to. His attitude is not to agonize, not to be trapped by what can't be undone; if you make a wrong decision, gather new information, be prepared, don't be afraid to lose face, and keep moving forward.

— Ola Källenius

Glossary

MBOS / Mercedes-Benz Operating System
Mercedes' self-developed holistic in-car operating system that can integrate partner software and control core functions like battery management.
L3 / Level 3 autonomous driving
A fully certified safety level, first permitted in Germany with the US following, raising assisted driving speed from 60 to 95 km/h.
CLTC / China Light-Duty Vehicle Test Cycle
China's official range test standard; the highest-range version of the all-new CLA is certified at 866 km.
ECU / Electronic Control Unit
Electronic control modules in a car; a traditional car may have over a hundred, each requiring a separate software package to be purchased and integrated.
