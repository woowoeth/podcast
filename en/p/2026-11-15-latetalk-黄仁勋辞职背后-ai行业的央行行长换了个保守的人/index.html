Major transformation requires founders, not professional managers

Tod argues strategic shifts demand founders, not managers maintaining operations. In 33 years, Huang drove 4–5 major transformations: 2006 CUDA ecosystem against board resistance, 2013 deep learning bet, 2019 Mellanox acquisition for datacenter shift. A professional manager wouldn't have made these calls. Nvidia has no clear number-two; Huang directly manages 60+ executives, creating governance single-point-of-failure risk upon departure.

AI industry's central banker replaced by a conservative

Liu Yilun metaphorically frames Huang as AI's central bank chief, aggressively providing industry liquidity—including backing $250B to OpenAI and 8G watts of infrastructure. His replacement, the ex-CFO, is historically cautious and previously opposed such massive guarantees that Huang overruled. Leadership change risks shifting industry liquidity from accommodative to restrictive, an impact deeper than any stock price move.

— Liu Yilun
8:31

Nvidia faced a founder-or-bust moment even without the scandal

Tod outlines three structural challenges meeting founder departure: (1) Nvidia extracts 70–75% of industry gross margin; everyone else subsidizes it, sparking eventual reversal; (2) like GPU-to-ASIC transition in Bitcoin mining history, once Transformer architecture and parameter scale stabilize, everyone shifts to custom ASIC chips, eroding GPU's generalist premium; (3) CUDA is fundamentally software, and AI translation tech reduces cross-architecture code pain, weakening CUDA's moat. These converge at the worst moment.

— Tod
11:48

Founder premium is evaporating as Nvidia flat-lined over a year

The rally commenced late 2022 on monetary loosening and GPT-3.5 establishing AI infrastructure narrative. Since mid-2025, stock barely moved despite earnings delivery—textbook value-trap dynamics of valuation compression alongside rising earnings. Critical point: long-term holders weathered 20–30% swings unchanged because they believed in Huang's industry leadership. That ‘founder premium’ dissolves with him; justifying long-term valuation ceiling becomes impossible.

— Tod
17:07

AMD and Broadcom caught Nvidia's losses but can't solve long-term problems

Nvidia stock declines saw AMD and Broadcom rally in tandem—Taiwan Semiconductor reallocating advanced packaging capacity. AMD's TSMC advanced packaging share rose from 9% in 2026 to 20% in 2027; freed capacity likely went to AMD and Broadcom (TPU manufacturer). This represents short-term trading reallocation, not structural shift. CapEx growth peaking, Democratic administration potentially constraining US datacenter builds, China compute business off-limits—structural headwinds hit AMD and Broadcom equally. Fundamental beta upside exhausted.

— Tod
21:11

Crushing competition backfires: blocking domestic players forces genuine innovation

Tod offers counterintuitively: if Nvidia's void can't be filled by AMD and Google TPU, only domestic GPU steps in—‘赌不如输’. True defeat of rivals requires market flooding with cheap goods leaving domestic players zero development space; instead, access blocks create exactly that space. Liu adds: domestic card makers' constraint isn't demand but upstream capacity (SMIC, North Chip). DeepSeek already adapts using open-source CUDA alternatives like TileLang; mainland GPU training transition may accelerate.

Huang must prove he didn't know, or knew but opposed it

Tod judges Huang's short-term return nearly impossible. This scandal fundamentally targets Trump, not Huang directly—standard playbook: damage reputation, reassign from key roles, construct charges, publish reputation reversal. Huang's immediate task: prove he didn't know about high-performance chip shipments reaching unauthorized destinations, or knew but disagreed and subordinates acted without authority. Failing to distance himself risks criminal exposure.

— Tod
57:10

US chip export controls now extend four layers deep

Liu systematically maps America's export control infrastructure: (1) destination controls—advanced chips to mainland China and Macau require license; (2) ultimate parent penetration—entities registered in Singapore or Cayman face restrictions if HQ is mainland; (3) end-user and end-use audits—even foreign buyers trigger controls if sellers know chips will transfer to restricted entities; (4) foreign direct product rule—overseas foundries like TSMC and Samsung face controls on chips using designated US technology even if produced abroad. This architecture continues tightening.

— Liu Yilun
1:01:48

Tech giants face hidden conflicts allocating compute resources internally

Liu reveals Alibaba Cloud's bind: to maximize revenue, it must rent capacity to external model vendors like Kimi and Zhipu, but Tongyi Qianwen uses Alibaba Cloud without internal billing, removing sales incentive to reserve cards for internal use. Balancing internal and external demand remains unresolved. Tencent takes the compliance path, using H20-class chips internally while offering third parties only Kunlun and Haiguang chips. Independent model companies chronically shortage capacity, exploring token-split: capacity providers earn 70%, model providers 30%.

— Liu Yilun

Glossary

ASIC
Application-specific integrated circuit custom-designed for particular algorithms; higher performance but less flexible than GPU.
CUDA
Nvidia's GPU programming ecosystem; the driver layer connecting hardware circuits to upper-layer software and a core part of Nvidia's competitive moat.
TileLang
Open-source operator-writing tool from Peking University researchers; analogous to Triton in CUDA ecosystem, helping escape Nvidia's software stack.
Foreign direct product rule
US export control mechanism treating any overseas chip using designated US technology as controlled even if produced outside America.
