Two million British elderly live in quiet poverty—they'd rather lie to family than reach out

Though eligible for pension credits, only 63% of qualifying elderly apply. What stops them isn't the missing money, but dread of admitting hardship to family and fear of putting what little they have at risk.

Elderly in food bank queues mark a hidden 2-million-person poverty crisis

Kingsley Purdom says the common view of elderly as affluent masks a real crisis: roughly 2 million older Britons live below the relative income poverty line. His team began studying children using food banks and noticed elderly people in the queues too—prompting a pivot to research elderly daily life directly. Interview subjects describe feeling ‘invisible,’ saying ‘nobody wants to know you when you're old.’

— Kingsley Purdom
5:18

Half of eligible elderly forgo benefits from shame and fear

Pension Credit, a controversial welfare program, has a deeper problem: only about 63% of those who qualify actually apply—and in doing so, forfeit linked benefits like healthcare and housing support. Kingsley argues the real debate should be whether money reaches those who need it most, not whether to spend at all.

— Kingsley Purdom
6:18

Scam victims lose faith in welfare: a £7,000 phone fraud consequence

Many elderly won't seek help partly because they don't know where to turn, partly because they've lost trust in systems. Kingsley's research includes an elderly person defrauded by a phone scammer posing as a washing-machine repairman, who demanded roughly £7,000 and threatened police. Such betrayals teach the elderly to distrust even genuine offers of support.

— Kingsley Purdom
8:31

Means-testing defeats itself: the poorest face the hardest barriers to help

Beveridge, who founded the British welfare state, warned that means-tested benefits would backfire. Today that fear is real: those most vulnerable face the most obstacles. Pension Credit applications run over 100 questions; phone support exists but face-to-face is scarce. Research shows face-to-face support is what actually gets money into elderly hands.

— Kingsley Purdom
11:42

Lone elderly women should get priority help: they live longest, poorest

Kingsley's conclusion: don't raise pension levels broadly (like the triple lock debate), but target the 2 million in relative poverty, especially lone elderly women. They live longer than men and face greater financial and health fragility—they deserve priority within an ‘active ageing’ framework.

— Kingsley Purdom
14:50

British elderly gain 20 years of life but lose them to disease

Judith Phillips leads the Healthy Ageing Challenge, a £98 million government initiative to ‘add life to years, not years to life.’ UK life expectancy now reaches 81, but healthy life expectancy stops at 62—nearly 20 years of living with chronic disease. The gap widens in poorer regions.

— Judith Phillips
19:37

Design for ageing capability, not care for ageing decline

Judith reframes the narrative: old age is not inevitable decay but opportunity—for individuals, for business, for growth. Healthy ageing becomes ‘the process of developing and maintaining functional capacity to do what matters to you’—bringing environment, capability, and equity into the definition, not just bodily health.

— Judith Phillips
26:56

Boxed memory travel brings the outside world to homebound elderly

Under the Connecting Through Culture program, Tabletop Travels sends curated boxes to homebound elderly—a ‘Sweden box’ with Swedish food, drink, and history—letting them relive past travels through taste and smell. Some care homes have now adopted the model.

— Judith Phillips

Glossary

Pension Credit
UK government means-tested welfare supplement for low-income elderly.
means-tested benefits
Welfare requiring income verification; typically involve complex forms and higher claiming barriers.
triple lock
UK pension indexing rule: pensions rise annually by the highest of wage growth, inflation, or 2.5%.
co-production
Involving service users as active partners in designing and conducting research and programs.
Beveridge
Founder of the British welfare state; warned that means-tested benefits could backfire on the poorest.
