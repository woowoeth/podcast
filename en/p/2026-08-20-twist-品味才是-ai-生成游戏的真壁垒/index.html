AI-generated games don't lack technology — they lack the taste of human hand-tuning

AI can turn a sentence into a playable game, but the real moat isn't generation — it's human taste and manual tuning: LLMs only copy, they don't innovate, and the editor is the second work the model generates for the user.

1:06

What gets supply moving isn't generation, it's publish-to-distribute

Astrocade isn't positioned as a ‘game generator’ but as an interactive content platform: games are just the largest category within interactive experiences, and educational content, birthday cards and the like count too. Users describe something in natural language, AI generates the content, and one tap publishes it into the feed, where you swipe up and down like TikTok. It already has 100k+ creators from over 100 countries. Jason's read is that the skill barrier to making games has been compressed from years of study and internships into a single prompt; but what actually gets supply moving is the publish-to-distribute product form.

— Amir
6:36

Users aren't lazy, the friction is just too high

The company's first office was at the founder's home, where the fridge was full of small, grab-and-go bottles of watermelon juice that were finished in two days; after switching to half-liter bottles, nobody touched them for two weeks, until cups were placed next to them and demand returned. From this the founding team distilled Astrocade's product theory: users aren't lazy, the friction is too high; remove the big obstacle of ‘making games takes 5-7 years of learning’ and demand appears on its own. This analogy explains why the platform has to make generation, publishing and playtesting one single chain.

— Amir
7:45

Experiences absent from the training data, AI cannot produce

After two and a half years working with creators, the team openly admits AI's limits: AI still can't make a complete game; for genres with massive training data like Flappy Bird it can copy, but the moment a new experience is involved, or an existing one needs changing, AI starts to struggle. That's why the best games on the platform all go through multiple rounds of wish — their name for a prompt — of iteration. This judgment pushes ‘fully automatic generation’ back to ‘human-led, AI-assisted’, and it also dictates that the editor must exist.

— Amir
11:01

A game's numbers can't be handed to a probabilistic model

‘AI in the loop’ doesn't mean fully automatic. Which upgrade tree, how to add gamification, how to configure the numbers — creators still choose; even changing a single variable from 25% to 50% makes a game boring because it's a guaranteed win. Amir's method is to let natural language describe structure and numeric input handle precise tuning: because LLMs are probabilistic output, while in a game ‘three lives, Boss 1000 HP, sword 10 damage’ requires determinism and reproducibility. This is a rare model of ‘deterministic vs. non-deterministic division of labor’ in generative products.

— Amir
13:03

The only moat is taste, not the model

LLMs have been trained on everything, so generated results slide toward the average, which makes ‘looks like every other game’ the norm; if you want a distinctive theme and art style, you have to define it more specifically. Amir's conclusion: the only moat is taste, taste is the new programming language. The platform also plans to retrain the model on creator data so it understands this cohort's preferences better over time. Translated into platform strategy: use human taste to set direction first, then use tailored data for incremental evolution.

— Amir
16:16

Paying by views has already produced full-time creators

Astrocade already pays creators by views, and some do games and interactive experiences full-time, earning thousands of dollars a month. Amir thinks this is replaying the market mechanism by which YouTube produced MrBeast. Jason pressed on whether it can go from consumer to prosumer, onto app stores, with in-app purchases; Amir first gave an existing scenario: someone turned a friend's photo into an interactive birthday card; then returned to the revenue question, saying some already do content full-time and earn thousands of dollars a month, and the platform is paying by views, forming a new creator economy the way YouTube produced MrBeast.

— Amir
18:10

Play is humanity's universal language, not a toy business

Why build interactive content at the scale of ‘a billion people’? Amir gives two reasons: first, impact — he wants to build consumer products, and B2B is heavily commoditized; second, play is humanity's most intrinsic universal language, and with two kids of his own he can see the primitiveness of play in them. Jason steers toward neuroplasticity: constantly playing new games and learning variant rules requires restructuring existing heuristics, which is great brain exercise. This judgment that ‘play is the underlying operating system’ explains why the AI-generated game category is not just a toy.

— Amir

Glossary

wish
Astrocade's name for a prompt; each time a user modifies or iterates on a requirement, that's one wish.
post-training
Continuing to train on top of a base model with specific data so the model gets better at generating interactive content.
creator fund
A mechanism where the platform pays creators by views; Astrocade already runs one.
prosumer
Both consumer and producer — an intermediate state between ordinary player and professional creator.
