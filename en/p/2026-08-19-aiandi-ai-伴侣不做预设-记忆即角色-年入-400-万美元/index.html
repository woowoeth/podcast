AI Companion Pulls In $4M a Year: No Preset Plot, Improvising Off Memory

Portola's Tolon ditches preset plots and character sheets, using memory as the character sheet and generation as improvisation, hitting $4M in annualized revenue. This "improv actor" methodology and mixed-model strategy is a product path outside of ChatGPT.

This episode isn't about the large-model arms race, it's about the "content craft" of AI-native products: how to define a good conversation, control latency, and use human editors as bridges. For application-layer teams it's frontline experience, not just opinion.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:00

Novelists are better suited to build AI companions than technical teams

Portola's AI companion hit $4M in annualized revenue, and went from $1M to $4M in just the past four weeks. Founder Quentin previously founded Even, which sold to Walmart for $300M; co-founder Elliot is a sci-fi author who has written 11 books. The AI character Clarence demoed at the open is a cat that teases the user, with natural emotion. This background makes the point: this is a "storyteller plus serial entrepreneur" combo building an embodied AI companion, not a technical team forcing its way into narrative.

— Host
10:00

Don't look for a pain point first, wait for model capability to hit a threshold

Quentin explains their motivation for pivoting from B2B SaaS to AI companions: the curves of generation speed, quality, and cost made them believe "you can have a real friendship with an AI." They initially built AI creative tools for children, got poor market feedback, and pivoted to young adults, especially women aged 18-24 — a group willing to invest time in emotional companionship. Note the logic here: they first saw the threshold of model capability, then looked for the people who most need a "relationship," rather than defining a pain point first and then finding a solution.

— Quentin
22:01

Half a second of latency is an emotional incident, not a performance issue

Quentin stresses that "memory" is not a database query, but how you choose memories to inject into the prompt. They found response time must stay under two seconds, or users churn. Once, because they added a reflection step, response time slowed to 2.5 seconds and every product metric dropped across the board. This shows that in AI companion products, technical latency directly affects emotional experience — users read "waiting" as "being snubbed." The choice of memory injection is itself product design, not a purely technical problem.

— Quentin
29:49

Narrative can't be pre-designed, it can only be picked up line by line

Elliot describes how their initial attempt at structured narrative (three-act structure) failed, and the model couldn't handle complex branching. They later shifted to a "hook" method: don't give the model an outline, teach it to pick up lines like an improv actor, borrowing the improv principles from Keith Johnstone's "Impro." The core difference: structured narrative is "set the ending first and work backward," improv is "just catch the last line and push the scene one step forward." Under this framework every turn of Tolon's conversation is an unpredictable branch, which also explains why they later introduced "situations" rather than "scripts."

— Elliot
39:52

The world shouldn't be defined first, memory grows into the character sheet on its own

Contrary to the Lucasfilm approach of defining a "character sheet" and "worldview" first, Tolon's characters and world all grow out of conversation. Internally they call them "situations" — circumstances the user and AI experience together, which eventually become memories, and memories become the character sheet. Elliot says this is more like Kevin Kelly's "infinite intern," but more accurately an "infinite improv actor": every conversation forks into an unforeseeable new direction. Because every user is different, the company is effectively running a "multiverse" rather than a single world.

— Elliot
1:01:02

What counts as a good conversation has to be defined by humans, not algorithms

Managing the chaos of the "multiverse" requires tooling. They use a "judge prompt" to evaluate conversation quality: first test new content at small scale, have research participants assess completeness, then write that evaluation standard into a prompt. It's like giving the "improv actor" an "improv editor." At the same time they constantly refactor "lower seeds" (the building blocks of world-building), and understand users' emotional connection points through research and interviews. Here you can see: scaling an AI companion doesn't rely on pure algorithms, but on a lot of humans defining "what a good conversation is."

— Elliot
1:04:11

Don't bet on a single model, split tasks by scenario

Tolon doesn't depend on a single model, but mixes several labs: Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic handle generation, Gemini is used for the memory system. Elliot says Anthropic is especially strong for creative writing, but is limited in latency-sensitive interaction scenarios; GPT-4o is described as a "behemoth" — fast, reliable, reasonably priced. The key to judging model quality is injecting "your own taste" into the prompt, rather than vaguely asking "how is this essay." The value of this passage for application-layer teams is: don't bet on a single model, allocate tasks by scenario.

— Elliot
1:15:25

The entry point is no longer a chat box, but a character with personality

Dan Shipper distills it at the end: users treat AI characters as "daemons" (from "The Golden Compass"), and the first entry point to all knowledge becomes this character — this is called "character-driven computing," a new HCI paradigm. He also draws an analogy to car history: ChatGPT is like the Model T, amazing but without personality; once cars became part of personal life, people wanted Mustangs and Cadillacs. He asserts we are accelerating this process. Finally he says this kind of creation is more like an artist than a scientist: not solving problems, but "creating what the world is missing."

— Dan Shipper

Glossary

judge prompt
Writing evaluation criteria as a prompt so the model automatically judges AI conversation quality, equivalent to giving an improv actor an editor.
lower seeds
Pre-written world-building building blocks in Tolon, from which the AI draws situational material during conversation.
situations
Narrative circumstances the user and AI experience together, which settle into memories and form the character sheet.
daemon
The embodied externalization of a person's soul in "The Golden Compass," used as a metaphor for the entry point binding users to AI characters.
