AI Data Centers Run 24/7: Chip Depreciation Is 5 to 10 Times More Expensive Than Electricity

In AI inference, chip depreciation is five to ten times the cost of electricity; in Bitcoin mining, it's only 1.5 to three times. This cost ratio difference explains why AI data centers run continuously rather than turning on and off to save electricity like mining rigs.

Stranded power plants funded miners first, then supplied AI factories

Industrial users often build on-site power plants rather than connect to the grid. When those users shut down, the plants become ‘stranded generation’—power still flows but has no buyer. Bitcoin miners were the ideal customer: flexible location, unlimited demand. CoreWeave rented GPUs to Ethereum miners using existing power contracts. When Ethereum demand waned, the same H100-filled data centers pivoted to AI inference and training, where the profit per megawatt was far higher.

Electricity flows too fast to store; grids survive on engineering workarounds

Power storage is prohibitively expensive relative to its value—not a limitation of battery chemistry alone, but physics itself. Electricity travels at roughly 0.6 times light speed through ordinary transmission lines. Power generators, especially nuclear reactors, need hours to ramp output up or down. These timescales are fundamentally mismatched. Electric grids survive only because of engineering compromises.

7:57

At 93 percent capacity, temperature swings alone can crash Tokyo's grid

Electricity demand peaks in afternoon and evening, driven on weekdays mainly by residential air conditioning and heating. Tokyo's grid needs about 20,000 MW just to keep the city running—this is its ‘base load’. Between that baseline and maximum capacity sits an 18,000 MW gap. The grid normally operates at roughly 90 percent of peak capacity, which is safe. But once demand approaches 93 to 97 percent, even a small temperature swing can push residential AC demand over the limit, risking shortage.

— Patrick McKenzie
9:53

Nuclear fission stops instantly; residual steam means the plant never does

What power plants can be flexibly dispatched is determined by physics and economics. Nuclear reactors can halt their chain reaction in seconds, but residual heat keeps steam driving turbines for hours—this locks nuclear into base-load operation. Coal plants face the same economic lock-in. Solar, wind, and some natural gas plants offer flexibility but at higher cost; they occupy a middle tier. Peaker plants, mostly fossil-fueled, have the worst marginal cost but can start and stop instantly. They might run only a few hours per day, or a few weeks per year.

— Patrick McKenzie
13:34

A contract to save power is economically equivalent to a power plant

An operator promising customers 40 megawatts but authorized to reduce service to 30 megawatts on five minutes' notice is economically equivalent to owning a hidden 10-megawatt power plant. SoftBank applies this logic to residential customers: it forecasts peak demand 24 to 48 hours ahead, calculates each household's typical use, and offers payment if that household uses one kilowatt-hour less during the predicted peak. There's no penalty for using more. Users click once to enroll. SoftBank's payment company delivers the reward in near-real-time at nearly zero marginal cost.

— Patrick McKenzie
13:34

Bitcoin miners held the spare capacity ERCOT desperately needed in crisis

During Texas's 2023 heat wave, ERCOT's grid was in acute crisis. Riot Bitcoin Mining held a forward contract giving it power at a fixed rate. It sold that power back to ERCOT at the peak spot price, earning tens of millions in a single transaction. The public blamed Riot for worsening the shortage. The author argues the opposite: miners represent ‘deferrable load’—they normally consume power that would go to waste. In an emergency, they become spare capacity the grid can activate instantly.

— Patrick McKenzie
21:05

AI chip costs are 5x electricity costs; Bitcoin miners pay 1.5x

For Bitcoin miners, chip depreciation is only 1.5 to three times the cost of electricity. This makes them hypersensitive to power prices—they search globally for stranded power and play sophisticated demand-response games to capture every marginal gain. For AI inference, chip depreciation is five to ten times the cost of electricity. Power is a minor fraction of total operating cost. The default strategy is simple: connect as much electrical capacity as available and run continuously. This makes data centers behave like base-load demand rather than flexible virtual power plants.

Glossary

Demand response
Grid notifies users in advance to reduce or defer electricity use in exchange for payment.
Virtual power plant
Aggregates flexible electricity consumption through contracts and software to function like a physical power plant.
Base load
The minimum electricity demand a grid must satisfy continuously, every hour of every day.
Peaker plant
Operates only during peak demand periods; has the highest marginal cost but can start and stop instantly.
Stranded generation
Generating equipment that loses its intended industrial customer and cannot find a new buyer.
CapEx / OpEx
CapEx = one-time capital investment in equipment; OpEx = daily operational expenses.
