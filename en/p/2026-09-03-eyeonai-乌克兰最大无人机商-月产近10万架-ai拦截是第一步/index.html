Ukraine's Largest Drone Maker: Nearly 100,000 a Month, AI Interception Is Only Step One

Ukrainian drone maker General Cherry produces nearly 100,000 drones a month, and its interceptors take down Russian Shaheds costing over $50,000 at a cost of about $2,000 each. AI guidance is already in combat, but full autonomy is still far off.

4:07

Nearly 100,000 a month, and capacity is still expanding

General Cherry was founded in 2023 and initially produced only about 10 drones a month; today monthly output reaches about 100,000 (the exact figure is confidential). The company has worked with more than 110 military brigades, and the best units consume more than 5,000 drones a month. Capacity is still rising, because battlefield consumption is enormous and drones have become expendables.

— Marko Kushnir
7:09

Ukraine has hundreds of drone companies, but only 10-15 at the top

The number of drone companies in Ukraine may be as high as 2,000, but the vast majority are garage-scale workshops. Only 10 to 15 large companies can truly produce 1,000 to 10,000 a month, and General Cherry claims to be in the top five. This fragmented yet enormous industrial ecosystem is the basis of Ukraine's rapid innovation, but it also means fierce competition.

— Marko Kushnir
8:14

AI models are built in-house, co-created deeply with the military

General Cherry's AI guidance system is entirely self-developed, backed by a strong R&D department, and the company works closely with different military units at the front. Mobile teams gather battlefield feedback every day so products can be iterated quickly. This ‘R&D-combat-feedback’ loop lets Ukrainian drone technology adapt rapidly to battlefield changes rather than depending on outside procurement.

— Marko Kushnir
10:20

The rocket-shaped FPV came from Red Bull, not Ukraine

This rocket-shaped FPV drone is not a Ukrainian invention; Red Bull first used it in 2021 or 2022 for sporting events. Ukrainian companies found that the shape could reach higher speeds than ordinary quadcopters, so they adopted and optimised it. This reflects the rapid learning and pragmatism of Ukraine's drone industry — it does not chase originality, only effectiveness.

— Marko Kushnir
17:24

A $2,000 interceptor against a $50,000 Shahed

General Cherry's interceptor drones (such as Bullet) cost about $2,000 but can shoot down Russian Shahed drones that cost $50,000 to $100,000. This asymmetric cost exchange is Ukraine's key strategy for dealing with Russia's ‘saturation attacks’ — using cheap drones to drain the enemy's expensive assets while avoiding wasting the expensive air-defence missiles supplied by the West.

— Marko Kushnir
21:28

Interceptors have confirmed more than 5,000 Russian kills

Since production began in February 2025, General Cherry's tactical interceptor drone (General Trajectory Air) has confirmed more than 5,000 Russian drones shot down, including more than 1,000 Russian ‘Moniya’ drones in December alone. The model that intercepts Shaheds also entered mass production in December 2025, with confirmed kills approaching 1,000. These numbers show that interceptor drones have become an important force in battlefield air defence.

— Marko Kushnir
23:28

The AI runs on the device, not in the cloud

General Cherry's AI guidance model runs on the camera chip aboard the drone itself, not in the cloud. After an operator detects a target by radar and launches the drone, the AI identifies and locks onto the target automatically, with no human intervention. This on-device AI design avoids battlefield communication latency and jamming, and is the key to making it viable in real combat.

— Marko Kushnir
27:32

Fully autonomous weapons are a terrifying future, but AI is now unavoidable

Marko admits that if drones became fully autonomous it would be terrifying — killing would no longer require another person, only a robot or a drone. At present they use semi-autonomous AI only on interceptor drones, because adding AI to ordinary FPVs significantly raises cost, and it is better to buy a few more without AI. But he predicts AI technology will spread quickly and prices will fall, making future battlefields even more brutal.

— Marko Kushnir

Glossary

Shahed
An Iranian-designed suicide drone used by Russia in large numbers for deep strikes.
FPV
A drone flown via real-time video feed from an onboard camera, often used for suicide attacks.
Interceptor drone
A drone designed specifically to shoot down other drones, at a cost far below that of its target.
Moniya
A cheap Russian reconnaissance/strike drone, shot down in large numbers by Ukrainian forces.
