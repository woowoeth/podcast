Ukraine's Largest Drone Maker: Nearly 100,000 a Month, AI Interception Is Just the First Step

Ukrainian drone maker General Cherry produces nearly 100,000 drones a month, with interceptor drones costing about $2,000 to shoot down Russian Shaheds worth over $50,000. AI guidance is battle-tested, but full autonomy remains distant.

4:07

Producing nearly 100,000 a month, capacity still expanding

Founded in 2023, General Cherry initially produced only about 10 drones a month; now monthly output is around 100,000 (exact number undisclosed). The company works with over 110 military brigades, and the best units consume more than 5,000 drones a month. Capacity is still growing because battlefield consumption is enormous—drones have become expendable.

— Marko Kushnir
7:09

Hundreds of Ukrainian drone firms, but only 10–15 at the top

Ukraine may have as many as 2,000 drone companies, but the vast majority are garage-style small workshops. Only 10 to 15 large firms can produce 1,000 to 10,000 a month, and General Cherry claims to be in the top five. This fragmented yet vast ecosystem is the basis for Ukraine's rapid innovation, but it also means fierce competition.

— Marko Kushnir
8:14

AI models developed in-house, deep co-creation with the military

General Cherry's AI guidance system is fully self-developed, backed by a strong R&D department, and built in close cooperation with military units on the front line. They gather battlefield feedback daily through mobile teams and iterate products quickly. This R&D-combat-feedback loop lets Ukrainian drone tech adapt to the battlefield fast, rather than relying on external procurement.

— Marko Kushnir
10:20

Rocket-shaped FPV originated with Red Bull, not Ukraine

This rocket-shaped FPV drone is not a Ukrainian original; it was first used by Red Bull in 2021 or 2022 for sports events. Ukrainian companies found the shape could reach higher speeds than ordinary quadcopters, so they adopted and optimized it. This reflects the fast-learning, pragmatic nature of Ukraine's drone industry—not seeking originality, only effectiveness.

— Marko Kushnir
17:24

A $2,000 interceptor drone against a $50,000 Shahed

General Cherry's interceptor drones (like the Bullet) cost about $2,000, yet can shoot down Russian Shahed drones worth $50,000 to $100,000. This asymmetric cost exchange is key to Ukraine's strategy against Russia's saturation attacks—using cheap drones to exhaust expensive enemy assets while avoiding wasting Western-supplied expensive air-defense missiles.

— Marko Kushnir
21:28

Interceptors confirmed to have downed over 5,000 Russian drones

General Cherry's tactical interceptor drones (General Trajectory Air) have confirmed over 5,000 Russian drone kills since production began in February 2025, with more than 1,000 Russian Moniya drones downed in December alone. The Shahed-interceptor model also entered mass production in December 2025, with confirmed kills nearing 1,000. These numbers show interceptors have become a significant force in battlefield air defense.

— Marko Kushnir
23:28

AI runs on the device, not in the cloud

General Cherry's AI guidance model runs on the drone's onboard camera chip, not in the cloud. The operator detects the target via radar, launches the drone, and the AI automatically identifies and locks on without human intervention. This on-device AI avoids battlefield communication delays and jamming, which is key to practical viability.

— Marko Kushnir
27:32

Fully autonomous weapons are a terrifying future, but AI is inevitable

Marko admits that if drones become fully autonomous, it will be terrifying—killing would no longer require another human, just a robot or drone. Currently they use semi-autonomous AI only on interceptor drones, because adding AI to ordinary FPVs would significantly raise costs, making it better to buy more non-AI drones. But he predicts AI technology will spread quickly and prices will fall, making future battlefields even more brutal.

— Marko Kushnir
