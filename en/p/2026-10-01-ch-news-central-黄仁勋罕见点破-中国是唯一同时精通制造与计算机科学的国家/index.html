Jensen Huang's rare admission: China alone masters both manufacturing and computer science

Before Chancellor Merz, Jensen Huang argued that after fifty years of US outsourcing to China, only three industrial powers remain—Germany, Japan, and China—with China uniquely excelling at both manufacturing and computer science.

Industrial AIDigital twinsPhysical AIChina-German cooperationManufacturing geopoliticsAI regulation
Nvidia's CEO discusses geopolitical industrial order, data sovereignty, and AI regulation at an official German event—his rare public assessment of China's dual manufacturing-and-computing advantage warrants close reading.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

3:06

Nvidia entered automotive through workstations, backed by a German partnership

In 2000, Nvidia was only a PC game graphics company. German partner ELSA brought it into workstations, then automotive. Audi installed Nvidia chips in 2005. By 2020, Nvidia was developing autonomous driving with Mercedes CEO Ola Källenius—a relationship spanning two decades, well before this AI boom.

— Jensen Huang
4:11

Every company will soon run two factories: physical and digital

In the future, every company operates a real factory and a digital factory ‘so realistic it barely differs from physical existence’. Most German workers will labor in their company's digital twin, designing ‘digital cars with digital brains’ while AI-powered robots handle manufacturing. The mission shifts: not just making products, but architecting the intelligence inside them.

— Jensen Huang
6:13

Mercedes' new S-Class will drive itself by year end

On stage, Chancellor Merz unveiled the new S-Class that Mercedes CEO Ola Källenius gave him, announcing the car will drive autonomously in Germany before year-end—the safest and most autonomous version. Merz used this to show Germany's goal: not just EU leadership, but global frontline status in AI-industry fusion, requiring Mittelstand to embrace data for AI.

— Friedrich Merz
9:19

General AI will never replace specialized industrial knowledge

Huang advised Germany: welcome AI to process your data, but never export data, culture, and expertise overseas to reimport ‘foreign intelligence’. Data is national resource. The next phase is physical AI—robots, manufacturing, heavy industry. Specialized knowledge in these domains will never be automated away. AI enhances capability; it cannot displace it.

— Jensen Huang
12:24

US outsourcing left Germany, Japan, and China as the only three

Huang traced two industrial eras: electrification (Germany, US, Japan as equals), then digitalization. Over fifty years, America outsourced heavy manufacturing to China. Today's three powers: Germany, Japan, China. Huang noted that China uniquely excels in both deep manufacturing and deep computer science because its economic transformation coincided with computing's rise. Chancellor Merz agreed, calling US manufacturing outsourcing ‘a major mistake’.

— Jensen Huang
18:34

Nvidia claims to be one of Germany's largest venture investors

Huang revealed that Nvidia invested nearly one billion dollars in Germany last year. This week, the company invested in four companies; it also supports German firms including agile robots, reflex aerospace, black forest labs. Half-joking with other VCs, Huang warned: ‘you'd better hurry, or I'll capture all the best deals.’ Nvidia employs 1,000 in Berlin, with core innovations emerging from its Berlin office.

— Jensen Huang
21:42

Regulate AI applications, not the technology itself

On AI regulation, Huang outlined three pillars: First, industry invents safety technology—not hope, but engineering. Second, companies follow best practices, especially young startups. Third, governments regulate specific applications, not AI itself. Internet regulation is hard; regulating specific apps on the internet is straightforward. Safety and capability aren't a trade-off; you need both.

— Jensen Huang
22:44

Merz carved out machinery from EU-wide AI rules

Merz responded that he successfully exempted the machinery sector from the EU's unified AI framework, granting it tailored rules instead of blanket restrictions. He called this a ‘major achievement’. Merz also noted that half of Germany's new startups over two years are AI firms, demonstrating positive correlation between regulatory flexibility and startup activity.

— Friedrich Merz
