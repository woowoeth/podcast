The Lite-App Red Envelope Isn't Growth — It's a Harvester of Algorithm and Monetization

After the demographic dividend disappeared, the red envelope, the lite app and the coupon playbook stopped being about growth and became about catching traffic with monetization and algorithms; in the AI era, buying traffic has nearly stopped working, and growth comes back to model capability and mind-share marketing.

3:13

Why growth hacking is obsolete

Tom argues that growth hacking was already expired by 2026: the global mobile internet has been fully cultivated, giants hold a monopoly (ByteDance and Tencent at home; Meta/Google controlling seventy to eighty percent of traffic abroad), and thorough monetization has drained organic traffic dry. Platform risk control and user mind-share both keep getting stronger, so high-leverage arbitrage tricks stop working. In the AI era product supply is no longer scarce, which makes growth more necessary than ever — but the old methods don't work.

— Tom
36:47

The coin model pollutes feed recommendation

Toutiao copied Qutoutiao into a lite app, then found that text-and-image news doesn't suit the pay-to-read model: users read content to complete a task and earn coins, not out of genuine interest, so they pick the short items and swipe away fast, badly disrupting the recommendation algorithm. Free novels and short video, by contrast, fit — story content tolerates inserted ads, and short video has both longer watch time and higher monetization efficiency. That's why Midu, Fanqie and the Douyin/Kuaishou lite apps are the ones that worked.

— Tom
57:17

Why big-company lite apps are doomed

The lite apps from iQiyi, Tencent Video and others all failed, and Tom breaks it into three layers: first, organizational mismatch — long-video companies run on an editor-in-chief/producer system, with no recommendation algorithm and no short-video content production pipeline; second, thin content supply — only film and variety clips, which can't sustain swipe-up watch time; third, business-model conflict — free ad-supported viewing cannibalizes paid membership. Douyin and Kuaishou lite apps succeed because their content is all-encompassing and their monetization can carry it (ads, livestreaming, e-commerce).

— Tom
1:23:21

The K-factor of fission

Fission can be mathematized: only when the K-factor is above 1 can a product scale, and above 2 is a very good product. Travel Frog was measured at a fission coefficient above 10, growing exponentially; Toutiao's Million Heroes was around 3; a Taiwan product Tom built early on exploited a Facebook login loophole so that one user could bring in 7, and Facebook itself featured it as a case study. But such opportunities depend on the WeChat/Facebook social graph or on timing you can't count on, and because ByteDance has no social graph of its own, it has struggled to recreate a viral fission hit.

— Tom
1:35:02

With no social graph, attack the OS permissions

After WeChat blocked sharing, ByteDance products turned sharing into exploitation of the phone's low-level permissions: a few cents to induce users to open up contacts, photo album, Bluetooth and more. The Douyin contacts that appear when you tap share come from address-book and photo-album face recognition; put on earphones and Soda pops up, using RT reactivation technology to detect Bluetooth state. The photo album, carrying GPS location and personal relationships, becomes a data source. Tom warns users to be careful about granting ‘always allow’.

— Tom
1:54:08

Dou Shengsheng's redemption rate overtakes Meituan

Douyin Local Life made coupon redemption rate its North Star metric, and after raising it from under twenty percent to a fairly high level, on certain days late last year Beijing in-store transaction volume surpassed Meituan's in-store business. A higher redemption rate means user mind-share shifted from ‘scrolling into a discount’ to ‘coming deliberately to spend’, and so the standalone app Dou Shengsheng was born. For merchants and users alike, Douyin coupons now by default rank ahead of Meituan's, especially in lower-tier markets, where many people's first group-buy happens on Douyin.

— Tom
2:25:31

The red envelope middle platform cross-promotes everything

ByteDance built a red envelope middle platform in 2019, packaging acquisition/reactivation/traffic-routing into an SDK that the whole product line plugged into. During Spring Festival, among dozens of tasks, traffic routed to Dongchedi, Xingfuli, Dolphin Stock and others was given a higher coin weight, because their iOS users hadn't been washed yet, they had many new users, and they could be pushed to the top of the App Store instantly. The number one spot on China's iOS chart needs roughly 300,000 daily new installs, and ByteDance used the red envelope as leverage to move traffic from one hand to the other, taking six of the top ten or more.

— Tom
2:52:57

Buying traffic in the AI era is a false premise

Tom argues that buying traffic for AI products at this stage is a false premise: model capability determines user perception, and if the number one is ahead by just 5%, users will pay only for the best. Doubao's overseas version bought 90 million users over the past 3 months, but DAU is only 16 million, and retention is poor because the model can't beat American competitors. So real growth is integrated marketing and mind-share: founder IP, podcasts, Twitter, Product Hunt — getting users to spread the word themselves because ‘you are the strongest’.

— Tom
