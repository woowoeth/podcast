The 2025 enterprise services IPO wave: do first, talk later, PS back below 10x

Over the past two or three years the entire enterprise services market was overvalued by 50% to 100%, with PS falling from 30-40x to below 10x. Beisen's IPO was a "beggar's version IPO", but it was the first shot in the systematic listing of Chinese SaaS, and 2025 will bring a wave of enterprise services IPOs.

Xiong Fei, partner at Matrix Partners China, uses the Beisen IPO to review the two-year rollercoaster of Chinese enterprise services, giving a timetable for the 2025 IPO wave, profitability standards and the path of PS regression. 2:02

Beisen's IPO is the first shot in the systematic listing of enterprise services

Xiong Fei believes the signal Beisen's listing sends is: Chinese enterprise services companies are beginning to have excellent companies systematically list in Hong Kong or domestic capital markets. He reviews the past two years of enterprise services as a rollercoaster, where valuations fell but company quality did not change, with the external environment causing distortion. Beisen in fiscal 2020 could achieve cash flow profitability, roughly several tens of millions, but after the pandemic the US dollar flood caused the entire sector's valuation to inflate three to five times. At least six to eight companies in the same sector each raised tens of millions of US dollars to increase investment, changing prices, costs and per-person efficiency dramatically, a prisoner's dilemma for all companies. Listing is combat; through listing you rebuild per-person efficiency and make gravity go upward all the way.

— Xiong Fei
5:04

Growth slows down, quality goes up

Xiong Fei observes that over the past two or three years the vast majority of companies were pushing 100%, 150% or even 200% growth, which brought doubled headcount and doubled losses, a mess. Now most of the companies he is involved with are working toward single-month break-even by the end of this year and full-year break-even next year, by cutting growth to 60%, increasing headcount only 10% to 20%, and raising per-person efficiency 40% to 50% annually. He cites Stripe as an example: profitable before the pandemic, valued at nearly 100 billion US dollars after, quickly becoming a company losing hundreds of millions of US dollars a year with per-person efficiency down 60%, with only two thousand people before the pandemic and nearly ten thousand after, and now optimizing by cutting a great many people.

— Xiong Fei
7:04

2025 IPO wave: 300-500 million revenue plus profitability

Xiong Fei says enterprise services companies are now much closer to listing than before. More than six to eight of the companies he invested in have revenue of 100-200 million or even several hundred million, and there are still many companies with tens of millions in revenue. He and first-tier investment banks share a consensus: there will be a wave of enterprise services IPOs in 2025. The reason is that these companies, like Beisen, may have 700 million in revenue, but more have 100-200 million to 200-300 million, and after two more years of growth to 300-500 million or close to 500 million, both Hong Kong and A-shares are very OK; second, through 2023 and 2024 they will have built profitability. On domestic listing standards, most application software goes to ChiNext, achieving 30-50 million or 40-50 million net profit that year with 30-40% or around 40% growth, which is the ChiNext consensus.

— Xiong Fei
13:56

The enterprise services market was overvalued by 50% to 100%

Xiong Fei says very frankly that over the past two or three years the entire enterprise services market was at a premium. Although his seat is in this sector, he believes it was overvalued by 50%, 100%. At the hottest point in 2021, many companies had only 20-30 million or 30-50 million in revenue but valuations of nearly a billion, at 30-40x PS, or 20-30x PS. He judges that in the second half of this year or the first half of next year, industry-wide PS will return below 10x, and excellent companies in the primary market can return to 6-8x. At the same time, most companies have low visibility, but excellent companies will achieve single-month or full-year profitability this year and next, and by the first half of next year at the latest we will see a batch of companies with full-year profitability, still growing at high tens of percent at around Series B, and around 40% growth from Series B/C onward, making PS reasonable.

— Xiong Fei
16:10

Airbnb and Uber significantly optimized operations after listing

Xiong Fei believes listing is a very correct, very wise decision. He cites Airbnb choosing to IPO in the worst period of 2020, holding itself to public company standards, with a financial situation in a fairly OK position among the new generation of unicorns; conversely, other companies that did not IPO did not hold themselves to that bar. He says when a company has 300-500 million in revenue, whether it goes to A-shares or Hong Kong, it is already a qualified company, and holding itself to qualified public company standards increases influence, is healthy and effective for the company, and lets it put down its burden and go at its own pace. He observes that after Airbnb and Uber listed, the entire company's economics, health and operational refinement were significantly optimized compared to before listing, because there is an invisible hand or a real hand making adjustments.

— Xiong Fei
20:15

Compared to 2015-17: real money, closer to capital, concentration

Xiong Fei compares 2015-17 with now and sees three differences. First, in 2015-17 many were concepts; at that time the first wave of foreign enterprise services, Jetforce and Workday, were valued at only a few billion or one to two hundred billion US dollars, and many Chinese companies had revenue of maybe tens of millions of RMB, while now there are many companies with 100-200 million to several hundred million in revenue. Second, these companies are much closer to the capital market and can be tested by it. Third, the market is entering a trend of concentration; 2015-17 was a big explosion of Korean weapons, but from 2020-22, especially after 2022, concentration in sectors like e-commerce, HR, CRM and electronic contracts has become very severe and clear, basically shrinking to competition among the top two or three, with very few opportunities left for new companies.

— Xiong Fei
45:40

Midjourney may be built on Stable Diffusion

Xiong Fei discusses Midjourney and Stable Diffusion: Stable Diffusion can build its own model and launched Dream Studio, with an interface like Adobe Photoshop; Midjourney is like Meitu Xiuxiu, very easy to get started. He believes there is also a professional market, where professional users are willing to spend days building Stable Diffusion themselves and training the model they want, turning it into their own digital asset, controllable, so that next time they draw the same character it will definitely come out as expected. He mentions external speculation that Midjourney is built on Stable Diffusion, because Stable Diffusion has not disclosed at all how the underlying layer is trained, Midjourney has very few people, only ten or twenty, and external speculation is that it simply cannot afford to build the underlying model itself, and Midjourney's new versions and Stable Diffusion's new versions update similarly.

— Xiong Fei
59:44

The iPhone moment has arrived, the Android moment has not

Xiong Fei believes ChatGPT will exist long-term, like Mac or iPhone, a closed, independent existence with significant advantages, but there will definitely be cheap, open source AI, just like Android. He believes the early stage will definitely be very wrong; now Dolly 2.0 and Stable LM are out and everyone complains about the gap, but one is 175 billion parameters and the others are 7 billion and 3 billion parameters, just like the first generation of Android was also very wrong. He gives an example: if OpenAI makes 10 billion US dollars in revenue a year, coming out of OpenAI to do an open source company is like Android, very profitable, because the downside is nothing to lose; open sourcing a pre-chain might be 0.3x as good as OpenAI, or even 0.2x as good, but 0.2x or 0.3x is already usable in many enterprises, because enterprises have sensitive data they do not want transmitted to OpenAI and want their own model.

— Xiong Fei

Glossary

PS / price-to-sales ratio
The ratio of market value to revenue, measuring valuation level.
NDR / net dollar retention
The proportion of revenue retained from existing customers through renewals and upsells.
PMF / product-market fit
A product finding a market with real demand.
fine tune
Continuing to train an existing large model with one's own data.
pre-chain / pre-trained model
The product of the foundational training stage of a large model.
