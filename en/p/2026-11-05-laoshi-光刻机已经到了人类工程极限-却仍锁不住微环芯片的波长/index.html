Lithography has reached human engineering limits, yet cannot lock down microring chip wavelengths

Silicon photonics once deemed microrings unproducible due to wavelength drift. This company built a laser annealing tuning device that achieved 99% wavelength yield, circumventing dependence on cutting-edge lithography.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

5:02

Compute growth far exceeds copper wire capacity

Over the past decade, global compute grew roughly 100 million times, nearly 10-fold annually, yet chip area grows only with the square of edge length, so the perimeter carrying data grows naturally slower. When bandwidth demand becomes 10x, 100x, or even 1000x greater, copper completely fails, and interconnect transitions for the first time from a computing byproduct to the core node determining AI cluster efficiency and power efficiency.

15:09

Microrings are two orders of magnitude smaller than MZI, enabling wide-and-slow design

Traditional Mach-Zehnder (MZI) modulators are roughly 3 millimeters; microring modulators around 15 micrometers in diameter—more than two orders of magnitude difference. MZI, constrained by edge layout, can only stack bandwidth by raising per-channel speeds; microrings, small enough to blanket an entire chip like sesame seeds, use many low-speed parallel channels instead, achieving higher total bandwidth density and lower power consumption.

22:16

Performance means nothing if it cannot scale to production

Microrings excel in the lab—pair them with a tunable laser and you get excellent transmitters. But at production scale, purchased laser wavelengths mismatch each microring's response wavelength, making mass manufacturing impossible. The advisor judged it clearly: the device performs well, you can obviously see that, but it cannot scale to production—the core industry concern is that microring wavelengths naturally drift, preventing all-in commitment to the approach.

— Zhang Weiwei
24:18

Lithography precision has reached its ceiling, yet cannot lock phase stability

Conventional approach: better lithography tightens line width to suppress wavelength drift. But even at 0.5 or 1 nanometer precision—the human engineering limit—such jitter stays perceptible to precision optics and causes phase shift. The solution: stop pursuing perfect lithography. Instead, separate line width control and wavelength correction into two steps: standard lithography first, then a dedicated device to tune each microring's phase.

— Zhang Weiwei
29:25

Tuning device achieves 99% wavelength yield

The device, named Zhiwei 1000E (智威1000E), uses tens of thousands of pulsed laser scans to trace amorphous silicon regions on the chip with picometer precision, pulling each microring's wavelength to target. On this single metric—microring wavelength—it achieved 99% yield. This means microring manufacturing no longer requires EUV or cutting-edge lithography, enabling mass production with mature process nodes.

— Zhang Weiwei
33:33

Custom lasers at $3,000 per unit drove in-house light source development

Each microring needs one wavelength; eight-channel microring arrays theoretically require eight separately tuned narrow-linewidth DFB lasers, priced around $3,000 each. Eight lasers lined up generate cross-heating and cannot be solved by stacking power; splitting them damages performance. The team self-developed comb lasers, letting one device produce 8 or even 16 wavelengths directly, drastically reducing light source cost and complexity.

39:37

Electrical chips translate between GPUs and photonics

GPUs' built-in SerDes targets 50-ohm copper impedance at roughly 400-800 millivolts, but microrings are nonlinear, requiring 1.6-2V or higher driving voltage, specific reverse bias, and extreme temperature sensitivity for real-time wavelength locking—none native to GPUs. The team designed an electrical chip between GPU and photonic chip, translating electrical signals into a form photonics can read, completely transparent to the GPU.

47:49

OIO is not a better optical module but a new computing paradigm

The team explicitly rejected becoming the next Zhongji Xuechuang (中际旭创), since optical modules target communications, while scale-up interconnect comes from compute—maximizing utilization, minimizing latency at any cost. OIO will define a new paradigm for computing optical interconnect. Against Nvidia's closed-loop full-stack ecosystem (the team calls this the 'Apple ecosystem'), they chose the reverse: an Android ecosystem, opening the technology to all GPU makers worldwide.

Glossary

CPO
Packages optical devices with switching chips together, primarily for switch-side optical interconnect.
OIO
Connects light directly to computing chip (GPU/CPU) pins, a new form of interconnect technology.
UCIE
Protocol standard for chiplet-to-chiplet interconnect, originally designed for distances spanning centimeters to millimeters.
microring modulator
Ring-shaped optical modulator only tens of micrometers in diameter, operating through wavelength resonance.
MZI
Traditional optical modulator achieving modulation through light path phase interference, millimeter scale.
WDM
Technique allowing multiple signals at different wavelengths to share one fiber simultaneously.
