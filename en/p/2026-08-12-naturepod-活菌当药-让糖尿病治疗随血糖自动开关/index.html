A diabetes drug that switches itself on with blood sugar: live bacteria secrete GLP-1 on demand

Scientists engineered E. coli to secrete GLP-1 automatically when blood sugar is high, improving glucose control in mice and macaques. Human trials are still far off, but it opens up the imagination of "on-demand dosing" living drugs.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:45

Fixed-dose drugs are inherently unable to keep up with blood sugar changes

Diabetes is a common disease affecting the world, and the WHO estimates about one in nine people are affected. Existing GLP-1 agonist drugs (such as Ozempic and Wegovy) are strikingly effective, but there is a fundamental mismatch: the dosing schedule is fixed, while blood sugar changes dynamically. Once there is an overdose, side effects are amplified. So the team wanted to build a live bacterium that can sense blood sugar and release therapeutic molecules on demand, letting the drug "work only when needed."

2:10

A molecular brake lets the bacteria make the drug only when blood sugar is high

The team engineered E. coli and added a regulatory circuit called a "molecular brake." When blood sugar is low, the brake keeps GLP-1 synthesis switched off; when blood sugar rises, a signaling molecule is produced inside the bacterium, releasing the brake and starting GLP-1 secretion; after blood sugar falls, the signal weakens and the brake is pressed back down. This closed-loop logic directly targets the flaw of "fixed dosing," matching the dose to the real-time blood sugar level, a kind of dynamic regulation similar to the body's own. The design was nicknamed Gift (glucose-responsive bacteria).

3:30

The live bacteria survive the stomach and still work, including in macaques

The researchers fed Gift orally to diabetic mice and macaques. In both animals, the bacteria reached the gut through the stomach and still worked: under high blood sugar the circuit was strongly activated, secreting GLP-1 and significantly improving glucose control and glucose tolerance. In addition, treated animals had lower liver fat accumulation and lower levels of kidney and colon damage than untreated controls, and mice also lost body weight and body fat. Although GLP-1 itself has a weight-loss effect, these results support that live-bacteria dosing is effective and may be additionally beneficial for diabetes patients, who often also have excess weight.

5:00

This is not a glucose-lowering drug, it is a living-drug platform

Guan stresses that this research is still a considerable distance from human trials. The team needs to figure out how long the bacteria survive in the body, how long they remain active, and how often they must be taken orally, and it must confirm long-term safety. But the bigger imagination lies in platformization: the same "sense-and-respond" live-bacteria system could in the future be programmed to recognize other disease signals and secrete the corresponding therapeutic molecules, giving rise to a new generation of smart, self-adjusting living drugs. This is the ultimate goal of the work, not just a glucose-lowering treatment.

6:30

Where cougars are present, deer-vehicle collisions drop 67%

Combining vehicle-deer collision data with GPS collar and infrared-trigger camera data, the study found that the presence of cougars (also called pumas) reduced the probability of deer-vehicle collisions by 67%. Deer actively avoid the forest edges where cougars are often active (including roadsides), and because cougars are nocturnal predators, deer become less active at night, which is when many collisions happen. The researchers hope to clarify this unexpected effect and help build public support for coexisting with large carnivores.

7:42

Water droplets do not just hit, they make the rock resonate too

Past research on water-drop erosion relied mainly on high-speed cameras, but could not see the force distribution after impact. The researchers switched to surface strain sensors, measuring material deformation beneath a glass plate. They found that in the first 2 milliseconds after a droplet lands, it produces a powerful force strong enough to begin damaging the surface; then from 2 to 20 milliseconds, the material and water vibrate in sync at close to the rock's natural resonance frequency, potentially extending microcracks and thus accelerating erosion. This offers a new microscopic mechanism for understanding landscape evolution.

10:00

During an eclipse the sun dims, but it can still burn through your retina

During an eclipse, the sun is partly covered and the light weakens, but the damage remains: looking directly at it can cause permanent retinal burns. There are two safe approaches: one is to use eclipse glasses that meet the ISO standard, whose filters let through only a tiny amount of light, so that ordinary objects are almost invisible through them; the other is indirect projection, such as using a pinhole camera, a colander, or gaps between leaves under a tree to project the sun's image onto the ground. Never look directly at it through binoculars or a camera unless they are fitted with professional filters.

17:00

To see the corona, two satellites create an artificial eclipse in space

The scientific value of an eclipse is that it allows observation of the corona, which is normally drowned out by intense light. The corona is the sun's outer magnetic atmosphere, with temperatures reaching millions of degrees Celsius, far higher than the sun's surface at about 5000 degrees Celsius, and it is also the source of solar storms. Space weather can disrupt satellites, power grids, and trains, so understanding the corona is crucial. The European Space Agency specifically launched the Proba-3 mission, using two satellites flying one behind the other to create an artificial eclipse so the corona can be studied continuously, while ground-based eclipses remain a rare opportunity.

Glossary

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1)
A hormone secreted by the gut that stimulates insulin secretion and suppresses appetite, an important target for glucose-lowering and weight-loss drugs.
Molecular brake
An inhibitory switch in a genetic circuit that blocks protein expression when there is no stimulus.
Proba-3
An ESA satellite formation-flying mission that uses two spacecraft to block the sun and artificially create an eclipse to observe the corona.
