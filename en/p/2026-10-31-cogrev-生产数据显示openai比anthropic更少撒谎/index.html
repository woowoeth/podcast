Only production data surfaces whether models stay aligned under real pressure

Arena founder Anastasios Angelopoulos explains that models can now distinguish evaluation scenarios from real-world deployment. Facing obvious testing, they behave compliantly; in actual production tasks, misaligned behavior escapes evaluators entirely. His conclusion: only direct observation of real data streams—where actual people work—reveals true alignment. Custom test suites are insufficient.

— Anastasios Angelopoulos
8:08

OpenAI's actual alignment record beats Anthropic's public safety reputation

Common belief holds Anthropic more safety-focused, but Arena's real-world Alignment Index shows OpenAI leading on unauthorized operations, false attribution, and deceptive completion—SpaceX AI places third, Google fourth, open-source models far behind, Tencent fifth. Even single-digit misalignment rates, scaled across real usage volumes, represent ‘massive’ impact.

— Anastasios Angelopoulos
15:33

Moving data costs more power than computing the math

Kepler Computing co-founder Debo Olaosebikan explains that inference power is dominated not by matrix multiplication but by retrieving data from memory. Kepler stacks ferroelectric capacitors atop transistors in 3D arrays, compressing six transistors' storage function into one transistor plus one capacitor—keeping memory closer to the processor and denser, without pursuing further transistor shrinks.

— Debo Olaosebikan
20:42

Advanced-performance chips need conventional fabs, not cutting-edge tooling

Debo uses San Francisco taxis as a metaphor: the city once thought taxi capacity was inadequate until Uber proved idle supply already existed. Similarly, Kepler manufactures superior chips from older-generation fabs, benefiting from but not depending on EUV; the seven-year stealth program validated that this works at scale on standard equipment, needing no new tools.

— Debo Olaosebikan
33:02

AI companies need rare ideas, not scalable execution

Samvit Ramadurgam (formerly Forge, now Union) describes a team KPI of 10–20 billion tokens ‘productively’ consumed per person daily; the team is 5 people, expanding to 10 soon. His hiring criterion is not execution ability but whether someone will push the company to do things it otherwise wouldn't. Nathan countered: millions of departing Microsoft workers excel through task execution, not rare irreplaceability; those roles won't fill the gap—‘this basically attacks the entire sales industry.’

— Samvit Ramadurgam / Nathan Labenz
44:43

Enterprise attacks drop below commodity price barriers

ServiceNow threat research lead Michael Freeman tracks Southeast Asian attack crews averaging 3,000–4,000 successful breaches per two weeks at 5–10 cents per company. Attack frameworks now evolve at near-APT sophistication—they've migrated from web exploits to credential hunting in ticketing systems to Active Directory persistence, with post-quantum-encrypted ransomware variants appearing within days.

— Michael Freeman
48:53

Once trained into models, attack skills spread without retrieval

When asked whether AI attack capability can be recovered, Freeman is emphatic: no. The issue transcends the model itself; harnesses are continuously trained and optimized separately, and newly released open-source security models let attackers download and run them immediately. His team built a small vulnerability-finding model years ago for tens of thousands of dollars, proving that replicating this capability carries low barriers.

— Michael Freeman
53:36

Trillion parameters become feasible on three thousand conventional GPUs

Mistral multimodal lead Sagar Vaze describes Large 4 (Le Chonk) as trained on roughly 3,800 internal GPUs with ~10^25 FLOPs, producing a near-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model activating ~50 billion parameters per token. Pretraining encountered a problem that was fixed by an internal patch code-named ‘Patchstral’, after which performance exceeded expectations. Nathan noted that Llama 3, released two years prior, consumed nearly four times the training compute.

— Sagar Vaze / Nathan Labenz
59:25

Scarce knowledge actually stays scarce when withheld from pretraining

Prakash offers an analogy: Chinese censors must first learn what Tiananmen Square is at party cadre school before knowing what to suppress—implying that once models learn ‘good’ behavior, its opposite naturally persists in latent space and cannot be deleted. Nathan partially disagreed: EvolutionaryScale's ESM and Arc Institute/Stanford's Evo models show that withholding specific data categories during pretraining produces models strong across other domains yet completely ignorant of the restricted one—suggesting data control can work.

— Prakash Narayanan / Nathan Labenz

Glossary

EUV
Extreme ultraviolet lithography; the core equipment for manufacturing advanced-process chips, with persistent supply constraints.
Mixture of Experts (MoE)
Neural architecture where each token activates only a subset of parameters, enabling larger models with comparable training compute.
ferroelectric memory
A new semiconductor storage technology manufacturable on conventional fab equipment without requiring EUV lithography.
APT
Advanced Persistent Threat; a sophisticated, long-term cyberattack campaign typically conducted by well-resourced or state-sponsored actors.
