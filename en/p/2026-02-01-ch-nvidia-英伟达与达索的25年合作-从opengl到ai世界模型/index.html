NVIDIA and Dassault Systèmes' 25-year partnership: from OpenGL to AI world models

Jensen Huang argues that next-generation design tools will no longer rely on humans writing structured geometric data, but instead on AI understanding physics laws and causal relationships through 'world models'—a step harder than language models.

2:15

Design shifts from structured data to generative computing

Huang says earlier-generation design software required humans to manually specify every geometric shape and material property—everything was structured representation. Now the entire compute stack is being reinvented as a generative computing model. Because AI is infrastructure for all industries, like water, electricity, and the internet, this drives a new round of industrialization in which every country and society will be AI-driven.

— Jensen Huang
9:20

Life is the hardest world model to build

Huang argues that understanding life first requires learning 'life's language'—how DNA, proteins, and cells interact. This step doesn't exist in physical design, because physical design begins with human imagination, whereas life existed before humans. Only after learning that language can you translate between different languages: human language and biological language, thereby generating new proteins, chemicals, and materials.

— Jensen Huang
13:24

AI predicts physics like a dog catching a ball, not through simulation

Huang uses an analogy to explain Physics Nemo: a dog catching a ball in the air isn't performing ballistic physics simulation, but simply predicting where it will land through observation. AI learning to predict physical phenomena uses the same logic—trained on physics laws but not solving equations step-by-step. This makes simulation speed 10,000 times faster.

— Jensen Huang
18:28

A gigawatt-scale AI factory costs $50 billion

Huang calls the present moment a new industrial revolution, requiring simultaneous scaling of three types of factories: chip fabs, computer factories producing supercomputers, and AI factories. He provides specific figures: a gigawatt-scale AI factory costs approximately $50 billion, and the world is simultaneously building dozens of gigawatt-scale factories—humanity's largest-scale industrial infrastructure project in history.

— Jensen Huang
24:36

Future designers will manage an entire team of AI companions

In response to whether AI will replace engineers, Huang's answer is reversed: in the future, every Solid Works designer will own a team of AI companions trained by that designer themselves. The number of 'users' using Dassault tools will thus expand from purely biological designers to biological plus AI, and total tool usage will instead grow explosively—not shrink.

— Jensen Huang
27:39

World models must obey physics laws, not language preferences

Huang distinguishes language models from world models: language models need to understand grammar, vocabulary, taste, and guardrails on what should and shouldn't be said; world models must obey physics laws, understand causality—for instance, that knocking over one domino makes connected ones fall—and understand inertia, friction, gravity, and contact relationships. These cannot be captured by language alone; they must be taught through simulation and abundant examples.

— Jensen Huang
32:43

Your AI companion encodes 33 years of expertise and remains private

Huang says everyone will eventually have an AI companion that remembers your preferences and encodes your skills and habits. This companion won't reside in the cloud, won't be public, won't be open-source, because it carries personally sensitive accumulated knowledge. He cites his own inbox as an example, noting that it already contains 33 years of his professional experience and judgment—precisely what an AI companion should capture.

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

world model
An AI model that follows physics laws and can understand causality and material behavior, distinct from language models that only process language.
Physics Nemo
NVIDIA's physics-aware AI simulation framework that trains predictive models based on physics laws, 10,000 times faster than traditional simulation.
MBSE
Model-based systems engineering; an engineering methodology using virtual models to design, simulate, and verify entire systems before construction.
