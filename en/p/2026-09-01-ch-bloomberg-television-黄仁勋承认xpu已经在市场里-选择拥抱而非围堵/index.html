Huang admits XPUs are in the market, chooses embrace over containment

A week ago on the earnings call he emphasized GPU's superior economics; now he says XPUs ‘are already in the market’and is selling NVLink networks and switches to XPU makers.

ComputeCustom chipsSemiconductorsCorporate investmentAI infrastructureSupply chain
Huang's direct responses to circular financing and XPU-threat questions are the most substantive moments; the rest is mutual business flattery.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:07

DGX Spark redefines what a Windows PC is

Jensen frames this partnership not as simply expanding foundry orders, but as embedding MediaTek's SOC into NVIDIA's GPU ecosystem using multiple generations of NVLink chip-level interconnects. The immediate fruit: DGX Spark, a one-petaflops desktop that runs agentic AI locally without cloud offloading. Next, this processor becomes the foundation for a new-generation Windows PC co-designed by Microsoft and NVIDIA—the goal is to redesign the PC as an agentic machine from the ground up, not just bolt an AI chip onto traditional PCs.

— Jensen Huang
2:09

Partnership flows both ways; neither displaces the other

NVIDIA opens NVLink Fusion, Spectrum X switches, and the full networking framework to MediaTek. The logic, as Jensen puts it: ‘when they win, we sell more; when we win, they sell more’. MediaTek's XPU wins automatically bring NVIDIA network business; NVIDIA is already embedded in nearly every cloud and data center, so MediaTek's XPU only needs to connect to NVLink to tap into these ready-made AI factories. Both treat the other's market share as their own upside, not as a boundary to defend.

— Jensen Huang
4:18

$3.5 billion in convertible bonds is a ten-year bet on trust

Asked why NVIDIA is investing in MediaTek, Jensen talks not about financial returns, but about trust—he and Rick have known each other for 25 years, and the investment is to prove NVIDIA's commitment to the roadmap: ‘this is not a one- or two-year thing, it's a ten-year thing’. Worth noting: MediaTek is already highly profitable and doesn't need this money; the investment's real value is binding both sides to the same AI factory architecture and PC/edge-chip roadmap, not simply a financial injection.

— Jensen Huang
7:28

Investing in a partner is not the same as propping up a customer

The host asks directly if this counts as circular financing. Jensen's answer: distinguish between the role of the investee. MediaTek is an independent company running its own business, not a customer that depends on NVIDIA orders; both have their own revenue streams. This response emphasizes the relationship's nature: buying equity in an already highly profitable, highly successful tech company, not pre-paying cash to a downstream buyer.

— Jensen Huang
10:38

Huang shifts from GPU-only to present-tense XPU

A week ago on the earnings call, Jensen emphasized GPU architecture's superior economics. This time, asked whether opening NVLink Fusion to XPUs concedes that NVIDIA accepts XPUs entering the market, he responds: ‘I see XPUs in the market. It's not coming into the market. It's in the market’. He defines GPU as a general-purpose accelerator and XPU as a specialized one, stressing that NVIDIA accelerates every phase of the AI lifecycle—from data processing through pre-training, post-training, to agentic inference, across proprietary, open-source, video, language, biology, physics, and robotics models. This, he argues, is why NVIDIA remains ‘the most rentable computing infrastructure’.

— Jensen Huang
12:41

Opening the platform to XPU works because NVIDIA's share keeps growing

Jensen stresses this is not a defensive move: ‘our share is growing in proprietary models and open-source models alike; our share across the entire AI opportunity is growing’. Because NVIDIA is not worried about XPU eating its lunch, it can afford to actively open its networks and switches to XPU customers—rather than force customers to solve NVLink interconnect, ethernet switching, scale-up/scale-out problems themselves, NVIDIA sells them the complete solution, turning would-be competitors into distribution channels.

— Jensen Huang
16:48

Per-gigawatt economic scale to NVIDIA more than doubles in three generations

Asked about NVIDIA's ‘content share’ in Vera Rubin-generation server design, Jensen provides specifics: Hopper generation delivered NVIDIA approximately $18 billion in economic scale per gigawatt; Grace Blackwell about $25 billion; Vera Rubin generation over $40 billion per gigawatt. The reason: NVIDIA keeps expanding beyond GPUs. An AI factory uses seven different types of NVIDIA chips; XPUs replace just one, while NVIDIA still sells networking, switching, and CPU components, so the total economic scale per gigawatt actually keeps growing.

— Jensen Huang
19:50

Custom HBM packaging becomes the new supply chain lock-in point

Jensen reveals the next-generation HBM is custom HBM, with the stack built on NVIDIA's own custom base layer—which has already been extended to NVLink Fusion partners, including MediaTek and Amazon. Because HBM needs to be stacked into the same package as bare XPU dies, the process is extremely complex; after NVIDIA standardizes this, partners can tap NVIDIA's supply chain scale, while NVIDIA gains access to theirs. Rick also concedes supply chain remains the biggest bottleneck—pressure from HBM to substrate—but MediaTek's position at Taiwan's AI infrastructure hub makes it easier to manage.

Glossary

NVLink Fusion
NVIDIA's open interconnect protocol letting third-party custom chips integrate directly into NVIDIA's networking and switching ecosystem.
XPU
Specialized AI accelerator chips custom-built by hyperscale customers, contrasted with general-purpose GPUs.
DGX Spark
NVIDIA's desktop-class AI supercomputer capable of running agentic AI locally without cloud offloading.
ASIC
Application-specific integrated circuit designed for a specific task rather than general-purpose use.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory technology stacked in package with AI chips to provide ultra-high memory bandwidth.
