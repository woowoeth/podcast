AI's real value in healthcare isn't diagnosis—it's catching traps in contracts

Healthcare giants hide price increases through thousands of shell companies, but once you sign, AI can't save you—the only answer is to feed your contract to AI before signing and ask: where's the trap?

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

11:19

Health giants hide billions through thousands of shell companies

Cuban notes UnitedHealth operates 3,000-plus subsidiaries with ~$150 billion in annual internal transfers—comparable to the 14th-largest state economy in the US. He explains how giants simultaneously raise premiums and deductibles while steering patients to undisclosed affiliates they own, generating extra profit through this structure.

— Mark Cuban
15:34

Long contracts leave AI helpless after you sign

Cuban argues the problem isn't technology but the contracts themselves—a Dallas PBM agreement spans 1,335 pages, and no amount of human effort can extract every hidden trap. He stresses that once you sign, AI is nearly powerless; its true value lies in pre-signature review, preventing you from signing a bad deal in the first place.

— Mark Cuban
20:49

Widespread AI contract review could collapse the whole system

Cuban calculates that more than 155 million Americans get coverage through employer plans—over 60% of the country. If those employers used AI to review contracts before signing, asking where the traps are, the entire BUCA and PBM system would collapse from losing its information advantage, and healthcare costs would crater.

— Mark Cuban
24:58

Shrinking drug manufacturing from warehouses to shipping containers

Cost Plus Drugs has already compressed what once required a football-field-sized manufacturing line into a truck-sized pod, swapping drug batches every 4 hours using AI and robotics. Each pod costs ~$20 million; government is the first customer. Cuban aims to move hundreds in two years, thousands in five—he jokes that selling $20 billion in pods annually would make even Musk nervous.

— Mark Cuban
36:41

Personalized drug design will trend toward zero cost

As large language models improve, Cuban expects the cost to design personalized drugs based on your DNA—even over-the-counter medications like ibuprofen—will fall toward zero. He suggests labs bundle this into subscription services the way 23andMe offers DNA testing for under $99, letting marginal costs spread across the user base.

— Mark Cuban
42:02

AI makes medical patents harder to defend

Cuban raises an underexplored tension: if doctors and researchers use AI to generate new therapies, the ideal of free information collides with IP holders' real interests. He foresees closed licensing models where you pay per inference using someone's model—complicating access to the data needed for training and reasoning.

— Mark Cuban
49:17

Replace employee health premiums with personal savings accounts

If advising the president, Cuban would replace insurance with savings: employers deposit the equivalent of an ACA Silver-plan premium into individual accounts, banks extend credit, government guarantees coverage, and federal stop-loss kicks in above the limit. If you never claim, retirement brings the balance plus interest—yours to keep. Unlike today's premiums, which you pay forever without benefit and still must fight to use.

— Mark Cuban
57:37

Only two kinds of companies will exist: good at AI and bankrupt

Closing, Cuban says companies split into two camps: those adept with AI and those going bankrupt. Most founders are stuck at the stage of not daring to talk to AI—like fearing Lotus 1-2-3 spreadsheets back in the day. Whoever gets comfortable first spots opportunities no one else sees yet, including the basement-bound solo founders Cuban says will become trillion-dollar builders.

— Mark Cuban

Glossary

ASO (Administrative Services Only)
Intermediary within large insurers that manages medical claims for self-insured employers, often with opaque terms.
PBM (Pharmacy Benefit Manager)
Middleman negotiating with drugmakers on behalf of employers and insurers, managing prescription drug benefits.
BUCA
Collective term for the largest national health insurers in the US.
ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application)
FDA filing required for generic drugs to launch, assessed and charged separately for each drug.
N of one
A therapy or drug formulated for a single patient based on their individual characteristics.
Stop loss
Reinsurance self-insured employers buy to cap losses from catastrophic individual claims.
