Benchmarks and Live Demos Show G3 Matching Pentium II at Half Clock Speed

Apple skipped price comparison and instead brought same-priced and pricier Compaq laptops to the stage with the iMac, running Photoshop and Director timing races that G3 won in every matchup.

A product launch focused on chip performance comparisons and iMac design details, with no industry analysis or debate. Suited for quickly scanning key points and quotable moments.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:03

Employee Turnover Fell from 33% to 15%, Becoming Apple's Turnaround Signal

Jobs listed employee retention as the primary indicator of company health. When he returned last summer, Apple's annual turnover rate was 33%, meaning a third of the workforce would leave within a year at that pace. Ten months later, the rate dropped to 15%, below silicon valley's average at the time. He attributed this to employees seeing Apple's potential to win again and the company becoming more entrepreneurial, plus issuing significant stock options to core staff.

— Steve Jobs
2:04

CompUSA Shop-in-Shop Grew Apple's CPU Revenue Share Fivefold

Apple opened shop-in-shop locations in roughly 170 CompUSA stores across the US. At launch, Apple products accounted for only 3% of CompUSA's CPU sales revenue. Within six months, that share jumped to 15%. This was Jobs' concrete example of how the distribution system overhaul was working.

— Steve Jobs
4:05

Back-to-Back Quarters Topped $100 Million Profit, Cash Reserves Hit $1.8 Billion

The management team was profitable in its first quarter: the December quarter showed $47 million profit, the following quarter $55 million, totaling over $100 million in six months—while other companies in the industry struggled around break-even or posted losses. Cash grew from $1.6 billion in December to $1.8 billion by the March quarter end. Market cap also grew by $2 billion from last July's $1.8 billion.

— Steve Jobs
5:05

Four Product Quadrants: Consumer and Pro, Desktop and Portable

Jobs said the team returned to basic business school logic and found that customers only wanted two types of products (consumer and professional) and two form factors (desktop and portable), so they narrowed all effort to four core products and stopped extending the product line further. This four-quadrant framework structured the entire launch event's product logic.

— Steve Jobs
7:06

At the Same Clock Speed, G3 Runs Twice as Fast as Pentium II

Jobs claimed that at 266MHz, the PowerPC G3 was twice as fast as Pentium II, equivalent to Pentium II running at 500MHz; a 300MHz G3 matched a 600MHz Pentium II. He cited Byte magazine's Bytemark benchmark as third-party evidence and noted that Intel had questioned the test results and asked Byte to rerun them, but the magazine maintained its original conclusions. He added that Intel later released a 400MHz Pentium II, but results "didn't improve much."

— Steve Jobs
8:06

New PowerBook G3 Offers Dual Batteries and Three Screen Sizes

The redesigned PowerBook G3 featured new processors, keycaps, and everything in between, offering 12-inch (800×600), 13-inch, and 14-inch (both 1024×768) display options—Apple's first portable to break past the 12-inch barrier. The chassis had two modular slots for batteries, floppy drives, CD-ROM, DVD-ROM, or third-party Zip drives with hot-swapping; dual batteries delivered up to 7 hours of runtime.

— Steve Jobs
12:10

Live Photoshop and Director Tests Show G3 Winning Every Match

Apple brought Phil Schiller, vice president of product marketing, to run live tests pitting a $5,400 Compaq Armada 7800 laptop with the latest 266MHz mobile Pentium II against new PowerBook G3s at 233MHz and 292MHz, both running identical 27MB Photoshop action files and 30MB Macromedia Director files. The 292MHz G3 finished first, the 233MHz close behind, with Pentium II trailing visibly. Schiller conceded it was the fastest Pentium notebook they could find and joked that to borrow it, they'd left other Windows customers empty-handed.

— Steve Jobs/Phil Schiller
18:01

iMac Is for Consumers but Packed with Professional-Level Components

Jobs criticized how consumer PCs at the time had dated processors, poor display quality, lacked networking, ran outdated I/O ports, and looked ugly. iMac's answer: 233MHz G3 processor with 512K L2 cache, 15-inch 1024×768 Apple-designed display, 32MB RAM (expandable to 128MB), 4GB hard drive, 24x CD-ROM, 100Mbit Ethernet, 4Mbit infrared, and two 12Mbit USB ports, completely abandoning Apple's legacy I/O standards.

— Steve Jobs
26:48

The Final Match: iMac Faces Top-Spec 400MHz Pentium II Gaming Laptop

For the finale, Schiller brought out what he called the "fastest and most aggressive" consumer-class product: a Compaq Presario 4880 with 400MHz Pentium II MMX processor, 100MHz memory bus, half-megabyte pipeline cache, and 2x accelerated graphics port, priced over $2,500 (without network and display; over $3,000 with both). iMac ran the same Director demo file in a head-to-head timing race as the event's climax.

— Steve Jobs/Phil Schiller

Glossary

Bytemark
Byte magazine's independent CPU benchmark test, regarded as an industry authority for processor performance evaluation.
AGP (Advanced Graphics Port)
Intel platform interface standard of that era for increasing graphics card data transfer speed.
USB (Universal Serial Bus)
New-generation peripheral interface standard that Apple pioneered at scale on the iMac.
