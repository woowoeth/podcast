H100 hourly rental rates bottomed last year, then rebounded after Anthropic released a new model—proving that GPU value swings sharply with AI demand rather than declining monotonically, which is precisely why futures contracts need to exist.

Any growth this fast has uneven second derivatives—volatility is the feature, not the bug

Don Wilson dismissed market jitters over CoreWeave bonds and pre-earnings volatility with a mathematical observation: anything growing this rapidly must have uneven acceleration and deceleration. The variation is expected, not alarming. He joked it might just be August vacations reducing token consumption. He was explicit: he read no deeper signal from the dips.

— Don Wilson
2:03

CoreWeave's 15% yields expose what happens without derivatives: no way to hedge

When CoreWeave bonds hit 15% yields, Don Wilson saw a market-clearing signal: desperate unmet demand for risk management. Neocloud providers selling to creditworthy clients face no problem, but they also want to capture lower-credit customers. GPU futures would let them buy and deploy hardware, sell a futures hedge, then offer excess capacity at spot prices to weaker credit counterparties. Markets reward basis trades like this with lower capital costs.

— Don Wilson
4:04

GPU futures could launch November 9th if regulators don't act—conveniently after the election

GPU futures remain unlaunched; the CFTC's comment period has extended. Absent regulatory action, they could list November 9—right after November 3rd voting. Don Wilson admitted he can't fully parse the White House's national-security concerns. He's heard various explanations, including doubt about whether the index is robust enough for pricing.

— Don Wilson
6:07

No one knows what a B200 is worth in four years—so futures contracts must exist

This is the trillion-dollar question: you can be bullish on compute growth overall and still uncertain whether individual GPUs hold their value. They depreciate; faster chips replace them. Because no one can say what a B200 or B300 will fetch in four or five years, futures contracts must exist. CME's initial contracts run three years, extending as liquidity grows. Don Wilson noted that last Thanksgiving, H100 rental rates reversed sharply upward when Anthropic released a new model and inference demand exploded; most recently, B200, B300, and H100 prices surged again—while Silicon Data's token index collapsed.

— Don Wilson
9:21

Leverage and oversupply fears miss how futures prices form: buyers and sellers must both agree

Tracy posed a sharp objection: if GPU values fall over time, won't hedging tools just let everyone borrow more money and overbuild into the same underlying risk, rather than actually reduce it? Don Wilson's answer: futures require both a willing buyer and seller. If anyone tries to sell at an artificial price ceiling, no rational counterparty appears to buy. His prediction: hedging demand will concentrate on the selling side, pushing distant contract prices downward. It takes return-seeking investors willing to buy futures contracts to provide the other side.

— Don Wilson
12:25

Heterogeneous hardware doesn't stop futures: H100 alone pools enough capital for a market

Critics argue chip diversity—different software stacks, AMD, Trainium, TPU—prevents building a tradable index. Don Wilson said that H100 or B200 alone has enough capital to support a standalone futures market. Other chips correlate tightly with the standard config; markets will price out the basis spread. He disclosed that DRW is already exploring futures on token output directly, framing the profits from buying spot GPU rental, running open-source models, and selling tokens as analogous to oil crack spreads or soy crush spreads—what they call internally the "crunch spread."

— Don Wilson
16:27

Nvidia's dominance will erode, but the company will still grow—market share shrinks, market expands

When asked how quickly Nvidia's grip could loosen, Don Wilson's take: the compute market is expanding, so Nvidia probably won't hold current market share. Yet he still expects the company to grow absolutely larger. Shrinking share and expanding revenue aren't contradictory when the total market expands.

— Don Wilson
18:42

Perpetuals are futures, not swaps—and they're how derivatives actually make markets fairer

Terry objected that perpetual contracts are really swaps masquerading as futures. Don Wilson disagreed: perpetuals are futures and work better. An oil ETF tracking a Brent perpetual directly would eliminate rollover slippage versus today's monthly re-ups. Retail holding hard-to-borrow stock can't access the high borrow fees that banks get—perpetuals route that cash mechanically to them, a genuine case where derivatives broaden access. He recalled asking Dodd-Frank drafters what separates futures from swaps and hearing "everyone knows swaps are riskier"—pure semantics, he said. A cleared swap and a futures contract impose identical economic risk.

— Don Wilson

Glossary

crack spread / crush spread / crunch spread
The profit from processing raw materials into finished goods, measured by price spread; DRW uses 'crunch spread' for GPU-rental-to-token-sales arbitrage.
perps / perpetual contracts
Derivatives with no expiration date that mechanically replicate futures leverage.
neocloud
Emerging GPU-rental cloud providers, distinct from hyperscale cloud giants.
IOSCO
International Organization of Securities Commissions—the global standard-setter for index construction best practices.
basis trade
Arbitrage or hedging using the price gap between related but imperfectly correlated assets.
