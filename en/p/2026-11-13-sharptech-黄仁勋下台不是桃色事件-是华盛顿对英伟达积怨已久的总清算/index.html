Huang's Ouster Isn't Scandal but Washington's Long-Overdue Reckoning with Nvidia

Nvidia's repeated defiance of China export policy and the national security system was the true trigger for Huang's forced exit; the sex scandal was merely the final straw.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:11

An affair with subordinates is ironclad grounds for board removal

The hosts first clarify an often-overlooked governance issue: boards are supposed to represent shareholders, but they also have motives that don't align with shareholder interests—like protecting their own seats. But this time is different. In US corporate culture, romantic relationships with subordinates cross a clear, irrefutable red line. Once established, a board has almost no choice but to fire, even if the stock price drops double digits, even if employees and most shareholders would prefer otherwise.

16:31

Nvidia doesn't sell chips, it sells systems

Around 2019, Huang's crucial move was acquiring Mellanox: he recognized not faster individual chips, but the system capability of linking multiple chips via high-speed interconnect into a single logical system, later evolving into MVLink. Nvidia's moat isn't just CUDA but this: selling systems, not chips. AMD's previous-generation chips had better single-chip performance, but AMD doesn't build systems—that game AMD simply never entered.

— Ben
20:35

Huang went all-out to keep Nvidia from becoming another Intel

If buyers were only Amazon, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Anthropic—a handful of megacustomers—they'd have the negotiating power to crush Nvidia's margins, exactly what happened to Intel when negotiating with AMD. Huang aggressively backed Neoclouds and various new labs, deliberately creating more buyers to avoid becoming a small number of giants' captive supplier. This wasn't charity.

— Ben
1:03:01

Downgraded-spec chips destroyed Nvidia's relationship with national security officials

Biden's first version of China chip sanctions limited memory bandwidth, not compute. Within a month, Huang released lower-bandwidth, worse-performing ‘compliant’ chips and continued selling heavily into China. Technically legal, but the security establishment saw this as open defiance. From that day on, the national security system branded Nvidia a ‘bad actor’ and Huang branded them ‘idiots.’ Their relationship became adversarial—this was the beginning of what became the final reckoning.

— Ben
1:09:01

Export bans have made Chinese chip independence a necessity

The core argument against chip bans: you can't conjure a chip ecosystem just by throwing money at it—Microsoft spent billions and couldn't save Windows Phone. As long as Nvidia chips are available, no Chinese government mandate can force programmers to abandon CUDA for alternatives. But a complete cutoff creates a hard necessity for independence—undoing fifty years of US tech-transfer management strategy.

— Ben
1:17:05

Corruption allegations changed the character of the entire affair

Beyond chip exports and policy shifts, a third layer of anger stems from more direct corruption suspicions—like the Trump family's financial interests in Malaysian data centers. The hosts argue that even if you can understand the Trump team's positions on China policy, adding corruption poisons the entire affair. Huang's scandal became a single outlet for all the accumulated resentment.

1:18:05

Nvidia's supplier financing model faces its biggest test

Nvidia has played banker to its ecosystem, rolling free cash flow into debt into equity investments, essentially bridging the entire AI bubble. Whether the interim CEO and CFO will continue Huang's aggressive supplier financing is an open question—and credit tightening in the Neocloud market actually began three months before the scandal, making the stock drop less damaging than what's coming.

Glossary

Neoclouds
Emerging cloud computing and compute-resale companies built around Nvidia's chip supply chain.
Mellanox / MVLink
The high-speed chip interconnect company Nvidia acquired, and the multi-chip networking technology derived from it—core to Nvidia's system-level competitive moat.
N-2 rule
Historical US policy of exporting only technology lagging two generations behind current versions, satisfying demand while maintaining technical leadership.
MSS honeypot
Alleged Chinese Ministry of State Security tactic of using romance to recruit assets—discussed here as a possible method behind Huang's scandal.
