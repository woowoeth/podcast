AI won't break SAP, but it will shatter seat-based pricing

AI won't flatten what enterprise software has accumulated, but it will shatter seat-based pricing. SAP's defense isn't training its own foundation model — it's open partnerships plus business understanding. The real battlefield is the last mile: process, data and organizational inertia.

12:54

The real disruption is the pricing model

AI's impact on SAP isn't at the code layer, it's in the business model. SAP's stock fell from a high of $300 to $160, but stretched out to 2021 it still grew more than 20%; Adobe dropped from about 480 to around 220, and Zoom has almost vanished. What's actually being replaced is point-solution SaaS. The core of the shock is the traditional seat-based pricing model, which will shift toward consumption-based or even outcome-based pricing — a challenge every enterprise software company will have to face.

— Yuan Xin
37:24

Don't train a foundation model — stay open

SAP doesn't train its own general-purpose large model, because foundation models evolve too fast and what SAP is good at is enterprise process, data and interaction. SAP's approach is to open up to all third-party models, and on model selection, pair the most suitable model with the most suitable scenario. At the same time it trains its own proprietary small model, RPT 1.5, on its own structured table data, for financial scenarios like receivables forecasting where accuracy needs to be close to 100% — because in finance, 99% accuracy is still unusable.

— Yuan Xin
43:46

The boss wants AI, but the dirty data is still there

What big-company CIOs dread most is the boss reading an article on WeChat and forwarding it with "I want that too." The gap is that everyone assumes once AI arrives, the dirty, grubby work no longer has to be done. AI can accelerate data cleaning, but it can't make that process disappear. To land AI, you still need FDEs to comb through historical data and organize it by business object, and at the same time to "distill" the veteran's unstructured decisions into enterprise memory, so that the share that can be automated keeps growing step by step.

— Yuan Xin
49:03

FDE and consultants will eventually merge

FDE is not a rebranded on-site engineer. Traditional ERP consultants are industry experts who enter from the business side and ultimately land requirements into a standard product; FDEs deploy the people who build the product to the customer ahead of time, and the capability they distill flows back into the product. Early FDEs leaned toward low-level engineering and lacked a converter to turn business needs into coding requirements, which is why Anthropic and OpenAI started partnering with consultancies like McKinsey and Accenture. The two groups will merge into a hybrid role that understands both business and technology — which is exactly the necessary path for spilling out of a small circle into the enterprise market.

— Yuan Xin
55:30

The customs agent's accuracy flipped

A large innovative company built its own agent to handle customs declarations for 33 countries, and accuracy was only 60-70%, so people had to review the remaining errors anyway, to loud complaints. After SAP came in, relying on the GTC global tax platform's basic understanding of 33 countries' tax rules plus document AI's template configuration — replacing long-context tokens — accuracy jumped instantly to the 90s, and what started as a pilot in one country had thirty-plus countries clamoring to go live. This shows that exploring AI's business value doesn't require discarding old accumulation; standard process plus incremental value is the effective path.

— Yuan Xin
1:06:03

28% attempt it, only 3% see returns

McKinsey interviewed more than 2,000 companies worldwide: 28% have already used AI on projects, but only 3% believe there is real business return. So very few projects actually go to production; most are still in POC and prototype stage. Big customers have money to burn on trial and error and move fast; small companies run fast with open-source tools; those struggling most are mid-sized companies in the few-hundred-million to roughly 10-billion range, where whether to embrace AI is strongly correlated with the founder's mindset — and Chinese companies are more willing to put money into the product side than the management side.

— Yuan Xin
1:23:02

Going global rides on a localization base

SAP's global platform itself covers operations in more than 150 countries, and its product organization includes a localization team dedicated to tax compliance localization for each country. Chinese companies going global can borrow this base directly and only worry about their business. Lenovo has worked with SAP from 20 billion to several hundred billion in scale, and now uses process mining tools to comb through more than 2,000 processes to find breakpoints and room for efficiency gains. When acquiring European companies, the other side is naturally an SAP user, so system integration has a built-in advantage.

Glossary

ERP
The core system SAP was built on, managing end-to-end enterprise processes like finance, procurement and production.
FDE
Forward Deployed Engineer: goes to the customer site to land AI, while distilling the results back into the product.
Human in the loop
After AI executes automatically, human review of key judgments is still required.
Process mining
Using logs to analyze the real business flow and find breakpoints and room for efficiency gains.
Physical AI
AI combined with physical things like robots and EVs, considered a direction where China has an edge.
