Siemens Ovens Skip AI While Chinese Appliances Race to Add It

Siemens and Bosch keep AI out of new appliances, while Skyworth has integrated Gemini into TVs and Haier made refrigerators automatically link to supermarkets—at the same show, two competing philosophies on AI adoption speed collided.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

5:07

Exoskeletons will halve in price, size, and weight within two years

Extribot's exoskeletons currently sell for around 2,500 euros in Europe, weigh three kilograms, and offer over 20 kilometers of battery range—still at a stage where they cannot yet become universally accessible. But the company has set explicit timelines: within one to two years, they plan to cut size by half, weight by half, and price by half. This means a technology confined to military and medical domains for two or three decades is now iterating at consumer-electronics speed.

— Ji Qu brand representative
13:11

Winning globally requires choosing depth in one category over breadth

Jigu spent ten years making only handheld fans and now sells in over 100 countries, with more than 70 million units shipped. The Jigu Live series alone pulls over 1 billion in annual sales. The founding team deliberately refused to expand horizontally into new categories, instead pushing one market segment to perfection. They emphasize that getting products to market and becoming a true global brand are different things. That gap is whether repeat customers exist, whether they think of you on the second purchase, whether they would recommend you to others.

— Summer (Ji Shu brand manager)
23:16

Wearable AI wins by understanding context, not transcribing speech

Mamacat, founded by a former Anker CMO, makes an AI memory device worn on the wrist, coexisting with your Apple Watch and intentionally distancing itself from the recording-app market. The founder emphasizes they are not a recording company or a meeting-summary tool—they are building continuous memory by linking scattered conversations and meetings. That is why they open-sourced a model: to help the system understand how same-topic content gets classified, connected, and evolves, sparing users the work of reconstructing and organizing threads themselves.

— Mamacat founder
27:17

Real-time translation belongs in earpieces, not on eyeglasses

As AI glasses increasingly pitch translation as a headline feature, Timekettle's founder disagrees: glasses offer one-directional, visual speed, which breaks the natural back-and-forth of ear-to-ear conversation. Translation as an AI glasses feature treats it like a selling point rather than a function built from how people actually talk. Germany, Timekettle's strongest European market, has 2 million migrants among 8 million residents—inherent demand for cross-language conversation was built into the region's needs.

— Leo (Shi Kong Hu founder)
37:31

The television category is being redefined as 'smart display'

Skyworth became the first brand globally to integrate Gemini into a TV. The CEO identifies three reasons users embrace it: traditional remotes are clunky—voice is faster; Google's ecosystem means you pull content straight from YouTube without watching the full film; the TV occupies the living room's prime real estate, positioned to work with cameras and other smart-home devices. Future products, he predicts, will be renamed—no longer "TV" but Smart Display or AI Display instead. The main focus right now is moving AI integration further down-market.

— Chuang Wei CEO
40:38

German appliance makers chose tradition while China chose AI as default

At the same show, Siemens' new appliances contain no AI. The brand confirmed this was a choice, not a capability gap. Bosch's booth showed traditional washers and dishwashers with no AI concept in sight. By contrast, Chinese brands treat AI as baseline: Haier's vision spans the full lifecycle—buying food, managing inventory, controlling other appliances, linking to local supermarkets to proactively suggest restocking. Passive reaction to commands versus active prediction: two fundamentally different product philosophies facing each other.

43:41

Global consumer electronics has entered the Chinese manufacturing cycle

The IFA CEO made an observation: consumer electronics has progressed through the Sony-led Japan cycle, the Samsung-led Korea cycle, and now enters the China cycle. Evidence appeared beyond the conference floor: a German server working a coffee bar nearby mentioned owning a Roborock robot vacuum and being very satisfied. It echoed a pattern the host observed throughout: Chinese brands are shifting from followers to category definers.

— Wang Qin Wen
