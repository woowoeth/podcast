DeepSeek's rate-limiting isn't about money—it's falling behind the US on computing power

DeepSeek's throttling of consumer service is not about saving money—it's because domestic chips lag far behind overseas leaders in both quantity and generation, so model companies would rather pull chips back for training than serve ordinary users.

Bytedance's abundance stems from early, aggressive purchasing

The host notes Bytedance has about one million GPUs, with 10,000 new H200s arriving in August 2025. This abundance explains why Bytedance's Doubao (豆包) model avoids DeepSeek-style user throttling—not a technical choice, but inventory depth.

Your chat data is worthless to model makers

Xiaobai explains that model training requires data cleaning and diversity; ordinary chat data introduces strange speech patterns requiring costly alignment fixes. Role-playing scenarios have proven commercially dead. What model makers actually want is agentic data—coding-agent execution traces that directly improve AGI capabilities. Chat services are pure cost, not value.

— Xiao Bai
15:56

China's leading chips are over two years behind the US

Timeline: Nvidia's main chips 2022-2024 were H100 to H800, still powering most Chinese data centers (plus the crippled H20). By 2025, overseas players run B200, GB200, B300 generation. Beyond generation gaps, scale matters: China's three largest companies combined have hundreds of thousands of chips; xAI alone has 780,000 running and nearly 600,000 more on order—totaling well over one million, with newer architectures.

21:21

Chip orchestration was once scarcer than chips themselves

Xiaobai argues model training bottlenecks were never just data and talent—they were AI infra teams' ability to schedule compute. A million chips distributed badly equals nothing. This explains why AI infra talent commanded premium pay: not chip shortage alone, but no one could truly optimize the cluster.

— Xiao Bai
27:48

Model training still needs humans—AI cannot yet replace the engineer

Xiaobai distinguishes: AI excels at translating inference approaches from CUDA to Huawei or Alibaba architectures—brute force works. But real model training is rife with novel optimizations and mysterious infra errors that AI struggles to handle. If engineers are replaced and that layer breaks, deeper optimization demands will find no one to meet them.

— Xiao Bai
33:32

China's latest models were trained overseas on the newest chips

Overseas labs fingerprinted models and found DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2, K2 Pro trained on B300/B200—meaning training happened on overseas cloud. As foreign data centers tighten remote-compute restrictions for China, this workaround is being shut down.

39:52

Huawei's 910C reaches half the performance of Nvidia's cards

Twitter reports show Huawei's 910C achieves H20-level performance at roughly half. Not cutting-edge, but adequate for inference—freeing Nvidia chips for training. This is considered the realistic interim solution.

55:30

Domestic chips lack ecosystem, not engineering

Xiaobai cites Google Colab: newcomers get free chips to learn on—critical for developing AI talent. Domestic chips lack this. Core reason: insufficient quantity—major companies hoard them, leaving nothing for personal developers. Supply will naturally drive ecosystem building.

— Xiao Bai
