Physical AI founder bets hardware first: when foundation models arrive, pure models thin

When Liu Songming founded LiberAI, he predicted foundation models like GPT-6 Astra would consume System 2—the perception and planning layer of embodied AI. From day one, he chose hardware and product as the moat instead of building a pure model company.

Technical judgment, not industry connections, drives early-stage capital decisions

Industry convention pairs young technical talent with a veteran industry executive. Liu chose differently. At this stage, the company's biggest expenditure is compute and data collection. Deciding where that money goes and which technical paths to pursue requires technical judgment, not just resource coordination. Without it, capital efficiency collapses.

— Liu Songming
35:23

Foundation models will consume the perception layer, squeezing pure robotics companies

Liu maps robot-world interaction into three layers: System 2 (perception, planning, reasoning), System 1 (converting instructions to action sequences), System 0 (low-level motor control). Through 2023-24, venture capital believed embodied companies could own all three layers and scale toward AGI. Liu predicted from day one that foundation models would inevitably capture System 2. GPT-6 Astra's arrival proved it. This makes pure-model robotics startups dangerously thin. LiberAI chose full-stack from inception—using hardware and product to thicken the narrowing moat.

— Liu Songming
59:37

Glove data with 25% teleoperation outperforms 100% teleoperation alone

Experiments show glove data combined with 25% real-robot teleoperation data outperforms models trained on 100% real-robot teleoperation alone. Teleoperation isn't perfect: operators watch sideways while robots move forward, creating a viewpoint mismatch with the robot's camera. Lack of force feedback makes operators slow. This motion pattern diverges from smooth human actions in internet videos. Gloves worn on human hands align naturally with human motion and extract knowledge from internet videos more effectively.

— Liu Songming
1:19:43

Hardware constraints go deeper into performance than training recipes can fix

The biggest surprise wasn't training—it was hardware. Different morphologies and sensor choices affect final performance far more than expected, and this impact can't be recovered through later training approaches. System 0 motor control algorithms couple deeply with System 1 action generation. Motor control couples tightly with the robot's joint structure, DOF, and motor parameters. This demonstrates hardware companies' structural advantage over pure model companies—but it also means iteration speed is constrained by hardware's long development cycles.

— Liu Songming
1:36:56

Foundation model labs lack the character for robotics' unglamorous delivery work

Consider rumors of OpenAI or Anthropic acquiring Physical Intelligence, and potential entry by carmakers or Huawei. Liu argues that real competition only emerges at delivery scale, where every customer is a hardened negotiation. This work demands patience that OpenAI likely lacks, and the incentives don't align: the same engineering effort applied to software yields 20x growth. Why chase service and manufacturing margins? Liu judges the real threat is non-standard automation specialists and prior-generation delivery firms.

— Liu Songming
1:48:01

Physical robotics will eventually consolidate into an Apple-scale giant

Liu expects physical AI applications to transition from today's fragmented commercial and industrial niches toward consolidation. Eventually a mega-company emerges at Apple's scale—among the greatest industrial products in human history. This company likely emerges from firms already pursuing full-stack, hardware-first approaches. This is what LiberAI positions as its highest-probability wager.

— Liu Songming
2:00:06

Build organizations that question decisions made too fast with too little information

Liu reflects on past errors: mistakes often stem not from bad option selection but from deciding too fast on incomplete information. People unconsciously filter incoming data through existing expectations. This trap is especially dangerous in familiar domains, where strong priors tempt you to skip verification. The remedy: deliberately preserve diverse voices and cultivate people who can stand up and audit—to coordinate information gaps and misaligned goals—rather than relying on one founder's final call.

— Liu Songming
