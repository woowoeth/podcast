Boards raise stock price, they don't judge morality

Stephen Witt argues that board duty is maximizing shareholder value, not ethical judgment. Once Trump demanded Huang's return, the board had almost no way to refuse—refusal would harm both shareholders and business. Nvidia's market cap jumped $1 trillion the moment reinstatement was announced, confirming Huang's status as ‘too big to fire’: the company depends on him, and the market depends on Nvidia.

5:14

Honeypot trap, affairs, gray-market sales: all swept aside

Witt describes this as Huang's ‘complete escape’: the honeypot trap, multiple affairs, gray-market chip sales to Southeast Asia through Singapore—none will face real accountability. The only variable he sees is Democratic congressional hearings next year, but for now, as long as Trump is in office, Huang faces almost no consequences, because he represents an extreme key man risk—the entire global economy now rests on his judgment alone.

— Stephen Witt
6:15

Lobbying bypasses the boardroom, goes straight to the President

Huang's team built a ‘war room’ with PR, lawyers, and allegedly Musk involved, but Witt says they never tried to persuade the board—they only needed Trump's support. Trump initially hesitated, Witt speculates, but once Nvidia's stock drop started dragging down the AI sector and the broader market, he pivoted to protect Huang: with approval ratings already low, he couldn't afford another market collapse.

— Stephen Witt
9:24

Board members have been his allies since day one, 1993

When asked whether the board would use this to refresh its ranks, Witt expects no major turnover: several directors have been there since Nvidia's 1993 founding—like Tench Coxe, who holds $4-5 billion in Nvidia stock and won't leave, and voted for reinstatement anyway. This reflects Nvidia's culture: senior leadership tolerates Huang's temperament and controversial conduct because standing beside him means tens of billions in returns.

— Stephen Witt
11:26

Robot brains run on Nvidia because Huang bet ten years early

Witt discovered while covering robots this year that twenty robot makers from different companies all run Nvidia platforms inside—because Huang spent ten years, before robotics even existed, building the ‘brain’ they would need. This long-term vision to predict industry dynamics a decade or two ahead is irreplaceable; operations and engineering talent can be replaced, but this foresight cannot.

— Stephen Witt
18:45

Vance's personal assurance to the board swayed the decision overnight

Jonathan Swan reports that Trump called Vice President Vance over the weekend and had him personally persuade Huang, then had Vance assure the board that Huang posed no personal national security risk. This call and the lobbying that followed was key to Huang walking back into office Monday morning without the board waiting for investigation results.

— Jonathan Swan
22:57

Rubio warned months ago; Trump chose to ignore it

Swan reports that Secretary of State Rubio wrote a memo on March 30 saying Huang's girlfriend maintains contact with Chinese intelligence officials; in May these concerns kept Huang off the official China trip, but Trump, seeing negative coverage, called to have him ‘join in Alaska’. Trump from the start framed this as an infidelity issue, not a national security one—once the President sets the frame, other officials can't openly push back.

— Jonathan Swan
26:02

Tech right's major victory, but congressional investigation still ahead

Swan confirms this is a major win for David Sacks and the tech-right faction close to Vance who long supported Huang. But Democrats will launch fiercer congressional investigation once they take House and Senate next year, and the government already has cargo records about chip shipments; the investigation will establish how much Huang personally knew about internal violations.
