Don't rush to buy tools; enterprise AI starts with building a working model

Wang explains that unlike older technologies like databases, everyone has their own understanding of AI, so widespread adoption won't work through brainstorming. Enterprises should first build a closed-loop working model in one department, then use that as proof to convince the rest of the company to follow.

3:02

Brain in a vat has no limbs

Wang's first reaction to seeing GPT-4 was: the problem of human thought is essentially solved—as an AI researcher working in applications, he immediately felt his past research was no longer necessary. But this new intelligence is just a "brain in a vat": no hands, no feet, only able to give answers in a chat window. Users have to manually copy generated code back to the editor. What FDE does is give this "brain" hands and feet, memory and scene-matching capability, turning it from a chat tool into an agent that can run in enterprise production systems.

— Wang Shuyuan
10:05

Problem feedback doesn't leave the company

Wang says Google Cloud FDE's advantage isn't "stronger models" but a shorter debug loop: when FDE discovers capability boundaries on the customer site, they feed that directly back to internal product or model quality teams, then verify again after updates ship—internal communication within the company. If independent or third-party FDE can't solve a problem, they're looking at another company's tools; cross-organization communication is orders of magnitude slower. Third-party FDE's advantage is staying neutral between model vendors and helping customers compare options, but that's a tradeoff, not a capability advantage.

— Wang Shuyuan
15:07

FDE sells model homes

Wang uses renovation as an analogy: a single house doesn't need a model home, just hire someone to renovate. But renovating an entire neighborhood means you must build a model home first—the model home may not have a toilet or mop, but it must have running water and electricity, meaning the demo doesn't need every feature, but the data pipeline must actually work in the real scenario and form a closed loop. His provocative take: enterprises can't push AI through brainstorming because unlike databases, where only one department understood the technology, everyone now has their own understanding of AI, making consensus impossible. The right approach is to make one department's model home beautiful and functional, then use that as proof to convince other departments to follow.

— Wang Shuyuan
30:21

The boundary between R&D and PM melts first

Wang observed that before the Agent era, the line between his AI R&D team and PM team blurred first—developers started talking directly to LLMs to write project proposals and argue for algorithm value, work that used to be purely PM; PMs took R&D documents and asked LLMs for strategic direction, work that used to be purely R&D. He calls this phenomenon "FDE BP" (business partner): as vibe coding spreads, pure technical ability becomes secondary, what matters is whether you can reverse-engineer from customer business problems what kind of agent workflow is needed. This is making teams reconsider whether whiteboard coding belongs in hiring conversations anymore.

— Wang Shuyuan
37:24

Don't ride the main channel, value is in the ripples

Wang describes a real case: a customer wanted to translate an entire ad image with pixel-level text from one language to another. He immediately objected because that sits squarely on the model's main channel—sure enough, two or three weeks later Google released Nano Banana and the whole custom system would have been obsolete. His conclusion: FDE should stand on the ship's sides looking for ripples, not at the bow. Needs on the main channel the model vendor will solve themselves soon. FDE's real job is finding the gaps to the sides and behind, the gaps the model still misses but customers genuinely need.

— Wang Shuyuan
43:27

Mechanism can't buy trust

Wang says mechanism alone can't build trust—it can only help eliminate the last tiny bit of doubt. He uses Manus as an example: when multiple cloud vendors and model companies were pitching, what won was Google's FDE colleague solving concrete problems with enough initiative that trust slowly accumulated; only then could they discuss mechanisms like early model checkpoint access. He's blunt: agents still can't replace FDE because building trust face-to-face between people isn't automated yet.

— Wang Shuyuan
48:32

Next wave explodes in knowledge workers

Wang's provocative take: the first AI wave already cashed out in programming; the next will blow up in knowledge work—sure, knowledge workers use fewer tokens per person than programmers, but feed the AI every meeting someone's attended and document they've written since joining the company, and tokens add up fast. He had a second ChatGPT moment using Gemini Agent for manager work: he doesn't have to tell AI which documents to look at or which meetings matter, just describe what he wants done and AI runs for a long time pulling context together. He calls this process "self-distillation" for knowledge workers and argues many enterprise AI failures aren't about AI itself, but about companies that skipped their digital transformation.

— Wang Shuyuan
52:54

What took two months now finishes in one pass with agents

Wang contrasts his lived experience: using the old workflow (requirements document, architecture document, progress document all kept in sync with every change tracked) to build his wife's career coaching website—database design and full-stack deployment—took about two months of spare time. After Manus, he re-ran the same project without maintaining three documents or tracking changes, end-to-end, and got a basically functional page in one go. He sees agent evolution in layers: first round judges answer quality, second judges task completion, the next round will judge whether it matches how human brains process information at their natural pace—sometimes he wants one sentence, but the agent produces a five-minute read, and that mismatch hasn't been solved.

— Wang Shuyuan
