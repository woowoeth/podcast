Open-source models will win; vLLM is the Linux of AI inference

vLLM is already the most active open-source project on GitHub, but You Kaichao's real judgment is this: model capabilities cannot stay hidden for long, open-source models will ultimately win, and the inference engine is the next Linux — the moat is fast iteration, not the model itself.

Tuning is not the bottleneck — hardware's multi-level cache is

You Kaichao left algorithm research for three escalating reasons. First, submissions to academic conferences grew exponentially while review quality fell, making submission feel like buying a lottery ticket. Second, he found that algorithmic results depend heavily on machine learning systems: Kaiming He's ResNet rested on large-scale experiments, while a lab with small data can only "do a Daoist rite inside a snail shell." Third, friends in industry reported that what Megvii engineers struggled with most was the hardware's multi-level cache, not hyperparameter tuning. Together these three pushed him from algorithms to systems.

— Kaichao You
1:07:12

An open-source project with no company behind it is bound to die

His advisor believed vLLM would surely die without company backing — just as Linux could not have gotten where it is without Red Hat, Ubuntu without Google, or PyTorch without Meta. An open-source community is not a legal entity: there is no one to sign an NDA, a big company donating compute can only hand over a single machine, and cluster-level optimization means begging everywhere and having it revoked at any moment. You Kaichao decided to start a company at the end of 2024, took a postdoc offer to wait two years for it, and kept saying internally, "if only there were a company."

— Kaichao You
1:20:36

The core skill in maintaining an open-source project is daring to delete features

vLLM uses tiered governance: a few "benevolent dictators" make the final call, about a dozen core maintainers own key modules, and a few dozen committers write code. The hard decision You Kaichao made was to delete beam search, even though recommender-system users kept complaining; he judged that mainstream AI inference no longer depends on beam search, and that maintaining it would only add unmaintainable complexity. That ability to make trade-offs is the key to setting priorities in an open-source project.

— Kaichao You
1:24:55

The assumption that "community users are all well-intentioned" has already broken

In 2025, ranked by contributor activity on GitHub, vLLM was the most active project. This brought a "happy problem": training bootcamps packaging students' resumes by submitting junk PRs, bot accounts filing 20 PRs in 10 minutes — breaking the assumption that "community users are all well-intentioned." Maintainers had to introduce verification mechanisms and block bots. As coding agents advance, code becomes cheap, and maintainers find it faster to rewrite a PR themselves than to review it; the community is turning into a two-layer structure of maintainers plus users.

— Kaichao You
1:53:27

If a model doesn't fit the hardware, it can't capture the compute dividend

He uses hydropower as an analogy: hardware is the natural resource, the model is the generator, and the inference engine is the power system. With the same water flow, generators designed differently differ enormously in efficiency — that is co-design. He points to the "hardware lottery": once Moore's Law fails, compute gains come from specialized chips, and a model architecture that doesn't fit the hardware can't capture the dividend. Transformer drew the GPU lottery ticket, and RoPE became its best partner because it doesn't modify the attention kernel. DeepSeek's strength is precisely that its algorithm people understand systems — for instance, the exploration of MOE from coarse-grained to fine-grained was all implemented first on its own inference engine.

— Kaichao You
2:17:56

A token is not a generic commodity — it bears the model's brand

Electricity is a generic commodity; you can modulate voltage and frequency to a unified standard. A token is different: you cannot convert a DeepSeek token into a Kimi token, because the token bears the model's brand. That is why model architectures are so varied, and the inference engine must support two or three hundred architectures while continuously trimming unnecessary complexity. This also explains why open-source models will win: once a model is used at scale, user data can train new models, and model capabilities cannot stay hidden for long.

— Kaichao You
2:36:07

For models that deal with people, million-level context is enough

You Kaichao offers two predictions. First, the context needs of models that deal with people basically stop at the million level; only special fields like biochemistry need tens of millions or even hundreds of millions, and long tasks can be handed off to external tools. Second, open-source models will win in the end, because a model is not like a nuclear weapon that can be kept hidden for deterrence — it needs large-scale use, and use accumulates data, which replicates new models. So the moat is not model capability but fast iteration.

— Kaichao You

Glossary

PagedAttention
Managing the KV cache in fixed-size blocks to reduce memory fragmentation; the core algorithm of vLLM.
KV Cache
Caching the keys and values of historical tokens during autoregressive generation to avoid recomputation.
Speculative Decoding
Guessing multiple tokens in parallel first, then verifying them in one pass, to speed up autoregressive decoding.
MOE
Activating only a small number of experts per token, scaling model parameters with less computation.
BYOC
A model where the customer provides the machines and the company provides software and inference service, billed by token.
