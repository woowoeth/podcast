Nations Now Prioritize Supply Chain Control Over Global Efficiency

Tianyang frames today's global trend as ‘sovereignty first’: countries have spotted hidden supply-chain vulnerabilities and are now systematically decoupling across chips, food, and energy. His example: China's moves starting with Syngenta to build seed autonomy eventually triggered US sanctions against ZTE and Huawei. Both sides are playing out self-reliance strategies on a decade-long timescale.

The Impossible Trilemma: Guns, Butter, and Sound Money Cannot Coexist

The framework: military and tech spending against China is fixed and off the table. Social media-driven polarization makes welfare cuts politically impossible. Historical pattern: when both are immovable, sound money yields first through continuous deficits. The present is on the same macro path as the 1960s before Bretton Woods fell.

— Tianyang
11:12

Gold's Rise Today Echoes 1968, Signaling a Bretton Woods Crisis

Tianyang compares the gold surge since 2023 to 1968, on the eve of the London gold pool's collapse: the dollar's gold anchor was being eroded by persistent inflationary policy, France and others began dumping dollars and exiting the pool, until Nixon formally ended Bretton Woods in 1970-71. He sees today's gold anomaly as releasing the same warning signal early—though the full rupture may take longer.

— Tianyang
14:14

To Make Financial Repression Work, Nations Must Control Capital Flight

An alternative to high debt is financial repression: maintain inflation at 4-5%, keep rates low to erode debt. But Tianyang argues this only works if capital cannot freely exit—so capital controls are quietly approaching. He points to Iran, China, and Russia imposing diesel-export bans, and Trump considering similar moves, as signals that ‘restricted flows’ are spreading from specific goods into a broader pattern.

— Tianyang
22:23

Nvidia's Funding Demands Are Infinite Sprints, Not Sustainable Growth

Tianyang uses the metaphor of ‘a marathon made of nothing but 100-meter sprints’ for AI funding: labs need constant rapid financing to keep training momentum, with no time for normal due diligence—the source of circular financing and balance-sheet inversion. As Fed tightening and inflation pressure rise, each sprint faces stronger headwinds, and no one knows which sprint breaks the runner.

— Tianyang
27:27

Government Funding of AI Will Determine Winners, Not Market Competition

Tianyang concedes private capital can't easily monetize AI alone, but if the logic is ‘nukes have no economic return yet deserve funding,’ governments will eventually join—with conditions. They'll mandate domestic-supplier preferences, trapping capital at home to inflate local assets while excluding foreign suppliers. AI's winners will be decided by ‘national team’ ranking, not market efficiency.

— Tianyang
35:36

Iran Conflict Could Trigger the Impossible Trilemma Decision

Tianyang sees the Iran conflict as a trigger for the trilemma choice, since war, once started, is hard to stop and keeps squeezing sovereign resources. He sees three paths forward: a Ross Perot-style populist push to cut spending, a Peronist-style extreme turn, or a public backlash against data centers and AI expansion—treated as a real outcome, not mere speculation.

Glossary

Impossible Trinity / Trilemma
Military spending, welfare, and sound money are mutually exclusive; one must be sacrificed.
Financial Repression
Using suppressed interest rates and mild inflation to gradually reduce government debt burden.
London Gold Pool
A 1960s multinational effort to maintain the gold standard, whose breakdown in 1968 signaled Bretton Woods' coming collapse.
Circular Financing
Capital flowing in circles through related companies (chipmakers, cloud providers, AI labs) that invest in and serve each other.
