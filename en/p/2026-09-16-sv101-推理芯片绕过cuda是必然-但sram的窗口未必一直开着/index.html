Bypassing CUDA for inference chips is inevitable, but SRAM's window may not stay open forever

Training is about compute, inference is about bandwidth. Groq and Cerebras both bet on SRAM, but one discounts for determinism and the other for yield; OpenAI chose the most expensive HBM4 because what it lacks is power, not money.

Training bets on compute, inference bets on bandwidth

In the training era, no one could make money from training itself, so costs were unquantifiable and everyone just wanted to bet on the strongest cluster; in the inference era, the math is clear—how much the user pays per million tokens, and how much it costs me to produce those million tokens. More fundamentally, it's about computational density: training has massive data to saturate compute, while the decode phase of inference is strictly autoregressive, generating one token at a time, and each token requires reading the entire model from storage into the compute unit. So the core contradiction of inference shifts from compute to memory bandwidth.

— Ziyang
16:39

SRAM is consensus, but the two companies diverge

Groq and Cerebras both saw the bandwidth demand before Transformers appeared, and both ultimately converged on SRAM—because SRAM's absolute bandwidth and bandwidth per dollar are two to three orders of magnitude higher than HBM. The cost is capacity: DRAM stores one bit with one transistor and one capacitor, while SRAM needs six transistors, so per-chip capacity may be two orders of magnitude lower than HBM. Thus the system must be scaled up, with hundreds or thousands of chips to hold all model weights, and the bottleneck shifts from capacity to cluster communication scheduling. Here the two diverge: Groq moves all scheduling ahead to compile time, while Cerebras simply stuffs a cabinet of chips into an entire wafer.

— Mark
19:34

Groq's static scheduling collides with MoE's dynamism

Groq designs hardware around the compiler, achieving ultimate determinism with no runtime scheduling overhead. But with MoE, each token activates 8 out of 128 experts, and which ones are chosen is decided at runtime, unpredictable at compile time. The static approach has only two options: conservatively send all tokens, leaving some chips idle; or change the way the model is partitioned, but current models are hard to partition cleanly along dimensions other than experts. Idling only affects cost-effectiveness, not its speed, so Groq remains a good choice outside of GPUs.

— Mark
25:18

The price Cerebras pays for yield

The maximum single-chip size is about 800 square millimeters, yielding thirty to forty chips per wafer, and with 50% yield you still get 20 good ones. But Cerebras's entire wafer is one product; if defects exceed the acceptable 30%, the whole wafer is scrapped. This is not mask cost (masks can be amortized), but that out of every 10 made, perhaps 9 are unusable. So it may achieve 10 times Nvidia's bandwidth, but at 3 times the cost of HBM, the bandwidth cost-effectiveness is discounted. Groq discounts for determinism, Cerebras discounts for yield cost, and the discounts ultimately hit token cost.

— Mark
33:58

In the inference era, CUDA will inevitably be bypassed

TPU and Trainium have no intention of being CUDA-compatible, yet people still find TPU software hard to use; Anthropic can buy in bulk because many of its engineers come from Google and know JAX. The key change is that in the training era, people were willing to pay for good software, but in the inference era, the balance tips toward performance and cost-effectiveness. As long as it's not many hard problems but one hard problem, writing all Transformer operators on a new chip makes business sense; plus AI Coding accelerates operator optimization, greatly lowering the software stack barrier. DeepSeek would rather write low-level PTX assembly for optimization, which is exactly this logic.

— Ziyang
44:25

Faster inference makes models smarter

After the large-scale explosion of Agents, a lot of tokens are not for humans to read but for other Agents to read; human reading speed tops out at 100 tokens per second. But the significance of inference speed is not faster interaction, but that in the same minute of waiting, the model can use 10 times more tokens for more internal thinking, thus becoming smarter. Jensen Huang's PPT has 'Smarter AI' on the horizontal axis and tokens per watt on the vertical axis. Conversely, some applications that give answers quickly may just be AI thinking less. Time is the essential constraint—if you ask AI to judge tomorrow's stock market, thinking for two days makes the answer meaningless.

— Ziyang
49:04

Grasp the invariants: FFN becomes stable after turning into MoE

Model structure will definitely affect chip design; for example, diffusion models are compute-intensive and suit another paradigm, so you must first make a heterogeneous choice: whether to do this. More critically, find the invariants—in Transformer, the attention mechanism is still constantly innovating, with every new lab publishing papers with new ideas; but the subsequent FFN has become a relative invariant since turning into MoE. RL in post-training also has no essential impact on model structure. So prioritize solving the part that has already converged, so the chip won't be left behind by algorithm iterations.

— Mark
1:09:40

Everything revolves around locality

Bill Dally's exact words are that computer architecture is like real estate, everything is about location, location, location. Temporal locality is that data used once will likely be used again; spatial locality is that after using a piece of data, you'll soon use the data around it; CPU caches are applications of these two. But in the decode phase of AI inference, algorithmic temporal and spatial locality are both flattened, so you must compensate for locality with hardware at the architecture level—putting model weights into SRAM is essentially buying the best locality.

— Mark
1:17:30

OpenAI chose HBM4 because it lacks power, not money

Jalapeño is a general-purpose inference chip that encompasses prefill, decode, attention, and FFN all in one chip, and can even run game demos; the next generation will also support training. It stacks HBM4 bandwidth to 15.4 TB per second per package, taking the most expensive path. The reason is that US data centers lack not capital and space but power, so it cares extremely about tokens produced per watt, doing a lot of work on power consumption, with tokens per megawatt greater than Nvidia's corresponding Rubin architecture. In China, electricity accounts for about 1/3 of cost, in the US about 2/3; different constraints lead to different convergence points.

— Ziyang
1:27:03

Heterogeneity inside the chip or at the system level

OpenAI proposed the concept of Dark silicon: when a chip does different things, some parts can be turned off or powered down, using more cost to buy power efficiency. This differs from the system-level heterogeneity of Groq and Cerebras—the latter is one chip cooperating with other chips, putting the attention KV cache on another chip. SRAM has low capacity cost-effectiveness, suitable for storing fixed model weights, not for storing continuously growing KV caches. So for scenarios where power is the first constraint, OpenAI's solution is good; for scenarios requiring extremely high bandwidth cost-effectiveness, it will still converge to SRAM.

Glossary

arithmetic intensity
The amount of computation per unit of data read, determining whether a chip is compute-bound or bandwidth-bound.
roofline model
An architectural analysis framework that uses arithmetic intensity to determine whether an application is limited by compute or bandwidth.
MFU
The ratio of actual compute to theoretical peak, mainly measuring utilization of matrix multiplication.
MBU
The ratio of memory bandwidth actually utilized, a key metric for high-speed inference scenarios.
Dark silicon
Parts of a chip are turned off or powered down during specific tasks to gain power efficiency.
Amdahl's Law
Overall system speedup is limited by unoptimized parts, explaining why heterogeneous systems only improve by 10x.
