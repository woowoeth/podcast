Stablecoins Are Not a Duopoly: Big Companies Want Their Own Coin

The multi-stablecoin debate returns: Rob says he knows of multinationals issuing their own stablecoins, Circle's USDC network effect is underrated, but at 2-3% rates its revenue gets cut in half.

The multi-stablecoin debate is not over

Santi had previously said the "hundreds of stablecoin thesis is dead," and Rob pushed back on the spot. Rob says he knows of multinationals issuing their own stablecoins: SoFi, Verve, two bank consortia, the Stripe consortium, MoneyGram, Western Union are all doing it, and Bridge already has its own stablecoin internally for treasury management. His mechanism: big companies hold a lot of cash, and if they can earn yield on it, they have an incentive to issue their own coin rather than use USDC or USDT. Santi pressed: don't these big companies have FX desks at JP Morgan and Morgan Stanley? Rob said it depends on the size of the company and the complexity of global treasury management — Western Union's remittance corridors in Africa and Latin America are something JP Morgan's correspondent banking network can't do well.

13:18

CLARITY failed, but nobody has really given up

The Senate voted down the CLARITY Act 49 to 50 on Tuesday; it needed 60 votes. Three Republicans — Susan Collins, Josh Hawley, Jerry Moran — voted against, and every Democrat they had been counting on voted against. Santi said not to over-read Tillis's turn, noting Kirsten Gillibrand said that same day she is still pushing the legislation. On Polymarket the odds of passage fell as low as 4%, then recovered to 8%. The market dropped on the news: Coinbase down 10%, Circle down 12%. Rob said it's worth remembering that dozens of people worked on this bill for a year, including staff on the Banking Committee and the Agriculture Committee.

— Santi
16:58

The SEC gives tokenized stocks a five-year exemption

The SEC issued an order granting tokenized securities trading venues (TSVS) a five-year interim conditional exemption, letting them trade tokenized NMS stocks through permissioned AMMs and liquidity pools without registering as exchanges, and liquidity providers got a limited market-maker exemption. Two key conditions: token holders must get the same rights as the underlying stock, such as dividends and voting; and third-party tokenization requires written notice with an opportunity for the issuer to object. Santi read it out and said this clause could directly break the current onchain tokenized stock practice — if AMC can object, you can't just throw AMC onto Hyperliquid. Rob said this is almost certainly coming, because onchain tokenized stocks currently create strange dislocations after hours and pre-market, and Robinhood pairing memecoins with tokenized stocks is an example.

— Santi
22:43

The winner in tokenization is the whole ecosystem

Rob said it's hard to point to a single winner. The obvious beneficiaries are issuers, existing exchanges and clearinghouses, new exchanges that want into the US market (Coinbase, Polymarket, Lighter, Hyperliquid, Backpack), global neobanks, new transfer agents, and core infrastructure providers like Alpaca. Santi said his first reaction was Superstate and Securitize, and Securitize was up 16% that day. Rob said this looks more like a rising tide lifting all boats than value concentrating in one place. He added that as more assets come onchain, everything onchain gets bigger — more liquidity, more users, better infrastructure, more stablecoin issuance.

— Rob Hadick
29:06

S&P buying Open Zeppelin is buying data

S&P is a ratings agency with a roughly $120 billion market cap and about $14 billion in revenue; this week it first led Kao's Series B extension (Kao raised its B four years ago, and this time may have raised about $50 million, bringing the B to roughly $110 million, with investors including RBC, DRW, Broadridge, BNP, Nasdaq), then acquired smart contract security firm Open Zeppelin. Rob said the acquisition surprised him — Open Zeppelin didn't seem like a company that would run a process and sell. His read: S&P gives ratings, and it can use Open Zeppelin for real-time risk benchmarks and data, to assess what's happening inside protocols, and sell that to clients. Santi said he heard this is the first of many, and that traditional capital markets data and analytics players (Moody's and others) will do more deals like this. Rob said many crypto companies raised a lot of money and have good products but aren't good enough as standalone businesses, and companies like S&P grow into conglomerates by continuously buying good niche products and then bundling them back to you through a terminal.

— Rob Hadick
37:40

Circle pushing memes on Arc's first day was a misstep

Circle launched Arc with more than 190 partners on day one and over $220 million in TVL pre-seeded in a Morpho vault. Rob said he doesn't understand why Circle came out on day one pushing a bunch of memes, since Arc had always been positioned as a payments-first chain, talking about agentic finance, privacy, FX liquidity, real commercial activity. He said this is the exact opposite of the story Circle has long told, and it left a lot of people with a bad impression. Santi said he went to Circle's launch event yesterday, and the room and Twitter were two completely different worlds — within an hour he met people from BlackRock, DTCC, ICE, Visa, MoneyGram, and Circle's convening power is extremely strong; if they had launched purely on the original positioning, it would have gone much better. Rob said Circle may have seen Robinhood chain's success, but Robinhood is a trading platform and Circle is a payments company, and having bought Taza Pay it should stay in its own lane.

— Rob Hadick
42:38

Circle's revenue gets cut in half by rates

Rob said he doesn't expect 4% to 4.5% rates to last forever, and that rates will normalize to between 2% and 3%, which means if Circle is just an interest-income business, its revenue at current TVL gets cut in half. He also said that as a payments stablecoin, if you can't share yield with end customers, there isn't much reason to hold large amounts of it. Circle's agreement with Coinbase requires giving 50% to Coinbase, which puts it at a disadvantage competing with new stablecoins. So Circle is working to become a payments company and get a payments company multiple, and Arc and Circle Payment Network are both part of that story. Santi said don't underestimate USDC's network effect — in DeFi there is now only one thing that matters for liquidity, and that's USDC; every DeFi founder needs USDC on their platform.

— Rob Hadick
46:14

Instinct's reckoning comes in month four

Rob said companies are now getting bigger faster and raising more faster, that venture capital as a market structure is broken, and that the hottest companies see valuations rise faster and faster regardless of fundamentals. He said a company's first big reckoning usually comes in year four, but Instinct's first big reckoning will come in month four — because the two biggest companies in the world are about to ship direct competitors, Grokbot and Meta's Muse. Santi said from his own use Muse is six to eight times better than Instinct; Instinct is clearly compute-constrained, Muse is sometimes 10 to 15 minutes faster on the same question, fails less, and can route around the roadblocks Resy set up for these products. Rob said he doesn't know how growth investors are comfortable at this valuation, and the only thing that could prove them wrong is Instinct sprinting to train its own model, but that takes a billion dollars to build data centers.

— Rob Hadick
51:26

Polymarket is fighting a talent war

Polymarket announced four major hires: Bird founder Travis VanderZanden as chief growth officer, former Amazon, EA and Delta CFO Warren Jenson as its first CFO, former DoorDash GM Colin McKinney Hill as VP of operations, and Zora founder Jacob Horne joining to lead DeFi. Rob said prediction markets are fighting a talent war, because 14 months ago these two companies basically had no revenue and now each is doing billions in revenue, growing extremely fast but with growing pains. Shane is a highly visionary founder who can get people to follow him into battle, but running a large regulated exchange is very hard, so bringing in a team of professional executives was inevitable. He also noted Polymarket had already hired GC Neil Kumar and Ari in charge of sports.

— Rob Hadick

Glossary

TSVS / Tokenized Securities Trading Venue
The term the SEC's exemptive order uses for tokenized securities trading platforms.
NMS stock
A listed stock subject to US National Market System rules.
transfer agent
The institution responsible for registering securities holders and transfer records.
DAT / Digital Asset Treasury company
A public company holding crypto assets as its primary treasury, often trading at a discount or premium to NAV.
CPN / Circle Payment Network
The payments network Circle launched, part of the Arc ecosystem.
