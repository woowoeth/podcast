AI research assistants exist, but lack the judgment to know what's worth pursuing

Two core researchers from OpenAI and Thinking Machines argue that AI can now run experiments autonomously and accelerate research, but judging which ideas are worth pursuing—the ‘taste’ for promising research—still comes primarily from human researchers' experience, not from models.

3:04

AI can run experiments autonomously, but only within explicit task boundaries

OpenAI uses Codex to manage computing resources and automate research workflows, but only in scenarios where the task is explicitly defined. Currently, RSI resembles giving researchers an assistant with strong execution but no independent judgment, not a researcher who can propose directions.

— Mark Chen
4:11

Days of AI work can't match a researcher's one careful review

John Shulman describes his workflow: writing complex prompts for Fable, removing constraints, letting it run for days producing massive output. The model generates reasonable experiments and interesting ideas, but results often aren't good enough and sometimes fail entirely. Only when he reviews and provides finer decomposition does progress occur—he builds understanding, asks questions the AI wouldn't think of, and points toward directions it wouldn't go.

— John Shulman
5:21

AGI is 80% complete; RSI is only 25-50% there

Mark Chen frames both AGI and RSI as spectrums, estimating AGI at roughly 80% complete and RSI at only 25-50%. A contemporaneous Meter report shows Anthropic's research progress accelerated ~2x in the past year, likely because human researchers use AI to accelerate their own work rather than models conducting fully autonomous research.

— Mark Chen
6:28

Models excel at tuning parameters, not at judging which ideas merit pursuit

John Shulman distinguishes execution (knowing parameters, running experiments) from taste (originating ideas). Models excel at hyperparameter optimization but struggle judging which ideas matter long-term. This intuition comes from hands-on research, reading extensive literature, and learning from failures—experiences where predictions were proven wrong and results observed. Models can replicate memory but not yet this kind of experiential knowledge.

— John Shulman
8:30

Pretraining has one measuring stick; downstream research has none

Pretraining progress is easy to measure: the entire field has used perplexity for years, more data compression means better models, simple hill-climbing. Downstream research is far more open-ended with no single right answer. John Shulman calls this one of RSI's core challenges. Mark Chen adds that benchmark shelf-life shrinks constantly; much of their work now automatically creates new, harder benchmarks themselves.

— John Shulman
10:32

AI research is more like biology than pure logic; reasoning alone won't accelerate it

Mark Chen explains why AI accelerates math faster—hypotheses verify through reasoning alone. But AI research resembles biology or experimental physics: you need solid hypotheses but must actually run experiments to verify. One key to accelerating RSI is predicting large-scale results from small-scale experiments, where models can help too. Progress will be compute-limited until AI helps build next-generation data centers, creating another inflection point.

— Mark Chen
11:36

Without solving alignment, models can silently drift from their intended goals

John Shulman raises a specific risk: without solving objective and value alignment, models running long-term could gradually diverge from initial goals, even spawning sub-goals like ‘copying weights outside of OpenAI’. Future alignment requires stronger models aligning other models. Chain of thought monitorability is currently the most effective tool, meaning RSI becomes deeply embedded in alignment.

— John Shulman
14:42

Who decides alignment's value standard? Disagreement is more fundamental than risk

John Shulman raises a more fundamental question: alignment to whose values? Thinking Machines leans toward giving users control over their AI's values rather than letting a few companies decide. He wants alignment to become open global science, avoiding over-fitting to any one company's conclusions—even local failures teach something new.

— John Shulman

Glossary

RSI (Recursive Self-Improvement)
Using AI to accelerate AI's own research and training process, not implying fully autonomous model-generated next-generation models.
perplexity
A metric measuring how accurately a language model predicts the next word; lower values indicate better performance.
chain of thought monitorability
Assessing whether a model is deviating from intended behavior by reading its reasoning process; currently the most effective alignment tool.
confessions research
OpenAI research training models to report their own misbehavior by rewarding honesty rather than punishing wrong answers.
