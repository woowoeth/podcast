Evidence destroyed, coroner still blames cocaine for mother's death

After 22 months of waiting for DNA results: hair, drinking glasses, and fingernail DNA were never tested or have been destroyed, yet despite two toxicology results coming back clean, the medical examiner still leans toward cocaine-induced delirium as the cause of death.

3:09

DNA testing took 22 months when it should have been 4 to 6 weeks

The mother's body was exhumed for autopsy on February 1, 2023. Lauren was told DNA results would take 4 to 6 weeks. By the end of 2024, the wait had stretched to nearly 96 weeks—22 months. Lauren says she's unsure of the typical timeline after exhumation but suspects she may have set a record. The extended wait has become emblematic of the season's slow investigative progress.

— Lauren Malloy
4:15

The 1993 autopsy report contradicts itself

The original medical examiner listed hypertrophic cardiomyopathy as cause of death, but the autopsy showed no heart abnormalities. The same examiner noted the mother was a known cocaine user, yet toxicology showed clean. This contradiction shocked Lauren when she read it in 2021—family had suggested overdose, but no drugs were detected.

— Lauren Malloy
5:15

Three key pieces of evidence were never tested or already destroyed

Light brown hair found at the apartment entrance appeared torn from someone's head and clearly wasn't from the dark-haired mother, but cold case investigators told Lauren it can no longer be tested. Two drinking glasses from the kitchen were never analyzed. DNA found under the mother's fingernails remains unexplained. These were the pieces of evidence Lauren hoped would provide answers.

— Lauren Malloy
7:20

Death to autopsy was half a day apart; toxicology still came back clean

The mother was found and pronounced dead on Sunday, March 7, with the autopsy performed early the next morning and completed by midday. Lauren asked: given such a short window, drugs should still have been detectable. Yet the 2024 retesting showed the same result as 1993—clean, no drugs detected. This undermined Lauren's theory that her mother might have been given an overdose.

— Lauren Malloy
8:24

Toxicology found no drugs, yet coroner pivots to cocaine-delirium theory

The cold case team told Lauren that the state medical examiner is considering another possibility: cocaine-induced agitated delirium leading to cardiac death—a condition that causes hair pulling and violent behavior. But Lauren points out that no cocaine was detected in her mother's system, no drugs or paraphernalia were found photographed or recorded in the apartment, and the scene description contains no relevant evidence. The explanation, she says, "makes no sense at all."

— Lauren Malloy
9:27

A researcher questions the cocaine theory when no cocaine was detected

Roxanne, an aunt working in cancer research, was asked if she'd heard of "cocaine-induced delirium." She said she was unfamiliar with the term, but pointed out that if the original autopsy toxicology showed no drugs and later testing also showed none, using that as a cause-of-death explanation doesn't hold up. She suggested confronting the medical examiner directly and questioned whether the cold case team simply wants to "close this case."

— Roxanne
