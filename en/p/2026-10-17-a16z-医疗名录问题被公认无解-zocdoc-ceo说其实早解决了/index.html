The Doctor Directory Problem Was Already Fixed—Just Not In The Way Everyone Thought

The real problem was never accurate directories—it's completing transactions

Oliver argues the doctor directory problem was solved long ago. Traditional directories function like repeatedly republished encyclopedias—obsolete the moment they go live. But once a system can actually complete appointment bookings, each transaction becomes self-correcting like Wikipedia: errors occur once, then auto-correct. This insight is why ZocDoc has spent twenty years building infrastructure integrating hundreds of scheduling systems.

— Oliver
5:18

What a clinic's sticky note workaround taught ZocDoc about integration

Early on, ZocDoc kept getting cancellations from one clinic. Investigating, they found the doctor had told his office manager ‘I'm off Friday’ in person, so the manager stuck a Post-it on the monitor—never entered it into the EHR. They fixed thousands of these "one-percent solutions" over years before EHR integration became reliable. Oliver jokes that 3M is actually ZocDoc's biggest competitor.

— Oliver
8:26

No company should own healthcare's primary entry point

Oliver opposes the walled-garden logic of "lock in patients and win." Healthcare isn't a building where you trap patients inside and lock the doors—it's a city where patients seek different entries based on current need: ER for acute cases, specialists for rare diseases, primary care doctors for conditions requiring long-term continuity. So ZocDoc built open infrastructure for all entry points rather than funneling bookings to its own site.

— Oliver
12:36

More AI assistants means ZocDoc becomes infrastructure, not a destination

AI assistants like Gemini make patient information more personal and actionable—but information can't book appointments, which is where ZocDoc fits. Since entry points are multiplying exponentially, doctors can't integrate individually with each AI platform. ZocDoc's promise: doctors connect once, availability distributes everywhere, with no requirement that bookings occur on ZocDoc's own app or site.

— Oliver
21:00

Payers can integrate without rewriting their own systems

After partnering with payers like Blue Shield of California, members book directly on the payer's directory with minimal implementation cost for the payer. Since ERs cost 10 to 12 times more than office visits with worse outcomes, getting patients to book primary care faster delivers direct savings—which is why after one use case lands, other teams inside the payer approach ZocDoc to expand.

— Oliver
24:17

ZocDoc's pricing model needed regulatory approval to work

ZocDoc initially charged doctors flat subscription fees, but volume variance meant some felt overcharged and others undercharged. After explaining the platform's value to patients, doctors, and payers, regulators approved a model where ZocDoc charges only on discovery bookings—new doctors matched after patient search—while referrals to named doctors stay free. This model means 97% of doctors who signed up in the past 12 months came unsolicited.

— Oliver
27:23

National wait times are rising while ZocDoc's are falling

The national average wait to see a primary care doctor has deteriorated from 17 days when ZocDoc launched to 31 days today, but through ZocDoc it averages under 3 days. The reason: doctors accumulate cancellations and no-shows creating last-minute slots. Drawing from airlines, where load factors rose from 60% in the 1970s to 90%+ after internet booking became standard, doctors currently run just under 70%—and ZocDoc captures that unused capacity.

— Oliver
30:30

Quick availability doesn't indicate low-quality doctors

When critics ask if 3-day waits mean low-quality doctors, Oliver counters: the best doctors often have worse utilization because their patients cancel more due to hectic lives, and doctors won't overboo to compensate. He cites a medical school dean booked out three months who nearly requested removal, yet still accommodated roughly 40 same-day squeeze-ins through ZocDoc in a month. The platform's admission bar also exceeds state medical boards, with unqualified doctors removed.

— Oliver

Glossary

EHR
Electronic health record system—the clinic software managing patient data and schedules that ZocDoc must integrate with individually.
Stark Law and Anti-Kickback Statute
US federal regulations limiting financial incentives in medical referrals; determine what ZocDoc can charge per transaction.
Discovery booking
An appointment generated when patients search broadly and get matched to a new doctor; ZocDoc charges only on these, not referrals to established doctors.
Load factor
Metric borrowed from aviation: the proportion of a doctor's available appointment slots that get actually booked.
