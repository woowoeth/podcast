OpenAI Skips Specialist Medical Models, Betting Instead That General Models Will Keep Getting Stronger

Specialized medical models will quickly be outpaced by cutting-edge general models, so OpenAI chooses instead to bake specialist data from hundreds of doctors into each generation of GPT, rather than building a separate medical-specific model.

Medical AI's real value lies between appointments, not in replacing them

American primary care waits are 40+ days; specialist appointments are weeks to months, and only 15 minutes long. Most people leave with questions unasked. ChatGPT gets 300+ million weekly health questions—mostly not asking to replace doctors, but to fill the gap: understanding lab results, preparing questions, digesting medical advice. What's scarce isn't medical information but the person managing the time between visits.

— Karan Singhal
7:07

Specialist Medical Models Get Outpaced—So Build General Ones Instead

Singhal built specialist models before and knows they get rapidly overtaken by general cutting-edge models because industry scales so fast. OpenAI does the reverse: feed specialist data from doctor networks into every training stage of each GPT generation, letting the model capture both the scaling rewards of general models and specialist medical training. The bet is that continuous model improvement beats betting on a standalone medical model.

— Karan Singhal
17:25

Medical Consensus Is Harder Than It Looks; Ask Five Doctors, Get Eight Answers

OpenAI initially wanted a few doctors to define how the model should handle edge cases—but discovered it doesn't work: the same question to five doctors gets eight different answers. The team built a network spanning dozens of specialties and practicing physicians across countries to aggregate consensus rather than rely on one or two voices. When users want reassurance but the condition is actually dangerous, the team puts safety over user satisfaction.

— Karan Singhal
21:32

Clinical Guidelines Travel Slowly—5 to 17 Years From Paper to Practice

Medical knowledge travels a chain: research paper → clinical guidelines (updated yearly) → how doctors actually practice. The final step typically lags 5–17 years; newest guidelines take time to filter into daily work. AI's value isn't inventing new knowledge but compressing that lag from years to weeks or hours—bringing the latest literature and guidelines directly to the doctor or patient at the moment of care, rather than waiting for knowledge to slowly seep into clinical practice.

— Karan Singhal
26:37

Integrating Epic EHR Means Breaking Down Healthcare's Data Silos

A major healthcare barrier is fragmented data and workflows. Many hospitals still run their core EHR on aging virtual machines with no ready AI hooks. OpenAI's partnership with Epic lets doctors call Epic data directly from ChatGPT and embed ChatGPT's sidebar into Epic workflows. UCSF was among the first hospitals to land this integration, serving as proof that fragmentation can be dismantled.

— Karan Singhal
38:53

Counterintuitive But Correct: AI Challenging Doctors on Antibiotic Overuse

Borrowing from AlphaGo's Move 37 against Lee Sedol—the unintuitive play that later became legendary—Singhal saw a medical parallel in a clinical doctor collaboration with Panda Health: the model repeatedly pushed doctors not to over-prescribe antibiotics for the case in front of them, but to think about antibiotic resistance as a larger system problem. Cases that integrate a decade of longitudinal records and predict what the human brain can't calculate will become increasingly common.

— Karan Singhal
44:01

New Models Cost 34 Times Less and Perform Better on Medical Benchmarks

Singhal sets the $5.3 trillion or more healthcare spend (larger than the world's third-largest economy's GDP) against another curve: AI's latest and cheapest API model outperforms what was the strongest model a month ago on OpenAI's hardest clinical benchmark, HealthBench Professional, while costing 34 times less. He bets that once model capability crosses the threshold patients and doctors will trust, costs for that threshold will keep plummeting like they always have.

— Karan Singhal
53:20

The Next 5–10 Years: AI's Biggest Health Impact Will Be in Drug Discovery

Asked what looks like science fiction today but will ship in 5–10 years, Singhal didn't point to chatbots but to drug discovery: AI generating new drug targets and molecules, and AI screening and validating clinical trial candidates. Candidate molecules far outnumber trial slots; AI can rank priority and move trials faster and cleaner. More personalized new drugs will likely be AI's single biggest health impact in coming years.

— Karan Singhal

Glossary

Move 37
AlphaGo's enigmatic move against Lee Sedol that initially seemed flawed but proved brilliantly insightful—now used to describe AI judgments that defy human intuition yet prove correct.
HealthBench Professional
OpenAI's medical model benchmark, co-developed with physicians, measuring performance on high-difficulty clinical scenarios.

Glycated hemoglobin; a measure of a diabetic patient's long-term blood sugar control.
