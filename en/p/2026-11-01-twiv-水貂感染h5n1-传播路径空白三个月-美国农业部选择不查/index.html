Two H5N1 lineages circulating simultaneously in US cattle show different severity in humans

Since the first H5N1 detection in Texas cattle in March 2024, two H5N1 lineages—B3.13 (clade 2.3.4.4B) and D1.1, later found in Nevada and Arizona—have circulated simultaneously in the US cattle population. The spillover pattern is strikingly different: B3.13 cases in cattle are mostly asymptomatic or mild, whereas D1.1 shows markedly greater severity in human infections. The episode emphasizes that this dual evolution exemplifies the principle that "avian influenza becomes more likely to cross into humans the longer it persists in mammals"—and this is now happening across two lineages at once.

— Vincent
19:45

Minks pose unusual spillover risk: human-like receptors, high flu susceptibility, and farm worker exposure

Mink and swine warrant special scrutiny because they carry abundant α-2,6 sialic acid receptors on respiratory epithelium—the same version humans have—making virus adapted in mink far more likely to infect humans. Mink are also acutely susceptible to influenza; the virus is frequently isolated from mink and farm workers commonly become infected, positioning mink farms as potential mixing vessels for reassortment between human and avian influenza. This risk prompted Spain to cull an entire mink farm after detecting H5N1 in 2022, and the Netherlands to shut down its entire mink farming industry in 2020 after SARS-CoV-2 emerged in farmed mink.

— Vincent
25:15

Utah case: USDA skipped culling and worker surveillance, unlike European precedent

The US adopted a different approach. Angie Rasmussen reported that neither Utah state nor the USDA ordered culling of the infected mink farm, nor did either agency monitor farm workers who may have been exposed. The USDA told state veterinarians it has no authority to vaccinate mink and no compensation mechanism for farmers asked to cull infected stock. The episode attributes the inaction to fur industry lobbying—not scientific limitation, but regulatory machinery never activated.

— Vincent
28:25

Transmission chain from Texas cattle to Utah mink farm is unmapped despite genetic connection

Genetic sequencing shows the Utah mink virus lineage most closely related to a Texas cattle strain sampled in May 2026, but with a three-month gap between sampling dates and no trace of the pathway between them. The farms are far apart; suspected routes include migratory birds or poultry carrying virus from cattle operations to mink farms, contaminated poultry or beef byproducts used as mink feed, or itinerant workers moving between both locations. The authors plainly state that inability to trace the source reveals a vast blind spot in US surveillance of high-path influenza in livestock.

— Vincent
36:35

Lack of testing is not a technical problem but an economic and political one

Alan Dove notes that the USDA carries a structural conflict: it both promotes US agriculture and advises Americans on diet. Agriculture operates on thin margins; any regulatory change scales across an enormous industry with visible costs. Expanded testing requires funding; European-style culling would legally obligate government compensation to farmers—politically unwelcome. The resistance to expanded testing and intervention rests on a broad political foundation that might persist across administrations.

— Alan
52:05

Bats mount measured antiviral response, humans trigger uncontrolled inflammation

A Cell Reports paper compared Egyptian fruit bat and human skin fibroblasts' response to double-stranded RNA stimulation. Bat cells chiefly induced antiviral proteins with direct antiviral function (like ZBP1) while suppressing inflammatory mediators (chemokines, IL-6). Human cells, by contrast, activated the full suite: chemokines, cytokines, Toll-like receptors, RIG-I, plus cell-death pathways. The authors conclude that humans' indiscriminate inflammatory response is the source of Marburg cytokine storm and tissue damage in human infection, whereas bats clear the virus in two weeks with minimal symptoms.

— Vincent
58:16

Conclusions from fibroblast models carry limits that authors carefully flag

Rich points out a crucial caveat: the experiments used skin fibroblasts, not immune cells or endothelial cells—yet Marburg pathogenesis is driven by immune and vascular-endothelial responses, not fibroblast-mediated ones. Reassuringly, the authors did not stop at culture; they validated their findings against published data from infected bat spleen and human tissue and found consistent trends. The paper is positioned as a "Resource" article in Cell Reports, meant as a curated gene list for other labs to directly test, extend, and build upon rather than as a final answer.

— Rich
1:20:06

Measles vaccine escape seems unlikely, but TWiV acknowledges it has never been truly tested

Listener Rona asked whether Florida's repeal of vaccine mandates, if it sparks a measles surge, could select for vaccine-escape variants. Rich and Vincent concur it is unlikely—measles is an RNA virus and theoretically prone to drift, but the current vaccine has been in use for fifty to sixty years without an escape strain emerging; the world has only one serotype. They attributed this to fitness cost: envelope protein has limited tolerance for variation, and any mutation that disrupts receptor binding destroys the virus's transmissibility before spread becomes established. Both acknowledged, however, that no one has truly tested this, and the question itself is compelling.

Glossary

clade / genotype
Viral classification units by genetic sequence, such as H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4B or B3.13 genotype.
TMRCA
Time to most recent common ancestor; the inferred date when two virus sequences shared a common ancestor, used to estimate true outbreak origin.
reassortment
When two influenza viruses infect the same cell and exchange gene segments, creating a new viral combination.
α-2,6 sialic acid receptor
Sugar-chain receptor abundant on human and mink respiratory epithelium where influenza viruses bind to initiate upper-respiratory infection.
interferon epsilon
An interferon subtype constitutively expressed without relying on conventional pathogen-recognition signal pathways.
