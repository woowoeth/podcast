The root cause of the data center backlash is twenty years of not building power plants

Vance splits the anti-data-center wave into two layers: one is basic NIMBY sentiment, the other is real electricity bills — his example is someone whose monthly bill was $290 a year ago and is now $580. His judgment is that this is not a data center problem, but that the US went an entire generation without building enough generating capacity, especially after blue states made new generation impossible, so the cost gets passed through to residential bills and backlash is inevitable. His comparison: in 2005 China's electricity generation had just overtaken America's, since then the US has been essentially flat, while China now generates 3 times as much electricity as the US. His conclusion is that many problems are downstream of this foundational one, and the only fix is to build more power, with the goal being electricity ‘cheap enough that you don't need a meter’.

12:35

The people building Frankenstein shouldn't come asking for regulation

Vance responds directly to AI pessimism: if frontier labs say ‘we've built Frankenstein’, the answer is not to set up a global AI governance structure, but — first, stop; second, if the cat is already out of the bag, build defensive mechanisms. He names the cyberattack tools that came with Anthropic's latest model, and says companies desperately need the corresponding defensive tools but are being denied access. He is careful to distinguish motives: he doesn't know Dario, but everyone who does tells him Dario is sincere, that this is deeply held conviction, not cynicism and not regulatory capture. The disagreement isn't about motives, it's about the solution: Vance thinks outsourcing the risk to government regulation is wrong, and the answer is to look inward.

— JD Vance
16:45

The $100,000 H-1B fee exists because Congress won't legislate

Vance says H-1B reform is being done administratively rather than legislatively, because Congress simply has no political will to pass a law changing the program. The administrative approach is a $100,000 fee, to make sure what you get with an H-1B is a genuine genius, not a foreign accountant earning $45,000 replacing an American accountant earning $60,000. He also says they are looking at stopping companies that use H-1B from simultaneously laying off large numbers of American workers — his scenario is a company that says it urgently needs people it can't hire, but whose history shows it laid off 5,000. He acknowledges the approach has already been sued, but believes it holds up both legally and as policy.

— JD Vance
18:50

The scale of federal fraud: between 10% and 20%

Vance cites the 20% Elon talked about at the peak of DOGE, which is $1.4 trillion, then says whether it's 10% or 20%, the magnitude is in the hundreds of billions. He thinks the biggest obstacle to investigating fraud isn't failing to find it, but that the states with the worst problems are the least willing to cooperate. He gives two specific mechanisms: California bills the federal government monthly to be reimbursed for SNAP costs, somewhere between $700 million and $900 million, and the federal government just wires the money and asks no questions, so it has no idea whether the people receiving SNAP are real people or legal residents; and Medicare fraud involving large numbers of entirely fictitious ‘people’, plus illegal-immigrant Medicaid spending in Illinois, New York and California. His estimate is that if the total is $700 billion, a few tens of billions of it is in blue states, and simply checking basic eligibility would handle it, but legally forcing blue states to cooperate is far harder than it should be.

— JD Vance
21:57

Israel is not the subject of American Middle East policy

Asked about the ‘America First, only America’ current inside MAGA and rising antisemitism, Vance's answer is to re-anchor the relationship to American interests. He says Israel is an important partner on military technology and intelligence sharing, but America doesn't always agree with Israel; he calls Trump more willing than any president in the past 40 years to part ways with Netanyahu. His irony: the ones criticizing Trump's relationship with Bibi are precisely Democrats, when Trump is the president who dares to say ‘Bibi is a good partner, but on this we see it differently’. He extends the logic to NATO: you can't subordinate American Middle East policy to Israel, just as you can't subordinate policy toward Europe entirely to NATO.

— JD Vance
25:19

Two messages for the midterms: don't hand over power, the other side is insane

Vance's two messages for the midterms are blunt. The first is the scorecard: tax cuts, including on overtime pay, Social Security and consumption, which he defines as tax cuts for middle-class workers rather than for Washington and New York elites; success on the border and immigration; and H-1B reform that prioritizes American workers over foreigners. The second doesn't depend on agreeing with the policies: even if you disagree with every one of our issues, the other party has been swallowed by insanity, swallowed by people who want to steal everything and hand the country to illegal immigrants and criminals, and they brag about it openly. His example is someone running for Congress in New York who said July 4th is the liberation of Puerto Rico and Palestine, and his conclusion is that this kind of person belongs in a mental institution, not in Congress.
