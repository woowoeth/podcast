1:04:37

A village internet celebrity whose audience is entirely urban

Kuaishou has produced many rural internet celebrities, some with tens of millions of followers, but their audiences are mostly in the countryside, county towns or further down — big-city people can't get into them. Shougong Geng has never left his village in Baoding, yet his audience is mostly urban. His own explanation: making things requires a lot of space and the willingness to make a mess, and those things are only possible in the countryside; city people, by contrast, find it unusual. Luo Yonghao added an analogy: some original musicians who sing about the poor are loved precisely by city people, even intellectuals, while the poor themselves don't care. The result is that the ads embedded in his videos reach an audience that is entirely urban.

— Luo Yonghao, Shou Gong Geng
2:13:03

‘Money I can't handle’ doesn't mean too much money

Shougong Geng has said he can't earn ‘money I can't handle’, which many fans read as ‘earning too much, can't handle it’. He clarified: it's not about the money, it's that his ability falls a bit short. Luo Yonghao pressed: you're best at making content, your traffic is still astonishing, you could earn several times more by taking more brand deals — why not? Shougong Geng repeatedly said his ability isn't up to it. Brands usually come with a creative idea; he doesn't think much of theirs, and he can't come up with one he's satisfied with himself, so he just doesn't do it, preferring to post less. Luo Yonghao had assumed he was lazy or free-spirited; after talking he found it was ‘no way to land on a creative idea that works for both sides’.

— Shou Gong Geng, Luo Yonghao
2:25:11

Making is time-bounded, coming up with ideas is not

Shougong Geng broke down his own production process: purely by time, making takes longer than thinking up ideas. But the key is that making is time-bounded while thinking up ideas is not. When a brand deal comes in, he'll spend three to five days, up to a month, on the idea; if he can't come up with one, he lets it go. Some ideas he's thought about for two years without cracking them; some take shape in a day. This explains why he posts so little — the bottleneck isn't production capacity, it's ideas. He discusses ideas with his older and younger brothers, but in the end most of the ideas are his, with others refining them. Now when he explains requirements to the team, they often don't understand; the requirements have gotten more complex than before.

— Shou Gong Geng
2:33:18

No faking on camera, and it works in reality too

Luo Yonghao asked him to his face: in all these years of videos, you've never cheated in the filming, right? Shougong Geng said he's never cheated on camera; if there were any requirement, it's that the principle has to hold up in reality too. Maybe there isn't enough time, or enough spare parts, so it can't be as perfect as it should be, but basically it works. The pleasure of making things for him is that it really works, and then he films it — not filming something fake and being satisfied when everyone says it's good. This matches his understanding of ‘useful’: everything he wants to make is something that can actually be used and run in reality.

— Shou Gong Geng, Luo Yonghao
2:48:27

Filming the upside-down hair wash, brother standing by with a sledgehammer

Luo Yonghao asked whether there had been accidents in the process. Shougong Geng said rarely, but once: making the goat horns required filling them with sand, baking them, then bending them slightly; while baking, the sand exploded and flew into his brother's eye, so they rushed to the hospital, and fortunately the doctor said it was nothing serious. For the upside-down hair wash, he didn't realize the danger at first — washing hair upside down seemed perfectly logical — and only when he actually tried it did he find it terrifying: as soon as the water covers your nose you panic, and then you have to adapt repeatedly to water over your mouth, then turn. During the shoot he had his brother stand beside him with a sledgehammer, ready to smash the tub if something went wrong; that acrylic tub is actually very hard to smash, but having someone there made him feel safe. Whether his hair would get tangled, he hadn't considered at all at the time.

— Shou Gong Geng
3:05:34

He gives himself fifty percent luck

Luo Yonghao has asked many guests the same question: how much of success is chance? Shougong Geng said chance is a fairly big part. His father said it outright: if you hadn't caught short video, hadn't caught this internet era, with your personality you'd never have succeeded. Shougong Geng's own review: if he really hadn't caught it, he might have kept working as a laborer, or gone into farming or something else — ‘that outcome would be much worse than now’. He says both luck and effort are there, hard to tell which is bigger, but he gives himself ‘50% luck, 50% himself’. He doesn't believe he'd have made it in a different lane, with a different personality.

— Shou Gong Geng
3:08:34

He dared to start a business because stainless steel doesn't depreciate

When he decided to go out on his own in '17, Shougong Geng's confidence came from a very practical judgment: stainless steel crafts keep for a long time, while the cost of human labor keeps rising, so if it doesn't sell now, it might sell later, and for more. It isn't a tech product, it doesn't depreciate that fast — unlike hoarding a pile of pagers you can't move. So the worst case is losing your own labor, and the stuff keeps without rusting. What he was thinking at the time was ‘at least there's no loss’, so it was worth a try. Even now he says he isn't very confident; his confidence back then only went as far as ‘worth a try’.

— Shou Gong Geng
3:19:39

A super IP nobody has picked up

Some netizens suggested Shougong Geng do a national tour, and have Baoding's culture and tourism bureau develop it into a theme park: two hours by car from Beijing, land isn't expensive, the works are all ready, just set them out and put up a shed, and ticket revenue would cover the upkeep. Shougong Geng said he'd never thought about that direction, and culture and tourism officials had never talked to him about it. Luo Yonghao said repeatedly what a shame, calling it a super IP. He also noted: these days computer CG and AI can make anything gorgeous, anything eerie, and after seeing enough eeriness in videos you stop finding it eerie — but walking through the workshop and touching each piece one by one, that's when it feels magical.

— Luo Yonghao, Shou Gong Geng
