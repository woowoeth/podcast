Three Months of AI Agents on Your Credit Card: From Zero to Millions per Week

Payment credentials upgraded agents from 'recommending you buy' to 'buying for you'; token vaults like VGS scaled from single-user to multi-user access, and a new agentic token standard hit millions of weekly transactions in three months, starting from zero.

0:00

Payment credentials are an agent's hands and feet

In the past three months, agents shifted from bystander to actor—the pivot point is access to payment credentials. Once an agent can safely hold a credit-card token, it stops just recommending you buy flowers and actually places orders for you or books piano teachers. This directly triggered the 'wallet wars,' with every wallet racing to connect to agents.

— Terry Angelos
3:07

VGS holds the card details of one-third of e-commerce

VGS is a token vault securing about one-third of global e-commerce GMV's payment data; the largest online merchants, newest banks, and leading AI labs all use it. Merchants don't want to touch user card numbers directly or get locked into a single payment processor—a $1 billion GMV merchant uses an average of 3–5 processors. VGS acts as the unified credential hub and token refresh service behind them.

— Terry Angelos
13:39

Every wallet is racing to connect to agents

For agents to be useful, they need a wallet with the broadest consumer reach and merchant coverage. Stripe Link has roughly 300 million consumers and 1 million merchants; ShopPay about 1.7 million merchants; PayPal over 400 million accounts and roughly 30 million merchants. This scramble is called the 'wallet wars'—the same logic as Visa and PayPal fighting over the 'default card' slot in mobile wallets a decade ago, except now they're fighting over the agent's default gateway.

— Terry Angelos
20:55

Stripe is like AOL in the proxy-commerce era

Stripe Link helped a user buy a chandelier from Walmart, but because Link has no direct Walmart connection, Stripe had to issue its own disposable virtual card and then collect from the user. The transaction went through, but when problems surfaced and the user called their card issuer, the bank only saw a Stripe charge—couldn't trace it to Walmart. The call eventually reached a Stripe engineer's personal phone because Stripe had built no chargeback support.

— Terry Angelos
25:34

New token standard went from zero to millions in three months

An open agentic standard solved Stripe's disposable-card mess: an agent declares ‘I'm a verified agent, max spend $100’ and receives a new 16-digit token tied to the user's real Visa card. When merchants process it, they're charging the real card; chargebacks and subscription upgrades follow the old rules. This token type was nonexistent three months ago; VGS now issues millions per week.

— Terry Angelos
31:47

No solution yet for whether an agent is acting in its right mind

Transaction validation traditionally has three checks: Is the card real? Is the holder present? Is the holder in their right mind to decide this (in a casino, drunk people shouldn't be able to charge)? Agents pass the first two; the third—whether an agent misunderstood the user's instruction or hallucinated—lacks clear accountability. There are real cases of users asking ‘Why did you spend $900?’

— Terry Angelos
36:00

Amazon locks out agents; Walmart opens the door

Amazon recently blocked agents like Muse—the core reason is that ads pay better than pure sales. Agents give Amazon no eyeballs to sell. This isn't just Luddites opposing change; Amazon has real financial incentives against agents. Conversely, retail giants like Walmart might embrace them because of strong last-mile delivery; small merchants in the Shopify ecosystem have little choice and must treat agents as a new channel.

— Alex Rampell
41:03

Underestimated: the consumption that agents will unlock

Neither expects agents to trigger panic withdrawals from savings accounts—cross-bank transfers still have friction. What's truly underestimated is pent-up demand—purchases that *should* happen but procrastination blocks. A friend recommends safer bike tires in the cycling chat; you mean to buy them but two years pass. If an agent is listening, that demand keeps surfacing and converts to actual spending.

— Terry Angelos
45:13

Credit-card rewards programs are vulnerable to agents

Credit-card rewards rest on asymmetry: you pick a card for certain purchases, then casually use it elsewhere. Once agents unified all your transactions and always picked the card with the highest return for each purchase, that ecosystem collapses—it relied on you *not* optimizing. Terms and rules would have to be rewritten.

— Alex Rampell

Glossary

VGS
Third-party token vault that securely holds credit cards and other payment credentials for merchants
PAN
The 16-digit number on the front of a credit card; the card's original account number
network token
A substitute card number issued by Visa or Mastercard, bound to a specific merchant domain, and more secure than a plain card number
PCI DSS
A hundred-page compliance framework that merchants must follow when handling credit-card data
agentic token
A new payment token designed for AI agent proxy spending: amount-limited and tied to the real cardholder
chargeback
A cardholder's refund dispute mechanism for challenging a suspicious or problematic transaction with the card issuer
