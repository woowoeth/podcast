Your front-line staff will warn you before your executives do

Before the pandemic, Ann Berry's laundry operations team in Las Vegas reported massive order cancellations from Asia. The company—which had already stockpiled PPE and disinfectants from prior avian flu experience—caught the crisis signal first. The lesson: managers must build relationships with front-line workers and not isolate themselves in executive suites, because the earliest warnings typically emerge first in scattered conversations from ground-level staff.

— Ann Berry
21:34

Operating a company teaches what board questions actually matter

After becoming CEO of a hotel laundry company during the pandemic, Ann Berry called CEOs from her past PE roles to apologize. She admitted the 'value-add suggestions' she'd pitched in boardrooms based on spreadsheet data had been annoying. Once she owned operations and held the P&L, she understood what boards should actually scrutinize and which due diligence questions had real weight.

— Ann Berry
37:52

Walmart became a tech stock by monetizing advertising data

In 2022, Ann Berry wrote that Walmart's e-commerce data was spawning an advertising business now worth nearly $65 billion annually. The company moved its listing to Nasdaq to reinforce the tech-company narrative. She views this as a legacy retailer's successful reinvention through data assets—not a dinosaur awaiting Amazon's extinction event.

— Ann Berry
43:56

State legislatures will likely ban dynamic pricing

She predicts California, New York, and similar states will lead in banning dynamic pricing—when retailers use license plates, shopping cart sensors, or credit scores to set different prices. Walmart once tested raising mayo prices when tuna was detected in a cart. Consumer backlash will accelerate legislation, especially as inflation already strains trust. Otherwise, an 'AI surcharge' layer compounds the damage.

— Ann Berry
45:58

Brands and retailers must now court AI shopping agents

At a Mexico City retail summit, Liverpool department store's CEO raised a novel question: as AI agents shop on behalf of consumers, brands and retailers must research how to win agent preference and visibility—mirroring how they've studied consumer marketing. Ann Berry considers this inevitable: it will reshape how retailers control data and manage product exposure.

— Ann Berry
49:00

AI capex isn't a bubble—it's that returns are unmeasurable

She rejects ‘this is the dot-com bubble all over again’ analogies. Today's funded AI companies have real revenue and profit, unlike Pets.com with just a concept. The genuine problem: scan earnings calls and analyst reports, and almost nobody explains what actual ROI these capital outlays will generate or when. Early-stage investing has also sobered considerably since the 2021 NFT and Web3 manias.

— Ann Berry
56:07

Tech giants acquire emerging threats before they can scale

Fei-Fei Li's World Models acquisition by AMD exemplifies Ann Berry's concept ‘flight to scale’: the billions raised by OpenAI, Anthropic and peers are deployed to acquire every early-stage company that could become competitive. She frames this not as safety-driven but as fundamentally about market dominance—well-capitalized incumbents preempting threats rather than purely defending turf.

— Ann Berry
57:10

When tech giants acquire companies, price gets mentioned last

In interviews with major tech company CEOs and CFOs on acquisition criteria, answers focus on technology fit, product adjacency, team, and culture—price surfaces only when Ann Berry specifically asks. She recalls Marc Andreessen once saying that investing works on home runs, so overpaying by 2x for Facebook wouldn't matter. Big tech now applies that same logic to AI acquisitions.

— Ann Berry
