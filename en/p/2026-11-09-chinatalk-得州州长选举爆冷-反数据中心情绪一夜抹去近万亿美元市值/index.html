Texas Governor's Upset: Anti-Data-Center Sentiment Erases Nearly a Trillion in Market Value

Anti-data-center sentiment is not a story of liberals hating tech—it's bipartisan consensus so powerful it toppled a gubernatorial race and caught Wall Street off guard, erasing vast market value overnight.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

12:26

Data centers emerged as the deciding factor in Texas's gubernatorial race

Kevin began tracking data center moratoriums in January and built a dashboard by March, identifying the issue as at least the top three nationally and first in some states. Wall Street barely anticipated that data centers would help Gina Hinojosa win the Texas governorship. When the results came in, related tech stocks shed ‘close to a trillion dollars’ in a single night, revealing that data center siting has evolved from a local issue into a political variable capable of moving national markets.

— Kevin
14:52

GPU acquisition accounts for 70 to 80 percent of data center construction costs

Kevin provided specifics: of a $100 data center price tag, electricity represents only $1–2, construction and labor (concrete, electricians) about $20, and $70–80 goes entirely to GPU procurement. His inference: power and construction costs are minimal, and tech companies can easily afford higher union wages and grid-upgrade costs. If legislators demand transparency and payment, companies will almost certainly pay. The White House might even sign on, because this is currently the only thing still holding up stock markets.

— Kevin
21:07

Unions are the only major constituency that does not oppose data centers

Among all those who dislike data centers, electricians and Teamsters members stand alone as exceptions—they need these construction jobs. Kevin notes that Democrats' largest voter loss in 2024 was union votes; the Teamsters president spoke at the Republican convention for the first time in the union's 120-year history. If Democrats can reframe data centers as a ‘pro-labor, pro-construction’ industrial policy rather than simply ‘pause AI,’ this could be the key to winning back the White House in 2028.

— Kevin
22:09

OpenAI's community compensation rounds to approximately $60 per resident

After the elections and rejection of all forty statewide data center moratoriums, OpenAI announced $4.8 million to one county and $4 million to another as community compensation—amounting to roughly $60 per person. Kevin called this a ‘shocking political miscalculation’: these companies plainly have the resources to double commitments from millions to tens of millions without difficulty, yet they resort to outdated factory-era tactics to appease local communities. The result: they fail to win county-level support, and watch helplessly as state-level blanket moratoriums take hold.

— Kevin
27:20

The new Congress will likely impose tighter export controls on China

Peter argues that because the Trump administration is ambiguous on China export controls and Congress traditionally defers to the president, a Democratic-led Congress now has an opening to ‘pressure’ the White House. He expects notably aggressive export restrictions to reach statute next year, including closing loopholes where China remotely accesses U.S. computing power through third countries. Legislation resembling the MATCH Act may also surface. Matt adds another dimension: semiconductor and memory shortages are actively raising consumer electronics prices, lending export controls an additional public-welfare rationale.

— Peter
33:03

Government protocols for warning about Chinese intelligence threats were not followed in Huang's case

The New York Times reported that Jensen Huang maintained a close relationship with someone suspected of being a Chinese intelligence officer. The Trump administration allegedly knew for months or nearly a year but took no action. Peter explains that when the U.S. government identifies someone as a target of foreign intelligence operations, the FBI routinely issues a defensive briefing—hundreds or thousands of Americans may receive such warnings annually, standard practice for the past decade. The absence of a briefing to Huang suggests someone inside the administration, dissatisfied with the decision, leaked word to journalists.

— Peter
57:24

Democrats can win 2028 with data centers if White House instability does not intervene

Kevin offered a clear verdict: so long as Democrats do not squander the data center card—turning it into a ‘pro-labor, pro-construction’ proposition rather than simple anti-AI—they win the presidency. Matt urges caution: the White House still poses real instability risks, especially in foreign affairs. Peter is more specific: next year's appropriations battles over the Justice Department and Pentagon budgets could well escalate into large-scale government shutdowns. This is the hard-fought battle Congress must undertake to block another Iran war and prevent weaponization of the Justice Department.
