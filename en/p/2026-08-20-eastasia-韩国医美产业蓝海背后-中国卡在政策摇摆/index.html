Korean Aesthetics Is Still Cheaper Than China Even After the Tax Refund Ended; China Is Stuck on Ambiguous Policy

Korean medical aesthetics has become a mass-market, tourism-driven industry: prices are opaque but broadly cheap. China has the demand but no policy, the market is chaotic, and Korea is pocketing the money.

In Korea, getting injected isn't plastic surgery — it's a dermatology visit

In the Korean context, the line between medical aesthetics and plastic surgery is very clear. Plastic surgery means cutting into flesh and bone; injections and energy-based devices fall under dermatology. When Koreans say in daily life that they're ‘going to the hospital dermatology department’, they mean non-surgical procedures like skin boosters and Botox. This classification shapes consumer mentality: double-eyelid surgery counts as plastic surgery because it requires a scalpel; injectables are treated as routine care. Korean society even treats plastic surgery as a post-college-entrance-exam summer project — hospitals give discounts on presentation of an exam admission ticket, and there's ‘buy one, get one free’ marketing.

Luxury-tier aesthetics costs only 20-30% more; the logic is what differs

Korean medical-aesthetics institutions broadly fall into two types. One is the assembly-line model: you walk in and name your procedure, doctors rotate randomly, more like fast food. The other is one-on-one doctor consultations, where the doctor sets the plan and a consultant negotiates the price — known as the ‘luxury’ tier. Quan Xiaoxing went to the latter this time: three injections plus one energy-based treatment, priced at roughly 2,000 to 3,000 RMB. The price gap between the two types is about 20% to 30%, but the service logic is completely different. The assembly line emphasizes efficiency; the luxury tier emphasizes consultation and personalized plans.

14:40

One procedure, seven or eight prices — the most expensive goes to foreigners

Korean medical-aesthetics pricing is extremely opaque, and many hospitals keep multiple price lists. Quan Xiaoxing has seen a single quote sheet with seven or eight prices for the same procedure, from the filed price to the internet price to the foreign-broker price, with the spread approaching double. The most expensive is the overseas broker channel; the cheapest is the internal employee price. He has been offered commissions by medical-aesthetics institutions, with rebates exceeding 50%. This pricing system makes it very hard for consumers to judge the real cost, and gives brokers and consultants enormous room to negotiate.

Chinese men's appearance anxiety tops out at 30-40%

On China's male medical-aesthetics market, Quan Xiaoxing judges that it will grow but has limits. He estimates 70% of young Korean men have appearance anxiety; China probably tops out at 30% to 40%, and currently may not even reach 5%. That gap implies huge incremental room, but China is unlikely to fully replicate Korea's level. Chinese male medical-aesthetics spending will concentrate in first-tier cities; the lower-tier market needs no attention in the short term. Fan Yuru believes penetration in first-tier cities is already relatively high, but the market has yet to explode.

35:24

Korean medical aesthetics sells for more in Hong Kong than in Korea itself

Korean medical-aesthetics services are being exported overseas, and Hong Kong is a typical sample. Korean doctors open clinics in Hong Kong, Korean hair salons are increasingly common, and prices are even higher than in Korea itself, because the ‘Korean face’ carries a premium. Some young Koreans go to Hong Kong on working holiday visas, work, then switch to a work visa to stay, doing skin care, hairdressing and similar trades. Mainland China has also opened up short-term practice permits for foreign doctors, but regulation is far stricter than in Hong Kong, so the Korean model is hard to copy directly onto the mainland.

41:34

Korean medical aesthetics got standardized by volume, not by crackdowns

Korea's medical-aesthetics industry moved from chaos to standardization, and the core was the accumulation of ‘volume’. There are plenty of medical-aesthetics institutions, but the number of universities that train doctors is limited; doctors have high status and a high bar, and must obtain specialist certification. Over the past seven or eight years, hospital signage has standardized the distinction between ‘dermatology hospital’ and ‘hospital with dermatology as a licensed specialty’, and having a resident anesthesiologist has become a selling point. Even so, problems like ghost surgery remain unresolved, and the tug-of-war over interests between physician organizations and nurses continues.

46:48

The tax refund is gone, and Korean medical aesthetics is still cheaper than China's

Korean authorities place medical aesthetics within the tourism industry — a sector they are actively trying to promote. Until the end of last year, medical aesthetics was still tax-refundable; this year it was abolished. Many people joke that the president is short of money, but even with the tax added, Korean medical aesthetics is still cheaper than China's. Unlike Japan, which takes a medical and wellness route, Korea takes a fashion-industry route. Japan has high-value-added items like health checkups and stem cells; Korea attracts overseas visitors with price and convenience.

— Quan Xiaoxing
49:56

What China's medical aesthetics lacks isn't technology, it's an official stance

The problem with China's medical-aesthetics industry isn't technology or talent, but that officialdom and mainstream ideology ‘neither promote nor oppose’ it — an ambiguous attitude. Grassroots demand is strong, and many people fly to Korea to spend, but the authorities don't treat medical aesthetics as an industry to push. Quan Xiaoxing believes that if China could view medical aesthetics as positively as Korea does, it could out-compete on equipment and raw materials, but right now Korea is pocketing all the money. He judges that in the next 5 to 10 years, as the younger generation gains a voice, attitudes will change, but the policy side may still not open up.
