Good Ayis Are Always on the Job: What a Domestic-Help Consultant Really Sells Isn't Information

Domestic help is a supply-driven industry. Good ayis never enter the open market. The consultant's real challenge isn't signing the deal — it's the after-sales replacement. Both employer and ayi are just passers-through of wages.

Good ayis are always on the job

This market has a circulation paradox: highly rated ayis never enter the open market. She works for one family for two or three years, and the moment she leaves she's referred straight to the next. What actually circulates on the market is usually the ones nobody is satisfied with after looking around. So the industry's core contradiction is supply — where good ayis come from and how they get discovered. A domestic-help company is essentially a human resources company: it has to recruit, train, and re-match.

19:37

Every woman in domestic help has a story

The ayi profile clusters in third- and fourth-tier cities or even poor villages, aged 30 to 45, many divorced or single. Live-in sisters spend little time with their own families, giving their time to another household. Two clear traits mark their choice of this work: they like children, and they hunger to earn — not a comfortable life planting fields and drinking tea back home, but packing everything they own, buying a green-skin train ticket, and squeezing into Shanghai.

— Yiting
26:00

This time you're the boss

There has to be boundary awareness and tact between ayi and employer — not just toward the ayi, but toward the employer too. Some people spoil the ayi past the limit, but people always push for an inch more. More subtly, a live-in ayi is together 24 hours a day and may know more about the family's private life than relatives do — that's a lot of pressure. Some moms are doing widowed-style parenting, with the dad absent, and it's the ayi who keeps the mom company longer; the two become teammates.

— Yiting
37:24

Employer and ayi are both passers-through of wages

To the employer she's like a pass-through for wages, and the same goes for the ayi: every month the WeChat transfer comes in, and immediately two kids' tuition goes out, then the family living expenses — gone just like that. For many yuesao and yuer sao, the income is the family's most important or even only source, which is why her role as wife in the family structure is so dominant. That also explains why employers care about the ayi's family background — you're not hiring a company employee; stability is directly tied to family circumstances.

— Yiting
41:09

How a ten-page job spec doesn't scare the ayi away

The longest yuesao job spec I've seen ran a full ten pages, yet the price was just the market average. The employer thinks writing out every minute will make the ayi execute like a robot, but people are emotional. The consultant won't forward it directly; first a voice message to lay the groundwork: asking about luggage, navigation route, "the client says just tell them what time you'll arrive," then explaining it's company policy that made the employer list requirements, and "if you have questions, come to me anytime." That way the ayi feels she's been handed a guidebook, not interrogated.

— Yiting
49:47

Signing isn't hard; replacing is the test

Yuesao are booked in advance; yuer sao are mostly urgent orders — an ayi can only enter the market after leaving her previous family, so demand is urgent. Many employers assume someone urgently available must be bad, but often it's the booked one who's the fake. Signing isn't hard for a consultant; as long as the employer has need and budget, someone can always be found. The real difficulty is long-term after-sales: the interview gets you excited, and later there's a certain probability you get let down. Replacing is what truly tests a consultant's skill.

— Yiting
56:17

The hardest order requires insight into the family's core relationship

The hardest order: a single-mother female executive who wanted a daytime child growth companion, but also expected this person to do housework to cleaning-standard results — while the companion's hourly rate is double the cleaner's. The consultant dealt with the assistant, and the core need was also influenced by the executive's mother. The compromise was cleaner for cleaning, companion for companionship, but the mother still had to be won over. The final hire was poles apart from the initial spec — originally under 35 with a bilingual kindergarten teaching background; in the end, a sister nearly 50 with an education background and live-in experience.

— Yiting
1:03:36

Yuesao start at 15k; yuer sao depends on age

In first-tier cities, a yuesao's 26-day contract price is generally 15,000–16,000 or more; for someone with two or three years' experience who has genuinely cared for newborns, basically 15,000 to 20,000. Shanghai rates for yuer sao: beginners from 7,000; experienced hands depend on age, skills, and work history, basically 8,000-plus; add English, bilingual, early education, driving, and it's 10,000-plus. Prices have risen in recent years partly because higher-educated people are entering, and partly because supply still falls short of demand.

— Yiting
1:12:23

Consensus and industry norms are what this trade lacks most

The live-in norm is that the ayi brings her own personal items and the employer provides big items like bedding, but employer and employee haven't formed a consensus yet. Some think it's lovely when an ayi who's been there a while develops feelings and spends her own wages buying things for the baby; others find it too close. A common situation is a mother unhappy that the child is too attached to the ayi. Consultant training teaches: in a family, the person who should have the closest contact is the mom — not the dad, not the grandparents.

— Yiting
1:39:42

Young people doing domestic work is long past being a meme

By the time something becomes a meme, the number of people doing it is beyond your imagination. The education-training industry's contraction sent a lot of talent into domestic help; child growth companions are very young, a cohort in their twenties, because jobs are hard to find and this trade provides room and board and you're responsible for just one child. After five to eight years of live-in experience, yuying shi and yuesao get older and can't keep up with the kids, so they shift toward training-instructor roles. But whether yuying shi or child companion, it's hard to make a three-to-five-year career plan.
