Good Ayis Are Always on the Job — What a Domestic-Help Consultant Really Sells Isn't Information

Domestic help is a supply-led industry: good ayis never reach the open market, and the consultant's real challenge isn't closing the deal but handling replacements after the sale; employers and ayis alike are just pass-throughs for a paycheck.

Good ayis are always on the job

This market has a circulation paradox: ayis with high ratings never reach the open market at all. She stays with one family for two or three years, and the moment she leaves she's referred straight to the next one. The ones actually circulating on the market are usually the ones nobody could settle on. So the industry's core contradiction is a supply problem — where good ayis come from and how they get discovered. A domestic-help company is essentially a human resources company: it has to recruit, train and re-match.

19:37

Every woman in this line has a story

The ayi profile clusters in third- and fourth-tier cities and even poor villages, aged 30 to 45, many divorced or single. Live-in sisters spend little time with their own families and give their time to another family instead. Two obvious traits mark their choice of this work: they like children, and they're hungry to earn — not living easy back home farming and drinking tea, but packing everything they own, buying a green-skin train ticket and squeezing their way to Shanghai.

— Yiting
26:00

This time you're the boss

There has to be a sense of boundaries and proportion between ayi and employer — not just for the ayi, but for the employer too. Some people spoil the ayi past the line, but people always push for more. More delicate still: a live-in ayi is together with the family 24 hours a day, and may know more about the household's private life than close relatives do — the pressure is heavy. Some moms are doing solo parenting with the dad absent, and it's the ayi who keeps the mom company longest; the two become teammates.

— Yiting
37:24

Employer and ayi are both pass-throughs for a paycheck

To the employer she looks like a pass-through for a paycheck, and it's the same for the ayi: the monthly WeChat transfer comes in and immediately goes out — two kids' tuition first, then the household living expenses, gone. For many yuesao and yuer sao, the income is the most important or even the only source for the family, which is why her role as wife within the family structure carries so much weight. That also explains why employers care about the ayi's family background — you're not hiring a company employee, and her stability is directly tied to her family situation.

— Yiting
41:09

How a ten-page job spec doesn't scare the ayi away

The longest yuesao job spec I've seen ran a full ten pages, at a price that was only the market average. The employer thinks that if every minute is spelled out, the ayi will execute like a robot — but people are emotional. The consultant won't forward it directly; she'll call first to lay the groundwork: ask about luggage, navigation route, tell her the client says just arrive at this time, then explain that company rules require the employer to list requirements, and she can come to me anytime with questions. That way the ayi feels she's been handed a guidebook, not interrogated.

— Yiting
49:47

Closing the deal is easy; replacing someone is the test

Yuesao bookings are scheduled orders; yuer sao jobs are mostly urgent — an ayi only enters the market once she leaves her previous family, so demand is pressing. Many employers assume that whoever is available on short notice must be inferior, but often it's the booked one who turns out to be the fake. Closing isn't hard for a consultant: as long as the employer has a need and a budget, someone can always be found. What's genuinely hard is the long-term after-sales — you get swept up in the interview, and there's a real chance of coming back down afterwards. Replacing someone is what truly tests a consultant's level.

— Yiting
56:17

The hardest deal requires reading the family's core relationship

The hardest deal: a single-mother female executive who wanted a daytime child growth companion, but also expected that person to do housework to cleaning-level standards — while a child companion's hourly rate is twice a cleaner's. The consultant dealt with the assistant, and the core need was also shaped by the executive's mother. The compromise was cleaners for cleaning, companions for companionship, but the mother still had to be won over. The person finally found was the polar opposite of the original spec — originally under 35 with a bilingual kindergarten teaching background; in the end, a sister close to 50 with an education background and live-in experience.

— Yiting
1:03:36

Yuesao start at 15,000; yuer sao depends on age

In first-tier cities a yuesao's 26-day contract price is generally 15,000–16,000 or more; for one with two or three years' experience who has genuinely cared for a newborn, it's basically 15,000 to 20,000. Shanghai rates for yuer sao: beginners from 7,000, experienced hands depending on age, skills and work history, basically 8,000-plus; stack on English, bilingual, early education or driving and it's 10,000-plus. Prices have risen in recent years partly because more highly educated people are entering the field, and partly because supply still falls short of demand.

— Yiting
1:12:23

Consensus and industry norms are what this trade lacks most

The live-in norm is that the ayi brings her own personal items and the employer provides big items like bedding, but the two sides haven't formed a consensus yet. Some think it's lovely that an ayi who's been there a long time grows attached and spends her own wages buying things for the baby; others find it too close. A common situation is a mother who's unhappy that the child is too attached to the ayi. Consultant training teaches this: the person in a family who should have the closest contact is the mom — not the dad, and not the grandparents.

— Yiting
1:39:42

Young people doing domestic work is long past being a meme

By the time something becomes a meme, the number of people doing it is already beyond what you'd imagine. The contraction of the education-training industry fed a lot of talent into domestic help; child companions are very young, a cohort in their twenties, because jobs are hard to find and this line provides room and board and responsibility for just one child. After five to eight years of live-in experience, yuying shi and yuesao get older and can't keep up with the kids, and shift toward training-instructor roles. But whether you do yuying shi or child companionship, it's very hard to build a three-to-five-year career plan.
