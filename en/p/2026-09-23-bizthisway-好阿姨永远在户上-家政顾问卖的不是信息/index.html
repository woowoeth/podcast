Good ayis are always on assignment: what a domestic-service consultant really sells isn't information

Domestic work is a supply-led industry: good ayis never reach the open market, and the consultant's hard part isn't closing the deal but the after-sales replacement; employers and ayis are both just passers-through of a wage.

Good ayis are always on assignment

This market has a circulation paradox: an ayi with good reviews never reaches the open market. She works for one family for two or three years, and the moment she leaves she's referred straight into the next household. The ones actually circulating on the market are usually the ones nobody could settle on. So the industry's core contradiction is a supply problem — where good ayis come from and how they get discovered. A domestic-service company is essentially a human resources company: it has to recruit, train and re-match.

19:37

Every woman in this trade has a story

The ayi profile clusters in third- and fourth-tier cities and even poor villages, aged 30 to 45, many divorced or single. Live-in sisters spend little time with their own families and give their time to another household. Two traits stand out in why they choose this work: they like children, and they want to earn — not to sit comfortably in the village farming and drinking tea, but to pack up everything they own, buy a green-skin train ticket and squeeze their way to Shanghai.

— Yiting
26:00

This time you're the boss

There has to be a sense of boundaries and tact between ayi and employer — not just for the ayi, but for the employer too. Some people spoil the ayi past the line, but everyone takes a mile when given an inch. More delicate still: a live-in ayi is together with the family 24 hours a day, and may know more about the household's private life than blood relatives do — the pressure is heavy. Some mothers are doing widowed-style parenting, with the father absent, and it's the ayi who keeps the mother company longer; the two become teammates.

— Yiting
37:24

Employer and ayi are both passers-through of a wage

For the employer it's like being a passers-through of a wage, and for the ayi it's the same: the monthly WeChat transfer comes in, and immediately two children's tuition goes out, then the household living expenses go out — transferred away just like that. Many yuesao and yuer sao earn the most important, even the only, income in their family, which is why their role as wife inside the family structure is a strong one. That also explains why employers care about the ayi's family background — you're not hiring a company employee, and her stability is directly tied to her family situation.

— Yiting
41:09

How ten pages of job requirements don't scare the ayi off

The longest yuesao job requirement I've seen ran a full ten pages, yet the price was only the market average. The employer thinks that if every minute of every day is spelled out, the ayi will execute like a robot — but people are emotional. The consultant doesn't forward it directly; she first calls to lay the groundwork by voice: asking about luggage, giving directions, telling her the client says just arrive at this time, then explaining that company rules require the employer to list requirements, and that she can come to the consultant with any question. That way the ayi feels she's been handed a guidebook, not interrogated.

— Yiting
49:47

Closing the deal isn't hard; replacing someone is the test

Yuesao bookings are advance orders, while yuer sao jobs are mostly urgent — an ayi can only enter the market after leaving her previous household, so demand is pressing. Many employers assume that whoever is available urgently must be bad; in fact it's often the booked one who's the fake. Closing isn't hard for a consultant: as long as the employer has a need and a budget, someone can always be found. The genuinely hard part is long-term after-sales — the interview can be thrilling, and afterwards there's a real chance of the thrill going flat; replacing someone is what truly tests a consultant's level.

— Yiting
56:17

The hardest case requires reading the family's core relationship

The hardest case: a single mother who is a female executive, wanting a daytime child-development companion, but also expecting this person to do housework to a cleaner's standard — while the companion's hourly rate is twice a cleaner's. The consultant dealt with the assistant, and the core need was also shaped by the executive mother. The compromise was cleaners for cleaning and companions for companionship, but the mother still had to be won over. The person finally found was the polar opposite of the original spec — originally under 35 with a bilingual-kindergarten early-education background; in the end, a sister close to 50 with an education background and live-in experience.

— Yiting
1:03:36

Yuesao start at 15,000; yuer sao are judged by age

In first-tier cities a yuesao's 26-day contract price is generally 15,000 to 16,000 or more; for one with two or three years' experience who has genuinely cared for a newborn, it's basically 15,000 to 20,000. Shanghai rates for yuer sao: beginners from 7,000, experienced hands judged on age, skills and work history, basically 8,000-plus; stack on English, bilingual, early education or driving and it's 10,000-plus. Prices have risen in recent years partly because more highly educated people are entering the trade, and partly because supply still falls short of demand.

— Yiting
1:12:23

Consensus and trade norms are what this industry lacks most

The live-in norm is that the ayi brings her own personal items and the employer provides the big items like bedding, but the two sides of the employment relationship haven't formed a consensus yet. Some think it's lovely that an ayi who's been there a long time grows attached and spends her own wages buying things for the baby; others think that's too close. A common situation is a mother who is unhappy that the child is too close to the ayi. Consultant training teaches this: the person in a household who should have the closest contact is the mother — not the father, and not the grandparents.

— Yiting
1:39:42

Young people doing domestic work is long past being a meme

By the time something becomes a meme, the number of people doing it is already beyond what you'd imagine. The contraction of the tutoring industry fed a lot of talent into domestic services; child-development companions are very young, a cohort in their twenties, because jobs are hard to find and this trade provides room and board and responsibility for just one child. After five to eight years of live-in experience, yuying shi and yuesao get older and can't keep up with the child, so they shift toward training-instructor roles. But whether you do yuying shi or child-development companionship, it's very hard to build a three-to-five-year career plan.

Glossary

chemistry
The feeling of clicking between employer and ayi, like a blind date — it can't be trained.
feidan (off-platform deal)
Employer or ayi cutting the consultant out and settling privately; very common in the trade.

Yuesao are booked months ahead by due date; yuer sao are mostly hired urgently because they only enter the market after leaving a household.

A recently popular in-home tutor role, mostly serving international-school pupils from primary to secondary level.
