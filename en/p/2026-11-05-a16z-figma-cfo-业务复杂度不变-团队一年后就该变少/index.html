Flat business complexity means smaller finance teams within a year

Reconciliation, M&A due-diligence prototypes, and investment memo generation have been compressed to minutes by AI. Figma CFO Praveer Melwani argues that once business complexity stops growing, finance team headcount needs will shrink as a result.

4:09

Automation once meant hand-writing SQL; AI scales what took specialists

Praveer recalls that when he joined Figma, automation meant hand-writing SQL himself—merging product usage data with Stripe financial data, writing 400-500 lines of queries at a time, waiting hours per run, often needing multiple attempts. Once stable, he'd need to bring in a data scientist to integrate the logic into an ETL pipeline for continuous delivery to Salesforce, Marketo, and other systems. His point: the productivity multiplier once built by hand is now within reach for anyone using AI tools.

— Praveer Melwani
9:12

You hire for judgment—AI can't replace your thinking

Praveer is hiring a VP Finance and deliberately gives candidates minimal information (the company is public with abundant data). What he's really testing isn't whether they build the model, but whether they truly understand the model structure, know which assumptions most affect results, and can explain why the model is built that way. Anyone can prompt a large language model to spit out a model. That's not the same as understanding the business. AI is a tool, but thinking cannot be outsourced.

— Praveer Melwani
12:20

M&A due diligence now includes: have AI rebuild the target's product

Figma's corp dev team has added a standard due-diligence checkpoint: use vibe coding to reverse-engineer the acquisition target's product and immediately assess how deep the technical moat runs. Further, they've built an AI personality model for each executive, then use these personas to simulate what questions Praveer, CRO Sean, and others would raise when drafting the investment memo. They resolve weak points before team meetings, cutting the back-and-forth discussion time.

— Praveer Melwani
19:32

Ninety percent of your new agents might never get used—build them anyway

Ivan mentions a growth-stage finance team that built 40-50 agents in a month or two, but roughly 90% ended up with no users. Praveer sees this "waste" as worth celebrating—the point isn't agent retention but shifting the team's default from "let's do this manually" to "could we automate this?" The real risk comes later: when similar tools get reinvented across audit, GRC, AP, and other teams. That's when someone needs to step in, consolidate the scattered tools, and make them reusable.

— Ivan Makarov
22:43

Finance engineers stay distributed; the CIO owns consolidation and reuse

Figma didn't centralize automation into one team. Instead, business experts build their own prototypes first—they know best what the workflow should look like. But Praveer won't let the same person both build systems and own upstream dependencies, maintenance, and handoff risk. That belongs with a structured CIO organization: identifying which bottom-up tools deserve to be productized and reused across everyone, rather than letting each team maintain its own version of the same thing. He describes new CIO Casper Sorensen's role as building centralized PM function.

— Praveer Melwani
32:00

Access to AI tools is now part of your recruiting pitch

Praveer noticed team members voluntarily saying they enjoy their work partly because the company gives them freedom and policy to explore AI tools. He's started pitching this as a selling point in hiring conversations, especially to non-technical candidates—it's their first chance to touch an entirely new world. More compelling than just saying you can use these models is letting candidates actually feel it during the interview: the company isn't just granting permission, it's actually using and building with them.

— Praveer Melwani
39:22

Flat business complexity means smaller finance teams within a year

Asked whether efficiency gains will plateau, Praveer is direct: if Figma's business complexity stays the same, it probably won't need the same number of people a year from now. But he's precise about the limits—in reality Figma keeps adding product lines and acquiring teams; complexity is always rising, so the organization is still racing that growth rather than simply cutting. He's clear, though, that efficiency gains will persistently reduce the headcount required per unit of complexity.

— Praveer Melwani
40:25

Excel doesn't get replaced; it's how you build trust in finance

When asked if Excel is due for retirement, Praveer flatly disagrees: it's how he understands the business and builds confidence. He compares it to Figma's own product—you can generate a design draft from natural language, but to tweak colors you're better off manipulating the color wheel directly. Models aren't yet good enough to output a trustworthy forecast model straight away. That hands-on adjustment, organizing your thinking through spatial layout, won't be easy to replace in the near term.

— Praveer Melwani
