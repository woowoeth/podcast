Jieliya's successor abandons billion-yuan goal: what's the point of scale?

Three years ago he declared Jieliya would break 100 billion yuan in revenue within three years. Now he's walked that back entirely—what he wants is a brand that lasts a century with sustained healthy growth.

Learn how Jieliya's second generation turned his father's cash-on-delivery principle, non-adversarial dealer relationships, and the high repeat-purchase rate of disposables into a playbook that scaled online from 200 million to 4 billion yuan.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

3:00

Unpaid invoices humiliated his father into building a brand

Shi Jing (石晶) recounts his father's early struggles running a factory: constantly chasing customers for payment. One year collecting at year-end in Guangzhou, a client not only refused to pay but was rude about it. That moment his father smashed a passing taxi in rage. Right then he decided to build a brand—Jieliya would never extend credit, regardless of a dealer's size. Cash on delivery, always. That rule has held from the 1980s to today.

— Shi Jing
4:00

He rejected civil service to return and exceeded expectations

Shi Jing graduated from Peking University; his family hoped he'd join the civil service. But he judged himself temperamentally unsuited—he prizes freedom and would be miserable as a bureaucrat—and decided to return to help the company through its difficulties and risks. Looking back, he says he ‘exceeded their expectations.’ He wonders aloud what would have happened if he hadn't returned: the company would likely have been ‘gradually eliminated.’

— Shi Jing
8:01

Celebrity IP collaboration cost millions but bought crucial education

After returning to the company, Shi Jing's first independent project was moving the sub-brand Jieliya Blue to Vipshop, riding the e-commerce wave and his unique ability to access group resources. The next project—celebrity IP collaboration—crashed. He invested heavily in product development, sat on inventory, and burned marketing budget. The products themselves were fine, but completely mismatched to Jieliya's dealer base, channel structure, and consumer makeup. The tens of millions in tuition taught him that the more power you wield, the more catastrophic your errors become.

— Shi Jing
11:03

High repeat-purchase wipes beat durable towels in the attention economy

Between 2015 and 2017, disposable face wipes surged and threatened the towel business. The group initially wanted to suppress competitors through low-cost towels, but Shi Jing saw that was futile—fixed assets and brand investment were sunk; they couldn't outcompete lean newcomers. He and his brother agreed to embrace wipes instead. The reason was repeat-purchase math: ‘A towel might go three years unwashed—guys even longer if you're sloppy—but a pack of wipes? Use them up in a month, they're gone. You can't wash them and reuse. You just have to buy more.’ As ad costs peaked, high-repeat categories drove organic traffic.

— Shi Jing
17:03

Non-confrontation with dealers, not direct competition, scaled to 4 billion

Jieliya's online channels grew from 200 million yuan to 4 billion yuan between 2016–17 and now. Offline share dropped from 95% to approximately 55% (online–offline parity). Store count on major platforms expanded from a peak of 5–10 to 200–300. Shi Jing's key insight: ‘Don't pit dealers against yourself. What makes you think if you can do something online, dealers can't? You think online matters—but why don't you think it matters to them?’ Brands don't win by going around dealers; they win by proving online works, then inviting dealers to replicate the model.

— Shi Jing
27:05

Douyin content matrix saved roughly 100 million in equivalent ad spend

The account ‘Towel Young Master’ generated tens of billions of Douyin views. Shi Jing says this is hard to reduce directly to sales—the real value is opening a new channel and cutting marketing cost. He ran the numbers: reaching the same brand awareness via paid ads would have cost approximately 100 million yuan. Better content begets virality; virality builds followers; followers reduce ad costs relative to competitors. The cycle compounds in the brand's favor.

— Shi Jing
28:05

He repudiated his billion-yuan target; now he chases century-long growth

In 2023, Shi Jing said Jieliya would break 100 billion yuan in annual sales within three years. This time he walked it back completely: ‘I have no need to chase 100 billion—what's the point of hitting 100 billion anyway?’ and ‘Once you hit 100 billion, you'll just want a trillion.’ What he wants now is not a number but a brand that lasts a century with steady healthy growth. Slow beats short-term.

— Shi Jing
34:08

Two-hour nightly walks keep him sane; he engineered his own discipline

To the public he's a dictatorial CEO who scaled online to 4 billion. His actual daily life: swimming, running, then two hours of evening walks—without them he cannot sleep. In university his weight neared 200 pounds. Over two years he skipped dinner and night snacks entirely, dropping to 130-some pounds. Then he used exercise to tighten loose skin. On those walks his mind mostly cycles through company problems—and his own.

— Shi Jing
