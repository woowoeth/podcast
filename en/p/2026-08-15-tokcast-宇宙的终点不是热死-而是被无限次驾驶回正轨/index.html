The universe doesn't end in heat death — it gets steered back on course infinitely many times

Tipler's omega point theory was wrecked by his own extravagant religious claims, but Deutsch says there's a valuable core inside: the universe must be continuously piloted to complete infinite computation in finite time.

8:09

Tipler's theory was destroyed by Tipler himself

Deutsch has a precise criticism of Frank Tipler's omega point theory: the theory itself is ‘about the fabric of all reality’, belonging not to any one of the four strands but irreducibly to all four — which is very high praise. But in “The Physics of Immortality” Tipler made extravagant claims for his theory, and the result is that most scientists and philosophers threw the whole thing out, missing the valuable core idea inside it along with it. Deutsch says that discarded core is what he is about to explain.

— Brett Hall
22:20

Tipler holds the line on unitarity against observation

Faced with observations of accelerating expansion, Tipler refuses to give ground. He invokes Hawking's result: an eternally expanding universe requires all black holes to evaporate via Hawking radiation, which destroys all the information inside them and thus violates the unitarity of quantum physics — probabilities must sum to one. Tipler's argument is: the accelerating expansion you see now is only a local fact about us at this moment; wait long enough and contraction will begin, because unitarity cannot be violated. Brett points out that Tipler is also engaged in a kind of motivated reasoning, trying to derive from the omega point theory that Christianity is true and that a Christian-style resurrection of everyone will occur.

— Brett Hall
36:34

The world is comprehensible because it is comprehensible

Deutsch's argument is: if we want to explain why the world appears comprehensible, the explanation may simply be that the world is comprehensible. And a theory that the world is ‘half comprehensible’ has no explanatory power at all; it merely restates the problem and introduces an inexplicable constant of 1/2. Brett says this is his new favourite sentence from rereading the chapter, a thorough refutation of those who say the human mind can never understand ultimate physics — including Neil deGrasse Tyson, who worries that only an alien mind could understand the successor theory to quantum mechanics. The reason is in “The Beginning of Infinity”: we are universal explainers.

— Brett Hall
36:34

The omega point needs someone at the helm the whole way

Deutsch points out a technical detail: the spacetime oscillations that make the omega point possible are of the classically chaotic, unstable kind, growing more violent the closer you get to the omega point. Any tiny deviation from the correct shape is rapidly amplified, enough to destroy the conditions for continued computation, so the big crunch would arrive after only finitely many steps of computation. So to satisfy the Turing principle and reach the omega point, the universe must be continuously ‘steered’ back onto the right trajectory. Tipler has already shown in principle how to do this by manipulating the gravitational field of the whole of space.

— Brett Hall
47:43

Cramming infinitely many thoughts into the last second

Deutsch's argument: the stabilisation mechanisms and the technology for storing information must be improved infinitely many times, with density and stress rising without limit, which requires continually creating new knowledge — and Popperian epistemology says new knowledge requires rational criticism, and therefore requires intelligent entities. So in the final frenzy, an infinite amount of stabilisation and knowledge creation happens in finite time. Someone will ask: with only a second left, why not rest? Deutsch says this is the biggest misunderstanding — these people's minds are programs running on computers with no upper bound on speed, and thoughts are virtual realities rendered by those computers. The subjective duration of a virtual experience is not determined by the time that elapses but by the computation executed during that time. Infinite steps of computation contain the time for infinitely many thoughts.

52:46

The Kardashev scale is pseudoscience

The host says flatly that he regards the Kardashev scale as pseudoscience. The reason: how do you calculate how much energy is there? You can look at the amount of luminous matter, make various assumptions, but we don't know what dark matter is, we don't know whether zero-point energy can be tapped, there are too many unknowns — especially when you're talking about the distant future and about ‘harnessing all the energy around a star’, you might find another energy source. He lumps it together with the Drake equation: not a law of physics but a string of choices about variables, some constrained, many completely open. He also grumbles: Musk is very keen on it, so tech people are keen on it too.

1:02:56

Dark energy and the Church principle falsify each other

There are three independent lines of evidence for accelerating expansion: type 1a supernovae (white dwarfs accrete to 1.38 solar masses and explode, giving a standard luminosity, and they measure as dimmer, hence farther, than they should be), the acoustic peaks of the cosmic microwave background being larger than they should be, and the matter distribution of baryon acoustic oscillations. All three are most parsimoniously explained by accelerating expansion. But the host says this poses a two-way problem: the Church principle makes the dark energy observations suspect, and the dark energy observations make the Church principle suspect. The possible ways out are that one of them is false, or that both are true and the universe will recollapse in the future, or cycle as Penrose says, with something odd happening at the end that lets information leak into the next universe.

1:25:09

Predicting future knowledge is self-contradictory

Deutsch cites Pa's argument: future human affairs depend on the growth of future knowledge, and we cannot predict which specific knowledge will be created in the future — because if we could predict it, by definition we would already have that knowledge now. This is not ‘prediction is hard’ but logically impossible. So any large-scale prophecy about future human or posthuman affairs cannot be reliable. The host says this sentence is the closing point of the whole picture of the future, and is the core content of “The Beginning of Infinity” appearing early in “The Fabric of Reality”.

Glossary

omega point
Tipler's proposed end state of the universe, in which life attains infinite computational capacity.

Any physically realisable computation can be performed by a universal Turing machine.

A classification of civilisations by the total amount of energy they can harness.
unitarity
The mathematical requirement in quantum mechanics that probabilities sum to one.
