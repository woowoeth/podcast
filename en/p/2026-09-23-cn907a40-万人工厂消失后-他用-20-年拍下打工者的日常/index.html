After the 10,000-worker factory vanished, he spent 20 years photographing the daily life of migrant workers

Others photograph the anomalies of an era; he insisted on photographing its everyday — eating, clocking in, learning English. When the 10,000-worker factory really did disappear, only a very few people had kept this archive.

The logic of the assembly line is to make a person's value unimportant

After the Foxconn suicides, Zhan Youbing was asked what he thought. He said he had seriously considered it: every assembly-line method works by subdividing the process to make manufacturing more stable and the people more replaceable. Translated, those two sentences mean — anyone can do it, and anyone will do it just as well. Though the machine had not yet replaced the worker, the person's value was already almost unimportant. This was the underlying motive for his later picking up a camera and aiming it at his fellow workers: inside a system that deliberately erases individual difference, to record specific people.

6:11

Riding the green-skin train felt like boarding a bandit's den

In 1995, after leaving the army, he went south. The carriage was already full at the Wuchang starting station — people above the seats, people below, people sleeping on the luggage racks, people everywhere in the toilets and the gangways between cars. At Chenzhou someone smashed the glass with a brick and shoved people onto the train like livestock. For more than thirty hours he could not sit and could not drink water; after getting off, his abdomen was terribly swollen. At the Guangzhou railway station he was then cheated by a tout into being ‘sold as a piglet’ — first they took twenty-odd yuan and said it was for Dongguan, then on the highway they demanded another ten for the expressway fee, and if you refused someone would come up and slap you, and finally they dumped you in a wasteland with no village ahead and no shop behind to change vehicles. He said he had used this word for so many years, and only last year did he understand what it meant.

— Zhanyou Bing
11:48

He did 102 push-ups for a security guard job

In 1995 it was very hard to find work; men had a far harder time than women, and factory recruitment notices stated outright that they only hired female workers aged 18 to 25. He saw a hiring notice by National Highway 107, and the security captain had all the applicants lie down in the parking lot and do push-ups to screen their stamina. The count was drawn out; by 20 people began to drop, by 40 only eight or ten were left, by 50-something only he remained. The captain said you're bragging, go ahead, and he did 102. After he got up they had him spar with someone; the other man did a split, and he turned sideways, caught the leg, and flipped him. That was how he got his first job: security guard at a hotel gate, 450 yuan a month.

— Zhanyou Bing
17:16

An 1,800-yuan camera and 48-yuan rolls of film

In 2002 he struggled with himself for a long time before buying his own camera: his wage was only around 1,500, he already had a child, and his wife was still back in the hometown. He called to discuss it, and his wife said, if you like it, then buy it. The camera was 1,800 yuan, and film, even bought ten rolls at a time wholesale, was 48 each, plus 5 for developing and another ten or twenty for enlargements — in that era it was a huge drain on him. At first he photographed factory events, visiting guests, employees in a tug-of-war, assuming that photography was just a matter of technique, of getting a clear shot. Later, reading Chinese photography magazines, he discovered there was so much learning in photography and that he had no standard by which to measure it, so he bought the New York Institute of Photography textbook, whose first lesson was the three criteria of a good photograph.

— Zhanyou Bing
24:00

A model contest photo hit ten million clicks, then he was told to delete it

In 2007 the factory began holding model contests; the workers went on stage in swimsuits, evening gowns, and uniforms they had made, and there was also pole dancing. In 2008 he changed his position, went to the stage backdrop and shot downward — the models in front, the dense black mass of the audience in the distance — and sent it to Tencent in time, where it was posted on the portal site and got tens of millions of clicks in a single morning. But online comments said this was choosing a mistress for the boss, a lover for the factory director; the public opinion was distorted, the boss came under enormous pressure, and he was told to delete it. Tencent said it could only be hidden, that other sites' viral reposting could not be stopped, and finally served him a notice reserving the right to pursue the matter.

— Zhanyou Bing
25:23

That photo of the female worker looking up — he had not planned to use it

On January 5, 2011, as a senior supervisor, he entered the clean room to inspect fire safety, and because the work required it he openly brought his camera. After two hours of work in the clean room there was a four-minute break; the place where they changed work clothes was hung all over with clean-room garments no one was wearing, and two people sat in front, one of them a fellow Hubei native. He pressed the shutter once or twice, and the fellow native heard the sound and looked up at him. He said the experience people had told him when learning photography was that ‘photos where the subject looks at the lens are not good photos’, so he had long avoided using this one. Later, Ren Yue, a teacher at Renmin University of China, organized the Off-Pix award, he submitted it, and it was unexpectedly used as the lead image. He later looked at this photo seriously and admitted it had its own appeal — different readers would have different spiritual responses to it, so a photographer should interpret his photos as little as possible.

— Zhanyou Bing
31:26

One set of ID papers lent to many people; after social security went online, his years of service were zeroed out

When he came out to find work in 1995, Hubei people were not wanted, Henan people were not wanted, Anhui people were acceptable, so he borrowed an Anhui man's ID card. At the time factories' information was independent, there was no networked computer system, and the social security system did not require contributions, so this set of papers was lent to many people and entered many factories. But after 2000 social security began to be networked, and the system did not recognize it — the ID number was the same, the name was the same, the household registration address was the same, and the enterprise decided you were dishonest and zeroed out your years of service. He said this won't do; our generation is about to reach retirement age, and those many years of service are real. When even present life is not secure, it is simply impossible to think about retirement twenty or fifty years from now.

— Zhanyou Bing
36:30

Density: he photographed for 20 years before discovering it was everywhere

In 2019 he curated a set of photographs around the thread of dense migrant-worker life, which won the Excellence Award and the Alpa Award at the Third Yingjishi Image Awards. Workers' assemblies shot from high above, heads packed with almost no gaps; collective dormitories hung all over with clothes, airtight when seen from a distance; and workshop switches, key cabinets, storage lockers for bowls and chopsticks — neatly divided structures that constitute a visual density. He said at first it was unconscious; he photographed every day with his phone, photographed when organizing security guard training, photographed whenever he randomly saw a fellow worker on the road, and over time it accumulated — he had been photographing for almost 20 years, on and off. When there were so many factories and so much of an era, he had not observed it in that way; then one morning he discovered how terrifying it was — even the switches looked spectacular.

— Zhanyou Bing
39:31

Time as the warp, space as the weft — using a lifetime to understand a society

The complete thread he worked out was: time as the warp, space as the weft, the life cycle as the guiding principle, and capital's impact on the industrial zone as the axis, forming a comprehensive three-dimensional record including sound, images, video, text, and physical objects. Later he changed methods: starting from the keyword ‘migrant work’, he found ten main keywords in Baidu Scholar, then searched all related terms on Baidu, got 322, and went into Baidu Images and began screenshotting them all — this is the internet's understanding of migrant work, to be presented together with his own understanding from actual photography in one huge exhibition space. He said this is us viewing the era from within an illusory era, while the real era is ignored. He compared himself to a Sherpa: the famous mountaineers get the credit, but it is the Sherpas who carry the loads — I'll just be that Sherpa.

Glossary

Selling piglets (卖猪仔)
In the mid-Qing, Cantonese were shipped to America to build railways like piglets being transported; later it referred to being cheated by middlemen and drivers who add layer upon layer of fees on the way to work.
Autoethnography (自我民族志)
The researcher takes himself as the object of study, recording the life of the group he belongs to, rather than studying an external ethnic group.
Magnum (马格兰)
Magnum, the internationally renowned photography agency; Zhan Youbing once studied the work of its photographers on its website one by one.
Off-Pix (Off-Pix 奖项)
A photography award organized by Ren Yue, a teacher at Renmin University of China; Zhan Youbing once submitted and had his work shown as the lead image.
