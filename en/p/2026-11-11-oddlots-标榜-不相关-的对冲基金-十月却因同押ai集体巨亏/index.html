Hedge funds claiming diversification suffered collective losses in October from concentrated AI bets

Hedge funds across nearly all mainstream strategies lost money simultaneously in October, showing that most gains over the past year were actually from implicit long AI exposure, not genuine diversified strategy returns.

Flagship funds claiming stability lost money collectively in October

Millennium, Point72, and Exodus Point—the funds with the most concentrated talent and assets, viewed by investors as most steadfast during crises—all lost money in October. Millennium, with ~14% annualized returns, fell 2.3% in a single month. By hedge fund logic, this is dereliction: investors buy hedge funds not to beat bull markets but to expect protection during downturns. In October, that protection failed entirely. The real shock is not the loss itself but the failure to hedge.

— Nishant Kumar
8:20

Funds claiming diversification were concentrated in the same few AI stocks

Vara, a fund with positions highly overlapping with certain AI narratives, crashed 33% in October yet remains positive year-to-date—showing how much that prior AI exposure returned. Whale Rock similarly fell 12%. These single-strategy funds' exposure is explicit: nearly all positions bet on the same small handful of AI stocks. When momentum reverses, no hedge can cushion the blow.

— Nishant Kumar
12:33

Data shows multi-strategy funds were herding into the same stocks

Pivotal Path's research shows the top 10 multi-strategy funds control ~$2.2 trillion in positions including leverage, yet the actual capital supporting those positions is only $300-350 billion—indicating extreme system leverage. This does not mean they were momentum trading; likely these firms independently judged but converged on the same liquid stocks that fit their risk frameworks. The danger lies in what happens when they all change their minds at once: forced selling cascades in sync, exactly as occurred in October.

— Nishant Kumar
15:40

Multi-strategy funds show no mercy to losing traders

Nishant explained the brutal internal risk controls of multi-strategy funds: a PM managing $100 loses 5% (=$5) and their allocation gets cut in half; if they continue to 7.5% total loss, they are force-liquidated and fired on the spot. This discipline is the core pitch of the multi-strategy model—prevent any one team's losses from dragging down the whole platform. But when all strategies turned bad at once in October, this very mechanism meant massive positions were cut simultaneously, actually accelerating the decline.

— Nishant Kumar
24:51

Money rushed to buy shares but top platforms closed to new capital

Millennium aimed to raise $20 billion this year, received over $30 billion in demand, ultimately took $22 billion. Meanwhile, D.E. Shaw, Point72, and Citadel combined returned $35-40 billion to investors. A paradox emerges: investors desperately want multi-strategy fund slots, yet the top platforms are capping size and returning capital. The reason: once you add enough teams, new positions no longer add real diversification—they just dilute the same underlying risk.

— Nishant Kumar
29:59

Thursday's crash was not emotion but mechanical chain reaction

Michael Ball attributes Thursday's crash to options market mechanics: when investors buy puts for downside protection, put sellers (market makers) must continuously sell to hedge their delta risk as prices fall, which itself depresses prices in a self-reinforcing spiral. Layer on leveraged ETFs, systematic vol-control strategies, and CTA trend-following funds all forced to cut positions on the same day—four mechanisms in sync—and you breach the S&P level that held for months.

— Michael Ball
37:05

New cloud operators halved in value, rate sensitivity is the weakness

Take Iron: a cloud stock once pitched as "Bitcoin miners transitioning to AI compute." Its price was $77 in November 2025, down to $21 at recording—roughly a 70% fall. These companies borrowed heavily to build data centers and are highly sensitive to interest rates and customer prepayment quality. Ball thinks the selloff is excessive because backlog orders and compute demand remain robust. But whether 2028 new projects get regulatory blocked remains genuinely unresolved.

— Michael Ball
41:11

Huang's governance turmoil failed to ripple through the market

Markets might have feared that Nvidia governance trouble would transmit through the AI chain. But Ball observed the actual impact was limited: Nvidia itself declined modestly and AI/semiconductor stocks rebounded days later. His reading: AI is now too large for Nvidia or Anthropic alone to move the needle. One company's governance headline cannot shake the whole narrative anymore. Stock moves now track mechanical forces like short covering more than fundamental narrative shifts.

— Michael Ball

Glossary

multi-strat / pod
Hedge fund model splitting capital among dozens of independent trading teams, managing overall risk through strict controls.
negative gamma
Options market makers holding short positions forced to continuously sell as prices fall, amplifying the decline.
CTA
Systematic funds trading commodity and futures trends, often accelerating selling during violent volatility.
de-grossing
Hedge fund simultaneous reduction of both long and short positions, lowering overall risk exposure.
