DebuggingTheMatrix 2007-01-09

iPhone bundled multiple devices' worth of features without raising the price

Jobs priced iPhone at $499, the same as a regular iPod, because this device packed features that other phones charged hundreds extra to deliver separately, yet added zero premium.

This is a transcript of a product launch keynote, not an interview-based deep analysis, but it densely shows how Jobs used comparative diagrams, live demos, and pricing logic to explain a new product category—ideal for anyone studying product launch narratives.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

3:30

Smart phones compromised on usability, dumb phones sacrificed capability

Jobs drew a 2D chart of smart vs. easy to use. Regular phones were neither smart nor easy, while smartphones of the era (Motorola Q, Blackberry, Palm Treo, Nokia E62) became smarter but harder to use because of plastic keyboards and fixed buttons. The core problem: each app needed a different button combination, but plastic keys couldn't be reprogrammed after shipping to support new ideas—this was the contradiction iPhone aimed to solve.

— Steve Jobs
6:37

Bet on ten fingers instead of styluses

To operate a large screen without a mouse, Jobs explicitly rejected styluses—they require taking out, putting back, and risk getting lost. Instead, he chose ten fingers, which everyone naturally has and can't lose, combined with self-developed multi-touch technology that precisely identifies touches, ignores accidental contact, and supports multi-finger gestures. He noted they'd already filed patents for this.

— Steve Jobs
9:53

Building software means you must build your own hardware too

Jobs cited a thirty-year-old observation by Alan Kay explaining why Apple built phone hardware rather than just software. iPhone ran the complete OS X, not a stripped-down OS, giving it true multitasking, strong networking, power management, and genuine desktop-class applications—a software breakthrough he called at least five years ahead of any other phone.

— Steve Jobs
22:29

Visual voicemail applies email logic to voice messages

Jobs pointed out that making calls was actually hard on existing phones—most people used recents as a contacts list. With voicemail, you had to listen through every prior message sequentially to find one you wanted. iPhone's visual voicemail let users pick which message to listen to directly, like email. This was his key highlighted revolutionary feature, requiring joint innovation with carriers at the network level.

— Steve Jobs
36:22

Yahoo's free push email gave iPhone parity with Blackberry

Jobs announced a partnership with Yahoo to provide all iPhone users with free push IMAP email service, equivalent to Blackberry. He emphasized Yahoo Mail was the world's largest email service with over 250 million users, and this partnership meant iPhone shipped with desktop-class inbox experience, not stripped-down WAP email.

— Steve Jobs
1:03:45

$499 bundled separate devices without raising the total cost

Jobs listed market prices at the time: $199 for iPod nano, around $299 for a smartphone (with two-year contract), plus a separate iPod totaling $499 combined. He asked: given iPhone's features far exceeded these individual products, how should it be priced? He announced the 4GB model at $499 and 8GB at $599—no premium at all—directly bundling what would otherwise be hundreds in separate purchases into a single price point.

— Steve Jobs
1:06:53

Public announcement before FCC approval prevented leak-driven disclosure

Jobs explained iPhone wouldn't ship until June because as a new product category it needed FCC approval, a process taking months. Rather than let the FCC approval process accidentally leak the product early, they decided to announce it themselves. The U.S. market would have Singular as exclusive carrier; Europe planned to follow in Q4; Asia would wait until 2008.

— Steve Jobs
1:16:17

One percent of the phone market equals ten million units

Jobs used data showing phones were a far larger market than game consoles (26 million units), digital cameras (94 million), or MP3 players (135 million)—the previous year saw nearly a billion phones sold globally. He concluded that capturing just 1% market share meant ten million units, and set 2008's (the first full sales year) target at capturing 1% of the market.

— Steve Jobs

Glossary

multi-touch
Touch technology that simultaneously recognizes multiple fingers, ignores accidental contact, and enables multi-finger gestures.
visual voicemail
Voice message functionality that displays messages as a list, allowing users to select and listen to any message in any order, like email.
push IMAP
Email protocol where the server actively delivers new messages to the phone without requiring manual refresh.
cover flow
Music interface that lets users flip through album covers by swiping fingers across the screen.
