AI is centralized power, and Crypto is the antidote

Vitalik says AI's power comes from compute and data, and the best AI is the biggest AI, which naturally tends toward centralization; Crypto's only job is to solve trust problems between people, so AI participates in Crypto's game, not the other way around.

Vitalik spoke in Chinese for 68 minutes, laying out the underlying philosophical differences between AI and Crypto, OpenAI's two-step compromise, and his own choice to buy a GPU and run local models, all in concrete terms. The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

10:26

AI can't help him with complex cryptography

Vitalik says he made an interesting discovery using AI himself: for doing things many people have done before but he hasn't, ChatGPT is extremely useful; but for doing truly frontier things, like especially complex cryptography, which only he and a thousand other people have done, AI is no help at all. This showed him a two-sidedness in AI — it can let some people do things they previously had no idea how to do, which is relatively equalizing; but on the other hand, ChatGPT itself is a very centralized thing, and when you use it you have to completely trust that it won't expose your data.

— Vitalik Buterin
13:32

OpenAI's two-step compromise: first drop open source, then drop safety

Vitalik breaks OpenAI's story into two steps: step one, for AI safety reasons, they said they would no longer open source, redefining "open" as "the service is open," turning into close AI; step two, this year they sacrificed safety for their own interests — after last year's conflict between the company and the board, Sam Altman won, and recently it was announced they would go from nonprofit to for-profit, with the board's power reduced to merely advisor. He quotes an American founding father: you cannot sacrifice freedom for safety; if you do, you will find you have neither safety nor freedom.

— Vitalik Buterin
21:43

LLMs are like biological life, not ordinary programs

Vitalik says LLMs differ from ordinary programs in one way: in an ordinary program, if you randomly delete a line of code, the whole thing may stop working — extremely fragile; but with an LLM, small changes don't affect its basic function, and the output only changes a little, so an LLM is very much like biological life. He also mentions Anthropic's research, looking at what concept each parameter in a relatively small LLM represents, and finding you can say specifically that this parameter represents red, that one represents plants, that one represents capitalism, that one represents Ethereum.

— Vitalik Buterin
25:47

AI participates in Crypto's game, not the reverse

Vitalik says Crypto and AI have an intersection that has existed almost from the start but almost nobody mentions: decentralized exchanges have lots of bots doing arbitrage and market making. He abstracts this example into a category — Crypto's role is to make a game, using smart contracts to execute the game's rules relatively safely; AI's role is to participate in that game, because if only humans participate, efficiency won't be high enough. He gives the prediction market Polymarket as an example: four years ago Augur's result quality was not high, this year it's much higher, because liquidity went from maybe 1 million or 10 million USD to 100 or 200 million, and AI's reaction speed is far faster than humans'.

— Vitalik Buterin
28:51

AI's power comes from compute and data, naturally centralizing

Vitalik says AI's power comes from two places: first, the power of computation; second, data. If you have more computing power, your AI will be stronger; if you have more data, your AI will also be stronger. So AI is an especially powerful thing, but the best AI is the biggest AI, and an easy way to make an AI is to put all the computing power and all the data together, in one place. Crypto is the opposite — communities and projects are especially dispersed; the Ethereum Foundation may have only 25% of its people in the US, with the rest scattered everywhere.

— Vitalik Buterin
37:01

The only way humans join superintelligence is to become part of it

Vitalik says something smarter than humans will definitely appear, maybe in ten years, a hundred years, a thousand years. There are two options: one is that it becomes an independent thing that surpasses us; the other is that we can become part of this thing, with biology and machines fusing together. He finds the second direction interesting, but it must be done the right way — if a technology like Neuralink is centralized and not open source, and just sends all your information to one server, that means one big company could read your thoughts, which is very dangerous. He hopes for open-source software and hardware that respects safety.

— Vitalik Buterin
41:07

If smart people all stop doing Crypto, the industry has failed

Vitalik says the AI boom has indeed brought a certain cooling to Crypto. He divides the people who left into three kinds: the first kind whose goal is to make money or take part in the most important thing for humanity; the second kind who care about money, privacy and other issues for specific reasons; the third kind who want to build but do things at somewhat lower quality. He has one worry: if all the especially intelligent people stop doing Crypto, the people left in this field won't have any interesting ideas, and the only applications will be issuing a coin, making a small exchange, then issuing another coin, and this coin has an open dog. He says those things are fun, but if they are the only things, our industry can be said to have failed.

— Vitalik Buterin
46:12

Before this year there wasn't enough technology for ordinary people to participate

Answering "why does the Web3 ecosystem have no real applications," Vitalik says that before this year there simply wasn't enough technology. Some very important technologies weren't mature yet, security problems weren't solved, many privacy problems weren't solved. The only successful application was DeFi, and the reason is that DeFi can make a lot of money — if you have a 30% chance your account gets stolen, but winning multiplies your money tenfold, you might still participate. But if your goal isn't to multiply your money tenfold, but includes your identity and participating in other applications, then with the technical difficulty and security problems unsolved, you won't participate. This year some problems are finally starting to be solved, so it's the best time to build meaningful applications.

— Vitalik Buterin
