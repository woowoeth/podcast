Undercover victim: the perpetrator confesses everything

Fourteen years later, a woman wearing a police wire confronts the perpetrator face-to-face, extracting full details about drugging, photographs, and sexual assault—but the evidence still fails to persuade the family member she most wanted to convince.

1:01

Two hidden recorders transformed a family meeting into criminal evidence

Vienna met her brother-in-law Justin fourteen years after he assaulted her, wearing two recording devices—one in her handbag, one in her bra. Three plainclothes officers sat at nearby tables; backup waited upstairs. She carried a prepared notebook with every question she needed to ask, determined to ask them all in one session and leave no room for follow-up. This was not a family conversation; it was a police-orchestrated evidence-gathering operation from the start.

He framed the drugging as a controlled safety experiment

At twenty years old, Vienna was persuaded by the thirty-one-year-old church leader to drink a mixed cocktail to ‘safely experience drunkenness’. Within minutes she lost the use of her legs and lost consciousness. The next morning, she retained a single, fragmented memory: watching her soul separate from her body as he removed her underwear.

— Vienna
13:27

Former colleagues' memories became evidence for a decade-old crime

To get police to reopen a twelve-year-old case based only on a fragmentary memory, Vienna and her sister Shaughnessy systematically contacted everyone who had become close to Justin only to distance themselves afterward. A former veterinary clinic employee confirmed that Justin had once asked him how much of a particular drug would be required to ensure unconsciousness. Another person reported that an office receptionist had discovered photographs on the clinic computer of a nude woman who resembled Vienna.

— Vienna
19:43

He used addiction therapy language to deflect responsibility

Justin had entered therapy for sexual addiction, and throughout the recorded conversation he repeatedly deployed therapeutic language. He emphasized having drawn his own lines—insisting he would not touch her breasts—as evidence of self-control. This construction allowed him to acknowledge the acts while remaining innocent in his own account, as if he were participating in her healing rather than perpetuating harm.

24:55

She questioned him systematically about thirty nude photographs

Following her undercover training, Vienna pressed Justin methodically through her prepared list: How many photographs? What did each one show? He described approximately thirty nude images, repeatedly insisting he ‘never spread her legs’ and ‘never photographed her genitals’—clinging to the boundary he had set for himself as proof he was not a bad person.

38:29

An unscripted follow-up question revealed undisclosed voyeurism crimes

After she exhausted her prepared questions, Vienna asked in passing whether there was anything else he had not mentioned. Justin then volunteered that he had photographed her through windows at two separate residences, across three or four separate incidents, capturing eight to ten images. This charge had not been part of the investigation plan; it emerged in the final moment of the conversation and supplied the factual basis for two voyeurism convictions.

41:35

Six percent of Canadian sex offenders serve prison time; he lasted six months

Justin faced one charge of sexual assault, two counts of voyeurism, and one count of administering a noxious substance. He pleaded guilty to everything except the drug charge. The court initially sentenced him to two years less a day of house arrest, but on Crown appeal, he was ordered to federal prison instead. He was released on parole after six months. In Canada, about six percent of reported sexual assault cases result in a prison sentence—Vienna's case is extraordinarily rare.

— Aviva de Kornfeld
44:40

The tape convicted him in court but failed to convince her sister

Vienna's fundamental motivation for orchestrating this meeting was to persuade her sister Sunny to leave Justin and remain her sister. But Sunny testified in Justin's defense and was kept out of the courtroom before the recording was played. Vienna does not know whether her sister has ever heard the tape.
