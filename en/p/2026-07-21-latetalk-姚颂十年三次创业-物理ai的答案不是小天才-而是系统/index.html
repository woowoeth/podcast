Yao Song: The Decisive Battle in Physical AI Isn't a Genius, It's a Systems War

From DeePhi to Orienspace to Striding AI, Yao Song judges that the decisive battle in physical AI is not one or two genius algorithms, but data, compute, talent pools, and commercial landing at every milestone. Even intelligence-first giants may not necessarily pick the fruit.

8:15

Tsinghua's 11 hours and boring/suffering

Yao Song recalls that after entering Tsinghua's Department of Electronic Engineering in 2011, he set a rule for himself: he must study 11 hours every day, pausing the timer even for bathroom breaks and meals. He admits that Tsinghua has too many people who are "both smart and hardworking," so he could only exchange diligence for a sense of security. It was not until the second semester of his freshman year that he realized there was more than one path through university. Later, a teacher summed it up: life is either boring or suffering. Yao Song chose the latter—he could not endure the idle life of a Silicon Valley engineer, and this fear of boredom drove him to jump into hard-tech entrepreneurship again and again.

40:04

Rejecting Nvidia and Intel

Before its angel round, DeePhi Technology met 50 institutions and was rejected by all of them, until Zhang Yutong and Lin Renjun of GSR Ventures understood the direction and invested. What he is most proud of is the strategic choice in financing: bringing Xilinx into the A round and rejecting Nvidia and Intel. His reasoning was that if the endgame is an IPO or acquisition, having three chip giants as shareholders simultaneously is actually dangerous—they compete with each other, won't truly provide business resources, and in a future acquisition might bid up the price and cause trouble, making the company unsellable. So he chose Xilinx, which directly competed with Nvidia and urgently needed to strengthen its software capabilities.

— Song Yao
57:20

After signing, falling into darkness

In 2018, DeePhi was acquired by Xilinx for over 3 billion USD. Yao Song says that after signing the contract he happily paced around the table for two minutes, and then his life fell into darkness. Because the direction had previously been set by his advisor Wang Yu, and for the first time he had to decide the next step himself, but there was no path before him, only a fog. At 26, he describes himself as having a midlife crisis, not even wanting to scroll on his phone, interested in nothing. This chaos of losing a sense of purpose was more tormenting than failure, and it became the starting point for him to work part-time at Matrix Partners and systematically search for direction.

— Song Yao
1:04:44

Zhang Ying gave information, not cash

To find a new direction, at the end of 2018 Yao Song worked part-time as a venture partner at Matrix Partners China. Zhang Ying told him: you don't need cash, you need information. All partner meetings and investment committee meetings at Matrix were open to him. He systematically scanned lidar, brain-computer interfaces, GPUs, and controllable nuclear fusion: Li Yifan convinced him that the barrier in lidar lies in engineering capability rather than technical route; brain-computer interfaces in China would require too long an approval cycle; GPUs were restricted by non-compete clauses from mergers; nuclear fusion was too far from industrialization. Eventually he waited for the commercial space window to appear.

— Song Yao
1:17:19

Good, many, fast, cheap—the order cannot be wrong

Yao Song breaks down China's commercial space into three keywords: China, commercial, space, and then prioritizes: good, many, fast, cheap. Good means high success rate—no customer dares to use a rocket with a 60% success rate; many means carrying capacity—satellite internet constellations need at least four to five tons, preferably five to six tons of capacity; fast means seizing the market window; cheap is placed last. Therefore Orienspace chose the solid-on-solid route, Gravity-1 became the world's first solid-fuel rocket with solid boosters, and also conducted China's first private rocket launch livestream. He knew the livestream might capture a failure, but insisted that space should not be too far from ordinary people.

— Song Yao
1:47:39

One or two little geniuses can't solve the problem

Yao Song is not optimistic about the narrative of "one or two little geniuses or young professors starting a company alone." He cites the history of deep learning: AlexNet came from Hinton's group, VGG from Oxford, GoogLeNet from Google, ResNet proposed by He Kaiming, R-CNN to RFCN relayed by different teams—no one stands at the forefront forever. So competition in physical AI is a systems war of data, compute, and talent resource pools, not a game for a few smart people. He quotes Cao Xudong's early words: 50% of algorithmic competitiveness comes from data, 30% from compute, and only 20% from human experience and inspiration.

— Song Yao
2:17:58

Language is the world? Impossible

Yao Song opposes "language is the world." His reason: the digital world is discrete semantics, while the physical world is continuous analog signals; using language to precisely describe the tilt angle of a cup or the distance between two people is simply impossible. A future general world model might need 50T to 100T parameters, but physical AI must be real-time and low-latency, and very likely can only infer on the edge or on small chips. Therefore intelligence-first models like OpenAI and Anthropic that pile up general models with massive compute may not necessarily pick the fruit of physical AI—this is why Striding AI adopts latent space and retains a physical backbone rather than generating pixels.

— Song Yao
2:41:16

Ten attack, zero defense vs. eight attack, two defense

Yao Song distinguishes between a startup founder and an entrepreneur: a first-time founder is often ten parts offense, zero parts defense, while he calls himself eight parts offense, two parts defense. The bottom line of defense is not going to prison, not going bankrupt, and not boasting about things you can't do. He dislikes the industry using teleoperation videos to pass off as autonomous demos, and requires his team to label every video whether it is fully autonomous. He says pursuing success and preventing failure are two completely different paths; once actions become distorted, the cost of short-term high valuation may be long-term loss of credibility. He is now on his third startup, relying on long-termism rather than going all in.

— Song Yao

Glossary

WAM / World Action Model
A world/action model developed from video generation; Yao Song believes its pixel-generation route is not suitable for real-time edge deployment.
VLA / Vision-Language-Action Model
A model architecture that uniformly processes vision, language, and robot actions.
latent space
A low-dimensional abstract space inside a model; Yao Song uses it to refer to a path that does not generate pixels but only learns physical laws.
egocentric data
Video data collected from a first-person perspective, used to train robot manipulation capabilities.
teleoperation
Remotely controlling a robot; the industry often passes off teleoperation videos as autonomous intelligence.
