Huawei's Ascend chose the hardest road: not compatible with Nvidia

For three or four decades Chinese IT has been a follower. Ascend is Huawei's first product that does not solve a chokehold problem but instead goes voluntarily into uncharted territory, starting at the same time as Nvidia. Xu Zhijun said: anything you do as a follower gets more exhausting the longer you do it.

16:10

Ascend's harshest decision was not being compatible with Nvidia

At Ascend's founding there were two choices. The first was whether AI chips should be specialized or general-purpose; Liao Heng, as chief scientist, judged that AI should be a general-purpose product, and the architecture choice has been proven right today. The second was harder: whether to be compatible with Nvidia. Many domestic chips chose compatibility, with the benefit that Nvidia's existing ecosystem and tools could all be shared. But Xu Zhijun was firm from the start that they had to find another way. Fang Xingdong believes this choice was extremely bold at the time, and that it faced enormous challenges many times along the way — because the entire Chinese IT industry has been a follower industry for three or four decades, and had never developed based on its own root technology.

— Fang Xingdong
19:11

Anything you do as a follower gets more exhausting the longer you do it

Fang Xingdong recalls having dinner with Xu Zhijun three years ago, when Xu Zhijun said, half to himself: Huawei has done this for so many years, and the discovery is that anything you do as a follower always gets more exhausting the longer you do it. There are two kinds of following: in standardized products, Chinese companies can always close the gap and even overtake, but by the time they overtake there is little profit left in the industry — 2G to 5G, PCs, phones all went this way; in non-standardized areas, such as competing with Cisco in routers for so many years, the gap has not closed. In Xu Zhijun's eyes, what Huawei did in the past was also following. So Ascend is special among the six products: the other five solve chokehold problems and address survival first, while Ascend was not choked in 2019 — it is a fully proactive compute product.

— Fang Xingdong
29:16

With process choked off, only system innovation can make it back

Industry chips gain roughly 20% or more performance each generation, mainly from the increase in transistor count driven by advanced process improvements. Huawei lost this premise and can only gain performance through system innovation and breakthroughs — stacked compute, interconnect, higher integration. Fang Xingdong explains with a car metaphor: before, you just had to make the engine stronger and stronger; now the engine is constrained and cannot be enlarged, so you can only make the car run well through architecture, weight reduction, heat dissipation and other factors. Nvidia's solution is to keep working on the engine, because TSMC can still manufacture it. In the interview Liao Heng used the metaphor of an 18-story pagoda: many companies are good at only one layer because of the great division of labor, while Huawei is forced to do manufacturing, ecosystem and chip design all at once.

— Fang Xingdong
34:21

Huawei has to pull the domestic supply chain up with it

Previously Huawei did design, relied on the global supply chain, had TSMC manufacture, could choose the best international partners for other components, directly buy American products and assemble them, sell at a good price with brand premium, and profits were decent. Now it cannot cooperate with foreign partners, TSMC cannot process for it, and domestic processes are still lacking in every respect, so Huawei has no choice but to support and pull up the domestic supply chain — only then does it have a way to live and can its products be manufactured. So the challenge of the past seven years is not only improving its own capabilities but also pulling up the entire supply chain — without that, independent autonomy is impossible. Manufacturing is not Huawei's main business, but it has to help companies like SMIC and Yangtze Memory keep advancing process, yield and quality.

— Fang Xingdong
37:40

Ascend's significance is not that Huawei survived, but that the industry has an engine

In the past, Huawei's product launches and iterations were still based on American root technology and the industrial ecosystem, and the high-value components of a phone were basically foreign. Now Huawei's phones, including Ascend, almost 100% pull up the domestic supply chain. Fang Xingdong believes the greatest significance of the Tao Law is not that Huawei itself can survive and be autonomous, but that China's entire semiconductor industry and entire IT industry have their own engine, which never existed before. Huawei's rapid development represents the rapid development of the entire industrial ecosystem. He also points out that the market, looking at reports, news or public opinion, has not yet realized that we are at this inflection point — people think Nvidia is impressive, and when they mention Huawei they say it is policy that makes everyone use it.

— Fang Xingdong
45:47

It is not a question of difficulty, but of cognition and resolve

Fang Xingdong believes doing communications versus doing IT is essentially not a question of difficulty but of cognition and resolve — whether you dare to do it. The most typical example is the Gauss database: among these six products Ascend stood out, and the least conspicuous may be Gauss, but the database is a half-century-old industry with a relatively stable market, and if it really gets choked off it is deadly. Huawei at the time wanted to acquire or support domestic vendors, but after research found none of them were up to it; domestic companies could only grow in the cracks of American giants, with small scale, weak R&D capability and weak product innovation. So Huawei decided to do it itself, benchmarking Oracle, and starting in 2011 and 2012 invested 1 billion, 2 billion, 3 billion yuan a year, without knowing whether there would be any output in the future.

— Fang Xingdong
1:06:04

The future is two centers, not one replacing the other

Fang Xingdong judges that global high tech moving from a single system centered on the US to two centers is the general trend; politics will not allow a continued single center — the US will not allow it, and China will not allow it either. He believes a single center is itself abnormal, and only two centers can create competition — only with two systems does every consumer, every company, every country, no matter how big or small, have a choice, and not be choked off and forced to pick a side. So the mission of Huawei's six products is to go from being the second choice to being the better choice; it is not a simple substitution relationship with Nvidia, but each has its strengths, they complement each other, and they compete and cooperate. He admits the American system is currently stronger, with its advantage mainly in advanced manufacturing, and that Huawei, under process constraints, relies on the Tao Law not to be left behind; once advanced process is also broken through, it can work both ways at once.

— Fang Xingdong
1:19:11

The tech war won in the short term, at a greater long-term cost

Fang Xingdong believes the US did indeed suppress Huawei through the tech war, but cannot solve the problem in the long term. He reasons: without the national confrontation of 2017 and 2018, Huawei might have been at least one-third of Nvidia, one-third of Apple, and possibly China's largest company by market value; in 2019 Huawei had already surpassed Apple, and its global shipments briefly surpassed Samsung for one quarter. Nvidia today is dominant; if it had been running neck and neck with Huawei in AI chips from the start, it could not have reached today's position. He judges that once the side being attacked builds a self-cycle that does not rely on the outside, and the other side cannot eliminate it even under the harshest conditions, it will be even harder to eliminate later; it will only grow stronger, turning from one monopoly into two competitors.

— Fang Xingdong

Glossary

CANN / Huawei's AI computing framework
Huawei's AI software ecosystem and operator development framework, benchmarked against Nvidia's CUDA.
Tao Law / system-level performance improvement path
When process is constrained, trading architecture, interconnect, packaging and other system optimizations for chip performance gains.
Red Army Blue Army / Huawei's internal adversarial decision mechanism
An internal mechanism that sets up pro and con sides to debate the same decision, used for strategy and solution validation.
Software-hardware co-design / joint chip-model design
Compute companies and model companies communicate in advance about next-generation features and jointly define the product.
