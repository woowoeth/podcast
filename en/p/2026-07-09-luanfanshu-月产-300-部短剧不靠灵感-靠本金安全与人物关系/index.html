Two Ways to Survive in Short Drama: Scale Plays for Principal Safety, Content Plays on Character Relationships

Short drama has two ways of living: the scale play relies on principal safety and the certainty of 300 episodes a month, the content play relies on character relationships to determine motivation; both sides agree AI cannot yet replace a real actor's micro-expressions.

Short-Drama Capital: Why Xi'an and Zhengzhou

Hengdian's backlots were designed for horizontal screens and deep depth of field, so converting them costs more than in Xi'an and Zhengzhou. Short drama was commercially born in Xi'an because at the tail end of the pandemic Xi'an was a tourism city, the real economy needed to recover, and people in entertainment were more eager to embrace short drama. What Zhengzhou and Xi'an share is convenient transport, a population above 13 million, an information-feed gene, lots of cheap labour and people willing to try. Zhengzhou is more cutthroat than Xi'an, its industrial structure is more complex, and even its top companies have more varied names.

— Hou Chao
5:17

300 Episodes a Month Is a City's Capacity Ceiling

At the peak, 200 projects were shooting at the same time. Counting the number of people in key skilled positions in Zhengzhou alone, capacity maxed out at roughly 300 episodes a month. Short drama is a coordination-heavy trade: the more projects, the more coordination pressure multiplies. 300 episodes is the city's carrying limit, and previously no company in the whole industry could manage one or two hundred projects at once. What supports that rhythm is a script IP library, genre tags, high-frequency reuse of the same directors and similar sets and props, and mid-office control.

— Hou Chao
8:33

Paid to Free: Resisted First, Then Validated

The biggest change in going from paid to free is the metric — completion rate replaces the strong hook. Li Jiajia (李佳佳) resisted it at first, felt inferior, thought her method could not keep up with the free rhythm; only when “时间节” was both a paid hit and a free hit did it validate that the character-relationship logic holds on the free side too, a milestone for the team. After that the method shifted from concentrating effort on the first ten episodes to spreading it relatively evenly across every episode, telling the story properly.

— Li Jiajia
11:35

Anti-Typecasting: Character Relationships Are the Cornerstone

The methodology is ‘character relationships determine character motivation, motivation determines events, events determine story’. The core of the hit “苏太太高调离婚了” is that each person is closer to or more distant from different people, so their tone and the information they reveal differ; the details are made solid and typecasting is avoided. A story is unwatchable precisely because of typecasting. Ensemble scenes are the hardest to shoot, because every line each person says must carry a different attitude toward each other person.

— Li Jiajia
33:28

Admit First That You Started a Business to Make Money

One guest admits he did a deep breakdown and concluded his own goal is actually to make money. The company's number-two is a super high-achiever who gave him ‘a small goal’ to keep him making money; he found he could not bring himself to throw away his experience, connections and team, so he kept going to work, making money and making dramas, forming a loop. He also judges that no long-form film/TV company has lasted by doing content well, unless the boss himself is that content person.

48:22

Two Post-Mortem Logics: Fatal Factors vs. Ceiling

In Hou Chao's (侯超) 300-person content team, only two or three people can really put 3-5 million into a single project, so the post-mortem looks only at ‘fatal factors’: genre dividends, whether an ordinary person can pull it off, shooting-scheduling difficulty — the fatal weak spots — and after checking, it is circulated that repeating them is banned. Li Jiajia's cases more often fell short of expectations without dying outright, so she analyses the ceiling from the production side and the topic-selection side; the two sides can complement each other, and the production side often fails to patch the hard flaws on the topic-selection side. A big hit always comes down to the long board; only a drama whose long board is exciting enough can blow up.

51:29

AI and Live Action Are Becoming Two Separate Lines

AI and live action are already two lines: AI is better at presenting spectacles people have not seen but can imagine, such as the Classic of Mountains and Seas, effects, animals; live action suits emotion and relationship-driven content like harem intrigue. A real actor's micro-expressions and a good actor's tension are things AI cannot learn in the short term, and as long as that difference exists, live action has room to survive. The defensive logic on the other side is only to run past the ‘kill line’ — a single episode not losing money is enough, protect the floor and chase the ceiling. AI has also landed in script tools, digital continuity sheets and overload-prevention scheduling, not just content assistance.

1:02:27

Interactive Film-Games: Not Betting on This One

Facing the interactive film-game boom, one guest says that after AI arrived he seriously considered dissolving the company and starting over, because of path dependency. But not doing interactive film-games like “盛世天下” is not because it is hard, but because there is still no genuinely proven demo, no industry consensus and no sound rules, and he is unwilling to be the first to eat the crab. The other adds: the newer the company, the thinner the talent pipeline, and the worst thing is scattered energy — ‘wanting to do everything, getting into nothing’ — the opportunity cost of the boss's and partners' time is too high.

Glossary

RY / return on investment (return multiple)
The metric the short-drama industry uses to judge a project's profitability; an RY above 1 means profit, and Hou Chao uses it to decide to greenlight batches.
DIT / digital imaging technician
The role responsible for on-set footage copying, transcoding and continuity-sheet management; costly and inefficient, and Hou Chao has replaced part of it with AI.
Kill line / break-even line
The break-even point for a single drama; run past the kill line and you can keep going — the minimum survival standard for the batch play.
Golden finger / protagonist-exclusive ability
An exaggerated ability or system unique to the protagonist in web fiction and short drama, such as X-ray vision, a treasure bowl, or stone gambling; a common element of hit genres.
