Keto reversed his son's bipolar; then Roblox's founder split decisions into four layers

David Baszucki lays out Roblox's decision-making operating system in four layers: the body's metabolic state governs major judgment calls, long-term perspective multiplied by fast iteration, general systems beat hard-coding, and when an old model stops working you replace the entire economic system rather than patch it.

Mental illness should be treated as a physical condition, like a broken bone

Baszucki's son was diagnosed with severe bipolar disorder around ten years ago. The family worked through drug after drug and protocol after protocol; doctors told them he would be on five medications for life, and there were repeated hospitalizations along the way. Then a friend who works in metabolic health mentioned offhand, "keto cured my bipolar," and they tried it as a last hope. Three weeks in, his son said, "I feel a little different," and the real person started to come back. That experience led Baszucki to see mental illness as a physical problem no different from a broken bone, and he and his family set up a foundation that has funded research in the area for the past four or five years.

Carbs put you into hibernation; save the big decisions for optimistic mode

He now wears a CGM and watches his blood sugar in real time, eats essentially no refined carbohydrates or sugar, and knows exactly what sends his glucose spiking. What he observed is that after a large carbohydrate load a person drops into a slow, hibernation-like state, while something closer to a fasted state produces the "I want to go do things" drive. He learned to watch himself from the outside — whether he is on or off, in optimistic or pessimistic mode — and then deliberately schedules every major long-term decision for optimistic mode. The goals that look impossible from the trough are the ones that look executable from there.

If you are not cutting bureaucracy, the company is going backwards

He believes bureaucracy accumulates inside a company on its own, like organizational entropy. Inside Roblox the phrase they sometimes use is "destroy bureaucracy," because there is no middle ground: if you are not cutting bureaucracy, the company is going backwards; if you are not improving, you are decaying. So the resistance to that natural drift has to be deliberate, rather than assuming that holding a steady state is safe.

— David Baszucki
8:44

Harsh feedback does not wound people; an aggressive tone does

Whether feedback works turns not on how tough the content is but on the tone. If the other person senses that your blood pressure is rising, that you are calm about it, even severe feedback will not turn into trauma; if the tone carries aggression, the damage exceeds the feedback itself. So he set himself a rule: when provoked, never touch text or email — walk away first. The email that feels great to send is the one you are relieved the next morning you never sent. Saying the same thing on a phone call or in person lands far better, because the thing you are optimizing for is not your own emotional release but the employee's learning and growth.

— David Baszucki
11:19

Shipping the blocky avatar everyone warned against was the right call

Roblox's earliest avatar was placeholder art a programmer threw together: legs 1×1×2, body 2×2×1, a face pieced on top. Everyone from the game industry warned them "do not ship this," and the team internally was unanimously against it too, arguing they needed a high-fidelity model with real musculature. They decided to ship first and iterate on feedback anyway. The industry's advice turned out to be wrong, and the episode settled into a kind of irreverence toward problems: he does not let ready-made industry consensus make the judgment call for him.

— David Baszucki
14:12

A long-term direction needs ultra-fast iteration; either one alone feels wrong

Roblox's values include both "take the long view" and "get stuff done." He describes a magic quadrant: the genuinely magic quadrant is a single decision that satisfies both conditions at once — long-term perspective sets the direction, and you iterate at extreme speed on the path toward it. Every other quadrant feels wrong. When his instinct conflicts with the data, or with how the community feels, he makes the fast decision that can be adjusted incrementally: get the direction right and calibrate as you go. The critical skill is recognizing the moment when adjustment is needed.

— David Baszucki
24:31

Better to open up a language than to build one game into the platform

Co-founder Eric Cassel and Baszucki had a pivotal disagreement in Roblox's early days. Baszucki wanted to write the flag from capture-the-flag directly into the core C++ code as a game template built into the platform; Eric insisted on not hard-coding anything like that, and instead on wiring in a general-purpose language, Lua, and building APIs so the system could generalize to any game. Baszucki did not trust that direction at first, but once it was built the power of the general abstraction went far beyond his hard-coded version, and it became one of the most important engineering foundations of Roblox's UGC ecosystem.

— David Baszucki
29:19

Replacing the whole system beats fixing 30 small problems

Roblox's early membership model started to break down: user numbers kept climbing while paying members actually declined, and revenue flattened and then fell. The team's default reaction was to list 50 small problems and fix 30 of them — and the problem was still there. The real answer was to step outside the current system: they launched an entire new set of systems at once, virtual currency, a creator economy, popularity charts. Within 8 hours of going live, creators immediately began reshaping their content around earnings, and the team knew they had it right. The story is about what to do when an old system is structurally wrong: patching it is only a delay.

Glossary

CGM (continuous glucose monitor)
A small device worn on the body that measures blood sugar continuously, so glucose swings can be read at any moment.
Robux (Roblox's virtual currency)
The platform's in-world currency, priced at roughly 1 cent apiece over the long run.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
A general-purpose interface protocol that lets AI systems connect to external tools and data.
Lua (the Lua programming language)
The lightweight game-creation language Roblox wired in early on.
