After Keto Reversed His Son's Bipolar Disorder, the Roblox Founder Split Decision-Making Into Four Layers

David Baszucki breaks Roblox's decision operating system into four layers: your metabolic state determines major judgments, a long-term view multiplied by fast iteration, general systems over hardcoding, and swapping out an entire economic system when the old model stops working.

Mental illness should be treated as a physical disease, like a broken bone

About ten years ago, Baszucki's son was diagnosed with severe bipolar disorder. They tried every drug and protocol; doctors said he would be on five medications for life, and he was hospitalized multiple times along the way. A friend in the metabolic health field mentioned offhand that ‘keto cured my bipolar,’ and they tried it as a last hope. Three weeks later the son said ‘I feel a little different,’ and the real him began to surface. The experience led him to see mental illness as a physical problem just like a broken bone, and he and his family set up a foundation that has funded related research for the past four or five years.

Carbs put you into hibernation; save big decisions for optimistic mode

He now wears a CGM to watch his blood sugar in real time, largely avoids refined carbs and sugar, and knows what sends his glucose spiking. He has observed that after eating a lot of carbs, people enter a slow, hibernation-like state, while a state closer to fasting brings a ‘I want to go do things’ drive. He has learned to observe from the outside whether he is in an on or off mode, optimistic or pessimistic, and then deliberately puts all major long-term decisions into optimistic mode — goals that look impossible in a trough only seem executable in an optimistic state.

If you're not cutting bureaucracy, the company is going backwards

He believes bureaucracy naturally accumulates inside a company, like organizational entropy. Inside Roblox they sometimes use the phrase ‘destroy bureaucracy,’ because there is no middle ground: if you're not cutting bureaucracy, the company is going backwards; if you're not improving, you're decaying. So you have to consciously resist that natural tendency, rather than assuming you're safe because you've stopped at a steady state.

— David Baszucki
8:44

Harsh feedback doesn't wound people; an aggressive tone does

Whether feedback works depends less on how harsh the content is than on the tone. If the other person senses your blood pressure rising, or senses you're calm, even harsh feedback won't become trauma; if the tone is aggressive, the damage exceeds the feedback itself. So he set a rule for himself: when provoked, never touch text or email — walk away first. That email that feels so good to send, the next morning you'll only be glad you didn't — saying the same thing by phone or in person is far more effective, because the thing you're optimizing isn't your own emotions but the employee's learning and growth.

— David Baszucki
11:19

Everyone said don't ship the blocky avatar; shipping it was right

Roblox's earliest avatar was just placeholder art a programmer rushed out: legs were 1×1×2, the body was 2×2×1, the face was pieced together. Everyone in the games industry warned ‘don't ship this,’ and the team was unanimously against it too, believing they needed a high-fidelity, muscular model. They still decided to ship first and iterate on feedback. It turned out the industry's advice was wrong, and the experience hardened a certain ‘disrespect’ toward the problem: don't let ready-made industry consensus make your judgments for you.

— David Baszucki
14:12

A long-term direction needs ultra-fast iteration; either one alone feels wrong

Roblox's values include both ‘take the long view’ and ‘get things done.’ He proposes a magic quadrant: the real magic quadrant is when a single decision satisfies both conditions at once — a long-term perspective sets the direction, and you iterate ultra-fast on the path toward it. Every other quadrant feels off. When intuition conflicts with data or with how the community feels, he first makes fast decisions that can be adjusted incrementally: get the direction right, then calibrate as you go; the key skill is recognizing the moment when ‘adjustment is needed.’

— David Baszucki
24:31

Rather than build in one game, open up a language

Cofounder Eric Cassel and Baszucki had a key disagreement early at Roblox. Baszucki wanted to write the flag from a capture-the-flag game directly into the core C++ code as a built-in game template for the platform; Eric insisted on not hardcoding it, and instead wiring in a general language, Lua, and building APIs so the system could generalize to any game. Baszucki didn't trust the direction at first, but once it was built, the power of the general abstraction far exceeded his hardcoded version, and it became one of the most important engineering foundations of the Roblox UGC ecosystem.

— David Baszucki
29:19

Fixing 30 small problems is worse than replacing the whole system

Roblox's early membership model began to fail: user numbers climbed steadily, but paying members actually shrank, and revenue flattened or even declined. The team's usual reaction was to list 50 small problems and fix 30 of them — and the problems remained. The real solution was to step outside the current system: launch an entire new system at once — virtual currency, a creator economy, a trending chart and more. Within 8 hours of launch, creators immediately began adjusting their content around earnings, and they knew they'd gotten it right. The story shows that when an old system is structurally wrong, small patches are just delay.

— David Baszucki

Glossary

CGM (continuous glucose monitor)
A small device worn on the body that continuously measures blood sugar, letting you watch glucose fluctuations at any time.
Robux (Roblox virtual currency)
The platform's in-game currency, priced at roughly 1 cent each over the long run.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
A general interface protocol that lets AI systems connect to external tools and data.
Lua (Lua programming language)
The lightweight game-creation language Roblox wired in early on.
