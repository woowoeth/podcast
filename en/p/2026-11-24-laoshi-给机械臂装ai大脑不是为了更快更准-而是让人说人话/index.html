Machines have forced humans to learn their language, not the reverse

Traditional control systems require humans to first learn the machine's language—pilots must understand hundreds of switches, buttons, and procedures to safely operate a plane in exact sequence. But humans don't always give precise instructions when speaking; saying ‘say hello’ doesn't specify whether the arm should swing 30° or 45°—what matters is the intent behind the action. Large language models work by recognizing the shared intent behind different phrasings and translating it into standard instructions the machine can execute.

6:12

Factory-scale production can't afford cloud AI on economic or security grounds

Cloud AI makes complete sense for personal devices, but factories are entirely different. A single semiconductor fab has hundreds or thousands of machines; their operating states, control commands, even camera feeds may contain highly sensitive production data, and transmitting this to public networks may be completely prohibited. Industrial equipment also typically needs 24/7 uninterrupted operation, while cloud AI services can update anytime—even a small model update could disrupt an entire production line, which is why many industrial scenarios keep AI local.

Spatial dataflow architecture is how NPUs cut power and bandwidth costs

The P100's NPU is based on AMD's XDNA2 architecture, whose core feature is spatial dataflow: imagine an assembly line where each station processes its item and passes it directly to the next, rather than sending it back to a central warehouse each time. The NPU's 32 AI engines each have compute and local memory resources, exchanging data directly through on-chip interconnects and reducing demand to move data back and forth from external memory, which significantly boosts energy efficiency. XDNA2 also supports BlockFP16 format, letting a group of numbers share exponent information and further reducing storage and processing costs.

— Lao Shi
11:15

Deploying large models to NPUs requires three transformation steps, never copy-paste

The development flow has three steps. First, use AMD's Quark tool to quantize the model, compressing weights to 4-bit unsigned integers while converting activations to BFP16, drastically reducing data movement while preserving as much model capability as possible. Second, use ONNX Runtime GenAI to convert the quantized model into a standard compute graph supporting KV cache management. Third, then optimize execution for the NPU. In other words, NPUs have their own set of operators, data formats, and runtime requirements—you can't directly apply models as you would on CPU or GPU.

— Lao Shi
13:19

Adding GPU to NPU makes inference slower, not faster

The author originally expected a hybrid mode where prefill runs on the NPU and decode on GPU to perform better by using more compute power, but testing showed this combination was actually slower than pure NPU running the entire inference with Full Fusion. This reveals inference performance isn't determined by compute alone—memory bandwidth, data movement, and scheduling overhead matter equally, so simply adding more computing resources won't automatically speed up inference. For the robotic arm's scenario of short conversations and minimal tool use, Full Fusion was ultimately chosen.

— Lao Shi
14:20

Three safety layers keep AI mistakes from turning into real-world disasters

Connecting a probabilistic AI model prone to hallucinations to equipment that actually moves is dangerous—a single misunderstanding could cause serious damage, like mishearing ‘turn left 20°’ as ‘turn right 200°’. The software design therefore has three safety layers: simple, precise commands are directly recognized by the rules layer and call the control interface without passing through the language model; ambiguous requests (like ‘apologize to the audience’) go to the AI model layer to interpret intent and decide which tool to call; finally, whatever the model generates, the validation layer checks that the tool exists and parameters are within allowable ranges before executing any action.

Glossary

NPU
A compute unit specialized for AI inference, distinct from CPUs and GPUs.
spatial dataflow architecture
Computing units pass data directly between themselves, reducing round-trips to external memory.
BFP16 (Block FP16)
A numeric format where a group of numbers share one exponent, reducing storage and compute costs.
Prefill / Decode
Two stages of large model inference: processing input context and generating output word by word.
Full Fusion
An inference mode where prefill and decode both run on the NPU without moving intermediate data between processors.
