Nvidia's $1.1 trillion market evaporation prices succession void, not scandal

After 34 years Nvidia has no succession plan; board members serve 15 years on average but none has been a current or former S&P 500 CEO; the stock collapse prices the cost of replacing top management, not Huang Renxun's (黄仁勋) personal life.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:02

Four days to lose the only boss

Huang Renxun (黄仁勋) had been the sole CEO since founding Nvidia in 1993. The New York Times revealed late October that he had close ties with Chinese businessman Alice Wang and possibly intelligence activities; Bloomberg and Reuters then reported a second and third relationship. On November 8, Huang admitted to infidelity, the board met overnight, and by Thursday morning he had resigned. No transition plan existed, starkly contrasting with Apple's CEO change this year: announced in April with the entire summer for handoff, and Cook stayed on as chairman.

10:25

Rules ban chips to China, not Chinese people

US rules restrict advanced chips sold to China but do not restrict Chinese companies renting computing power overseas. Megaspeed placed a data center in Malaysia and leased compute to Chinese customers including Alibaba; this offshore rental arrangement is entirely legal. The Biden administration sought to close this loophole in May 2025, but the Trump administration repealed the rule, leaving it intact across two administrations. Within four days of Alice Wang taking control of the company, Nvidia certified it as an official cloud partner.

12:26

Sales staff audit their own customers

Washington feared Megaspeed was reselling chips to China and asked Nvidia to audit itself; Nvidia reported all hardware accounted for. Bloomberg reporters cross-checked Nvidia's figures against Malaysian and Indonesian trade records and found roughly 50,000 chips unaccounted for. Huang told Bloomberg TV that the company investigated every discrepancy and found no diversion; an Nvidia spokesperson called it ‘a growth plan for a startup company in a friendly nation, an opportunity for America, not a danger signal to be feared’. Nvidia claimed in June 2025 it blacklisted Megaspeed internally, but five months later the company still imported approximately $787 million worth of top-tier GB300 chips through Malaysia and remains listed as an official cloud partner.

16:42

Two-ton servers declared as power adapters

Huang had dismissed smuggling concerns by saying ‘servers weigh two tons and don't fit in a backpack’, but the actual method is more bureaucratic: in a case with guilty pleas, restricted Nvidia hardware was declared as ‘adapter modules’—the kind of small power adapter you'd use to plug a hair dryer into a foreign outlet, except these weighed 2,000 pounds. In March this year, Supermicro co-founder Wally Liao was indicted for allegedly running a $2.5 billion smuggling operation, just days after Huang signed a Supermicro server in gold marker: ‘Supermicro is super’.

22:00

Apple took the whole summer to hand off; Nvidia took four days

Around 60 executives reported directly to Huang, far exceeding typical CEO span of control; he also required everyone to write ‘top five things’emails that he read roughly 100 of each day. This system helped him spot the AI opportunity early, but the cost was total company operations orbiting one person, with no succession plan. Compare Apple's CEO change this year: announced the transition in April, spent the whole summer on handoff, and kept Cook as chairman as backup—the Economist commented that Nvidia's succession plan lasted four days.

25:06

The board has a taco seller but no chip expert

Nvidia board members serve roughly 15 years on average, double the S&P 500 norm, yet none has been a current or former S&P 500 CEO; the board includes a former football marketing executive and a former Taco Bell president instead. The company lacks a formal chief compliance officer—the role is shared with the general counsel—yet Nvidia sells one of the world's most heavily regulated products. An institution specializing in board effectiveness ranked Nvidia 609th out of roughly 650 companies.

28:22

The chip seller became the AI industry's shadow central bank

Over the past two years Nvidia not only sold shovels but financed the gold diggers: by end of July it had committed $124 billion in equity investments, completing a new deal every two and a half days on average. Huang's approach was ‘as long as the customer keeps buying chips, terms are negotiable’, while CFO Colette Kress pressed on how these guarantees were priced and what returns they offered. In July, news emerged that Nvidia was considering providing a $250 billion guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio data center; the next day Nvidia stock fell 5% and market cap evaporated roughly $250 billion—the market priced the guarantee almost one-to-one.

33:35

One leather jacket saves not money but decisions

Huang has worn the same black leather jacket for at least 20 years; he told CBS reporters it was to ‘remove three or four choices from daily decisions’. This sartorial logic was imitated by other Silicon Valley figures, fundamentally a person at the power center deliberately dressing like an outsider. The host noted his own jacket worn for 20 years has shoulder armor, because it was designed assuming ‘something could actually go wrong at any moment’—more foresight than Nvidia's board displayed.
