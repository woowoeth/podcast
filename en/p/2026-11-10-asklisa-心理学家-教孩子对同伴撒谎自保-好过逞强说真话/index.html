Most lies aren't born from bad intent

She sorts children's lying into three categories: hiding wrongdoing, avoiding responsibility, or protecting privacy. The privacy type is most often misread by parents—when a child doesn't volunteer details like which park they visited, they're exercising normal developmental autonomy, not lying, but parents interpret it as deception: 'why would you lie about something so small?'

6:12

Teach kids to lie for safety rather than risk harm

At a party, if someone offers weed, teach your kid a safe lie like 'my parents do random drug tests'—it doesn't have to be true. She'd rather kids escape with a kind lie than be pressured into risk because they lack a dignified refusal. Safety first; honesty is a later conversation.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
11:21

What kids firmly believe as true often isn't actually true

When a child sincerely describes events that contradict your reality, don't deny them outright. She says: 'the same event looks different from different angles.' If they persist, play 'the perspective game'—you fully state their case, they fully state yours. Often advances the conversation better than debating facts.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
15:34

Stop asking if they did it—just name what you both know

Quoting child psychologist Haim Ginott: skip the trap question 'were you there?' Just say 'we both know that's not true.' Then ask 'why didn't you tell me'—shift from catching them to understanding the lie.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
19:52

When they say 'I don't know' why they lied, believe them

When kids say 'I don't know why I lied,' they likely mean it—they haven't worked it out themselves. Don't push. Say: 'This is hard, let's talk tomorrow.' Emphasize: 'You're good, lying bothers you too.' Come back the next day.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
23:58

Tie freedom to honesty, not lifetime distrust to a lie

Don't say 'if you lie again I'll never trust you'—it blocks redemption. Instead: link freedom to trustworthiness. A lie means their freedom quota drops now. As they rebuild trust through honesty, freedom returns gradually. Trust is not a death sentence; it rises and falls with their choices.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
26:01

Model honesty yourself before you demand it from your kids

Two checks: Are you honest? Kids sense hypocrisy immediately. Second: have you ever punished honesty or hit harder after a confession? If so, apologize. Tell them your past reactions made it harder for them to tell the truth.

— Dr. Lisa Damour
