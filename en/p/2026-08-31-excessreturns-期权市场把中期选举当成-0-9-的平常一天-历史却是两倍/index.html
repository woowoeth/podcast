Options Market Prices Midterms as a 0.9% Ordinary Day; History Says Twice

Options pricing implies just 0.9% single-day move for the midterms, but 2022 saw 1.8%; 70-80% of PCE components still run above 2%, and the Fed's credibility narrative is crumbling. Markets aren't worried—and the scariest thing is their own complacency.

3:03

Fed's no-forecast shift makes FOMC a coin flip

The backdrop: Warsh has already withdrawn forward guidance. Jack notes that before every past FOMC, markets almost precisely predicted the outcome; this September it's 55/45, nearly a coin flip. That means each meeting will now generate volatility that was previously pre-absorbed, spilling from short-end rates into equities. The other host's prediction is heavier: he bets Fed officials won't survive the first market test—when a crisis hits, letting markets decide will be forgotten, and every past chair has caved under plunges and presidential pressure. The real question isn't today, but when this new framework first meets a crisis.

17:31

Midterm volatility priced at half the historical level

Market-maker Kevin Muir normally doesn't buy short-dated vol—he's made money shorting vol for years. But this time he explicitly suggests buying S&P 500 forward volatility in September. Three reasons: one, seasonality—September historically marks the turn from low to rising vol; two, implied correlation among components sits at historic lows, leaving almost only upside, which will drag index vol higher; three, event risk. Options price a 1.8% single-day move for the 2022 midterms, 1.25% for 2018, and just 0.9% today. He sees Trump's recent actions as laying groundwork for not accepting a loss narrative. Even if he's wrong, sentiment will push prices up between now and election day.

— Kevin Muir
21:40

Inflation breadth is more dangerous than its level

Headline CPI/PCE is just the surface. Ahan shows two layers of data: one, breadth—over the past few months, roughly 70%-80% of PCE components still run above 2% year-over-year; such a broad distribution means price rises aren't from isolated supply shocks. Two, driver decomposition—demand-driven inflation contributes about 3.31 percentage points, supply only 0.38, and the latter is what fluctuates with oil. The recent cooling came almost entirely from supply-side declines; the demand trend remains intact. His view: demand-driven inflation tends to be sticky, leaving limited room for price declines later this year, and oil events will only make the Fed's job harder.

— Ahan Manan
33:31

Fed credibility narrative takes a sharp turn

Ben Hunt's narrative tracking shows Fed credibility volume peaked when Trump threatened Powell, then gradually faded; expectations turned optimistic after Warsh took over. The turning point was the late-July press conference—the rate hold wasn't the point; Warsh's words were seen as just talk, and the Fed-credibility narrative surged within weeks. He reads gold as 1 divided by trust, so August's sharp rally in gold mirrors that loss of trust. Credibility won't be restored by renewed promises; a price must be paid first.

— Ben Hunt
40:01

Fed and Treasury face four ticking bombs

Ben argues the Fed and Treasury must defuse four bombs simultaneously: the Iran war has no easy exit, and oil will keep inflation expectations elevated; the fiscal stimulus from the 'one big beautiful bill' is ebbing, and tariff pressures will return; cracks in insurance and private credit could morph into systemic risk; and the deadliest fourth—long-end rate pressure—if 10- and 30-year yields become uncontrolled, it's over. All four require credible policy responses, but the ammunition—Fed credibility—has just been spent, leaving only verbal suppression of financial conditions. Ben calls it 'jawboning financial repression.'

— Ben Hunt
46:23

No one hedging is the biggest tail risk

On Jackson Hole day, the 0DTE straddle implied move was only about 38 handles, and the near-term term structure sat at 90-day lows. Brent says that in his experience, Fed events embed an event-risk premium in options—a tax buyers pay. But this time the market is compressed to extremely low levels: put open interest is very low, almost no one is buying protection. In this structure, the market just needs one sentence it dislikes: if an unexpected surprise hits, crowded short-vol positions will amplify the one-sided shock. What he observes is a market that has shifted from pricing risk to betting everything is fine.

— Brent Kochuba
54:35

NVIDIA faces a wall of call resistance above

NVIDIA trades around $225-226. Brent's calendarized gamma map shows market participants (mainly hedge funds) have sold a large volume of calls above $250, with $270-275 as the true positioning weight zone before September expiry. The stock can keep rising until main chips reach that zone, but once touched, market-maker hedging will create hard resistance. This isn't a fundamental valuation call; it's the near-term ceiling drawn by options positioning: to go higher, the stock must first absorb that wall of sold calls.

— Brent Kochuba

When authoritative writing is handed to AI

Druckenmiller's WSJ op-ed was detected as 100% AI-generated, and he chose to own it, saying it writes better than he does and captures his views well. Jack's stance: authenticity isn't about whether AI wrote it, but whether the views match the person's consistent expression—Druckenmiller's piece still holds. But the discussion gets thornier: the process of forming an investment view requires writing to clarify thinking. You can have AI polish the prose to perfection, but if you outsource the thinking itself, all you get is a retweet.

Glossary

forward guidance
Central bank communication of future rate paths to guide market expectations; Warsh abandoned it upon taking office, returning markets to uncertainty.
implied correlation
The expected co-movement of index components derived from index options; at historic lows, index volatility tends to be underestimated.
0DTE straddle
A same-day-expiry combination of a call and a put, used to gauge expected single-day move magnitude.
financial repression
Government policies—low rates, yield-curve control, capital controls—that push real rates below inflation as a hidden debt-reduction tool.
calendarized gamma
A heat map of market-maker hedging positions across expiries over time, used to read support and resistance for a stock.
