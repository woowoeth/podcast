Jensen Huang raises forecast: GPU demand visibility through 2027 from $500bn to $1 trillion

Jensen Huang raises last year's demand forecast of $500 billion to at least $1 trillion through 2027—his reasoning: AI compute demand has surged roughly one million times over the past two years, and the bulk of that consumption is shifting from training to inference.

The first 38 minutes and final section are mostly songs and applause—skip those. The middle two and a half hours are dense: Nvidia's full-year business logic, token pricing tiers, and the real rationale behind the Groq acquisition, all worth hearing in full.

1:08:54

Accelerated computing is fundamentally about applications, not chips

Huang says accelerated computing has been misnamed—it's fundamentally about ‘application acceleration’, not chips or systems. Because every industry's algorithms and deployment patterns are entirely different, Nvidia had to become ‘vertically integrated but horizontally open’: mastering each domain's algorithms internally, then opening the tech to all cloud providers and hardware partners to integrate, rather than just selling a generic chip. He calls this the logic that explains Nvidia's entire strategy.

— Jensen Huang
1:23:06

AI-native companies have claimed the largest venture capital allocation in history

AI-native companies that emerged over the past year absorbed $150 billion in venture capital alone, with deal sizes jumping directly from millions to billions—the largest venture allocation in human history. Huang's explanation is straightforward: this is the first time ever that nearly every new company's survival depends on compute and tokens—either generating tokens themselves (like OpenAI or Anthropic) or adding value on top of someone else's tokens. That reality forces capital to sink billions into infrastructure from day one.

— Jensen Huang
1:25:11

Three leaps moved AI from generation to execution

Huang divides the past two years into three inflection points: ChatGPT brought generative AI, transforming compute from ‘retrieval’ to ‘generation’; o1 brought reasoning, letting models reflect on themselves, decompose problems, ground facts in research, and become trustworthy; Claude Code brought agentic capability—the first time requiring AI not to answer ‘what is this’ but to ‘create and execute’: read files, write code, compile, test, iterate. Stacked together, they mark the arrival of the ‘reasoning inflection’—AI must think, act, and read, requiring reasoning at every step, structurally raising compute demand.

— Jensen Huang
1:39:58

Inference is the genuine hard problem—and where the revenue lives

Someone once told Huang ‘inference is simple’; he countered that inference is the ultimate hard problem and directly determines revenue, because tokens are the future commodity. His industry-wide inference benchmarking via semi-analysis shows: tokens per watt (throughput) and inference speed per token (quality) are two opposing dimensions, while a gigawatt data center's 15-year depreciation costs roughly $40 billion—so even a free architecture is ‘not cheap enough’ if it's not efficient enough.

— Jensen Huang
2:01:21

Tokens will stratify into tiers like any commodity

Huang foresees token pricing stratifying like any commodity: free tier, standard tier, then $3–$6 per million tokens, up to premium research-grade at $150 per million. High throughput with slow speed maps to free/cheap tiers; low throughput with extreme speed maps to premium. That's exactly the gap Nvidia's new Groq acquisition fills—low-latency, massive on-chip SRAM. Using Dynamo software, Nvidia orchestrates Vera Rubin and Groq chips, with each handling different market ends: throughput on one side, ultra-low-latency on the other. At the premium tier, throughput improves roughly 35x.

— Jensen Huang
2:26:15

OpenClaw matched thirty years of Linux impact in weeks

Huang calls OpenClaw the most popular open-source project in human history, reaching in weeks what took Linux thirty years. He likens it to an operating system for the age of agents—a universal framework with every element: resource management, tool invocation, task orchestration, sub-agent scheduling, and multimodal I/O. So every company's CEO today must answer a new question: ‘What is your OpenClaw strategy?’ Huang sees this as equivalent in scale to what HTML and Linux meant for the last wave of the internet and open source.

— Jensen Huang
2:34:04

Agents inside enterprise networks are a genuine security problem

Huang spells out that OpenClaw-style agents can access sensitive data, execute code, and communicate outward—together, that theoretically lets them read employee info, supply chains, financial data, and exfiltrate it, which is unacceptable. So Nvidia worked with top security experts to refactor OpenClaw into an enterprise-grade reference architecture, NemoClaw, adding policy integration, network guardrails, and privacy routing to run agents safely inside the enterprise.

— Jensen Huang
2:45:23

Robo-taxis gain a cross-manufacturer coalition

Huang announces four new partners—BYD, Hyundai, Nissan, Geely—joining Nvidia's ‘robo-taxi ready’ platform. Combined with previous partners Mercedes, Toyota, and GM, the coalition produces 18 million vehicles annually. He also announced multi-city cooperation with Uber to bring these robo-taxi-capable vehicles onto its network. He calls this autonomous driving's ‘ChatGPT moment’—the first time the previously repeated autonomous driving tech roadmap gets broad, production-scale carmaker endorsement.

— Jensen Huang
