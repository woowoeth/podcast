It's not a dead cat bounce, the volatility mechanism changed

When asked if this is a dead cat bounce, Altman says no—but not because fundamentals improved. VIX fell from Friday's 40 to 30, which still means normal daily moves near 2%. The S&P fell roughly 7% last week, the worst week since COVID, so large swings both ways should be normal expectations, not anomalies. This reframes whether the bounce is credible as a pure volatility mechanics question, not a narrative one.

— Alexander Altman
7:12

Friday's selloff looked exactly like the COVID moment

Altman describes Friday's last two hours of S&P volatility as strikingly similar to COVID's pattern. Behind it: roughly $35 billion in leveraged ETF selling, plus Thursday being the 58th worst trading day for liquidity in the past decade. Dealers held short gamma positions near S&P 7100; when the index broke that level, it triggered more delta selling, a negative feedback loop. This, not fundamental panic, is the true mechanism of Friday's crash.

— Alexander Altman
10:13

Jensen Huang's return cannot answer any real questions

Altman is direct: in August, their case for being bearish on AI had two parts—how will funding materialize, and can revenue track the trajectory after so much capital deployed so quickly. Those questions had no answers then and have no answers now. Jensen's return changes nothing here. The quantitative model has turned to bullish signals, worthy of tactical buying, but that's just a technical judgment that risk asymmetry has shifted. The fundamental questions haven't budged.

— Alexander Altman
13:31

Trump administration stepped in to prop markets, complicating traders' bets

On whether the White House is backstopping Jensen and AI trades, Altman compares this kind of political volatility to tariffs: sentiment can flip ‘on a dime or a tweet or a Truth Social post’. Making year-end bets on a fragile narrative is tricky. But the data tells a story: 35% of US household net wealth is stocks, two-thirds of households own stocks—the highest equity exposure for households since the late 1920s. That explains why the government has strong motive to prop this up.

— Alexander Altman
22:55

Fed December rate hike is priced in, but language carries the risk

The market's sensitivity isn't about whether the Fed hikes in December—that's priced in. The risk is whether Fed language reveals ‘obliviousness’ to the broader funding environment. Altman points to datacenter bonds like Coreweave, whose spreads haven't tightened; SpaceX's recent bond issuance proves higher financing costs are real. This thread runs straight back to whether AI buildout can keep accessing capital.

— Alexander Altman
24:59

Having the 2018 tightening warning's author repeat the mistake: ironic

Altman draws a parallel to December 2018, when Druckenmiller and Kevin Walsh co-authored a warning against excess tightening. The irony: if the FOMC ignores market internals and tightens anyway, the person who warned about that mistake could be repeating it. If the Fed is completely out of sync with market signals, then this bounce truly is a dead cat bounce.

— Alexander Altman
27:01

Semiconductor stocks' crash is itself the most important new data point

Asked whether the past three weeks brought new data bolstering or undermining overbuild fears, Altman's answer is: price is data. Semiconductors fell 30% to 40%; SK Hynix trades at an extremely compressed forward P/E. This deleveraging cleared out bad sentiment—positions like 3x longs on Hynix, 3x shorts on Nvidia. Bad assets leaving the system itself supports the bounce, even if the valuation floor could go lower.

— Alexander Altman
33:13

Trump's deep governance involvement leaves behind political land mines

Here's a new tension: Washington is explicitly backing Jensen and Nvidia's recovery. Normally you don't want to oppose the Fed or the government. But many view Trump as a lame duck. His deep involvement in a major US company's governance becomes a glaring target for Democratic investigation—especially if Democrats take the White House in 2028. This political risk remains completely unresolved.

— Joe Weisenthal
