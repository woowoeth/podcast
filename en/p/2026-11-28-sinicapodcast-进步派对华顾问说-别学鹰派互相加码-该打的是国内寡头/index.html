Progressive Advisor: Stop Out-Hawking the Hawks; the Real Fight Is Domestic Oligarchs

Trump's unexpected China détente has put Democratic progressives and the president on the same side; but advisor Matt Duss warns the genuine danger isn't excessive restraint on China, but the establishment using ‘the China threat’to dodge accountability for tech oligarchs like Nvidia and Anthropic.

Even Trump's critics found themselves agreeing with him on China

Host Caesar frankly admits this is something he never thought he'd say: on China policy, he now actually aligns more closely with Trump than most of Trump's congressional opponents. Guest Sam agrees the feeling is bizarre, almost like self-censorship—admitting presidential détente has become ‘politically incorrect’. Both stress they arrived here for entirely different reasons than Trump, but their conclusions converge.

12:31

Beijing's real strength is constraining capital, not ideology

Matt Duss notes even Bernie Sanders admits China is ‘more capitalist than he is,’but China's system deserves attention not for its ideological label but because it unabashedly constrains capital. He sees an important post-2016 American political shift: recognizing that neoliberalism's ‘capital constrains state’logic is backwards—it should be the state constraining capital, which China actually does, though its methods shouldn't be copied wholesale. This extends to tech oligarch regulation: China heavily regulates tech, yet innovation surges regardless.

— Matt Duss
19:53

Three Democratic factions pursue fundamentally different China strategies

Duss divides Democratic China strategy into three camps: first, the ‘strategic competition’faction following the Biden administration and Jack Sullivan's 2023 Brookings speech line, which nominally leaves neoliberalism behind but actually wants to rebuild domestic political consensus through the ‘China threat’; second, what he calls ‘restrained curiosity’—restraint advocates who support strategic restraint while still valuing human rights; third, antitrust advocates (like Rush Doshi and Matt Stoller's wing), whose consensus isn't specifically about China but against all concentration of power, whether American oligarchs monopolizing domestic economic and political life or China controlling the supply chains America depends on.

— Matt Duss
33:56

Sanders has always doubted the prevailing consensus on China

Duss traces Sanders' arc: in 2000, he opposed granting China Permanent Normal Trade Relations (PNTR), when Washington's consensus was ‘let capital flow freely and China will naturally democratize’. Sanders has always been ‘calling bullshit’on that. His 2021 Foreign Affairs piece against a new cold war applied the same skeptical spirit to puncture a different emerging consensus—that the U.S. and China are destined to be locked in competition or even conflict over world hegemony. Duss sees the same logical thread running through both doubts.

— Matt Duss
38:54

Nvidia and Anthropic split the hawks over how to wield monopoly power

Sam notes the hawk consensus that briefly fractured over Nvidia was already internally divided: on one side, those prioritizing chip export controls and insisting Nvidia's interests must be protected; on the other, AI companies like Anthropic that constantly invoke ‘China is an existential threat’to block regulation. Duss responds: the core split is whether you believe these drug-fueled evil nerds must be allowed to grow wildly unchecked to win the supposed AI race. He finds this logic absurd, especially since Chinese AI firms—operating in more fragmented, competitive, and heavily regulated environments—are actually developing faster.

— Matt Duss/Sam
44:54

Trump's corruption is not new; his honesty about it is

On Trump's family openly declaring they'll be among America's wealthiest by the end of his second term, Duss says this naked corruption long predates Trump—his ‘genius’is merely stating aloud what should stay hidden. Previous politicians felt some shame about corruption; Trump simply announces he's here to make money. Duss sees an opportunity: this lets America openly discuss how campaign finance is fundamentally legalized bribery.

— Matt Duss
55:42

Taking time to think before answering about war is wisdom

Asked about AOC's long pause when questioned about the Taiwan Strait at the Munich Security Conference, Duss—who was there—says there's nothing wrong with it. Congress has too many people spouting off about war without thinking; that's irresponsible. AOC's eventual answer was right: pursue ‘de-escalation’rather than conflict. He'd only suggest trimming those one or two seconds of silence, but the answer itself was sound.

— Matt Duss
1:00:54

The antidote to China-hawk rhetoric is naming the actual problem

Faced with Sam's ‘Democratic dilemma’—the security establishment insisting China is an existential threat versus voters who (polls show) won't abandon tech regulation to ‘win the AI race against China’—Duss proposes: focus on the actual enemy. What hollowed out the rust belt and outsourced jobs overseas isn't China; it's U.S. politicians bought by corporations and the corporations themselves. Trying to defeat China by propping up these oligarchs is absurd on its face.

— Matt Duss
