How Porn Sites Learned to Create Addictive Content Using Click Data—Before Big Tech

In the 1990s, pornographic content pioneered free access, user-generated content, and data-driven creation; this logic of ‘reverse-engineering content from clicks’ later became the underlying template for the entire internet platform economy.

Free pornography started with stolen content

The shift from paid DVDs and magazines to free online content wasn't driven by traditional porn moguls but by early programmers and website administrators. Most of this content was stolen without performers' consent, and it was these same technologists—the first to explore how to monetize the internet through ads and traffic—not the porn industry itself, who built the first successful ad-driven business model around it.

— Patricia Nilsson
7:17

Porn sites invented livestreaming and online payments before Big Tech

Many internet technologies taken for granted were first pioneered in the porn industry: mid-1990s camming sites essentially invented real-time video interaction; early online payment systems also relied heavily on sex worker and pornography transactions—a former Visa executive she interviewed disclosed that at the time, as much as 10% to 15% of online transactions were sex-related.

— Patricia Nilsson
9:27

Mobile and ad-blocking forced data-driven creation into existence

Around 2010, as users migrated to mobile and started using ad-blockers, the model of pulling new users with banner ads collapsed. Platforms had to pivot to studying user behavior data to improve retention. By 2019, Pornhub's parent company was recognized as the industry leader in ‘data-driven creation’—reverse-engineering content from user clicks, dwell time, and return visits to produce the most addictive possible material. This model was later adopted by Amazon, Spotify, and every major content platform.

— Patricia Nilsson
12:36

Algorithms favor extreme content; political polarization operates on the same principle

Infinite-scroll porn sites chase intensity and novelty, so niche BDSM, humiliation, and domination content gradually became ‘mainstream’ pornography—now visible in young people's actual sexual behavior. The author further argues that understanding political polarization requires the same logic: the domination and humiliation narratives around Andrew Tate and even Trump are exactly the material that algorithms have already tested and verified in pornography as most triggering to users.

— Patricia Nilsson
17:51

Prague's porn industry runs on undocumented migrant women

Before the war, many female performers in Prague's humiliation porn came from Russia and Ukraine, where pornography production is illegal. They were flown to Prague under the guise of ‘tourism’ but couldn't report crimes because they lacked EU work permits. The author emphasizes that once discussion becomes purely about ‘content’, it's easy to forget the unprotected real people behind the screen.

— Patricia Nilsson
22:58

OnlyFans customers demand self-harm to prove authenticity

Multiple OnlyFans and livestream performers the author interviewed mentioned that customers had asked them to self-harm. One performer believed customers wanted something that would ‘leave marks’—scars persist on the body after the camera stops, and that permanence made customers feel the interaction was ‘real’ rather than just performance.

— Patricia Nilsson
29:18

OnlyFans sex chat is actually outsourced to ghost writers

Performers can't chat with hundreds of thousands of followers simultaneously, so the ‘flirting’ work is outsourced to chat workers in places like the Philippines at as low as $1 per hour, with compensation based on tips or content purchases they generate. Even when users know it might not be the performer, the interaction still ‘feels more real’, and the author argues that this trained parasocial intimacy is exactly why people so easily form emotional dependencies with AI chatbots.

— Patricia Nilsson
