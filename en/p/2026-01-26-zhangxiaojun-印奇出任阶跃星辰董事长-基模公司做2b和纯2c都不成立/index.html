Yin Qi Takes Over as Chairman of StepFun: A Foundation Model Company Can't Make It in 2B or Pure 2C

A foundation model burns at least 3 billion RMB a year and must be sustained for three to five years, so neither 2B nor pure consumer software adds up; StepFun's bet is foundation model plus devices, trading software-hardware integration for a seat at the table.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

17:13

A foundation model company can't make it in 2B or pure 2C

Yin Qi uses elimination to explain the business model: doing foundation models means at least 3 billion RMB or more in annual investment, sustained for three to five years, with basic R&D investment at least on the order of 10 billion RMB. Under this premise, doing 2B at the same time doesn't hold, because 2B monetization cycles are long and the ceiling on revenue and profit is limited; doing pure consumer software applications at the same time also doesn't hold, because that is the domain where existing internet giants excel—they have users, data and a data flywheel. So StepFun chose a consumer scenario leaning toward software-hardware integration, doing some 2B services in the middle, ultimately landing on AI plus devices.

— Yin Qi
25:20

StepFun's bet is foundation model plus devices

Yin Qi splits the differentiation of large model companies into two layers: the first layer is whether to do foundation models or not, which is the biggest bet, and it will be hard to persist for the next three years; the second layer is the commercialization path. StepFun's path is to use devices to pull the model, because models are not better the bigger they are—Gemini Flash also proved this—efficiency, cost and specialized capabilities matter equally. So StepFun doesn't obsess over topping a single benchmark, but focuses on whether the model architecture fits future device scenarios.

— Yin Qi
40:29

Coding is the best reinforcement learning environment, but you can't collect money

Yin Qi says that Chinese large model companies are basically all grinding on coding now. Coding is valuable from the perspective of improving intelligence, because coding is a scenario with results, where right and wrong can be judged, and it is complex—the best reinforcement learning environment. But coding itself has very limited value from a commercialization perspective: the giants do well in this area, open source has DeepSeek pulling the water level very high, aggressive competition from giants will drive prices very low, and coding is closely related to its downstream environment. So this track, like Chatbot, has no meaning for new companies.

— Yin Qi
42:29

Chatbot is not a long-term product form

Yin Qi believes Chatbot is a阶段性 achievement, and may not even be at the level of search. The reason is that its interaction is very unnatural: putting AIGC capabilities in a dialog box has factually not been very successful. He judges that replacing search with AI large models is a certainty, but what the product form is is still unclear. Chatbot may be a阶段性 product overseas, but its moat is not solid—Gemini's daily active users have risen very fast, and now no one has a particularly large barrier.

— Yin Qi
49:30

Autonomous driving will be highly concentrated, cockpits will return to car companies' in-house development

Yin Qi judges that autonomous driving and cockpits are different: cockpits are highly personalized, related to human-machine interaction experience, and have much room for value-added services in the future, so the proportion of in-house development by car companies will be high; autonomous driving is a safety component, and will return to the hands of Tier 1s like the safety components of Bosch back then. Because profit margins are not high and R&D investment is very high, the space to charge per vehicle is limited, so scale is a must, and ultimately there will be around three core suppliers—Huawei will certainly be one, and there may be two other comprehensive suppliers, plus suppliers needed by small and medium-sized car companies; it may not be two plus one or two plus two.

— Yin Qi
1:14:38

Smart people are not scarce, those who persist long-term are

Yin Qi says that among the 40 people Megvii benchmarked against back then, there were a bunch of OI gold medals, IPHO gold medals, math gold medals, but smartness falls into two categories: one can do team collaboration, the other is strong on their own but hard to collaborate with. He values smart people who can persist long-term more, because smart people have many choices, and being smart gives you many shortcuts—unless there is a mission to complete, why not take the shortcut. The second point is to use the correct dumb method to do one thing long-term; smart people tend to want to use shortcuts, which actually makes it easy to go wrong. So he thinks being smart is not that important; you also need business sensitivity and wisdom.

— Yin Qi
1:19:44

AI organizations must fuse top down and bottom up

Yin Qi believes that each generation of enterprises is the most leading organizational form at that stage, and stands out by adapting to the technology and business of the time. From manufacturing to ICT it is generally top down, internet companies are overall bottom up, and the AI system needs a fusion of both: because any major project is a multi project, consuming huge resources, requiring concentrated effort, but at the same time the organizational culture and talent tolerance must have bottom up vitality. He also judges that future AI leading companies may also have to be ten or twenty thousand people, but ten or twenty thousand people with extremely high talent density.

— Yin Qi
1:39:59

The winning hand is technology that holds up plus applications that close the loop

Yin Qi gives a math problem: technology R&D starts at 3 billion a year, he predicts 3 to 5 billion, and if you look at five years that's a baseline investment of 15 billion; conversely, after three to five years, can there be a core application that gives you the possibility of 3 to 5 billion in profit per year. Both things must be satisfied to have a chance to reach that stage. He also says that China's 2B market is essentially a 2G market, covered outward from the government and central state-owned enterprises, and small and medium B is more like C, so in China it cannot be 2B.

— Yin Qi
