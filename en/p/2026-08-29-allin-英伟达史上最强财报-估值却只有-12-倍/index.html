Nvidia's Best Quarter Ever, and a 12x Multiple

The market isn't doubting $60B in net income — it's betting competition is coming. The most profitable quarter in history paired with the cheapest valuation in history is the most counterintuitive thing in this episode.

9:03

The best earnings ever come with the cheapest valuation ever

Nvidia did $96.2B in revenue this quarter, more than double year over year, against Wall Street expectations of just $92B. More important is the guidance — 70% growth next year, when the market had only expected 45%. Net income of $60B makes it the most profitable core-business quarter of any public company in history. Yet even after the stock rose 8% that day, Nvidia trades at just 12x earnings. The reason isn't that the market disbelieves the numbers — it's that the market believes competition is coming. That combination is the most counterintuitive thing in this episode: the strongest earnings ever, paired with the cheapest valuation ever.

— Jason Calacanis
12:05

Stage two of AI is giving the brain hands and eyes

Chamath splits AI into stages: stage one is the model, essentially a brain; stage two is the harness, i.e. the agent, which amounts to giving that brain eyes, hands, a notepad and a keyboard. But he argues stage two isn't enough — what enterprises are really missing is making that autonomous agent more informed and more contextual, so it can actually do the work of a lawyer, a support rep, a salesperson. Whoever owns that context owns the next stage. That's the underlying logic behind his May call that Salesforce was oversold and his insistence on staying long.

— Chamath Palihapitiya
21:11

Agents are Salesforce's power users

Benioff himself raised an underrated point: Salesforce is full of ‘trap value’ — the platform's features are so vast that ordinary users can never find them, but an agent can. The agent will proactively tell the user ‘I see these actions in Salesforce that match what you asked for, want me to run them’, and once the user clicks ‘always allow’, the agent can unlock the full power of Salesforce. That means enterprises no longer need to hire a fleet of expensive consultants to figure Salesforce out. Freeberg's read: Benioff knows full well that wiring Salesforce into Claude carries disintermediation risk, and still hands over the customer relationship voluntarily, because he knows what customers want is to build agents inside AI and let those agents run across every SaaS platform.

— David Friedberg
27:16

The SaaS apocalypse is a product of naive extrapolation

Freeberg reduces this whole narrative cycle to one error: naive extrapolation. The SaaS apocalypse argument runs ‘AI agents will write code, therefore all software goes to zero’; the jobs apocalypse argument runs ‘AI can do knowledge work, therefore all knowledge workers lose their jobs’. Both ignore that people can turn around and use the tools. His conclusion is blunt: those who adopt the technology live, those who don't die. He also notes that Leopold's SaaS short may not be a judgment problem but a leverage problem — to get enough leverage, you have to short the most liquid large caps.

— David Friedberg
30:16

Nvidia is grabbing the distribution layer of open source

Nvidia reportedly acquired Hugging Face for $12B and did an acqui-hire of Poolside for $6B, roughly $20B combined, less than 1% of Nvidia's market cap. Chamath reads it as Jensen hitting back after being provoked by Sam Altman's AMD deal and by in-house inference chips like ‘jalapeno’: he wants to own the distribution layer of open source. The bigger point is that the boundary between customer and supplier is melting — hyperscalers build their own chips, so Nvidia builds its own models, runs a cloud, serves APIs, and eventually every company will do everything.

— Chamath Palihapitiya
39:21

Bessent can't save the long end

Over the next 12 months the US federal government has to refinance $10 trillion of maturing debt, which means going back to the Treasury market to sell new bonds and borrow new money, and borrowing new money costs more, which makes the deficit bigger. So the speaker argues Bessent cannot meaningfully affect the long end no matter what he does — the most buying he can muster is $1 trillion, while he still has to sell $10 trillion. The point of Druck's memo isn't prediction, it's cover for Bessent: push the blame back onto Congress and the White House, and say this isn't the Treasury secretary's job, it's the people who control the budget who have to cut spending.

44:27

30-year at 6% is where the death spiral starts

The speaker's threshold call: if you see the 30-year hit 6%, that's the start of the death spiral. Not an immediate explosion, no reason to panic, but the beginning of extreme pain, and that pain lasts for years. The only fix is getting the budget under control, and that's an act of Congress, and it has to happen, this year or next, it's only a matter of time. Sacks adds that nobody controls spending: 435 representatives and 100 senators each have a vote, plus the president, and no single one of them controls spending — the power of the purse originates in the House, and the president has no line item veto.

1:22:56

The cancer vaccine is a process, not a new drug

Moderna went from a $20B market cap to $60B in two weeks on positive data for a so-called cancer vaccine. But Freeberg points out this neo antigen immunotherapy has existed since the late 1990s: take a tumor sample, sequence its DNA, find the mutations unique to that cancer, then build proteins or mRNA from them and inject them to activate immune cells to attack. Melanoma is the biggest target because UV-induced mutations are distinctive enough. It's more a process than a traditional drug, and the process is customized per person. What bothers him most is that everyone treats this as a one-of-a-kind breakthrough — people have already gone to Montana, printed peptides from their own cancer sequence, made their own neo antigens, injected them and been cured; today you can pay $50,000 to have someone do it, plenty of clinics do it, and it isn't an FDA-approved drug, while Moderna plans to charge $500,000 for the same process.

— Freeberg

Glossary

harness
The layer that wires a model to tools, memory and an operating interface, so the brain can actually act.
acqui-hire
An acquisition made mainly to get the team rather than the product.
yield curve suppression
Government bond buying that pushes bond prices up and implied yields down, lowering the cost of new issuance.
neo antigen
A unique protein fragment produced by a tumor's DNA mutations, specific to that cancer, that the immune system can recognize.
CAR-T
T cells taken from a patient, reprogrammed and reinfused to hunt down proteins unique to the cancer.
