Time Doesn't Exist, and the Universe Didn't Come from Nothing

Cosmologist Brian Keating makes a counterintuitive case: time may not exist, and the universe didn't arise from nothing. He also warns that AI can't replicate human intuition and can't be fully controlled.

0:00

Time doesn't add up, maybe because it doesn't exist

Keating argues that physics can explain space, matter, and energy, but not why time behaves as it does—possibly because time doesn't exist at all. He quotes Frank Wilczek's definition, "Time is what clocks measure," calling it a tautology. The real question he studies is "What happened on the Sunday before the Big Bang?"—how time emerged from nothing.

— Brian Keating
18:55

The universe didn't come from nothing: equations don't instantiate things

Keating directly refutes Lawrence Krauss's claim that the universe came from nothing as nonsense. Equations describe things but don't instantiate them. He asks what existed before the Big Bang—perhaps a previous universe or an eternal energy field, but definitely not "nothing." This statement is the key to understanding his entire argument.

— Brian Keating
41:27

Government canceled the South Pole array; only one similar experiment remains, in Chile

With the cancellation of the BICEP array in Antarctica by the U.S. government, the Simons Observatory at 5,200 meters altitude in Chile is now the only project of its kind still collecting data. It generates about 1TB of data per day, analyzed by 450 scientists, and is powered by solar panels for the first time. Keating's team is using it to make a decisive measurement of the cosmological time field.

— Brian Keating
46:34

Detecting primordial gravitational waves would rule out all other universe models

If primordial gravitational waves are detected, it would strongly support inflation theory while excluding all other models, such as an eternal universe, cyclic universe, eventual evaporation, or black hole origins. Keating stresses this isn't a final proof, but like Newton's laws being sufficient to guide moon landings, such evidence would still be powerful.

— Brian Keating
1:08:04

Lawmakers claim non-human beings exist but never show evidence

Keating criticizes the UAP field for its extremely low information environment. Despite authoritative figures like Congresswoman Luna claiming non-human beings exist, no physical evidence has ever been presented. He argues that if real evidence existed, it would have been made public by now; otherwise, it's a waste of public attention.

— Brian Keating
1:14:10

AI has been trained to be an apology machine, internalizing self-denigration

Responding to Peter's concerns about distrust in the state and science, Keating argues that Britain has contributed enormously to the Earth, yet Britons leave. He criticizes judging Churchill and Newton by today's standards and points out that AI has been trained to be an "apology machine," refusing to answer certain physics questions. He insists we must stop apologizing for human greatness, or AI will internalize self-denigration.

— Brian Keating
1:18:17

LLMs are just matrix multiplication; they can't invent chess

Keating explains that LLMs are based on matrix multiplication (linear algebra), not advanced mathematics; GPUs were originally optimized for gaming, not superintelligence. He gives examples: LLMs can't invent chess or the Rubik's cube; they only build on existing human data. He cites Jevons' paradox: the more AI is used, the lower the cost but the higher the usage. LLMs can't train themselves—they degrade like mad cow disease—and need new human data.

— Brian Keating
1:25:22

The real risk isn't AI running wild; it's that we can't live without it

Keating raises two major concerns: we don't understand how AI works, and we can't predict or control it. He references Turing's halting problem as an analogy for AI's unpredictability. His bigger worry is that if the AI bubble bursts (e.g., OpenAI losing $10 billion a month), humanity would be paralyzed by over-reliance.

— Brian Keating

Glossary

BICEP Array
A cosmic microwave background polarization experiment in Antarctica, searching for primordial gravitational wave imprints from inflation.
Simons Observatory
A cosmology experiment on the Atacama plateau in Chile, measuring the polarization and temperature of the cosmic microwave background.
Jevons Paradox
Technological progress that increases efficiency may instead lead to a rise in total resource consumption.
UAP
The official term replacing UFO, covering unidentified phenomena in the air and across mediums.
Cosmological Time Field
A hypothetical field that encodes the rate of time flow and can be observed, providing a mechanism for the arrow of time.
