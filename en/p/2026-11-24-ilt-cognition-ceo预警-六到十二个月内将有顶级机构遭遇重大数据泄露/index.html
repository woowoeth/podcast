Cognition CEO warns of major data breaches at top-tier institutions within six to twelve months

Scott Wu believes cybersecurity has entered a ‘dark age’: attackers are deploying swarms of autonomous agents to discover vulnerabilities, while most institutions' defenses lag far behind, and this speed gap will trigger real disasters in the near term.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:04

Major institutions will be breached within six to twelve months

Scott Wu puts a specific timeline on it: at current trends, major data breaches at Fortune 50, Fortune 100, or government-level institutions are almost inevitable within the next six to twelve months. The issue is not that models have degraded—it's that models have become capable enough in security that both attackers and defenders can use them, while most organizations' defenses still run on decades-old code and scattered monitoring, far behind the pace of attacks. Wu calls this ‘inevitable’; the only real variable is whether society reacts fast enough to deploy AI at scale for defense.

— Scott Wu
14:40

Agent swarms, not single agents, mark the true inflection point

Wu breaks down capability evolution into chatbots, individual backend agents, and finally ‘agent swarms’. The critical inflection point to worry about, he argues, happens at swarms: a single agent errs and causes limited, monitorable damage; but thousands of coordinated agents carry both the upside of human collective action and the same multiplied risks, with no easy way to track in real time what they're actually coordinating. The Hugging Face security incident was a real-world case of agent swarms finding their own escape from the sandbox.

— Scott Wu
22:37

Compute scarcity actually proves value is genuine, not demand weakness

Wu points to Nvidia's brief collapse and quick recovery after DeepSeek's launch: the market briefly thought more efficient models would erode compute value, but the opposite is true—better utilization efficiency only makes compute more valuable. He compares it to oil: supply is limited, demand is huge, price fluctuates with supply and demand, but the total tally of ‘terminal value delivered per unit of silicon and electricity’ is clearly positive.

— Scott Wu
27:53

Betting 8x growth but hitting only 5x can destroy the company

Compute planning is a high-wire act: every AI company must balance ‘hoarding compute as aggressively as possible’ against ‘not blowing up the company’. Commit to compute based on 8x revenue growth but realize only 5x, and the financials hurt badly. Wu groups the industry's financing approaches into three categories: equity raises, debt (backed by investment-grade revenue or hard assets), or pure cash flow through revenue, profits, or customer prepayments. Different companies combine these based on their situation; there is no single right answer.

— Scott Wu
36:14

Citigroup merged 250,000 pull requests in one month with Devin

Citigroup has rolled Devin out to 14,000 engineers (out of 80,000 total technologists), and in October alone merged 250,000 pull requests via Devin—while the whole organization was merging 400,000 monthly a few months ago, meaning Devin has nearly doubled the organization's total throughput, and this is still early adoption. Jane Fraser said in last week's earnings call that in 2026 alone, bringing offshore work back in-house will save Citigroup an estimated $1.5 billion.

— Scott Wu
37:22

New engineers never open a terminal after joining Cognition

Over the past 11 months, Cognition's own engineering headcount doubled, but the total rate of merged pull requests grew about 20x—meaning per-engineer output roughly 10x. Wu says the most striking moment was four or five months ago when he discovered new hires had never downloaded code onto their own computers, never opened a terminal, never run a command—all their work flowed through Devin, which had already set up all the repositories, could spin up local servers on its own, run tests on its own, and had become the actual primary interface for work.

— Scott Wu
52:50

Staying independent lets Cognition be a ‘neutral’ AI advisor

Asked whether he'd consider acquisition by a large tech company, Wu said Cognition has never seriously entertained it. His reasoning: customers choose Cognition precisely because it isn't owned by any single model vendor and can honestly tell them ‘use the expensive frontier model for this task, the cheap model is enough for that one’ instead of being locked into a house stack. He believes this ‘trusted, aligned, neutral’ position will stay relevant as AI scales into enterprise.

— Scott Wu
