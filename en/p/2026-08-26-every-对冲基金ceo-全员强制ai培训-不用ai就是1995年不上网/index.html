Not Using AI Is Like Refusing the Internet in 1995: A Hedge Fund's Mandatory Training

Walleye's CEO mandates AI for all staff, saying not using AI is like refusing the internet in 1995. AI is leverage, not cheating; future companies will have mostly AI robots as employees.

AI transformationhedge fundmandatory trainingdata strategyquant trading
A hedge fund CEO managing nearly $10 billion personally drives an all-staff AI transformation, offering concrete practices and his mental journey—directly instructive for managers.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:08

If disrupted by AI, the blame lies with you, not the technology

Will says he was originally an engineer and math PhD, long using statistics and AI. In recent years, tools have become more powerful and more accessible to non-technical people. His curiosity led him to notice efficiency gains, and he felt a responsibility: if he were disrupted by AI, it would be his fault. He started internal AI projects two years ago; this is not a future story.

— Will
7:08

The internal AI product began with an analyst wanting to replace himself

In March 2023, a TMT analyst used GPT-3 to build a tool to boost efficiency, attempting to replace his own analyst job. Will was initially skeptical, but after a demo, he was convinced of the direction. That project became the internal AI product Current, used for fundamental investment analysis by all teams, with usage spiking during earnings season.

— Will
10:10

AI training cannot be voluntary; it must be mandatory for everyone

After listening to Chris Saka's podcast, Will decided to make AI training mandatory for all staff, regardless of department or technical background. He sent a company-wide email with the subject 'AI at Walleye: A Challenge to All of Us', saying that not using AI is like refusing the internet in 1995, and that as a hedge fund, ignoring the tool would be a disgrace.

— Will
16:11

Treating using AI to write emails as cheating is an academic mindset

Will emphasizes that using AI to write emails is not cheating; that is an academic notion. In business, AI makes you more efficient, like hiring an assistant. He encourages employees to manage AI like employees; in the future, everyone will manage AI robots. He opposes ignoring tools because they are imperfect; one should embrace and iterate.

— Will
22:16

Quant has long used AI; the new addition is reading unstructured data

Will says quant trading has used AI for years, and recent LLMs have improved the ability to process unstructured data for sentiment analysis. 75% of employees use ChatGPT weekly, and a third use AI coding tools like Windsurf. The internal product Current is indispensable for fundamental investing.

— Will
27:22

All companies should get used to meetings being fully recorded

Will says the company records nearly all meetings and calls, stores them in a data lake, and uses LLMs to process them for insights and predictions. He calls it 'Borg', from Star Trek, with the goal of integrating all company information to form collective intelligence. He believes all companies should get used to data being recorded.

— Will
33:32

AI saves not thinking but phrasing

Responding to Dan's concerns about AI writing, Will says the tool does not negate the need for thinking; rather, it frees up time for thinking. He distinguishes between concepts and linguistic syntax: AI handles the latter, humans focus on the former. He warns against fully outsourcing your brain and advises finding the optimal point of human-machine collaboration.

— Will
40:38

In future world-class companies, most employees will be machines

Will cites the technological upheaval from the Civil War to World War I, arguing that AI change will be faster. He predicts future world-class companies will still need thousands of employees, but most will be machines. He admits to being optimistic but stresses the need to adapt or be eliminated.

— Will
