Global liquidity peaks: the companies that spent most aggressively on AI in H1 are discounted first

A rising capital market needs new money to keep taking the other side. Global liquidity is already showing signs of peaking, and the companies that invested most aggressively in AI in the first half are shifting from being rewarded to being discounted.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:33

A low IPO break rate is not a good signal

Do not take the low first-day break rate of Hong Kong IPOs as a sign of a strengthening market. In the first half of this year the break rate was only in the teens, far below the roughly fifty-fifty level normal under a market mechanism, meaning the main participants in IPOs are short-term speculative arbitrage funds, not long-term investors. Recently the supply of listed companies has decreased and IPO-related funds have blown up, yet the two newly listed companies still broke issue, showing that temporary supply-side control has not solved the fundamental problem: Hong Kong also faces peak liquidity.

12:07

The more aggressive on AI, the sooner punished

Market attitude reversed within a quarter. Before Q1, the capital market rewarded aggression: the louder the AI CapEx, the higher the valuation; by the time half-year reports were concentratedly disclosed at the end of July, any large internet company that had made aggressive capital expenditures in the first half, especially those that spent all free cash flow, was discounted, and even Google was not exempt. Q2 was precisely the quarter of the most FOMO across the whole supply chain, with everyone afraid of price increases and rushing to spend money first, while inventory could still support gross margins; by Q3, inventory is exhausted and the impact of price increases returns, and unfavorable factors will appear together.

15:54

Nvidia is closer to Cisco in 2000

Nvidia's earnings look good, but its cash flow is changing. Before the dot-com bubble burst in 2000, Cisco introduced "lease-to-buy" to help customers finance, recognizing future revenue as sales revenue in advance; Nvidia is now also pushing lease-to-buy. In the financial report, free cash flow halved quarter-on-quarter, and accounts receivable on the books are far above historical levels—this is speculated to be partly related to the credit enhancement and collection structure brought by lease-to-buy. Therefore Nvidia may have reached a stage where it needs more aggressive financial tactics to maintain capital market expectations, similar to Cisco's situation back then.

33:21

Primary-market money comes from bullets fired three years ago

The answer lies in the timing of fund fundraising. The money concentrated in AI and robotics from H2 2025 to H1 2026 mainly comes from funds raised at the market peak in 2020-2022; they barely invested in 2023-2025, until they saw state capital betting and earning the most during a period when no one dared to invest—the stories of ChangXin and ChangCun, two large models, and two large chips stimulated market-based funds, and everyone's mentality was: copy the state capital's homework. So this is not a rational long-term increment, but more like a delayed emotional release.

47:31

New property policy removes households from the financing chain

The key to recent real estate policy is not to save developers, but to pull households out of the old financing loop. In the past, buying pre-sale housing meant households played the role of financing objects in the investment-driven chain: pay first, receive housing later; after the policy pushes the chain toward marketization, buyers get completed housing and only bear the consumer role, with risk reset back to developers and financing institutions. The policy was introduced when housing prices were near the bottom, in order to first stabilize the denominator on the asset side and avoid panic from dismantling the chain during a decline. Japan after the 1990s and the consolidation of China's coal and mining industries have followed similar paths.

1:01:34

A slow bull is not a slogan, it is chip structure

A slow bull does not rely on shouting, but on layering market chips by duration: the bottom layer is low-valuation, high-dividend blue chips taken up by insurance, as long money that rarely trades; above that are public funds and index funds, performing a quasi-stabilizing function; Central Huijin and China Securities Finance adjust the index at a higher layer; the top layer is retail, private equity and active management funds. The new insurance regulations require liabilities and assets to match in term, yield and liquidity, which institutionally ensures long money for long investment, medium money for medium investment, short money for short investment—not allowing short-term public money to be invested in ten-year low-liquidity assets.

1:22:49

Crises are rescued ever larger, bubbles at least one and a half

After the US dollar left gold in 1971, global central banks became increasingly accustomed to directly intervening in crises by printing money, acting faster each time. The quantitative easing after 2008 was supposed to clear out during the rate hike cycle, but was interrupted by political intervention, and then the pandemic hit in 2020, so a larger flood of money was layered on top before the previous bubble had dissipated—today at least one and a half bubbles have accumulated. Central bank governors have learned to print more money immediately when a crisis first appears, and the endogenous result is that inflation, rising financial assets and wealth polarization intensify simultaneously. As long as the bubble is not allowed to clear, the world may face long-term stagflation in some form.

Glossary

Capex
Money a company invests in long-term assets; in this episode it mainly refers to capital expenditure on data centers and computing power.
FOMO
Fear of missing out on money others are making, causing the whole supply chain to spend before price increases.
Mega 7
The seven largest US technology companies by market value, used in the episode to observe whether the capital cycle is still rising.
