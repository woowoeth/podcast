Global liquidity peaks: the companies that spent most aggressively on AI in H1 are the first to be discounted

A rising market needs new money to keep taking the other side. Global liquidity is already showing signs of peaking, and the companies that invested most aggressively in AI in the first half are turning from rewarded to discounted.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:33

A low break-issue rate is not a good signal

Do not read Hong Kong's low IPO break-issue rate as a strengthening market. In the first half of this year the break-issue rate was only in the teens, far below the roughly fifty-fifty level that a market-based mechanism would produce — meaning the money subscribing to new listings is mostly short-term speculative arbitrage, not long-term investors. Recently the supply of listed companies has shrunk and subscription-related funds have blown up, yet the two newest listings still broke issue, showing that temporary control on the supply side has not fixed the underlying problem: Hong Kong faces the same liquidity peak.

12:07

The more aggressive the AI spending, the sooner the punishment

Market attitude reversed within a single quarter. Before Q1, the capital market rewarded aggression: the louder the AI CapEx talk, the higher the valuation. By the time half-year reports were disclosed in late July, any large internet company that had made aggressive capex in the first half — especially those that burned through free cash flow — was discounted, and even Google was not exempt. Q2 happened to be the most FOMO quarter across the whole supply chain, with everyone rushing to spend before prices rose, and inventory still able to hold up gross margins; by Q3, when inventory is exhausted and the price increases come back, the negatives will show up together.

15:54

Nvidia is closer to Cisco in 2000

Nvidia's earnings look good, but its cash flow is changing shape. Before the internet bubble burst in 2000, Cisco rolled out rent-to-buy arrangements to help customers finance purchases, pulling future revenue forward into reported sales; Nvidia is now pushing rent-to-buy as well. Free cash flow in the report halved quarter on quarter, and receivables on the books are far above historical levels — which is suspected to be partly tied to the credit enhancement and collection structure that rent-to-buy brings. So Nvidia may have reached the stage where it needs more aggressive financial engineering to sustain capital-market expectations, a position similar to Cisco's at the time.

33:21

Primary-market money is bullets from three years ago

The answer lies in when the funds raised. The money being deployed into AI and robotics from H2 2025 through H1 2026 comes mainly from funds raised at the 2020-2022 market peak; from 2023 to 2025 they barely invested, until they saw state capital place bets in a period when no one dared to and earn the most — the stories of ChangXin and ChangCun, two large models and two large chips stimulated market-based money, and the mentality became: copy state capital's homework. So this is not a rational long-term incremental wave; it looks more like a delayed emotional release.

47:31

The new property policy cuts households out of the funding chain

The key to recent real estate policy is not rescuing developers but pulling households out of the old financing loop. Buying a pre-sale home used to mean the household played the role of financing counterparty in the investment-driven chain: pay first, receive the home later. Pushing the chain toward a market-based model means buyers get completed homes and bear only the consumer role, with risk reset back onto developers and financing institutions. The policy was timed for when prices were near the bottom in order to first stabilise the denominator on the asset side and avoid triggering panic by dismantling the chain during a decline. Japan after the 1990s and China's coal and mining consolidation followed similar paths.

1:01:34

A slow bull is not a slogan, it is a chip structure

A slow bull does not come from shouting; it comes from layering market chips by duration. At the bottom is low-valuation, high-dividend blue chips taken up by insurers as long money that rarely trades; above that are mutual funds and index funds, performing a quasi-stabilisation function; Central Huijin and China Securities Finance adjust the index at a higher layer; only at the top are retail investors, private funds and active managers. New insurance rules require liabilities and assets to match on maturity, yield and liquidity — institutionally ensuring long money invests long, medium money medium and short money short, and forbidding the use of ordinary people's short money to buy ten-year illiquid assets.

1:22:49

Crises get bigger with each rescue, and bubbles at least one and a half times

After the dollar left gold in 1971, global central banks grew ever more accustomed to intervening directly in crises by printing money, and each time faster than the last. The quantitative easing after 2008 should have been cleared out during the rate-hiking cycle, but political intervention cut it short, and then COVID hit in 2020, so a bigger wave of easing was layered on top of a bubble that had not yet dissipated — at least one and a half bubbles accumulated by today. Central bank governors have learned to print more money the moment a crisis appears, and the endogenous result is inflation, rising financial assets and widening wealth inequality all at once. As long as the bubble is not allowed to clear, the world may face a long period of stagflation in some form.
