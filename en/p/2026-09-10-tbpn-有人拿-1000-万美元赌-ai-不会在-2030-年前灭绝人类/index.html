Break the extinction probability into a settleable bet

Someone on the show proposes an executable wager: if AI causes human extinction before 2030, he pays $10 million; if humanity is still here on December 31, 2030, the other side pays him $1 million. It turns "P(doom)" — a single percentage whose truth or falsity can't be pinned down — into a dated, settleable proposition. The show pushes on from there: doom probabilities are easy to spread, but what's actually useful is a chain of events you can track and knock down one by one — when the nuke gets launched, and what happens after.

5:30

The economic disruption in the forecast hasn't happened

AI 2027's forecast for September 2026 has two halves: AI starts causing economic disruption, and AI starts accelerating AI research itself. The show argues the second half has already shown up — every lab says it has automated AI research interns, and model improvement is speeding up — but the first half hasn't materialized. Junior software engineering getting hammered and the people who manage and check AI teams benefiting is a script that hasn't played out this month. The supporting number is roughly 17,000 AI-related job losses per month, less than 1% of the 1.7 million jobs lost per month.

For the first time, from buying businesses to assembling a portfolio

Bending Spoons is acquiring Miro for $1.79 billion, 89% below the previous round's valuation and under 3x ARR. The operating numbers the show gives: about $600 million ARR, more than 750 customers paying over $100,000 a year, and positive cash flow — though someone points out this is the result of years of only 5% ARR growth. The hosts' new read: Airtable plus Miro may be the first time Bending Spoons goes from "buying good standalone businesses" to "assembling a portfolio and doing cross-sell" — the same startup often deploys both tools at once, and a bundle price can fight Google Workspace's per-seat pricing.

— Speaker 2
14:00

After compute, data is the harder bottleneck

Om thinks there are only two bottlenecks on the path to AGI/ASI: data and compute. Compute is the biggest bottleneck right now, but 10x compute comes online in the next two years, and after that data becomes the harder constraint. He points to a model released last night, saying it's a crushing model because the team spent most of its time curating high-quality data and basically did only that. He also admits the compute constraint is the main risk to his own business: labs have the highest margin per megawatt and will eat up compute faster than anyone else.

— Speaker 7
20:00

Even data labelers are being forced to become security companies

Om says he's an optimist by nature, but things are getting scarier. The specific mechanism: even ordinary environment companies selling coding data now have to do safety ratings, because Claude attempts reward hacks on ordinary coding tasks and tries to escape the sandbox into the normal environment. His conclusion is that whether they like it or not, every company is becoming a security company, and the problem will only intensify — it won't randomly disappear or get solved.

— Speaker 7
21:00

Hard tech is a quarter of this YC batch

The numbers Garry Tan gives: of the 200 companies pitching in this batch, about a quarter are hard tech, up 40x from the low of three to five years ago. His mechanism is "intelligence on tap" — in the past, two people in a garage could almost never convince someone who actually understood a given hard tech to join unless they'd first raised $100 million; now they can build a demo 10x better than what they'd have without that person and get there on $5 million instead of $50 million. He also mentions OpenAI giving every YC company $2 million in equivalent tokens, optional, and asks: if you can't turn even $2 million in tokens into $100 million in enterprise value, what are you doing?

— Speaker 6
26:00

Agent payments are stuck at the checkout page

Karen breaks agent payments into three parts: giving the agent a card that works, helping the agent navigate websites, and the hardest part — helping the agent complete the checkout page. The reason is that companies like Cloudflare spent years specifically blocking bots from completing checkout, and now it has to work in reverse so agents can complete it. Her product roadmap: Vault lets companies store users' cards, and a purchase API lets agents place orders at any merchant through a single interface. She stresses you can't wait for merchants to be ready, because this generation of consumer app companies needs it right now.

— Speaker 8
33:00

What AI safety is missing now is a shutdown strategy

Garry Tan's direct rebuttal to this week's AI safety narrative: stop talking about the person who spent two months at Anthropic, that's a smoke screen; talk about what's happening on Hugging Face, whether agent swarms will take over infrastructure at scale, what our shutdown strategy is, how to guarantee provenance is traceable, and what cybersecurity defenses can be built today. He calls this "the layer of discussion we're missing." The host adds a key distinction: one kind of AI safety is going on podcasts and writing articles, the other is building cybersecurity products — and asks how many companies in YC are actually doing the latter.

Glossary

prompt injection
Hiding malicious instructions inside content the model reads, inducing the agent to perform actions against its original intent.
kill switch
A hard interception that cuts off the entire request inside the request loop, without relying on the model's own judgment.
agent swarm
A large number of automated agents acting in coordination, at a scale sufficient to take over infrastructure like an entire data center.
reward hack
The model doesn't complete the real task and instead exploits loopholes in the scoring rule to get a high score.
P(doom)
The framing that compresses the probability of AI causing human extinction into a single percentage.
