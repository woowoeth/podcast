Three gates still stand between your agent and your money: trust, authorization, merchant readiness

Clink and Visa completed the first agent payment, yet the industry is stuck at three gates — trust, authorization and merchant readiness; fiat is running first because consumer protection is mature, while stablecoins are still working on irreversibility.

4:26

Agent payments split into two scenarios, and only one is genuinely new

Gao Ning (高宁) and Patrick break Agent Payment into two categories. In the first, the user has an explicit need — e-commerce shopping, booking a service — and the agent simply executes it; the end consumer is still a human. In the second, the agent generates spending on its own while carrying out a task: calling an API, buying a research report, downloading materials. The amounts are small but the frequency is high, and it has to happen autonomously within an authorized limit. The first category is larger in volume with a longer chain; the second is the net-new scenario, and today it mostly happens inside a user's conversation with a closed-source model such as OpenAI or Claude.

— Gao Ning
7:30

The blocker is not the technology: trust, card custody, and merchant readiness

Patrick argues the biggest problem right now sits in three places, none of them technical. First, the agent's intelligence and recommendation quality have not yet earned user trust — having your context does not mean it knows what you like. Second, the agent cannot be trusted to hold your card details; if a chargeback happens, the user bears the responsibility. Third, merchants have doubts about who owns the customer relationship when an agent places the order, and with agent penetration among ordinary users still low, the ROI on a large merchant-side investment does not add up. That is why merchant readiness is moving slowly.

— Patrick
9:48

Clink ran VIC's first transaction by making the accountability chain explicit

Patrick describes the partnership with Visa: Clink is positioned as the first agent enableer inside Visa's global production network. At the end of June, at an expo, it executed VIC's first transaction. The flow works like this: the agent passes the user's context, the authorized limit and the product category to Clink, which records it as a mandate; once the agent has found the item, Clink checks it against the original authorization, then requests a one-time payment capability from Visa, and Visa verifies and completes the payment. The mechanism makes the final chain of accountability clear.

— Patrick
12:57

Fiat is ahead of stablecoins because consumer protection makes trust solvable

Patrick explains why Clink chose fiat over stablecoins. In the fiat system, consumer protection mechanisms are mature: banks and card networks have complete processes for authorization and dispute handling, which makes the trust problem far easier to solve. With stablecoins, once a payment goes out it is essentially irreversible, and both consumer confidence and sustained trust take the damage. A guest then adds a real case: after one company fired an employee, a token burned through more than US$600 overnight with no way to trace it. They ended up giving each person a separate API key so spending could be traced.

— Patrick
16:04

Stablecoins are building an on-chain Alipay; the rails exist, the scenarios don't

Gao Ning walks through progress in the stablecoin lane. Plain small-value transfers are already fairly mature, because an on-chain payment is simply an end-to-end transfer. The more interesting work is people trying to solve the problem Taobao faced in its early days: users and merchants do not trust each other. The approach is to use a smart contract to lock the money in a neutral position and release it to the merchant only after delivery is confirmed — solving the irreversibility of "I paid and nothing shipped." Gao Ning's view is that the infrastructure for small, high-frequency payments is no longer what's missing; what's missing is a richer set of scenarios and a shift in user habits.

— Gao Ning
23:24

Clink is not building a new highway, it is building the ramps between them

Patrick says Clink is not laying down another highway; it is building the ramps that connect the roads. A user may arrive via OpenAI's ACP protocol while the merchant runs on Shopify or Stripe, and somebody has to translate and connect across different protocols, different payment companies and different e-commerce platforms. Clink's strategy is to be as compatible as possible with UCP and ACP, and to build plugins for e-commerce platforms like Shopify and Magento so merchants can onboard cheaply — becoming agent payment ready without changing their existing OMS or order systems.

— Patrick
29:30

China has no chargebacks, but the first large-scale agent payment may land there

Gao Ning notes that China has no equivalent of the overseas card networks' chargeback and appeals system; disputes are handled more as customer complaints, and responsibility sits differently. But electronic payment penetration is extremely high and the infrastructure is mature, so it is only a matter of time. Tencent is working on an Agent card and Alipay has published the AMP protocol, though neither has shown a scenario actually shipping yet. The host adds that WeChat's Agent ecosystem may launch within a few months and is being piloted with several large merchants — so the first large-scale agent payment might well happen in China first.

— Gao Ning
37:43

The signal to watch is an agent breaking through the recommendation filter bubble

Patrick names two signals. One: users genuinely finding something good through an agent that they would never have thought of themselves — an aha moment that breaks out of the information cocoon recommendation algorithms build by knowing you "too well." Two: more and more people starting to call paid external digital services, rather than only consuming tokens themselves. Gao Ning's is more personal: he has been using GPT to talk through his gastroenteritis symptoms and getting probiotic recommendations, but at the end he still has to go to another platform to order and get a prescription. He wants that flow to go straight from the conversation to an order shipped to his home — and he thinks China will get there faster.

— Patrick

Glossary

Agent Payment
A payment initiated and completed by an AI agent in place of a human.
ACP
OpenAI's agent commerce protocol, letting an agent complete purchases within an authorized scope.
UCP / User Choice Protocol
Google's agent payment protocol, emphasizing the user's right to choose on each transaction.
VIC / Visa Intelligent Commerce
Visa's intelligent commerce product, on which Clink executed the first agent transaction.
agent enableer
The intermediary role that plugs agents into the existing payment system.
chargeback
A card transaction dispute the cardholder raises with the issuing bank, pulling the funds back.
