Two brothers traced their biological father's address: his barber shop is a five-minute drive away

Twin brothers used a forgotten DNA test to piece together their biological father's name, discovered his barber shop five minutes away, yet waited nearly a year to push through the door—unsure even after confronting him whether they could truly find closure.

2:02

He recorded all his private life

Host Ira Glass introduces this series by noting creator Sayre Quevedo recorded nearly all his private conversations over years, including those with twin brother Connor that grew increasingly complex with passive-aggressive undertones. The work pieces together real audio into a complete picture of a person's life rather than interviews around a single event—a rare approach in podcasting.

— Ira Glass
7:10

Two freedoms, two different prices

The narration frames the brothers' divergence as an old unconscious choice: Sayre chose the freedom that brings stability and safety, paying in student loans, quarterly reviews, and happy hours with colleagues; Connor chose land projects, temporary work, remote cannabis farms, paying in persistent hoarseness and being chased with a knife in New Orleans. Both brothers use their estrangement to settle the bill for their choices.

17:20

A forgotten DNA test fills in the name

Years ago the family bought a DNA test but didn't follow up. Connor contacted a distant relative, asking if anyone in their family shared their biological father's name. Days later—more than a year after Connor first asked to find their father—Connor got the father's correct, full name for the first time. A public records search showed the address in a Los Angeles suburb forty minutes from Sayre's home.

20:26

One casual Google search, the father five minutes away

In June, a year later, Sayre casually searched the father's name plus "Los Angeles" and found two new photos: a man standing beside a barber chair, like a barber from a 1950s Time magazine. The address led to a shop across from a restaurant where Sayre had eaten with friends two days prior. Maps showed his home was just five minutes' drive away.

32:01

An email, two brothers debating every word

They revised the draft repeatedly, trying to be "firm but not aggressive" while staying "humble but not subservient." Connor wanted to ask about money several times—"I might want to ask if he could pay for my tuition"—but Sayre begged him not to wreck this before they met. The final email settled on: We're almost thirty and have always wanted to know who you are.

— Connor
44:44

Watching him cut hair through the barber shop window

Four months of silence from email and phone calls later, Sayre stood across from his father's barber shop, watching through the window as he cut a customer's hair. Afterward he described the feeling as contradictory: on one hand feeling "you can't hurt me, you can't stop me from being here," on the other a dull ache—the man's life continued entirely unbroken by this, as if nothing were amiss.

— Sayre Quevedo
52:55

Two minutes before walking in, the plan almost fell apart

The brothers booked the father's last appointment slot that day, planning to hand him a note asking him to meet them across the street. At the door, Connor wavered, suggesting they call and say "we're your sons" instead, even saying that if he hung up, "we'll just go in and say it, fuck it," and that he'd "always wanted to say that to Dad." In the end they stuck with the plan and handed him the note.

— Connor
56:01

A note with a name on it, turning an ordinary day

The narration imagines the day as unremarkable in the father's mind: coffee, work, haircuts for regulars, chat about seventy-degree weather—until he glanced at his watch and saw his last two clients, including one curly-haired boy who could barely look at him, who pulled a note from his pocket with the father's name on it, handed it over, then the two of them left without a word. In seconds, an ordinary day became a defining one.
