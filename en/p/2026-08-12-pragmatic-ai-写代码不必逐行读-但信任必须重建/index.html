The Pragmatic Engineer 2026-08-12

You don't have to read AI-written code line by line, but trust has to be rebuilt

AI in 2025 is where cloud was in 2010; the real question isn't whether to read AI-written code, but when not to, and how to rebuild trust with tests, evals and the on-call bill.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

10:13

Whether to read AI-written code is no longer worth arguing

Charity says 2025 for AI is what 2010 was for cloud. At first she treated AI as "a bigger feature than a programming language" rather than a generational change; the real turning point came before Opus 4.5, and what changed was the harness and tooling, not the model itself. She admits her first skepticism was right and her second was wrong. So "should you read AI-written code" is not worth arguing anymore — the real question is "when" and under what conditions. Whether that day comes is no longer the question; when it comes is.

— Charity
14:16

Once rewriting gets cheap, editing in place just accumulates entropy

Infrastructure has long had the principle "never fix something running, replace it," and AI pushes that up to the application code layer. When rewriting gets cheap, editing in place accumulates entropy while replacing resets it to zero. Charity says generating 10,000 function variants is faster than hand-writing one, and the industry will be pushed by cost toward "generate + verify" rather than manual modification. That explains why "delete it and start over" becomes the default move in AI workflows, and it means the engineer's core skill shifts from writing code to judgment and verification.

— Charity
26:44

If you don't read code line by line, you have to rebuild trust with evals

If you don't read code one line at a time, you have to rebuild trust with tests and evals. She calls out conformance testing, confirming that performance boundaries haven't drifted. Deterministic systems won't disappear, and AI has to be "domesticated" into predictable channels. She gives HackerRank's ATS as an example: running the same resume 100 times locally, the score floats between 66 and 99, which shows "AI is not the right tool for every scenario." This turns "AI is unreliable so be more careful" from a slogan into a concrete engineering constraint.

— Charity
34:11

You shouldn't send anything you haven't read yourself

The bottom line is you shouldn't send other people something you haven't read. If it takes the recipient longer to read than it took you to generate it, it's probably slop. Any send is a claim on the other person's time and attention. AI should be used for deeper thinking, not to avoid thinking; but for non-native English speakers and neurodivergent people, using AI can actually be a form of respect. You don't need to invent new standards of quality and respect — the existing ones are enough, just apply them. This gives "what counts as AI slop" an actionable test: before you send, ask yourself whether you've read it.

— Charity
40:23

The AI victory narrative leaves out the on-call bill

Charity mentions that Meta tracks SEV zero (the highest severity level) internally, and there was a run of them over roughly the past two months, at Instagram and WhatsApp, right after the trust and safety/reliability teams in those divisions were removed. She says she can't deny the connection, though it's only circumstantial evidence. At the same time she praises Intercom for publishing real data: 18 months of steady decline in reliability and code quality, only recently maybe starting to recover. Optimists see only the wins from rewriting and automating toil; the people on call see only the system getting worse. Her request: tell the whole story, and say the cost out loud.

— Charity Majors
44:34

AI going after software first is an optimistic signal, not bad news

Why is software AI's killer app? Because software is made of logic and language, and AI is also made of logic and language, so you can build in guardrails, checks and validation — something other domains can't do. She contrasts it with lawyers who filed hallucinated briefs in court — no structured data, no way to verify. She also thinks that if AI can't do something in the software industry, it probably can't do it in other industries either, because here you have compilable code as excellent training data. That line flips "AI eats the software industry first" from a pessimistic narrative into an optimistic signal.

— Charity Majors
50:44

DevOps' twenty-year core goal has already failed

The core goal of the 20-year DevOps movement was to build a feedback loop connecting the people writing code to how that code behaves in production, but that goal failed — they're still two separate domains. Platform teams manage the infrastructure, engineers deploy onto it, and there's no loop. Still, she thinks that separation is a healthy separation of concerns — should you be responsible for the stability of the code you deploy, or for the user experience that code produces? Anthropic also has platform teams and Applied AI teams internally, and the two sides don't even agree on whether engineers will be made obsolete.

— Charity Majors
1:19:36

Don't wait for top-down permission — do it, then report back

The way to fight AI fatigue is "proactive experimentation": agree with your team not to write AI-generated PR descriptions, or not to use AI on Wednesdays, and do it first, report later. Most leaders will welcome a team proposing something to try; better still, after doing it, tell your leader what worked, what didn't, and what you learned. Don't wait for top-down permission, because leaders don't know what to approve either; bottom-up action is what actually takes back control of your time and calendar. This turns anxiety into concrete action, and it's the episode's landing point: gaining agency through action amid uncertainty.

— Charity

Glossary

harness
The machinery outside the model that handles invocation, validation and assembling the results.
conformance testing
Testing that verifies a system's output still meets established performance boundaries.
slop
Low-quality AI-generated content that even the sender hasn't read.
SEV zero
The highest severity level of service outage, affecting users or revenue.
doom trolling
Repeatedly provoking other people's emotions with doom prophecies.
