Moore's law slowing to 1.6x, NVIDIA broke protocol and redesigned six chips to deliver 5x performance

Moore's law slowed transistor growth to 1.6x annually, so NVIDIA broke protocol to redesign all six chips, achieving 5x AI performance gain in this Rubin generation.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

5:05

This is not one platform shift but two stacked

Jensen Huang argues this isn't just applications built on top of AI, but the entire five-layer computing stack being redesigned simultaneously: software is no longer programmed but trained, runs not on CPU but GPU, and applications are no longer precompiled but generated on-the-fly each time based on context—every pixel, every token. He quantified this transformation through three flows of capital: a $10 trillion computing infrastructure built over the past decade is being retrofitted; hundreds of billions in venture capital pour into this new world annually; and within the global $100 trillion industry, a few percentage points of R&D budgets are shifting from traditional methods to AI—that's where the money is coming from.

— Jensen Huang
10:13

NVIDIA itself entered the arena to build open-source frontier models

NVIDIA doesn't just sell chips—it builds frontier models in almost every domain: proteins (La Proteina, OpenFold3, Evo2), weather (Earth2, ForecastNet, CorrDiff), language (NemoTron), world models (Cosmos), robotics (Groot), and autonomous driving (Alpamayo), and open-sources the training data alongside them. Jensen's logic: only when both models and data are open can others truly trust how the models came to be. This also explains DGX Cloud, which the outside world misread years ago as 'NVIDIA wanting to become a cloud provider'—it's actually for internal use, supercomputing dedicated to training these open-source models.

— Jensen Huang
18:18

Intelligent agents of tomorrow route between specialized models

Jensen recalls watching Perplexity call different models at different points in the reasoning chain and thought it genius—AI should, like humans, summon every excellent AI in the world to solve problems. From this he sketches the basic architecture of future applications: multimodal, multi-model, multi-cloud, hybrid-cloud, with an 'intent router' up front that distributes tasks to the most suitable model based on prompts. Privacy-related queries stay local (e.g. DGX Spark), the rest go to frontier model APIs. Enterprises train their own proprietary skill models on their own data while always maintaining access to the most advanced models.

— Jensen Huang
28:23

Robots lack not language but commonsense physics

Object permanence, causality, friction, inertia—things that are common sense to children are blank spots for AI, and such 'commonsense data' is desperately scarce: real video alone can't cover all scenarios. Jensen's solution divides work among three computers: one for training, one for inference (running in vehicles, robots, or factories), a third purely for simulation (Omniverse), paired with Cosmos—the 'world foundation model'—to turn rough simulation outputs into physically plausible, AI-learnable synthetic video data. He calls this 'turning compute into data.'

— Jensen Huang
34:35

Alpamayo goes open source, end-to-end, and explains its reasoning

Jensen announces Alpamayo as 'the world's first autonomous driving AI that can think and reason,' trained end-to-end—directly mapping camera input to steering, brake, and throttle outputs. Training data includes both real human driving miles and Cosmos-generated synthetic data, plus hundreds of thousands of manually annotated samples. What sets it apart: it doesn't just execute actions but first states what action it plans, why, then outputs a trajectory. This handles long-tail scenarios: you can't enumerate every country and situation, but any long-tail scenario typically decomposes into combinations of ordinary subscenarios the model already understands. The entire model and training data are fully open-sourced.

— Jensen Huang
43:37

Mercedes gets top safety rating by running two separate stacks

The Mercedes-Benz CLA released alongside Alpamayo just received NCAP's global top safety rating. Jensen says it's the only system he knows where every component from chips to every line of code has undergone safety certification. The key: NVIDIA didn't stake everything on end-to-end Alpamayo alone. Running in parallel is another stack—developed independently over five to seven years, fully traceable, a classical AV safety architecture serving as fallback. A 'policy and safety evaluator' judges in real time: if the scenario is within the bounds where Alpamayo can confidently and safely drive, it gets the task; the moment uncertainty arises, it switches back to the simpler, more conservative classical safety stack. He says this is the world's only car running both AV technology stacks in parallel.

— Jensen Huang
1:04:21

Internal rules broken: all six chips redesigned from scratch

Jensen admits the company has long had an internal rule: no more than one or two chips should change per generation. This time, they broke it and redesigned all six chips—Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, ConnectX-9, BlueField-4, NVLink 6 switching fabric, and Spectrum X Ethernet switch—because Moore's Law has slowed dramatically. Rubin GPU transistors are only 1.6x those of Blackwell, yet model scale grows 10x yearly, token generation grows 5x yearly, token cost falls 10x yearly—transistor scaling alone can't keep pace. The only way out is 'extreme co-design' across the entire chip stack. The key innovation: NVFP4 tensor cores drive performance to 5x on a 1.6x transistor base.

— Jensen Huang
1:13:46

KV cache is forcing GPU memory to evolve into a storage tier

As conversation and reasoning chains grow longer, AI's working memory—KV cache—has long burst past a single GPU's HBM. Last year they extended it via Grace-connected Blackwell; even that isn't enough now. Pushing KV cache further into the company's storage network just saturates the network, and Jensen says this is the pain point tormenting nearly everyone doing high-volume token generation—AI labs and cloud providers alike. The solution: BlueField-4. It runs the entire Dynamo KV cache management system directly on east-west fabric within a rack; each BlueField supplies 150TB of cache, which spreads to 16TB per-GPU of backplane storage per rack at the same speed as other intra-rack traffic—200Gb/s.

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

NVFP4 tensor core
Dynamically precision-adjustable 4-bit floating-point processing unit, not a simple data format.
KV cache
AI's working memory during inference, read and written for every token generated.
extreme co-design
Simultaneous redesign across chips, network, and storage to overcome transistor scaling limits.
physical AI / AI physics
Physical AI understands and acts on the physical world; AI physics learns physics laws themselves.
test time scaling
Spending additional compute during inference to reason longer and produce better outputs.
