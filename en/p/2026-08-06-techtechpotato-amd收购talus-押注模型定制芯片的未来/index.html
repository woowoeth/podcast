AMD buys Talus: burning AI models into silicon, 17,000 tokens per second

AMD has acquired Talus, a startup that builds chips customised for specific AI models and hits 17,000 tokens per second. It marks AI inference moving toward non-programmable, model-specific silicon.

AI chipsAMDTalusModel-specificInference acceleration
This episode surfaces a new trend in AI chips: model-specific silicon. For readers tracking AI infrastructure and chip investment, it is the key signal for understanding where inference cost and performance will inflect.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:05

Talus's founding idea: burn the model into the chip

Talus was founded by Ljubisa Bajic, the founder of Tenstorrent, and its core concept is burning an AI model directly into the chip, producing non-programmable custom silicon. The idea drew scepticism at launch, because models iterate fast and custom chips can go obsolete quickly. But Talus argues that even if a chip only works for one specific model, its 100x speedup is enough to offset the cost of replacing it.

— Host
5:09

Hardcore One: 53 billion transistors, 17,000 tokens/s claimed

Talus's first chip, Hardcore One, is built on TSMC's 6nm process, is close to reticle size, packs 53 billion transistors, and draws at least 300 watts. On Llama 3.1 8B it claims 17,000 tokens per second, far above Nvidia H200's 230, Groq's 600 and Cerebras's 2,000. That figure was verified in a demo, where it actually reached 14,200 tokens/s.

— Host
8:13

AMD keeps Talus as an independent team under Boppana

AMD has signed an agreement to acquire Talus. The team will report to AMD AI head Vamsi Boppana and will keep operating as an independent team. Ljubisa previously worked at AMD and knows Jim Keller, so the acquisition can be seen as a homecoming. The deal value was not disclosed and is expected to close by the end of the year.

— Host
10:16

Talus slots into AMD's mix-of-chips AI strategy

AMD CEO Lisa Su stresses that AI deployment is a mix of many kinds of chips. Talus's chip suits the small-model calls inside agentic AI, complementing AMD's Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs and the rest. AMD already partners with Cerebras, but Talus's model-specific nature offers another option.

— Host
12:17

Sally Ward Foxton: all-SRAM, best paired with a GPU

Sally Ward Foxton argues Talus resembles Groq in using an all-SRAM architecture, needs multiple chips to support large models, but is well suited to handling the decode portion alongside a GPU. She says the acquisition is not surprising, because Talus's software stack requirements are low and the technology is distinctive.

— Sally Ward Foxton
14:18

The price guess: billions, because Nvidia reset the bar

Sally guesses the acquisition price could be in the billions of dollars, because Nvidia's $2 billion deal for Groq pushed up valuations across chip startups. Talus had previously raised only tens of millions of dollars, so a multi-billion-dollar valuation is reasonable.

— Sally Ward Foxton

Glossary

SRAM
A high-speed memory used for on-chip cache; fast but small in capacity.
reticle size
The maximum size of the mask in lithography; a chip near this size has a very large die area.
agentic AI
AI systems that can decide and act autonomously, usually involving multiple model calls.
