Consumption Downgrade Narrative Gets Slapped: Chinese Fashion Brands in the 1500-2000 Yuan Tier Are Growing Six Times Faster

Tmall data shows that Chinese domestic brands in the 1500-2000 yuan luxury-lite price tier are seeing transaction growth six times faster than the entire apparel e-commerce market this year, as consumers buy for aspiration rather than value.

2:05

Double Eleven data exposes the consumption downgrade narrative as false

Sandunbansong (三顿半松) and Zhihe (之和) saw sales double year-on-year in the first stage of the Double Eleven shopping festival. Sandunbansong (三顿半松) ranked third in Tmall's apparel category, behind only Uniqlo and BOSIDENG, with growth nearly twice that of Yinglerfen (英乐芬). Looking more broadly, the number of brands with customer unit prices above 1500 yuan increased 50% year-on-year on Tmall from January through October. Within the 1500-2000 yuan luxury-lite segment, transaction growth is six times the apparel and footwear e-commerce market average. Industry average customer unit price rose from 1100 yuan last year to 1300 yuan this year, an increase of nearly 20%.

14:12

Social media shattered the myth that fashion brands must stay niche

Traditionally, a designer building a thousand loyal customers through offline channels and mass media was extraordinarily difficult, and word-of-mouth spread slowly with limited reach. Today, algorithms on platforms like Xiaohongshu (Little Red Book) precisely distribute content matching user interests—if someone expresses interest in a certain design aesthetic, the platform continuously feeds related content, making it straightforward for a designer to accumulate tens of thousands of followers across platforms. This breaks what investors had repeatedly criticized as fashion's fundamental bottleneck: the belief that niche brands cannot scale.

— Leng Yun
19:15

Premium brands sell solutions, not specifications

Leng Yun described a real consulting case: a bedding company habitually led with technical parameters in titles—dimensions, warmth rating. She suggested reframing: if you have insomnia, perhaps examine the blanket you're using. Her principle: ordinary brands sell products, emphasizing fabric quality and specs; premium brands sell solutions. They use evocative language—‘wrapping warmth around yourself in icy wind’—to build imagination. Technical specifications alone cannot constitute evidence of premium positioning.

— Leng Yun
23:19

Repeat purchase rates approaching luxury levels dispel doubts about designer brands

Leng Yun had previously been skeptical about designer brand sustainability, but this year's data changed her view. Shanyixiayousong (山下有松) has repeat purchase rates exceeding 40%, while Zhihe (之和) reaches 68%—meaning nearly seven of every ten customers who bought Zhihe return for another purchase within the year. This loyalty level approaches luxury brands, proving these are not one-hit wonders riding trend cycles but have developed stable, repeat customer bases.

41:44

Kering's strategic investment in Zhihe marks a watershed for Chinese fashion

French luxury conglomerate Kering (owner of Gucci) made a strategic investment in Chinese womenswear brand Zhihe (之和) this year. Leng Yun recalls that a decade ago, industry consensus held it would take 20-30 more years before international luxury groups invested in Chinese brands. She views this not as an isolated transaction but as international groups extending the portfolio of mid-size brand investments and acquisitions that historically occurred in Europe and Japan to China for the first time. Behind this lies their recognition of the maturing life cycle of Chinese fashion brands moving upmarket.

— Leng Yun
58:54

Flagship stores build brand equity; livestream channels chase short-term sales

Huang Hai observed that Tmall flagship stores are evolving from pure transaction channels into digital brand showcases. They deploy lookbook-style photos of real customers and styling guidance in livestreams rather than discounts, compensating for the physical atmosphere that online can lack. Meanwhile, livestream e-commerce on information feeds and short-video platforms routinely sees 80% return rates, with sellers sending three units per order as standard practice. This impulse-driven commerce generates almost no brand equity. For mid-to-premium brands, true brand awareness appears when consumers actively search the brand name and purchase directly—not when recommendation algorithms push products at them.

— Huang Hai
1:17:14

Capital markets have not repriced the upmarket fashion trend

Despite these mid-to-premium fashion brands posting strong operating data, capital markets have barely responded—related public companies' stock prices haven't been repriced, and China has seen almost no new fashion brand IPOs in the past decade, particularly on the A-share market. Huang Hai attributes this not to investor skepticism but to the prevailing narrative of consumption downgrade and trading-down crowding out investor attention. The story of fashion brand upgrades simply hasn't registered in most investors' field of vision.

— Huang Hai
1:29:26

Chinese fashion companies typically lack a dedicated creative director

Leng Yun identifies capital and talent as the two greatest bottlenecks for Chinese fashion brands moving upmarket. The talent problem's most acute symptom: China's fashion companies usually lack a creative director position—design directors do double duty, though these are fundamentally different roles. Design directors manage concrete execution; creative directors coordinate with marketing, retail environments, and product design to ensure brand concepts translate consistently across all channels with unified values and visual identity. Without this role, many brands end up disconnected between product, store display, employee presentation, and marketing narrative—they don't read as one company.

— Leng Yun
