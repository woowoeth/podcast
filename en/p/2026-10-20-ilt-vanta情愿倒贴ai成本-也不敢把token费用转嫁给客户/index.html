SaaS valuations halved despite stronger margins; investors misprice growth profiles

Vanta combines historically high margins and rapidly improving growth, yet valuations fell to one-third of peaks. The gap: low-margin AI companies command ten times higher multiples. Christina calls this cognitive dissonance. Investors only fund extremes—lab experiments and early-stage ventures—and avoid mid-stage stable-growth SaaS. Meanwhile, top talent gets poached by higher-valued competitors.

— Christina Cacioppo
9:18

Token charges fail when customers notice; Vanta absorbs costs and scales adoption

Vanta's agents act autonomously—proposing actions like drafting access-revocation scripts and asking for approval. This consumes enormous token volumes. When Vanta tested token-based billing, customers instantly spotted markups and began comparing raw model performance, forcing Vanta into direct competition with Claude. Rather than mark up tokens, Vanta absorbs the cost, accepts lower margins, and focuses on scaling agent-adoption from 25 percent to 75 percent through better user retention and experience. Pricing strategy comes later.

— Christina Cacioppo
12:22

Outcome pricing: everyone wants it, nobody can define what counts

Christina, an economist, notes everyone wants outcome-based pricing—where incentives align. The problem: what's an outcome? Closing a support ticket is output; real outcome is solving the customer's problem. Even retreating to ticket closure, forty-plus measurable units exist: draft documents, close alerts, revoke accounts, etc. Users can't calculate what each agent action should cost. Result: every company wants outcome pricing but nobody can implement it, so they use point-to-token ratios that smart users see through.

— Christina Cacioppo
14:27

Agents need modular codebases; eight-year-old systems need architectural rewrites

Agents work better on modular codebases—it's a token-context problem. Vanta's eight-year-old codebase required a year of modularization so agents could understand whole modules instead of drowning in legacy code. Otherwise an expert needs to monitor the agent constantly, defeating the purpose. Christina frames this as an old problem amplified: new engineers used to need ramp time; now Vanta wants 9,000 instant agent engineers. She grades herself low on this work.

— Christina Cacioppo
19:40

Teams ship proven patterns over risky LLM, unless explicitly forbidden

When Vanta redesigned customer onboarding, the team kept proposing a four-step progress wizard despite feedback to use LLM. The blocker: they felt "not allowed to fail" on this project. The wizard was proven; the LLM approach risked failure. Once leadership explicitly said "this must succeed," the team shipped an LLM-based solution the next day—and it worked well. Christina didn't anticipate this psychological blocker.

— Christina Cacioppo
23:46

Top talent compensation jumped tenfold in five years; still undervalued

On talent wars, Christina notes that compensation bands are a joke—the real band is whatever competitors offer. For the same role, total compensation has grown roughly tenfold compared to five years ago, and she thinks it should be higher. Her philosophy: you never pay top talent enough. She describes their qualities—high agency, execution, deep business understanding, sound judgment, iteration—as worth millions, yet they sound like clichés. The only force keeping salary growth in check is investor worry about equity dilution, but that concern is nearly gone now.

— Christina Cacioppo
33:01

French companies chose Chinese models to hedge against supply cuts

During recent Paris customer visits, Christina found that many French software companies now primarily use Chinese AI models. She initially guessed nationalism, but the real reason: after Trump blocked certain models for non-US users, companies realized their business continuity risk. No guarantee US models stay available. So they switched to Chinese models wholesale. Not values—risk management.

— Christina Cacioppo
52:40

Losing the billboard to a cash-rich startup signals market shift

Vanta's iconic billboard at the 101/280 interchange, famous for "compliance that doesn't suck too much," got stolen during renewal by a competitor—Vanta only had a placement order, no long-term contract. Christina offered double, then triple the price, but the competitor said "we have positive cash flow" and wouldn't sell. Vanta lost the location. She joked it felt like watching market capitalization decline in real time.

— Christina Cacioppo
