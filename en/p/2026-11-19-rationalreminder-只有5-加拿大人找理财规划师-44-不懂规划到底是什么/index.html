Only 5% of Canadians work with a financial planner; 44% don't understand what planning is

Most people conflate buying funds with financial planning, but retirement withdrawals, estate planning, and tax coordination are the core of planning and the least accessible parts.

AI transcription turns accumulated judgment into scalable software

PWL pipes AI meeting transcription into a multi-year ‘financial planning knowledge base’ (checklist). The goal: let newly hired advisors tap the judgment logic of the firm's most senior advisors. Advisors' initial reaction was ‘how much work will this add?’; now they ask ‘how much will this save me?’ Currently used as a sales demo; the next step is offering it directly to customers as a marketing acquisition tool.

Resistance to alternatives turned out to be right

When peers moved into alternatives, PWL held back. Ben's view: the index-based Dimensional philosophy is ‘not weakening but strengthening’, confirmed by money flow data. The validation came later when others' alternatives underperformed—making PWL's restraint appear as competitive advantage rather than conservatism.

— Ben
54:50

Consumers can't distinguish advisor from planner, and regulators can't fix it

Canada has 38% of people interacting with some financial professional, but only 5% specifically working with a ‘financial planner’; 59% have no professional contact at all. The country has 17,457 licensed CFPs. Dan Bortolotti points out the public can't distinguish between ‘portfolio manager’ and ‘investment manager’. Regulators tried title-protection laws, but consumers don't know what these titles should map to in terms of services.

— Dan
1:10:14

Pension withdrawal choices reveal true conflicts in AUM-based advice

Dan illustrates a real conflict in AUM-based pricing: at retirement, a client chooses between a lifetime annuity and commuted value. If he recommends taking the commuted value into an account he manages, his fees rise; a lifetime annuity might better serve the client but generates no fee income for him. The solution isn't to avoid these recommendations but to disclose the conflict—he notes he recommends the lifetime annuity ‘99 times out of 99’.

— Dan
1:30:00

AI replaces the mechanic part of advisory, not the guide

Brian Portnoy's phrase, quoted in "How I Invest": advisors act as both ‘mechanic’ (fixing the car) and ‘guide’ (telling you where to drive). Technical expertise is becoming AI-commodified, but the relationship work—building trust, understanding real circumstances—cannot be automated. This echoes Tia's point: the industry will need ‘excellent people’, not just ‘technically proficient people’.

— Dan
1:40:25

Canada's largest fund still charges 2% while America's runs for 3 basis points

The contrast is stark: Canada's largest fund is RBC's balanced fund, an actively managed, commission-based product at approximately 2% total expense, mostly sold through bank branches. America's largest is Vanguard's Total Market ETF, holding approximately $2.3 trillion and running at approximately 3 basis points. This shows Canadian financial advice flows through product commission distribution, not independent planning.
