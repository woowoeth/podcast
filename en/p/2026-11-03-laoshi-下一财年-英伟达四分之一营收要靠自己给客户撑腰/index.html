Next fiscal year, Nvidia must finance a quarter of its own revenue

Nvidia packages GPUs as financeable assets while funding AI labs and extending credit to customers buying its chips, propping up revenue through circular financing and driving accounts receivable to $63.1 billion.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:02

Computing power is revenue—Jevons Paradox means cheaper chips drive volume growth

Huang has inverted the old accounting. GPUs were once an expense to minimize; now they're production assets that generate returns. But his wager rests on Jevons Paradox: as compute gets cheaper, the surge in demand activates new use cases—contract reviews, email, simulation—that were economically unviable before. Volume must grow fast enough to offset falling prices. If it does, Nvidia widens margins despite cheaper chips.

— Lao Shi
4:02

AI agents never sleep—they parallelize and multiply compute demand by orders of magnitude

Agents differ from humans: they reason, plan, call tools, iterate repeatedly, work without sleeping or procrastinating, and can spawn hundreds of copies running in parallel. An engineer might manually run dozens of simulations a day; an agent can generate thousands of parameter combinations and optimize them autonomously. Human software interactions are dropping while background compute demand explodes. Agents don't just need GPU—they need CPU for scheduling, memory and storage for state, networking to connect tools and systems. One machine's gains lift everything around it.

— Lao Shi
5:03

Tokens are intermediate products; value is solving problems correctly, not token count

A steel mill produces a ton of steel, sells it, makes cash. An AI factory produces ten million tokens that might be worth nothing. An agent might spin for an hour burning millions of tokens to fix a bug, only to make the code worse, forcing engineers to spend extra time cleaning up the mess. Nvidia cares about tokens-per-watt and tokens-per-dollar. Users care about spending money to solve one thing correctly. When they align, everyone wins. When algorithm innovation lets models use fewer tokens for the same task, that's good for users—but not necessarily for vendors selling hardware by token throughput.

— Lao Shi
7:03

Per gigawatt, Nvidia captures revenue from $18 billion to $40 billion across chip generations

The AI factory's total efficiency—not a single GPU—determines pricing power. By Nvidia's own math, for each gigawatt of power a customer receives, the device-purchase revenue flowing to Nvidia was approximately $18 billion in the Hopper era, $25 billion in Blackwell, and $40 billion in the Vera Rubin generation. That extra money isn't higher GPU prices. It's that Nvidia has more to sell: networking, software, the full stack ecosystem. Even if customers use competitors' GPUs or custom silicon, networking and systems still flow through Nvidia. It keeps collecting rent.

— Lao Shi
8:03

DSXPORT cuts data center deployment from six months to as little as two weeks

DSXPORT pre-assembles entire AI factories in modular containers—racks, wiring, liquid cooling, system validation—all done in the factory. At the site, power, cooling, and networking just need to be ready. Deployment time drops from six months to as little as fourteen days. Huang's calculation: an AI factory generates $40 billion annually in revenue; three months of delay is tens of billions in sunk loss. But gross margins on infrastructure components don't match GPU margins, and this quarter's 75% overall gross margin may face seasonal pressure ahead.

— Lao Shi
13:08

Circular financing inflates revenue while extending credit disguised as long payment terms

Nvidia's management keeps repeating three words—productive, fungible, durable—to convince Wall Street that GPUs are stable, long-term, resellable assets. Customers can then use equipment as loan collateral. Nvidia itself invests heavily: supply and capacity commitments now exceed $279 billion, plus nearly $50 billion into AI labs. This quarter's $96.2 billion revenue grew 106% year-over-year. Yet accounts receivable hit $63.1 billion with collection periods around sixty days. More revenue isn't real cash. It's extended as long payment terms, accounting entries sitting with customers. Nvidia is using its own balance sheet to funnel hidden loans.

— Lao Shi
17:10

Every AI strategy—U.S., China, Europe—must pay Nvidia's infrastructure toll regardless of approach

The U.S. pursues frontier models plus high-value services; the challenge is whether billion-dollar investments pay back and custom chips reduce Nvidia reliance. China pursues open ecosystems and extreme engineering efficiency; DeepSeek and other open models lower the barrier to entry, paired with manufacturing-sector AI deployment; the challenge is competing under compute constraints. Europe pursues industrial applications and sovereign AI—adapting open models to local industrial data for domain-specific training. The earnings report signals the outcome: ACIE customers (non-hyperscaler data centers) contributed roughly $40 billion, up 138% year-over-year, outpacing overall data-center growth. Every path requires paying Nvidia's toll.

— Lao Shi
19:14

Nvidia's model survives only if three conditions hold: productivity gains, capital access, market share

Three balance sheets determine how long Huang's economics last. The technology account asks: does AI actually raise productivity, or does it stop at keynote demos? The capital account asks: is AI infrastructure expansion funded by customer subscriptions or increasingly by Wall Street financing and Nvidia's own balance sheet propping it up? Circular financing that re-invests its own returns is a snowball that eventually needs an accounting. The industry account asks: after markets mature, will open models and application companies capture a larger slice? Huang's historical reminder: the biggest winner in a technological revolution isn't always whoever solved the infrastructure bottleneck first.

Glossary

Jevons Paradox
When technology improves efficiency and costs fall, total consumption often rises as new use cases become economically viable, paradoxically increasing overall usage.
DSXPORT
Nvidia's modular, pre-assembled data center product that ships as containerized infrastructure units, slashing deployment time from months to weeks.
Productive / Fungible / Durable
Three properties Nvidia emphasizes to convince financial institutions that GPUs are long-term, tradeable assets suitable as collateral for customer loans.
circular financing
Nvidia invests in or funds customers, who then use that capital to purchase Nvidia equipment, creating a self-reinforcing financial loop.
ACIE
Nvidia's category for data center customers beyond large cloud providers: AI labs, industrial customers, and enterprises requiring customized infrastructure.
