1:29

The reversal isn't a new breakthrough — scaling still works

In October 2025 AI sentiment was pessimistic: GPT-5 was seen as a failure and agents as unreliable. Two months later Claude Opus 4.5 changed everything, programmers found agents genuinely usable, and figures like Karpathy shifted their stance. The key isn't breakthrough technology but that scaling and RL keep working: as models get smarter, error rates on long-chain tasks fall. The Epoch Capabilities Index shows that after reasoning models appeared in September 2024, the pace of progress accelerated 2-3x and has held there since.

— Rob Wiblin
4:46

Gross margins rose to 70% — this isn't buying revenue at a loss

OpenAI and Anthropic grew annualised revenue 700% over the past six months, and 1600% over the past three. Anthropic grew annualised revenue 8400% over the past 5.5 months, reaching a $47 billion annualised run rate in May. Gross margins rose from 38% to above 70%, showing this isn't loss-making sales. Revenue growth proves real value, investors keep putting money in, and the industry is in a positive loop.

— Rob Wiblin
9:30

The doubling period is down to 4 months — but only on clean tasks

METR's task-completion timeline shows AI can complete tasks that take humans 12-24 hours, and the doubling speed has accelerated from every 7 months to every 4 months. But be careful: this holds only for clean tasks like coding, where feedback density is high; AI companies focus their investment there; the benchmark compares against new programmers rather than people familiar with the code; and 50% success is an arbitrary threshold, while real use needs higher reliability.

— Rob Wiblin
15:38

That jump may just be a bigger model, not faster progress

Anthropic's Mythos model produced a jump in the ECI index, equivalent to 6 months of progress in 3 months. But progress returned to its previous pace over the following two months, suggesting the jump came from training a bigger model (roughly 5x the parameters of the previous generation) rather than a new acceleration trend. Jumps like this may repeat every 1-2 years, but they aren't continuous acceleration.

— Rob Wiblin
17:37

80% of code is Claude's, but the evidence is coding-only

Anthropic reports that 80% of merged code is written by Claude, that employee code output rose 8x, that code quality matches humans, and that it expects to surpass humans within a year. Claude's success rate on open-ended tasks rose from 10% to 75%. But most of the evidence comes from coding, and employee surveys may overstate AI's contribution.

— Rob Wiblin
22:29

Let AI run a shop: it earns $10k, humans earn $60k

AI performs excellently on clean tasks like coding and math, but still lags on grunt-work tasks like running a business. GDPval shows AI beating humans on specific tasks, but in Vending-Bench 2 the AI earned $10,000 versus $60,000 for humans; the AI Village project produced limited results; and Andon Labs' cafe and shop operated but ran at a loss.

— Rob Wiblin
33:46

AI disproved a math conjecture and still didn't move the timeline

An unreleased OpenAI model solved the unit distance problem conjecture by proving it false. The method was human-like, but relied on cross-domain knowledge and patience. Mathematicians think humans missed it because they believed the conjecture was true, lacked cross-domain knowledge, and found the proof tedious. Rob thinks this doesn't update the timeline, because math is an area of AI strength and there was no sign of AI having a flash of inspiration.

— Rob Wiblin
38:54

AI does the same work for 3% of the human cost

Redwood Research's analysis shows AI completes tasks at about 3% of the human cost, and that hasn't risen. Although chains of thought have grown longer, the tasks have grown larger too, and token costs have fallen. Rob previously worried that inference scaling would make AI uneconomical; he now thinks it isn't a big problem.

— Rob Wiblin
