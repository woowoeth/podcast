In a decade of economic transition, elite art held steady—cutting-edge collectibles crashed 90%

Over the past decade China's elite assets went nowhere—yet Yangzhou Eight Eccentrics masterpieces rose from 23 million to over 60 million. But collectibles with expandable supply left buyers down 90%. Only genuine scarcity weathers economic transition.

6:06

High-dimensional experience at low price reduces purchase hesitation

Li Yonghao encountered Tamburins in Seoul, a fragrance brand staging its store as high-end ruins, a giant mushroom as focal point, minimal actual product display—pure efficiency failure by domestic standards. Yet 200–300-yuan fragrances and sunglasses sold briskly in the upscale bar-like setting. He frames this as psychological arbitrage: high experiential expectation minus low actual price equals reduced purchase friction and higher conversion.

— Li Yonghao
9:06

Consumption bifurcates to niche brands and bulk retailers; middle market disappears

Li Yonghao foresees consumption splitting into two poles: niche brands competing on aesthetic and values, and warehouse operators (Costco, Sam's Club) competing on staple efficiency and lowest price. The middle—products with neither distinctive vision nor scale advantage—suffers. Homogeneous mall stalls and tourist-trap snack bars fade; destinations with authentic character attract visitors regardless of distance. He's arguing the mushy middle cannot sustain.

— Li Yonghao
17:09

Complaint escalates to overnight reversal: denied refund becomes full refund

Li booked a 3-day speed-diving course and was rejected by the instructor (he cannot swim); refund denied. He filed complaints with city hotline 12345 and police 110; officers arrived within an hour and negotiated partial refund. The next day the shop volunteered a full refund. He infers the umbrella travel agency feared administrative sanction and reputational spillover, forcing the shop's hand. He reads this as proof platform oversight is working and service-consumer dispute resolution is better than assumed—enough to make him more willing to try service purchases.

— Li Yonghao
23:15

Meme culture flipped: hustle worship gave way to sleep and hydration praise

Online slang used to celebrate grinding and sleeplessness ('Why sleep now when you'll sleep forever?'). Now it preaches rest and hydration ('Strong people drink plain water, weak people drink sugary drinks'—tea, boba, coffee all weak). Li's interpretation: in the boom era, people mortgaged present consumption against unlimited future upside. Post-transition, that trade stopped working. Now people focus on what they control directly and see immediate feedback—fitness, diet, sleep. It's not simple consumption downgrade; it's value reordering.

— Li Yonghao
30:24

Ten-year art prices reveal elite purchasing power stalled while scarcity held value

Art investor Zhang tracked Yangzhou Eight Eccentrics masterpieces (scarce ancient paintings): 23 million (2008) → 70+ million (2016) → 60 million (2026). Ultra-high-net-worth purchasing power flat or slightly down over a decade, yet the asset held value. By contrast, contemporary artist works with elastic supply saw collectors face 90% losses. He concludes scarcity—supply that resists easy replication—is the sole moat against transition.

— Xing Chen
38:26

Trillion-yuan valuations meet ten-million-yuan daily volume: no real market

Hong Kong market sees companies raise in private rounds, then IPO at 300–500 billion to 1 trillion yuan valuations. Daily secondary trading volume under 10 million yuan. Essentially no liquidity. Li attacks the exchange: this dilutes already scarce liquidity, forcing primary-market capital (exiting founders, employees) onto secondary-market buy-and-hold holders. It's why he's gone flat on Hong Kong and wary of tech lately.

— Li Yonghao
46:34

AI compute demand is real, video-driven, but Chinese cost edge eroding

Against claims of phantom AI compute demand, Chen notes Nvidia cards remain scarce and expensive—most demand is real, concentrated in coding and video generation. China burns 55% of all tokens on video gen, exemplified by red-fruit web dramas. But mainland compute faces dual pressure: (a) process constraints yield lower efficiency vs. Nvidia; (b) the pricing advantage that justified offshore capacity—cheap power—eroded as overseas operations became efficient; power cost is now marginal. The cost edge is vanishing.

— Xing Chen
49:37

Old-era wealth stores—art, jade, property—losing moat to new-era capital forms

Li extends the analysis to jade, property, art as prior-era wealth stores: their old monopoly or scarcity advantage erodes like any consumer brand. His podcast (hundreds of thousands of followers across platforms) is a new-era wealth vehicle—arguably as valuable as a Shanghai aging, lower-rent flat. His conclusion: invest toward the future, not off accumulated past advantage. Once that logic breaks, even bargain prices are risky entry points.

— Li Yonghao
