Zhihu's enemy isn't Douyin, it's a scale-driven version of itself

Zhou Yuan's post-mortem: after the IPO, video and short-form content were two strategic retreats, and the root cause was running a community like a scale business. A community can only be ecosystem-driven, growing by overflow, not by adding rows.

3:08

When GPT3.5 came out, the first reaction was disconnection

Zhou Yuan says the timing wasn't a month before the release but late October 2022, when GPT3.5 came out, just days after a strategy meeting had wrapped up, and his first reaction was "this strategy needs to be updated again." What he describes is a sense of disconnection: for the past two or three years the theme for Chinese tech and internet companies had been cost cutting and efficiency, while across the ocean the technology suddenly took a step forward. Zhihu then contacted OpenAI engineers, and quickly found that "the things you can think of, everyone would have thought of at that time" — many Chinese companies were contacting the same people, and in the end most of the engineers they reached had no intention of changing jobs.

— Zhou Yuan
6:11

Offering $100 million to push large models was small thinking

The terms Zhou Yuan offered a friend whose background suited building large models were: Zhihu has plenty of scenarios, and you can take $100 million to push this. The friend politely declined, and in the first half of 2023 his team quickly raised a few hundred million dollars. Zhou Yuan says, "Looking back now, that was still small thinking." This is one of the few moments in the episode with a specific figure attached to a self-correction.

— Zhou Yuan
9:13

Making AI pose as a user is a dead end

Zhou Yuan says that in early conversations with big companies, some judged that AI doesn't need sleep and doesn't tire, so you could turn AI into NPCs in the community to talk with people and pose as users, and the data would go up. He thought at the time that this definitely wouldn't work: communication between people requires trust; a user simply talking with AI is fine, but mixing them together so you don't know the other side is AI and think it's a user would challenge the incentive mechanism. His analogy is a digital virtual singer performing on the same stage as a real singer — the problem with a virtual singer isn't singing for 24 minutes, it can sing for 24 years, and it can hit every high note, so it becomes meaningless.

— Zhou Yuan
19:18

AI isn't just a medium, it changes information in transit

Zhou Yuan offers an abstract but useful framework: understand AI as a kind of medium; information goes from point A to point B, and what's different about AI is that it can change the information during transmission. So if you treat the whole community as one big medium, AI can play more roles inside it and help people improve communication. He also concedes that in the BERT year (2018 or 2019), what small models could do had value in the community but was very limited.

— Zhou Yuan
20:18

Hot list summaries went offline: compression kills native value

Zhihu built hot list summaries, many users used them, but they were later taken offline. Zhou Yuan explains: the hot list has a lot of open-ended discussion, and the value lies in the discussion itself and in reading different viewpoints; after summarization and compression, much of the native value subject is lost — you don't necessarily have to read 10 or 100 answers to reach one consistent conclusion, you might reach several different conclusions, and that's more valuable. His characterization of this kind of product experiment is "not necessarily in the small building, and it doesn't necessarily reach 5%."

— Zhou Yuan
26:22

Where search engines should be disrupted, they already have been several times over

Zhou Yuan treats search engines and the web pages and sites they can index as one whole ecosystem, with two kinds of demand inside it: finding information, and completing various tasks (booking flights, finding images, ordering takeout, borrowing books). The latter isn't done by the search engine, it's done by going to the site. He says, "Where search engines should be disrupted, they've already been disrupted several times over." Ordering takeout is a pull-down search, but it carries supply chain, delivery workers, and review information, and traditional search engines find indexing and interaction very cumbersome. So the search market is a highly fragmented market: as long as the commercial flywheel of production and consumption can close the loop at lower cost, there's a chance to become an independent vertical search.

— Zhou Yuan
29:23

What Perplexity lacks is where the new developers are

Zhou Yuan maps Perplexity to "Perplexity plus the model behind it that compresses all web pages," leaving a piece in the middle: the services that traditional search engines can index. Some services have already become closed-loop companies like takeout; the rest, he thinks, may need to be solved through more agents. He sees the small and mid-sized site operators behind the original big flywheel as developers, and the whole open web as an algorithm-driven big app store. So the question becomes: where are the new developers, and what are they. If the two together can solve consumer problems at lower cost and higher efficiency, that's very imaginative.

— Zhou Yuan
1:02:48

A community is ecosystem-driven, not growth by adding rows

Zhou Yuan attributes the post-IPO wavering to one essential difference: a community's growth is ecosystem-driven, growth by overflow, not growth by adding rows. The ecosystem has to be very good and healthy to overflow and attract more users; scale-driven is more number-oriented, roughly equivalent to budget-driven, which is to say it wants a numerical result. Ecosystem-driven takes longer to build, like the value of a piece of land that needs cultivation. So doing things on community soil in a scale-driven way, whether video or short-form content, can deform them. He says video and short-form content today should both be very organic supplements to community content; the problem isn't the medium itself.

— Zhou Yuan
1:04:48

The first month after the IPO was rough, then the inflation began

Zhou Yuan admits he was a bit inflated in the period right after going public. The first month was rough because the day of the listing happened to be the day a certain China concept stock blew up, and all China concept stocks avalanched; Zhihu listed that very day, and he felt at the time that his luck was especially bad and that knowledge value was undervalued. Then, because they had raised a lot of cash, and after 2021 commercialization growth and user growth were relatively fast, from a model perspective he began to inflate internally, thinking that as long as you kept adding investment and kept expanding scale, all problems could be solved. He says this was an illusion; the more real thing is still the very real experience of every creator in the community.

— Zhou Yuan
1:08:50

He couldn't make peace with the stock price for months, then split it into two layers

Asked how he made peace with a stock price like a drop tower, Zhou Yuan says for the first few months he couldn't really; every night he'd take a look, and seeing it fall again he'd think, "who's buying our stock now." Later he entered another state, splitting the question into two layers: one, the overall problems of China concept stocks over the past period; two, not being too affected by external changes, not catering to the market with numerical disclosures or strategic moves, but being very firm about being yourself, with the core being sustainable development and sustained profitable growth.

— Zhou Yuan
1:16:51

A CEO's loneliness has nothing to do with whether anyone is with them

Zhou Yuan poses a paradox: many people say a company CEO is a relatively lonely person, but this has nothing to do with "whether anyone is with them." A CEO's loneliness shows in the fact that some decisions only you can make; even if someone can discuss it with you, the part they can share is relatively small. It doesn't show in daily communication, and it doesn't show in whether the people who started the company with you are still on the same boat — even if they are, this CEO may still be just as lonely when it's time to be lonely.

— Zhou Yuan

Glossary

PMF / product-market fit
The state in which a product matches market demand and users keep using it.
LTV / lifetime value
The total revenue a single user brings in over their entire lifecycle.
NPC / non-player character
A character in a game controlled by the program rather than played by a real person.
Perplexity / AI search product
A search product that generates answers with AI and attaches sources.
