100K Users, a $10B Valuation: Personal Agents Are Priced Ahead of the Product

Instinct has fewer than 100,000 users and its product isn't officially launched, yet its valuation went from $50M to $10B in half a year — in this round of personal-agent hype, capital grabbing a ticket matters more than real demand.

0:00

100K users prop up a $10B valuation

Instinct only began small-scale invite testing in February this year, and by the end of August it still hadn't reached 100,000 users, yet its valuation went from $50M at the start of the year to $500M in August, $2.5B at the end of August, and this month the market is circulating a new-round price of $10B, with Sequoia and Benchmark both in talks to lead. The founder is a 23-year-old nobody, with no big-company experience, no technical patents, no industry background. Pan Luan (潘乱) put it bluntly: what capital is buying is neither the founder nor the product, it's the story of the next OpenAI — you can grab a ticket at the price of the next-generation personal operating system.

— Pan Luan
15:16

The line between tool and person is initiative

The biggest difference between this wave of agents and the last is that it is no longer another tool, but a person. Two differences between a person and a tool: first, long-term understanding — a tool has to be given the context all over again every time, whereas a person understands what kind of person you are, like a friend or colleague; second, initiative — in Manus, setting up an automated task still depends on a prompt, on you spelling out the rules and intent, and that isn't initiative. Real initiative is subjective agency — like the colleague who understands your goal in doing something and comes over on their own to say, "I have a plan, should we discuss it?"

— Suki
18:09

Office agents organize by project, personal agents by person

The core difference is the dimension along which context is built. Cloud Code builds context along a mission or project dimension, and switching to the next project means starting over; this wave of personal agents builds context centered on you as a person, knowing your profile, preferences and history. Open Codex or Manus and the list on the left is all the tasks and projects you've sent out; open Grok Bot and beside you are AI companions you can drive together. In personal scenarios people care less about model capability and the harness layer, because individuals don't have that many complex tasks — it's office agents that really care about task completion rates.

— Suki
30:28

Town starts by trading email for the user's mandate

The four products are trying to solve the same problem, just with different approaches. Town has a mobile app, but 70% of its data traffic is on PC; it focuses on the email scenario, solving one high-frequency scenario well before expanding. It understands which emails you send and receive often, auto-files them, learns your writing style, drafts replies for you, and sends them automatically once you confirm. It goes deeper on the email scenario than any other agent, and overseas it has already achieved fairly viral spread, especially in school-related scenarios. In one sentence: use a high-frequency scenario to build user trust, and acquire the user's ultimate mandate bit by bit.

— Suki
39:33

Poke didn't break out for lack of strategy, not tech

Poke and Instinct are both agents that live inside IM and don't make a standalone app, but Poke didn't break out. Suki thinks it wasn't a technical limit — Browser Use was already very mature by the end of last year, and Poke could absolutely have done it at the time; the difference was team strategy: Instinct went deeper in the agent direction, taking the full-mandate route, able to access credit cards, screen and microphone, booking flights and buying things for you — things users hadn't seen before, which produced viral spread; Poke put its energy into content push accuracy, taking the proactive-push route, with email notifications arriving even earlier than the inbox itself, but it never took the step of "finishing the thing for you."

— Suki
42:39

A cloud agent has to handle three environments

Mark says building a cloud agent was a painful lesson: a local agent and a cloud agent are completely different things. To do personal AI you have to be usable anytime, anywhere — you can't be unreachable the moment the computer screen goes off. On the cloud you have to handle three environments — the user's device, the cloud service, and the agent's execution environment — and task state, task interruption and recovery, and context passing between the three are a very complex system. They started in February, and just getting the agent to run stably on the cloud took at least two months; setting aside detours, polishing a sincere piece of work takes at least three to four months.

— Mark
50:39

Hiring an assistant and being a middleman are two businesses

Town started out doing corporate tax filing, and after pivoting it leans more toward enterprise scenarios; the relationship is more like you paying for a personal assistant, which naturally filters for people willing to pay, with a typical profile of white-collar workers who are extremely busy and have no time or energy. Muse is more like a middleman, a shopping guide, a broker: although it has a paid subscription, the main projected revenue source is still commission from matchmaking transactions, aimed at mass users, blending into everyday WhatsApp chats. Two completely different logics, cutting different scenarios and filtering out different users.

— Suki
1:14:07

Your saved folder may be noise to an agent

The story Zuckerberg told when launching Muse was: extract what you like to eat from the ingredients you saved on Instagram, generate a shopping list, adjust the menu by dinner party size and dietary restrictions, then complete the shopping and the relationship maintenance — turning years of interest profile into money, going from a discovery platform to a decision-and-transaction platform. But Suki suspects this is to save face for the free business narrative: helping users complete transactions is absolutely not among the top scenarios users want solved — users would rather solve the annoying things they don't want to do themselves. There isn't that much high-quality context in saved folders; the truly high-quality context comes more from offline, IM and email, and Douyin and Instagram saves may themselves be noise to an agent.

— Suki
1:20:12

GUI is a maze map for ordinary people

Today didn't originally want to make a standalone app, and was very channel-heavy, plugging into WeChat and Telegram, with most scenarios completed inside the channel. But it quickly discovered the discoverability problem: users staring at a blank chat box had no idea what to do. So it had to step into GUI, and doing GUI requires a standalone app, and in the end it still built chat into the app. Suki's takeaway is that GUI today no longer carries the user's operation entry point the way it used to, but is more like a map — to ordinary people an agent is more like a maze full of treasure, and GUI gives ordinary people a map of the maze; follow it and you can find the treasure.

— Suki
1:33:20

If 90% of effort goes to generic capability, pivot

Suki originally built the AI education product Monspeak, and while making 2.0 this Spring Festival discovered that to build a truly personalized teacher, 90% of the effort ended up going into how to make the agent run stably and have personalized memory. If 90% of the effort is going into a very generic capability, then conversely those generic agents might eat the vertical scenario just by finishing it casually. So the judgment was that the consumer track won't end up with that many players — it will be highly concentrated, winner-take-all — and Suki switched to joining a startup to build personal AI together.

— Suki
1:55:42

This wave has no network effect to lock users in

Suki thinks this AI shift is very different from the last mobile internet wave: the biggest change this time is technological innovation, not the narrative of scaling up, burning money and building network effects. There is no network effect or scale effect that can lock users in — as long as your product is good, I'll switch to another product the very next day, because no two-sided network platform has formed, and there's no reason users must stay with you. People stay on Xiaohongshu or Douyin because a two-sided network platform has formed and they can't easily migrate. So innovative companies are actually easier to fight than in the last mobile internet wave — just hurry up and innovate, and win users with the best experience.

— Suki
2:02:53

The most valuable layer is the processing mechanism in the middle

The last question was where the most valuable position is in the personal agent era. Suki picks trust and habit — context is only the entry-level foundation; ultimately it's the dependence, habit and trust formed through long-term use; if forced to choose, Suki leans toward the end closer to the user, because model makers are still chasing each other, and the merchant and MCP end will grow naturally with the market. Mark thinks it's neither context nor the ecosystem layer: just getting a lot of context is completely insufficient, it doesn't directly deliver value, and memory is far more complex than imagined — something said last week may already be invalid this week, and if it stays in memory it's serious noise. The most valuable thing is the processing mechanism in the middle, a bit like Douyin's recommendation algorithm; how the execution mechanism works is each company's secret.

— Mark

Glossary

Personal Agent
An AI that organizes context around the user themselves, understands you long-term and proactively gets things done for you.
harness
The engineering layer that wraps the model and is responsible for calling tools and completing tasks.
MCP
An open protocol ecosystem that lets AI call external tools and data.
Browser Use
The ability for AI to operate web pages through the browser interface in place of a person.
Computer Use
The general ability for AI to directly control a graphical interface to complete tasks.
context
The information about you that the AI can see, including history, preferences and the current task.
