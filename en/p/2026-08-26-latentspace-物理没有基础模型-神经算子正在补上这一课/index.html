Physics Has No Foundation Model, Neural Operators Fill the Gap with Weather Prediction

Anima Anandkumar uses neural operators to bring weather forecasting into the AI era: tens of thousands of times faster at the same accuracy, running on consumer GPUs, and showing how the physical world can move toward foundation models with small data and geometric inductive biases.

The bottleneck in AI for science is verification, not hypothesis generation

Anima says the bottleneck in AI for science is not generating hypotheses but verifying them. Torch Lean lets you write neural networks in Lean using PyTorch-like abstractions and then prove properties about them: upper bounds on output changes under input perturbations, the impact of finite precision, and the robustness that control systems care about. This allows neural networks to enter the control loops of drones or nuclear reactors with formal guarantees. She also admits that Lean is currently CPU-based and scaling to Transformer-level sizes is still far off.

— Anima Anandkumar
13:14

PINNs optimize from scratch for each equation and inevitably fail on turbulence

Physics-informed neural networks write PDEs as loss functions, but PINNs must optimize from scratch for each equation; for time-dependent, turbulent, or chaotic problems, the optimization landscape simply cannot handle it. Neural operators take a different path: instead of solving from equations, they learn mappings from function space to function space using large amounts of data, giving answers directly at test time, with physics constraints used only for guidance. Because they have seen correct answers during training, they bypass the dead end of optimizing from scratch.

— Anima Anandkumar
16:15

Modeling the world as continuous functions enables super-resolution

Standard neural networks have fixed input and output dimensions: language has a vocabulary, images have resolutions, and once trained they cannot change. Physical phenomena are naturally multi-scale; coarse-resolution data still has fine-scale physics underneath. Neural operators model inputs and outputs as continuous functions, allowing arbitrary discretization; after training, they can do zero-shot super-resolution from coarse data and can also impose physics constraints at finer resolutions. This is the core difference from ordinary neural networks.

— Anima Anandkumar
33:29

Meteorologists said impossible, then it was tens of thousands of times faster

In 2021, when the team wanted to use neural operators for weather, meteorologists said it was impossible because traditional physics models had decades of accumulation. But after training, the model achieved accuracy close to traditional methods and was tens of thousands of times faster; a single consumer GPU could do what previously required a supercomputer. FourCastNet was the first open-source AI weather model that allowed commercial use; DeepMind and Huawei followed only a year later. Small institutions could thus gain fidelity that was previously only available to large ones.

— Anima Anandkumar
36:30

Short-term weather and long-term climate should not be two separate models

Traditionally, short-term weather forecasting and long-term climate simulation are two separate systems. Anima's stance is that there is only one Earth, and a foundation model should handle both nowcasting and climate. FourCastNet 3 incorporates spherical geometry, allowing the same trajectory to roll out stably for months or even a year; other models that treat the world as a rectangle blow up quickly in long runs. Climate prediction relies on ensembles: adding noise to initial conditions to generate multiple trajectories, then applying probabilistic statistics to the ensemble. AI models are fast enough that ensemble sizes can be far larger than with traditional methods.

— Anima Anandkumar
43:37

Extreme events have few samples but are actually easier to learn

Intuitively, rare events have few samples, so AI should learn them poorly; but at first visualization, the model can capture hurricanes and storms. The reason is that extreme events have very specific physical characteristics, and the physical world has a lot of structure. Fusion plasma has only a few thousand samples, yet it can accurately predict disruptions and is a million times faster than traditional simulation. Anima's judgment: traditional numerical methods aim for solvability in all cases, while AI reveals from data how solvable a problem actually is.

— Anima Anandkumar
1:03:51

AI should not bet on a fusion route; it should accelerate all approaches

Beyond weather, the team uses neural operators to create digital twins of plasma evolution in tokamaks, a million times faster than traditional simulation. The next step is to design control and simulation together, aiming to predict and prevent disruptions to make fusion sustainable. The project collaborates with the UK Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA) and several US laboratories; Anima says as an AI researcher she does not want to prematurely bet on any one fusion route but rather let AI accelerate all approaches.

— Anima Anandkumar
1:12:55

AI should not verify human designs; it should directly produce designs

Anima says that currently there are foundation models for language and vision, but not for physics. In the future, we should train models that can handle multi-physics coupling and go further to inverse design: not where humans design first and then simulate for verification, but where AI directly gives the optimal design, with physics constraints as safety guardrails. Examples include photolithography masks, quantum dot gating, and nonlinear photonics. These highly nonlinear combinations are inefficient for humans to search manually, but AI can find truly workable designs in a closed simulation loop.

— Anima Anandkumar

Glossary

Neural Operator
A neural network that learns mappings between function spaces; inputs and outputs can be at arbitrary resolutions, suitable for physical field modeling.
Fourier Neural Operator
A neural operator that models global dependencies in the Fourier domain with near-linear complexity, balancing efficiency and expressiveness.
Physics-informed neural networks
Neural networks that use PDEs as loss constraints; effective for simple problems but prone to failure on time-dependent and turbulent problems.
Certified Robustness
Using formal methods to give upper bounds on output changes under input perturbations, used in safety-critical scenarios.
Reanalysis data
Historical meteorological data produced by assimilating satellite observations with physical models, used as training data for AI weather models.
Ensemble prediction
Generating multiple trajectories by perturbing initial conditions, then statistically summarizing to obtain probabilistic forecast results.
