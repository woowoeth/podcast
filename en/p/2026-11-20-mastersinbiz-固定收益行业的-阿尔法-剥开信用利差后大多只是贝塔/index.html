Most industry 'alpha' is actually credit beta

David Eichhorn points out that many fixed-income managers outperform the Bloomberg Aggregate Index because that index is half Treasuries—an asset class "hard to enhance"—so managers overweight credit bonds, ABS, and high-yield debt to beat it. When those excess returns are measured against credit-only indices or S&P benchmarks, they shrink dramatically, revealing that managers are simply long credit systematically and shouldn't collect active management fees. NISA's approach removes that 50% Treasury "seductive problem" from the benchmark, making it harder, so the outperformance they deliver is genuine skill-based excess return.

— David Eichhorn
25:28

Fixed income is easier to fake outperformance than equities

Beating the stock market long-term is rare; genuine practitioners appear once a generation. Fixed income is different. Managers can quietly overweight credit spreads—sell Treasuries, buy corporates—and generate consistent outperformance until a credit spread blowout (an ‘air pocket’ moment) exposes them. The 2008 financial crisis revealed this strategy's true face, but the Fed's rapid COVID rescue re-buried the problem.

— David Eichhorn
30:31

Real alpha can be found trading bonds from the same issuer

A stock has one ticker in the S&P; the same issuer (say, AT&T) may have fifty to sixty different CUSIPs in bond indices, priced differently due to over-the-counter trading. NISA trades within the same issuer's bond complex—swapping underpriced cheap bonds for overpriced expensive ones—earning pure spread on the same credit risk. This is among their highest information-ratio alpha sources.

— David Eichhorn
40:45

AI megacaps, not government, are pushing up Treasury yields

The conventional story is government debt crowding out private investors. This time, AI megacaps' issuance rivals nation-building capital needs; they're crowding out others and pushing rates up continuously. These issuers' credit spreads have widened, but most are highly profitable with no near-term default risk. Spread widening is purely from their relentless new issuance and persistent market pressure.

— David Eichhorn
43:49

Market still trusts the Fed on inflation; real rates are rising

Despite tariff shocks and war-driven oil prices, the break-even inflation rate between TIPS and nominal Treasuries remains just above 2% on the long end, signaling markets still trust the Fed's inflation control. With nominal yields rising but inflation expectations flat, real rates are climbing—30-year real Treasury yields near 3.5%, which he views as unreasonably high for the current economy.

— David Eichhorn
52:09

Private credit is fine; the leverage wrapper is the problem

NISA's math: unleveraged private credit loans deliver roughly 3% liquidity premium before fees, a genuine and healthy risk spread. Most investors access private credit through BDCs or interval funds, which layer on leverage (borrowing costs roughly SOFR plus 200 bps), compounded by the manager's high fees. That leverage cost can eat the entire premium or turn it negative. Twenty years of backtesting shows a high-yield index plus high-yield CDS (a more liquid leveraging method) consistently outperforms publicly traded BDCs.

— David Eichhorn
58:20

Mass pension buyouts to insurers could embed systemic risk

Companies outsourcing entire pension liabilities to insurers (pension buyouts) looks efficient, but the U.S. PBGC is well-funded but only covers pensions staying within the corporate system. Once transferred to insurers, pensions fall under state-run, underfunded guarantee associations. Historically, consortiums of large insurers have bailed out failing small ones, but if a large insurer fails—especially as many are aggressively deploying private credit—government rescue and taxpayer losses become possible.

— David Eichhorn

Glossary

LDI (Liability-Driven Investing)
Pension asset allocation based on the duration and magnitude of future liabilities to hedge interest rate risk.
Portable Alpha
Using derivatives to combine one strategy's alpha with another asset class's beta exposure, separating return sources.
Information Ratio
A metric measuring the efficiency of active management excess returns relative to tracking error volatility.
Interval Fund
A semi-liquid private credit fund that permits limited redemptions only during specified time windows.
PBGC (Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation)
The U.S. federal agency that guarantees corporate pension benefits, but excludes pensions transferred to insurers.
