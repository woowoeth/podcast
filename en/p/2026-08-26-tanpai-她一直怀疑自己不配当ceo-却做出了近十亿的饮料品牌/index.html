The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

16:06

All enterprises are born toward death—this is her operating principle

This mindset comes from her time living in a Tibetan monastery. She believes all enterprises eventually die—some survive 200 years, others just three years—so entrepreneurship's first step is accepting that companies must die, then asking how to make things magnificent in the finite time alive. She returns to this baseline whenever facing operational pressure or funding volatility.

— Sun Mengge
22:10

In 2024, she doubted whether she belonged as CEO

That year was one of extreme self-doubt. Watching other beverage brands grow faster, she began questioning whether she was holding her company back from hitting growth targets. She didn't overcome this doubt by finding answers; instead, she accepted her flaws, thickened her skin, and pushed forward more aggressively on strategy.

— Sun Mengge
29:12

What the market imagines as her billion-yuan brand hasn't actually hit that mark

Her actual revenue is under 10 billion yuan—the real goal is to cross 20 billion first. Her two core products, Wangshan Hawthorn (旺山楂) and Yimi Water (一米水), are each worth 3–5 billion yuan, roughly equal to each other, not yet at billion-yuan-product level. Crossing this gap requires continuously launching new products, not just scaling existing ones.

— Sun Mengge
32:13

She dared enter kombucha only once market conditions matured

She'd considered kombucha for years but didn't dare. The market had too much ferment flavor, her fermentation process wasn't stable enough, and consumers didn't understand the category. She entered recently because conditions shifted: Wangshan Hawthorn gave her fermentation expertise, top member stores were already educating customers about kombucha, and the first-place position looked unclaimed—so she decided to compete for it.

— Sun Mengge
38:14

Twelve billion impressions on a three-million budget: creative content beats media spend

Her Douyin campaign ‘Eat spicy, drink Wangshan Hawthorn’generated roughly 12 billion impressions. Douyin told her that 12 billion topic-level impressions typically costs 10 million yuan or more on their platform. She spent under 3 million. Her strategy: invest the budget into content creativity itself—hire musicians to write and perform earworm remixes, then reuse that same song for secondary distribution—rather than buying media placement.

— Sun Mengge
47:18

When her investor predicted she'd lose first place, she competed anyway

Before launching the product, she called her investor Lao Tang. He told her directly: you won't be able to take first place in this category; I absolutely will, and you'll get crushed by me. She said she wanted to try anyway. She later experienced firsthand how uncomfortable being number two is. This experience solidified her operating philosophy: whenever entering a category, she must compete for absolute first or not enter at all.

— Sun Mengge
52:20

She won't leverage capital because she hasn't truly mastered the industry

She has cash on hand but won't deploy it. Before 2025, she was still cautious about the industry—hadn't mapped out the battlefield yet, so lacked confidence. After 2025, the reason changed: she realized her existing two core products aren't the kind that scale through heavy spending. She points to Yuanqi Forest (元气森林) as the only company that reached nearly 100 billion in 10 years—and notes that such organizational strength is almost impossible to replicate industry-wide.

— Sun Mengge
1:02:22

Male founders need crisis for awakening; female founders build it through slow accumulation

After interviewing many male founders, she noticed their breakthroughs often come from extreme low points or sudden events—like a loved one's death. They have event-triggered awakenings. Female founders, she's observed, tend to be different: gradual, with many small accumulations that suddenly cohere into confidence and clarity. Her own 2020–2025 was all exploration; only after 2025 did she have real decision-making power. Women's awakenings come through accumulation, not singular triggering events.

— Sun Mengge
