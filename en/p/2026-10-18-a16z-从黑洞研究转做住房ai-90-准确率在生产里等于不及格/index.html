0:00

90% accuracy in production is catastrophe, not a passing grade

A 90% success rate looks respectable in research papers. But EliseAI handles roughly 60 million housing and medical calls yearly; that 10% error rate translates to millions of failed conversations directly affecting people's lives. This gap between theoretical performance and production consequence drove Mario from theoretical physics into production AI—problem complexity didn't shrink, it grew.

— Mario Martone
7:29

Not every problem deserves an LLM solution

Many newcomers treat LLM as a universal tool, but LLM performs poorly where you need determinism—identical inputs must yield identical outputs—and precise probability estimation. EliseAI's tenant-retention predictor is built on proprietary deep neural networks, not LLM, because they have massive historical data; classical machine learning is more accurate and faster. Teams must diagnose the problem first, then pick the tool.

— Mario Martone
8:30

Each additional nine in reliability is a summit by itself

Academic papers discuss 90% reliability for LLM as a solid achievement. But EliseAI's 60 million yearly calls mean 90% reliability triggers millions of complaint conversations rippling into real tenant and patient lives. This is the chasm between benchmark and consequence, which is why the team assembled both academics and engineers to obsess over evaluation methodology.

— Mario Martone
9:36

Small-sample testing reveals nothing about method differences

Test five or six sample conversations and most approaches look equally good. Run the same methods on a million conversations and their quality diverges sharply. EliseAI applies scientific method to product: systematic evaluation at true scale, not gut feel or handpicked samples.

— Mario Martone
11:41

Internal benchmarks beat external hype for predicting production

EliseAI's speech system is entirely self-built, so they own every bottleneck. When new speech-to-text, text-to-speech models, or hyped benchmarks appear online, the team doesn't trust published results. They run it internally against real data immediately. They have already automated to Slack + Kubernetes for experiments and are moving toward full automation.

— Mario Martone
16:56

Rental automation from search to move-in is already possible

EliseAI has automated the entire rental chain: browsing listings online, booking viewings, using access codes for AI-narrated walkthroughs, signing the lease, and moving in—all without manual handoff. Maintenance requests are mostly automated too—the AI judges urgency, contacts vendors, schedules visits. The only step requiring human labor is the actual repair work itself, which awaits a robot.

— Mario Martone
20:00

Subsidized housing represents your highest-leverage automation opportunity

Healthcare and housing account for roughly 40% of US GDP. That is why EliseAI goes deep rather than broad. The team is now focused on reducing operational costs for subsidized housing—a domain of extreme complexity, highest impact on the most vulnerable, and almost zero automation. Cracking it would directly improve residents' lives.

— Mario Martone
23:11

Production AI proved harder than fifteen years of black-hole physics

Mario reflected at the show's close: despite fifteen years researching black holes and string theory, production AI's problem complexity exceeded theoretical physics. He attributes this to AI's pace of change and the requirement that production systems answer for real consequences—a compound difficulty theoretical research never faced.

— Mario Martone
