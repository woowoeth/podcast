6:04

AI's stage hinges on how you define penetration rate's denominator

Numerator is straightforward—Anthropic plus OpenAI's year-end ARR may hit $200+ billion. The hard part is the denominator. If AI equals coding, set against $550 billion in US programmer wages or $1.2–1.3 trillion globally, penetration is already not low, putting AI in mid-to-late stage. But if AI equals all tasks computers can do, the denominator becomes $50 trillion in global white-collar wages, making penetration quite low. How you define the denominator is itself a judgment about AI's stage—and this disagreement on framing is the root of industry disagreement.

— Tang Zong (Powell)
14:12

Open-source and applications are taking away closed-source profits

Last phase: Anthropic and OpenAI captured nearly all value; open-source and application companies had no leverage—Cursor, for example, paid Anthropic for nearly everything, leaving zero margin. Now open-source models are approaching frontier capability and will compress closed-source 90% margins; profit flows to open-source layers and downstream applications. In parallel, domestic models have crossed the usability threshold—from elementary-school level to university level. Frontier models are PhD-level, unaffordable; but university-level is sufficient, and domestic demand will explode.

— Tang Zong (Powell)
20:13

Whether downstream ROI is real demand or just eliminating redundancy

Hao raises a sharp question: when big tech firms use AI to replace workers, are those workers redundant organizational-friction positions to begin with? Is cutting them real ROI, or just eliminating fake headcount? He even questions whether giant internet companies could cut 60–70% workforce with no issue. Tang counters by examining supply: domestic coding plans sold out instantly and sold out again after price hikes, proving demand exceeds supply. Person-to-person communication always has friction costs; AI agent-to-agent context passing is genuine organizational efficiency gain, not mere headcount reduction.

— Hao Ge/Tang Zong (Powell)
34:17

Storage bulls face a bear case that cannot be disproven

Storage stocks have surged this year, yet bear narratives always existed—just ignored. On supply: domestic expansion will grab share. More threatening: on demand, if algorithms improve context-window efficiency at all, it collapses huge capex spending. This is completely unfalsifiable and likely to occur. Micron beat earnings across the board; stock rose one day then fell. Sentiment has shifted. Hao is emphatic: storage only for shorts, never for long holds. Unlike overseas compute capacity, storage technology paths can flip without warning.

— Hao Ge/Tang Zong (Powell)
41:21

AI is infrastructure like electrification, not internet—builder phase obscures use cases

Tang spent roughly 10 hours working through electrification history with AI. AI capital spending is about 2% of global GDP; that scale only matches infrastructure-tier revolutions like electrification. Internet (Cisco, dark fiber) was under 1% of GDP—too small to serve as analogue. Electrification went through about 20 years of competing technology routes, then about 20 years of infrastructure buildout. During buildout, manufacturing productivity growth was only about 1%—use cases were completely invisible until the late stage arrived. On this timeline, today's AI is roughly electrification around 1919, infrastructure is barely showing effects.

— Tang Zong (Powell)
56:26

AI tracking feedback uncovers information sources you'd never think to request

Tang breaks research into six components: idea capture, prioritization screening, quick research, deep research, decision, and tracking feedback. AI handles three: idea sparking, information collection, and tracking feedback. The striking case: he asked AI to track one Nordic data-center firm. AI auto-decomposed the task into monitoring US domestic construction protests, EU new-build announcements, even scraping municipal websites for land-transaction records. It unprompted reported who signed with city today, acreage purchased, price paid—none of which he explicitly requested. The tool surfaced information sources he never would have thought to ask for.

— Tang Zong (Powell)
1:04:27

Domestic compute has solid odds and favorable payoff ratios right now

Tang is most bullish on the domestic compute chain. Domestic models just crossed the usability threshold (on par with Opus 4.6+ when agents ubiquitously deployed); layered with Chinese big tech lowering adoption barriers, explosive adoption is a timing question, not an if. Once domestic vendors saw training and inference were profitable, capex intensity rose; yet supply remains constrained, much like the EV industry during its own lucrative window. Win rate exceeds 50%, payoff profile is decent; choosing between large-cap vendors, small-cap vendors, and upstream-versus-downstream is judgment-call territory. Application layer involves deeper specialization and wider disagreement.

— Tang Zong (Powell)
1:08:28

Quantitative factor models are down to minute-level granularity; retail day traders always lose

Tang studied quantitative trading and concluded: daily-timeframe factors have degraded to too-low-frequency, zero alpha. Now it's all minute-timeframe factor machine-learning wars. No human can win at that resolution. Worse: as quant scale grows, they cannibalize each other, not just retail. This year, quant long-only alpha has evaporated. Tang's adaptation: abandon short-term timing, stretch to 3–6 month windows, bet only on major shifts like domestic model breakthroughs, then enter at an acceptable price. He will not race quantitative reaction time.

— Tang Zong (Powell)

Glossary

Jevons Paradox
Efficiency improvements and cost declines paradoxically increase total demand, causing resource consumption to rise rather than fall.
AI for Science
Using AI to accelerate scientific discovery such as drug development; considered AI's next high-value application domain.
World Model
Research direction aiming to let AI understand how the physical world operates; enables embodied AI such as robotics.
AR
Annualized run-rate; projected annual revenue based on current operating pace; standard metric for measuring AI company growth.
Agent
AI program capable of autonomously decomposing and executing multi-step tasks, distinct from single-question-single-answer chat models.
