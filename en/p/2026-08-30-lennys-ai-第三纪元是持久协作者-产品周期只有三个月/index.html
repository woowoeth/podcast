AI's third era is persistent coworkers, and product cycles are only three months

OpenAI's product lead Tara Seshan argues that AI products are moving from chat to agents to persistent coworkers, and building must be done against model capabilities two to three months out; future work is steering, not rowing, and writing-as-thinking will never be automated.

0:02

Build for model capabilities two to three months out

Tara divides the evolution of AI products into three eras: the first is chat, the second is working with agents, and the third, which is coming, is working with a persistent coworker that can accomplish things alongside you. She stresses that when building products, you should neither base them on current model capabilities nor on predictions of what models will be like in a year, but on what models will be capable of in two to three months, because both extremes lead to failure.

— Tara Seshan
11:06

The future of work is steering, not rowing

Tara believes future work will be more about 'steering' than 'rowing': agents handle execution, humans handle direction. The level of abstraction for steering will rise higher and higher, but ultimately humans still need to make decisions with conviction. She emphasizes that even as the level of steering rises, human judgment and accountability at critical junctures remain irreplaceable—this is both a responsibility and a source of value.

— Tara Seshan
29:14

The end state is users not having to choose tools

Tara explains that ChatGPT's 'work mode' is built on Codex, but with a different UI. The product's north star is to let users not have to choose—the system automatically picks the right tool for the task. She emphasizes that 'done is better than perfect,' meaning get users using it first, then fix based on feedback; fast iteration matters more than polish, and she says 'this is the worst the models will ever be.'

— Tara Seshan
39:21

Codex didn't change; users' perception did

The host observes that the conversation on Twitter about Claude Code and Codex has shifted over the past few months, with more people leaning toward Codex. Tara responds that the Codex team has always been user-oriented and fast-iterating; the internal way of working hasn't changed, but the market and users are gradually realizing this. She believes the change is not in the team but in users' perception.

— Tara
53:30

The part of writing that is thinking should never be automated

Tara distinguishes between 'writing as thinking' and 'writing as reporting.' She would never automate the former, because it's key to organizing thoughts; the latter she fully delegates to models. She advises writing documents to 70% and then seeking feedback, rather than aiming for perfection. At OpenAI, she still writes a lot of documents, but mainly for herself, because long documents are no longer a signal of depth of thinking.

— Tara
1:02:43

Product marketing fit comes before product-market fit

The most important lesson Tara learned at Sutter Hill is that product-market fit is important, but she underestimated 'product marketing fit.' Before building the product, you should refine the narrative and positioning through a lot of selling. She believes excellent product marketing can determine a company's success, and mentions that Mike Speiser is unmatched in this regard.

— Tara

Glossary

persistent co-worker
An AI agent that works alongside you over the long term and proactively completes tasks, as opposed to one-off chat or short-term agents.
product marketing fit
The degree to which a product's narrative and positioning match the needs of a market segment, emphasizing refining positioning through early selling.
