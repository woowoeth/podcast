Cosign's professional network lets AI agents find the people you should know

Cosign shifts professional networks' trust credentials from resumes to ‘who vouches for you’and opens the platform to AI agents to automatically scan lists daily and find people worth connecting with.

4:11

Finding the right person is harder than finding talent

According to Bella Nazari, the market never lacks talent—the difficulty lies in the rare alignment when the right person happens to be available, and a potential co-founder or company happens to be searching for them at that exact moment. Static resumes like LinkedIn's can convey only limited information, especially for newly graduated engineers with AI skills who might be ‘mythical 100x engineers’; LinkedIn can't judge their true capability from credentials alone. Cosign shifts the basis of judgment from ‘what someone wrote’ to ‘what they did and who will vouch for it’.

— Bella Nazari
5:11

The most influential people in organizations often aren't the ones on the org chart

Bella Nazari makes a counterintuitive observation: the most influential people in a company often aren't those at the top of the org chart, and even the most prominent and influential people over the past five years may not now be the ones actually driving things. Cosign seeks to capture moments when a VP vouches for a junior IC engineer, or when someone with product intuition who can actually build things gets noticed—sorting not by title and seniority, but by demonstrated impact.

— Bella Nazari
7:18

The 'cosign' button was deliberately created, not borrowed from existing products

The team acknowledges that recommendations themselves aren't new—LinkedIn and similar platforms have had them for years. But the ‘cosign’action and button is deliberately invented as a new term and interaction, not merely a feature tweak. The awards currently feature 21 judges and 49 awards total, split into 18 people categories, 13 company categories, and 18 product categories, all pre-defined by the team, though they plan to adjust based on feedback in the coming year.

10:38

The awards don't give medals, and vote counts don't matter

David Booth clarifies this is not a ‘most votes win’competition. After voting closes, the team works with judges to narrow each category down to 5-10 people for an invited list, rather than awarding a medal. They even ask invitees for feedback—‘did we miss anyone on the list’—turning the selection into an ongoing refinement process rather than a one-time tally.

— David Booth
12:27

The real outcome of this event won't be visible for five years

When asked what they hope the in-person award ceremony will produce, neither David Booth nor Bella Nazari offered short-term metrics. Instead, they drew an analogy to former PayPal and Palantir colleagues who later founded their own companies: the ‘serendipity’from an event like this is nearly impossible to measure directly; you can only trace it years later when someone says ‘I met someone at that event, and we started a company together.’

— David Booth
14:31

AI agents have begun actively finding people for users

The episode's closing reveal shows Cosign's newest and most striking user behavior: some users have connected their AI agents to Cosign (via the MCP interface at cosign.co/agents), enabling robots like Grok to check the feed daily and, when new lists appear or new people are mentioned, proactively search for people worth knowing. David Booth describes it as ‘your agent found people for you before you even started looking.’

— David Booth
