The final step of US federal retirement approval is manual filing in a Pennsylvania limestone mine

After retirement applications complete all stages, physical documents are sent to a converted limestone mine cavern in Boyers, Pennsylvania for manual archiving. What blocks modernization isn't technology—it's that no one is willing to bear the accountability risk of fixing the system.

a16z was founded without any job descriptions

Mark and Ben's work description for Scott was simply "we go invest, you handle everything else." On his first day, Scott didn't even have a company email—he used his personal Gmail to send the first capital call notice to 50 LPs. One LP thought they'd been impersonated and called Mark and Ben in alarm. The chaos itself reveals that a16z was built entirely by figuring things out as they went, not executing from a blueprint.

7:10

a16z imported CAA's brokerage model into venture capital

What Michael Ovitz broke at CAA was the old "one agent per star" model, replacing it with an entire expert team surrounding each star. a16z applied the same logic to GPs: previously VCs relied on one person like John Doerr who had accumulated every CFO, sales contact, and political relationship. That model didn't scale, and after software ate the world, there were too many specialist fields required. So a16z decomposed the GP role, hiring specialists to do only talent networks, only government relations, only business development, leaving GPs to focus solely on investment judgment—this is the true origin of the services platform model.

12:30

Skype deal rewrote a16z's funding tempo

Shortly after closing their first fund, a16z partnered with Silver Lake to acquire Skype, which eBay had shelved. This completely diverged from what they'd pitched to LPs about "early-stage investing," and got entangled in IP disputes with the original founders—widely seen as reckless. But 18 months later they sold it to Microsoft at 4x the price, which pushed the second fund to oversubscription ahead of schedule. It was the management fees from that second fund that actually funded the services team expansion—fortune precedes model; model scales only after fortune materializes.

17:45

Venture capital once treated marketing as taboo; a16z reversed it

The old generation of VCs drew power from capital scarcity and had no need for marketing—people came seeking them out. When a16z started, they had no track record and no network, so they couldn't compete by old rules. Instead they actively told a new story: "capital plus services." Sandhill Road rivals mocked them for "just hype, nothing built yet." But that marketing—viewed as rule-breaking—created a category that didn't exist before.

— Scott Kupor
31:03

99.7% of federal employees pass their performance reviews

Historically, only 0.3% of two million federal employees have fallen below passing. That's mathematically implausible. In the seven-thousand-person senior executive cohort, 90% are rated in the top two tiers. The result: bonuses become motivationally inert—paying 90% means no differentiation. OPM has now changed the rules so the highest rating can't exceed 40% of the group, and 60% of the bonus pool must go to that 40%.

— Scott Kupor
36:07

The federal government's workforce is aging faster than America

Only 7% of federal employees are under 30, while 23% of the US labor force are—a gap exceeding 3x. Employees over 50 comprise 44% of the federal workforce but only 33% of the general US average. On that trajectory, nearly half the federal workforce will retire within 5 to 10 years with no young people to replace them—this is the core pressure driving all subsequent hiring reforms.

— Scott Kupor
40:19

Federal hiring treats degrees as ability proxies, but wrongly

A 19-year-old who dropped out of Northwestern joined the Doge team and performed brilliantly, yet a government employee said publicly, "We'll never hire him because he has no college degree and five years of work experience." This made Scott realize that virtually every government job description uses credentials as a proxy for ability—but that proxy itself is broken. OPM is now replacing self-assessment questionnaires with real coding tests, rating by demonstrated ability rather than credential years.

— Scott Kupor
47:23

Federal retirement documents end at a Pennsylvania cavern for manual processing

Each year 100,000 to 200,000 federal employees retire. The process: paper forms mail to HR, then to an independent payroll department, then into a converted limestone mine cavern in Boyers, Pennsylvania. Eight hundred people manually calculate pensions, processing taking 3 to 12 months. A missed signature adds another month. Starting June 30, 2026, paper submissions end, and hundreds of millions of existing paper documents must be digitized with newly purchased scanning equipment. This problem has been reported since the 1970s and 80s but no one has touched it—fix it wrong and Congress holds you accountable; don't fix it and service merely stays poor, no one loses their job over that.

— Scott Kupor

Glossary

OPM
Office of Personnel Management; the US federal government's human resources department overseeing recruitment and personnel policy for approximately two million federal employees.
GS-14
Federal civil service grade 14; a relatively senior individual contributor level in the US federal civil service rank structure.
permissioned risk
Government-approved managed risk, contrasted with Silicon Valley's permission-less innovation; government can innovate only by having risk formally authorized and approved.
time-in-grade
Promotion system based on tenure in a position rather than demonstrated ability; time served becomes the criterion for advancement.
phalanx
Client-centered services model originated at CAA: rather than one agent per client, an entire team of experts surrounds and serves the client.
