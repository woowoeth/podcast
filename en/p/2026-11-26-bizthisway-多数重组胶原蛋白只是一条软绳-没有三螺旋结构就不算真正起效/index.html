Most recombinant collagen is just soft rope without triple helix structure

Many commercial recombinant collagens produce only a single amino acid chain that never folds into the triple helix structure needed to fit into cellular receptors and function. This—not marketing differences—is the real technical moat.

10:34

Protein folding has more possibilities than all particles in the observable universe

Zhang Ying (张颖) offered a concrete comparison: break down everything visible in the observable universe into atoms and you get roughly 10^80; but a protein with hundreds of amino acids has 20 to the power of several hundred possible arrangements from pure combinatorics, a number larger than all atoms in the universe. This means protein structure cannot be solved by brute-force computation, explaining why this field has advanced slowly for decades—not because technology is crude, but because the problem's combinatorial space is more terrifying than the cosmos.

— Zhang Ying
15:27

AI predicts protein structures, but prediction is just the starting line

He emphasized that prediction and experimental verification are two different things: prediction is like a blindfolded artist being described what to draw—they can get the outline but never the details; experimental verification is actual photography and reconstruction. Even with DeepMind, accuracy improved from 30-40% to 90%, but that's just obtaining a rough model; work then returns to the lab for validation. He also noted DeepMind's team has disbanded within Google, with Hassabis starting Isomorphic to focus on drug pipelines, suggesting these tools' real value lies in the first 5-10% of drug development, not the finish line.

— Zhang Ying
27:13

Animal-derived collagen is being eliminated for ethics and safety together

The industry long extracted collagen from pigs, cattle, and other animals, but Zhang noted that cosmetics giants like L'Oréal and Shiseido established animal-free, chemical-free strategies over a decade ago. One reason is ethical—unwillingness to kill animals for extraction. The other is safety: poor quality control of animal materials risks introducing animal pathogens into human bodies, risks nearly impossible to fully eliminate in production. This ensures recombinant protein technology using fermentation to make human-sequence collagen will gradually replace animal extraction.

— Zhang Ying
29:14

Structure determines function; without it, a protein isn't a protein

Zhang explained why Liying (丽颖) chose Tri-Helix as its brand name: there are 28 types of collagen in the human body, but all their functional regions must exist in triple helix form to fit into cellular surface receptors like puzzle pieces and transmit signals. Most recombinant collagen on the market is just soft polypeptide chain without this spatial structure, so there's an industry saying—‘without structure, it's at best just a polypeptide chain, it doesn't actually work.’

— Zhang Ying
35:16

Korea's largest cosmetics group screened hundreds of companies before them

Korea's #1 beauty conglomerate and #1 medical aesthetics company invited Zhang to visit, where he learned they had tried cross-linked hyaluronic acid a decade prior but abandoned it for poor results. This time they screened roughly 400-500 related companies in China through a third-party consulting firm, narrowed to 5-6 for site visits, nearly gave up—and found Liying at the final stop. The critical factor: their CTO had biochemistry training and understood protein must emphasize structure; every other company failed to clearly explain and convince them about ‘triple helix structure.’

— Zhang Ying
41:54

During hair growth testing they accidentally discovered wrinkles shrank

Liying initially focused all literature and animal experiments on hair growth signaling pathways. After a partner's chairman tried it, third-party safety testing unexpectedly found test subjects' wrinkles decreased nearly 40% in one month. The team then tested on employees across age groups and found obvious improvements in pores and fine lines—because poor cellular environments (such as from cheap petrochemical skincare ingredients irritating cells) make pore cells enlarge, while collagen makes cells healthier, shrinking pores. This unexpected discovery prompted the team to pivot toward the skincare market.

— Zhang Ying
46:18

They quit pure ingredient sales and built their own brand instead

Liying initially planned to sell materials to major cosmetics brands as a supply-chain partner, but Zhang did the math: selling raw materials has limited scale, hitting ceiling around 10 million in revenue, and brands won't prioritize your ingredient, diluting its unique value. He believes good technology must become good product to preserve pricing power, otherwise technology gets ‘drowned in a mediocre product.’ This was the core logic behind his decision to launch the Tri-Helix brand himself.

— Zhang Ying
54:41

At 31 with British permanent residency, he fled a predictable life

Zhang obtained British permanent residency at 31 in 2017, but six months later felt bored—he could see the next 20, 30 years of his life laid out and wanted to escape that certainty. Combined with post-Brexit decline in UK standing, his father suffering health scares needing care, and China's biotech gap with the West still substantial, this pushed him to start a company back home rather than continue deep lab work on protein structure alone.

— Zhang Ying

Glossary

Tri-Helix
The triple helix spatial structure collagen requires, formed by three chains entwined; without it, the protein cannot function.
Animal-free
Cosmetics industry strategy of abandoning animal-derived raw materials in favor of synthetic or recombinant alternatives.
Cryo-EM (cryo-electron microscopy)
Experimental method of freezing protein molecules and capturing multi-angle photographs to reconstruct three-dimensional structure.
