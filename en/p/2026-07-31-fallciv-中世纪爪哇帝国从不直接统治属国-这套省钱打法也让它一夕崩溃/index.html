Majapahit ruled by tribute alone—the loose empire that fractured in one night

Majapahit extracted tribute and soldiers from vassal states but deployed neither officials nor garrisons. When civil war broke out, this cost-cutting model of rule meant every tributary switched sides in a single night.

Raffles ignored the curse and lost his office within the year

In 1815, Stamford Raffles, the British governor of Java, traveled to the ruins of Trawulan, the ancient capital of Majapahit, despite his guide's warning that the site was cursed and anyone who visited would lose their office within a year. He dismissed the warning and pressed on, finding only red brick fragments and statues scattered among teak trees—nothing like the grand city he had imagined. Less than a year later, the Napoleonic Wars ended. Britain returned Java to the Netherlands, and Raffles was removed from office. Ten years afterward, a cerebral hemorrhage brought on a stroke, and he died the day before his forty-fifth birthday.

Javanese fleets sailed to Africa for ivory, slaves, and riches

In the tenth century, the Persian writer al-Buzurjumehr recorded a fleet called the Wak-Wak that arrived with a thousand ships to attack Qanbalu on the East African coast, only to find its defenses impregnable. When locals asked why they had come so far, the raiders answered: for ivory, tortoiseshell, leopard skins, and ambergris—and to enslave able-bodied Zanj men for labor, a voyage that lasted an entire year. Scholars matched details from Persian accounts describing the Wak-Wak as rich in gold with inhabitants using gold chains to leash their dogs, alongside later European records of Javanese custom, concluding that Wak-Wak was likely Javanese.

1:42:51

The king mutilated an envoy's face and provoked Kublai Khan's invasion

In 1289, Kublai Khan sent an envoy to Singhasari demanding that King Kertanagara swear allegiance, pay tribute, and send royal family members to China as hostages. Kertanagara had already refused tribute twice; this time he chose to humiliate the envoy instead. Historical records describe his face burned with hot irons and his ears severed—a punishment reserved for thieves and pirates. Kublai Khan erupted in rage, mobilized the Fujian fleet, and assembled a force of 20,000 soldiers, a thousand ships, and 40,000 silver taels to punish Java.

1:55:36

A secret tantric ritual left the king defenseless for his assassin

The Javanese royal court practiced a tantric ritual called Chakrapuja, in which the king would drink from a skull cup and perform secret dances and music that no one outside the court was ever to know. It was a standing date in his calendar—and a fatal vulnerability. The powerful courtier Gajahmada quietly assembled an army under no banners and without sound, circled through southern mountain passes toward the capital, and when the king lay deep in ceremony and helpless, breached the palace walls, took the city, and killed Kertanagara. Kertanagara's son-in-law Raden Vijaya was celebrating the repulse of a diversionary attack on the northern front; by the time word reached him, it was too late.

2:09:05

He seized power with Mongol aid, then stabbed his allies in the back

When the Mongol expedition arrived, the deposed Raden Vijaya proactively reached out to the Mongol commander Shi Bi, provided him with maps of river ports, and requested aid to overthrow the usurper Gajahmada. The Mongols helped him defeat Gajahmada and prepared to place him on the throne as a puppet king. The moment he seized power, he used the pretext of returning home to prepare tribute to the court; on the way he murdered his two Mongol escorts, and when the main Mongol force was preparing to sail home on the seasonal winds—unprepared and off-guard—he attacked from the forest. The Mongol army retreated in chaos; more than three thousand were killed. When Kublai Khan learned of it, he flogged Shi Bi seventeen times and confiscated a third of his property. It was also Kublai's last invasion, and he died the following year.

2:39:38

The prime minister swore to eat nothing until every island fell

Gajah Mada rose from foundling and common birth to high rank, earning appointment as prime minister under Queen Tribhuwana after proving himself in a rebellion and protecting the ruler. Before the assembled court, he swore a public oath: he would neither eat cloves nor rest until he had conquered the entire Nusantara archipelago, and he listed a long inventory of still-unconquered targets—the Riau Islands, Srivijaya, Tanjung Pura, Bali, Sundanese lands, Temasek (today's Singapore), and Palembang. Over the next thirty years, he led naval expeditions across the sea, transforming Majapahit from a kingdom confined to Java into a maritime empire spanning a thousand islands.

2:58:30

Majapahit's wooden junks shrugged off Portuguese cannon fire

Majapahit's oceangoing junks, called jong, reportedly reached eighty meters in length, their hulls built in three or four layers of timber, capable of carrying a thousand soldiers and thousands of tons of cargo. In 1511, a Portuguese fleet intercepted a Javanese ship in the Strait of Malacca and opened fire, only to find their cannon could not penetrate the hull below the waterline. The Portuguese captain described it as so massive that beside it their own ships looked like they weren't ships at all. Only by destroying its two great rudders could the Portuguese force the crew to surrender. This carrying capacity was what enabled Majapahit to monopolize the spice trade in the Moluccas and swiftly project military power across the islands.

— Paul Cooper
3:18:05

Succession troubles fractured the empire; tributaries switched sides as civil war dragged on

Hayam Wuruk had no legitimate son; his daughter Kusumawardhani and his illegitimate son Wirabhumi both held claims to succession. Before his death, he divided the kingdom into eastern and western courts to placate both sides, but sowed the seeds of the Paregreg civil war—paregreg in Javanese means ‘broken into pieces’. The civil war raged for two years without resolution, and Majapahit's loose model of rule—extracting only tribute and soldiers from vassals, deploying neither governors nor garrisons—became its fatal weakness. When vassals saw the dynasty consumed by internal strife, they stopped paying tribute. Malacca in Sumatra switched its allegiance to Ayutthaya. The pirate Chen Zuyi seized Palembang and crowned himself king. Brunei and West Borneo soon followed, turning to the rising Ming dynasty.

— Paul Cooper

Glossary

Mandala empire
A model of imperial rule radiating from the capital, extracting only tribute and soldiers from vassals without direct governance, occupation, or appointed officials.
Wayang
Javanese shadow puppet theater, traditionally performed all night, dramatizing myths, epics, and royal histories.
Keris
Javanese traditional wavy-bladed dagger, functioning both as a status symbol and a spiritual talisman.
Jong
Javanese and Malay traditional large oceangoing sailing ship, capable of reaching up to eighty meters in length.
Mahapati
High office in Majapahit's court combining the authority of prime minister and military commander.
