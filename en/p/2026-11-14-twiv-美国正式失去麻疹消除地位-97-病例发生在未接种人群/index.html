Injecting flu vaccine directly into melanomas reverses tumor immune escape

A phase 1 trial gave standard intramuscular flu vaccine to 18 melanoma patients, then added two booster injections directly into the tumor. Pathology analysis showed 79% of samples had increased tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes; nearly 40% showed tumor regression, including 3 cases with complete pathologic response. Plasma cytokines IL-15 and GM-CSF increased significantly; the TH1/Treg ratio rose 1.5-fold; myeloid-derived suppressor cells decreased 70%; exhausted CD4 T cells decreased 65%. This attempted to directly reverse the tumor's immune escape mechanisms, based on findings from mouse models.

— Daniel Griffin
12:45

Primary dengue infection is equally likely to cause death

A woman who contracted dengue from a mosquito bite while walking in Florida died—the only death in the local outbreak. Griffin corrected a common misconception: the public widely believes severe dengue cases mainly result from antibody-dependent enhancement (secondary infection with a different serotype), but globally, dengue deaths occur mostly in children, and one-quarter of travelers hospitalized with dengue are on primary infection. Primary infection is equally likely to be fatal. The US currently has no approved dengue vaccine.

— Daniel Griffin
20:00

Expanding vaccine use without proven efficacy undermines trial integrity

The Democratic Republic of Congo Ebola outbreak has exceeded 11,000 cases and 5,358 confirmed deaths—the second-largest and fastest-growing on record. Médecins Sans Frontières urged WHO to lift research-use restrictions on Ervebo and expand access widely. Griffin opposed this directly. The vaccine's efficacy has not been proven; now is the moment to verify it through clinical trials, not to deploy it at scale while uncertain of its effect. He also noted that the Congo's true shortage is contact-tracing personnel and resources, not vaccine.

— Daniel Griffin
24:11

High vaccination rates elsewhere contained Canada's measles outbreak

Ontario achieved measles elimination in 1998; only 101 cases occurred between 2013 and 2023. An October 2024 exposure following a New Brunswick wedding triggered a 49-week outbreak with 2,377 cumulative cases. In November 2025, the Pan American Health Organization formally withdrew Canada's measles elimination status. Over 90% of cases concentrated in one community with chronically low vaccination; 97% were unvaccinated; three-quarters were under 19; there were 10 cases of congenital measles and 1 death. The key difference: the outbreak was contained by the surrounding population with over 95% vaccination rates and did not spread into the general population—entirely unlike America's society-wide unvaccination, driven by RFK Jr.-promoted vaccine skepticism.

— Daniel Griffin
42:03

Vaccines prevent severe disease, not infection itself

Responding to listeners' skepticism about mRNA-1345's '60% prevention of infection' against RSV, Griffin clarified that this figure measures symptomatic infection, not blocking the virus's initial replication on mucous membranes—vaccines cannot achieve that. He cited a past CDC slogan to summarize a vaccine's true effect: turn the wild into mild. The trial showed 81.8% protection against RSV-related hospitalization.

— Daniel Griffin
46:09

T cell memory outlasts antibody response in vaccinated children

A longitudinal study in Montreal of children aged 3 to 14 found that one month after the second vaccine dose, anti-RBD IgG averaged 1,374 BAU/ml. By six months, levels had declined 856 units, essentially returning to baseline. T cell responses, however, showed nearly no significant decline over 12 months. This mechanism directly informed subsequent advice on whether a child with high fever and vomiting after injection should receive yearly vaccinations. Parents can weigh: antibody protection is short-lived; T cell protection is more durable.

— Daniel Griffin

Glossary

vaccine-derived poliovirus
Live vaccine strain that regains virulence and circulates in low-vaccination populations.
antibody-dependent enhancement
Antibodies to one dengue serotype enhance infection by another, worsening disease.
BAU (binding antibody unit)
WHO-standardized unit for comparing antibody levels across laboratories.
turn the wild into mild
Vaccine effect: reducing disease severity rather than preventing infection.
