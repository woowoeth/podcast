Three Marriages to Navigate France; Brittany Annexed Eighteen Years After Her Death

Anne married three times and was crowned French queen twice; through marriage clauses she compelled France to recognize Brittany's independence; but after her death, the king violated her dying wishes and annexed Brittany into France.

11:16

An eleven-year-old girl inherited a dukedom gutted by war and plague

Her father's unexpected fall from a horse meant she inherited the dukedom at eleven, but she inherited nothing but problems. War and plague had devastated the duchy's resources. Four cities had already been surrendered to France. Treaties had driven away her English allies. Many nobles had defected to France. The treasury was empty—so empty that soldiers' wages went unpaid. On top of it all, she was trapped in a plague-ravaged region and besieged by unwanted suitors. Biographer Helen J. Sanborn captured the hopelessness of her situation through a series of parallel clauses: what harder position could a young girl face?

14:18

A proxy's knee-touch to a bed could make a marriage binding

To escape the marriage options her father had left behind, Anne secretly completed a proxy marriage to Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian I. Because Maximilian was away fighting wars, he couldn't attend in person, so he sent an agent instead. The agent's gesture of consummation was symbolic—a bare knee touching the bed where Anne lay. The marriage was meant to tie Brittany and the Habsburgs together against France. But it immediately angered one suitor left out of the arrangement, Alain d'Albreth, who responded by handing over the fortress of Nantes to the French.

— Tracy
16:21

A two-month siege forced her toward marriage with the French king

When French King Charles VIII learned of Anne's secret marriage to Maximilian, he marched an army to Rennes, the duchy's capital, and began a siege. He even offered 120,000 crowns to buy the entire dukedom outright. After two months of siege, even Brittany's own parliament advised Anne to marry Charles to end the war—not because they liked him, but because their military situation was untenable. Anne's army depended on foreign aid from England and Spain. Maximilian, nominally her husband, had abandoned any pretense of defending the marriage. Anne decided that marrying Charles offered Brittany its best chance for survival. The Pope annulled her marriage to Maximilian.

— Tracy
20:28

The Duke of Orleans threw a ball Anne believed taunted her grief

Anne's only son with Charles to survive infancy died at age three. The grief sent the king's health into decline. His physicians prescribed entertainment and distraction. The Duke of Orleans—who would inherit the throne if Charles had no heirs—organized an elaborate masked ball. The spectacle was so over-the-top that Anne became convinced Orleans was celebrating her son's death. Her suspicion that he was gloating over the loss was so strong that she had him sent to Italy, where he was fighting in the ongoing wars. Years later, with a cruel twist of fate, this same Duke of Orleans became her second husband as Louis XII.

21:31

The king died from a low beam at a tennis match

Several days before his death, Charles's health had been deteriorating. One afternoon he accompanied Anne to watch a tennis match. As he entered the venue, his head struck a low horizontal beam. Attendants caught him before he fell, escorted him to a seat—and then he suddenly collapsed and never regained consciousness. This was not the only royal death attributed to tennis; the exact cause of Charles's death has remained a matter of historical debate. The grief devastated Anne so completely that she locked herself away and wept for two days before emerging to sign the documents that reestablished her authority as Duchess of Brittany.

23:34

He claimed to be bewitched to escape his first marriage

Anne's marriage contract with Charles stipulated that if he died without a male heir, she would marry the next king. By now that was Louis XII, though he was already married to Jeanne. Louis sought an annulment. He claimed Jeanne's body had a defect that prevented consummation. Jeanne had him investigated. She produced witnesses who testified that Louis had boasted about their sex life to anyone who would listen. When the testimony turned against him, Louis changed tactics and claimed he'd been bewitched. The Pope granted the annulment. Jeanne received a pension, went on to found a convent, and was canonized as a saint in 1950.

— Tracy
29:41

Her vision for Claude's marriage would preserve Brittany; Louis's would end it

Anne's fiercest struggle with Louis XII erupted over the marriage of their daughter Claude. Anne had arranged, even when Claude was still an infant, for her to marry Charles of the Habsburgs—a marriage that would let Brittany maintain its independence through dynastic ties. But once Claude married into the French royal family, Brittany's absorption into France would become inevitable. Louis unilaterally broke Anne's arrangement and betrothed Claude instead to Francis, the heir to the throne. Anne's anger was absolute. She left the royal court and retreated to Brittany, refusing to return. Louis, in turn, grew increasingly bitter toward her.

33:48

Eighteen years after Anne's death, France annexed Brittany anyway

On her deathbed, Anne left explicit instructions: Brittany should go to her younger daughter Renée, not to Claude, who was destined to become a French queen. Anne hoped this would preserve Brittany's independence. But the new king, Francis I, ignored her wishes entirely. In 1528 he married Renée off to the Duke of Ferrara. In 1532 he invited the Breton parliament to formally unite with France, and they consented. The lifetime of marriages, diplomatic maneuvering, and political struggle that Anne had waged to keep Brittany independent was erased eighteen years after her death.
