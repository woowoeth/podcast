Large models' most insidious error in long documents: silently omitting information

Benchmark testing reveals a paradox: leading models excel at extracting from long documents, yet silently lose information in ways production systems can't detect.

2:02

The founder pivoted to documents even after securing a YC offer

Starting in memory augmentation, Reducto received a YC offer but decided to pivot on day one. The reasoning: agents would increasingly handle high-stakes domains like medical records, where accuracy determines patient outcomes. A misread checkbox isn't a small error—it can change treatment decisions. This insight drove the pivot from memory infrastructure to documents as the foundational bottleneck.

— Adit Abraham
9:19

AI agents' self-correction outperforms human annotation on structured extraction

Reducto's Deep Extract framework doesn't just extract; it makes agents verify their own output—checking invoice line totals against declared totals, for instance. On many structured extraction benchmarks, agents outperform human annotators. The reason: human taggers skip rows, take shortcuts, or fill partial answers. These errors became embedded in ‘ground truth’ datasets. Adit calls this the first time he's seen agents genuinely beat the human baseline.

— Adit Abraham
11:24

At one cent per page, processing everything becomes the default strategy

Reducto's $0.01-per-page pricing emerged after processing 7 billion pages. At this cost, economics flipped: instead of choosing which documents deserve high-precision treatment, you run them all through. The company now processes billions of pages monthly and faces the next challenge—maintaining cost and quality at hundred-billion-page scale. Adit argues past AI products were retrieval-gated chatbots over limited data, but real agents need economic freedom to search widely without cost anxiety.

— Adit Abraham
19:30

Frontier model labs won't build document infrastructure because they're racing to AGI

Asked why leading model companies don't solve document processing, Adit points to misaligned incentives: AGI labs are in an arms race for super-intelligence, while document processing—however hard—is infrastructure work. Specialization wins. A general vision encoder must handle selfies-to-Ghibli synthesis and document reading; Reducto's model only needs to excel at one task. This focus delivers extreme cost and speed advantages over frontier models at the same accuracy.

— Adit Abraham
21:33

Long-document extraction fails on recall, not precision—models silently skip content

Reducto's independent benchmark with MicroOne produced a counterintuitive finding: on long-document extraction, leading models achieve good precision—what they extract is usually right. They fail on recall: models omit information without flagging anything is missing. Deep Extract solves this by adding agent validation rules that know when to double-check. This silent failure is nearly impossible to catch in production.

— Adit Abraham
24:43

Published benchmarks are marketing noise; measure on your own data distribution

Adit critiques how AI benchmarks became marketing theater. Take a public benchmark, rotate documents one degree and add light blur—the vendor degrades while competitors hold steady. Most users never test these perturbations. His advice: use LLMs as judges, test against your actual data distribution rather than published leaderboards. When everyone claims SOTA, benchmarks become useless for real decisions.

— Adit Abraham
32:49

One word wrong—chest pain vs. pain—and an insurance claim gets denied forever

Reducto's medical customer faced a real case where OCR confused ‘chest pain’ for ‘pain.’ When doctors queried patient history by keyword, nothing matched; the claim was denied. Understaffed medical systems can't manually verify OCR output across thousands-of-page records. In this scenario, Reducto found OCR accuracy sometimes fell below 50%. A single term error cascades through systems designed to trust extracted data.

— Adit Abraham
33:49

80% of Medicare appeals overturn original decisions, exposing systematic approval failure

Millions of Medicare claims face prior-authorization denials yearly; 80% of appeals ultimately reverse the decision. This isn't operator error—it's systemic failure in paper-based workflows. Similar gridlock appears in USCIS immigration backlogs. Adit's insight: if accuracy, cost, and latency approach zero-friction, frontier models could automate these reviews and eliminate the appeals process itself. The reversal rate is evidence of process breakdown waiting for AI.

— Adit Abraham

Glossary

Vision Language Model (VLM)
A model that understands both images and text semantics, replacing template-based OCR systems.
Intelligent Document Processing (IDP)
The traditional industry term for document processing; Reducto sees its mission as broader infrastructure work.
Harness
A framework surrounding a model for verification, validation, and self-correction—not a single model call.
Recall
The percentage of information that should be extracted and actually is; long-document extraction's metric most likely to fail silently.
