Invest Like the Best 2026-11-06

When VC consensus forms around a playbook, its returns begin to fade

As VCs crowd into passive shareholding in consensus picks like Databricks and Anthropic, Naimi warns: history shows almost no strategy that everyone finds simple sustains long-term outperformance.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

2:09

Second-tier allocations in the same round now cost 3 to 5 times as much

VCs that miss the main allocation get a second tier in the same round, priced 3–5x the main. Silicon Valley calls this the "cuck tranche." Naimi reframes it not as market failure but as these companies being capital-intensive enough that founders won't close in one draw. Most VC shops have been frozen out of one or two allocation tiers at least once.

— Ramtin Naimi
10:29

Mega-funds deploy 2 to 3 times faster than their budgets assume

VCs size funds on 18–24 months of planned deployment, but mega-platform funds have actually deployed in 12–18 months—two to three times faster than projected. This forces a binary choice: maintain position size but write far fewer checks, or write the same number of checks and accept dilution. The first tests stock-picking skill; the second bets that diversification hedges not being a top-tier picker.

— Ramtin Naimi
12:35

Liquidity for most LPs depends on success in just four companies

Some LPs put 35–50% of assets into private equity. Drill down and 30–50% of that sits in just four names: SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Databricks. Real LP cash doesn't flow from sector health—it flows from exposure to these four. LPs without these bets are drawing almost no liquidity this cycle.

— Ramtin Naimi
15:43

Burning billions to reach 85% margin demands new finance tools

Anthropic's S-1 revealed $140 billion cumulative fundraising, $10 billion burned, $132 billion on the balance sheet, and 85% gross margin. Extreme burn married to high margin forces innovation: General Catalyst's customer-value fund, lending on "every $10M deployed unlocks $30M revenue," has proven viable. But the industry still needs purpose-built infrastructure finance instead of pure equity.

— Ramtin Naimi
21:53

Nearly 100 model labs burn annual compute budgets in single training runs

The NeoLab landscape exploded from two or three players to 80–90 companies at $1–5 billion valuations. Naimi's example: one firm is raising a second $1 billion for a single training run next year, then expects another $5 billion the year after. Real constraint isn't equity—it's securing 5–10x leverage as non-dilutive debt at rational terms. Recent stress in public infrastructure debt has spiked that cost.

— Ramtin Naimi
24:01

Endpoint assumptions jumped from $100 billion to $1 trillion; seed valuations followed

In 2016, best-case endpoint was $10 billion. By 2021, Stripe and Databricks pushed it to $100 billion. Now SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI have proven $1 trillion is real, so every early company gets priced as if it can reach $1 trillion—even entering at $100 million.

— Ramtin Naimi
26:05

Backing unfashionable bets early separates top 5 percent from top 25 percent

Passive plays on hot sectors top out around 25th percentile. Top 5% requires early conviction in neglected bets. Example: Conduit, a brain-computer interface company. Nine months ago, BCI was ignored. The team built a headset reading neural activity at 80% accuracy. Eight to ten weeks later, BCI went hot—Conduit's round drew 13 term sheets and massively oversubscribed. Smarter investors see the wave before the crowd.

— Ramtin Naimi
33:18

Crowding into "safe" mega-rounds is itself the largest risk signal

When early markets are chaotic, one hedge is mega-sized positions in proven winners. Stripe, valued at $100 billion in 2021, is now worth $179 billion, yielding 11–12% annualized over five years—still placing in the top 25% for VC returns. But over the past 18 months, VCs have embraced this same playbook: huge checks into a handful of known bets, stay passive. History shows almost no strategy that everyone finds simple sustains long-term market outperformance.

— Ramtin Naimi
39:32

What looked reckless ($100M in 2018) now looks prudent ($800M in 2026)

Abstract's Fund I, $100 million in 2018, drew LP complaints for being oversized. The latest, $800 million, is praised as disciplined. In retrospect, Naimi's regret: undersizing each fund. The same returns could have been generated with twice the capital, and he'd have had more dry powder for leads instead of being pushed into follow-on checks.

— Ramtin Naimi
