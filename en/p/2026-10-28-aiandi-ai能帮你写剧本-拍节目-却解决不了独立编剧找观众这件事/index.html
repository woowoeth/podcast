AI Can Help You Write and Produce—But Can't Solve Finding Your Audience

This Emmy-winning screenwriter breaks content creation into three stages: ideation, production, and reaching audiences. AI excels at the first two, but there is no working infrastructure yet to get one thousand viewers willing to pay you monthly.

What an accidental Tumblr taught him about audience

While running Best Week Ever, Fred opened a Tumblr just for internal production notes and discovered 10,000 to 15,000 people watching. The accident revealed a core truth: ‘opening your work to the audience is your lifeline.’ This insight shaped every career move since—from TV producer to Twitter's head of content, each step brought him closer to his audience.

— Fred Graver
13:43

AI is a writers' room, not a ghostwriter

Fred's method for writers using AI: first, write a ‘who I am’ document with your influences and taste; second, write a ‘what I'm making’ document, even if vague. Then ask AI three questions: does this idea fit this persona, is there an audience for it, where do they gather? The AI doesn't write for you—it functions as a multi-faceted writers' room, helping you think through an idea.

— Fred Graver
19:57

Creation splits into three stages where AI handles the first two well

Fred breaks the workflow into ideation/architecture (producing scripts), production (pre-production through final cut—his AI-animated series uses a writing team, animation studios in India, and engineers in Zurich), and reaching audiences (marketing and distribution). Hollywood's marketing teams already use AI to rewrite trailers hourly based on ticket-purchase data. Scorsese using AI to generate storyboards shows where the workflow meets resistance.

— Fred Graver
25:32

Tools are universal; craft is rare

Against fears of ‘AI slop,’ Fred points to a lesson from Annie Leibovitz: everyone now carries a camera better than the one she used for thirty years, yet few shoot like her. Tools spreading never means everyone produces quality. The difference lies in depth of self-knowledge before you begin—knowing who you are and what you make—and AI cannot substitute for that work.

— Fred Graver
26:40

Empty yourself for a day before touching AI

Fred's practice: before opening any AI tool, spend a day or two handwriting or dictating everything you know about this project. His reasoning is stark—once you open AI, you lose the chance at purity, at something that belongs entirely to you. This ‘emptying yourself’ step is the easiest to skip and the most critical in the entire creative pipeline.

— Fred Graver
31:48

Claude learned to think like him after hundreds of exchanges

Working with Compound Writing and Claude Code, Fred iterated on ideas through dozens of exchanges. Claude then independently proposed a series like Brooklyn Coffee Shop but starring 60- to 70-year-olds running a nursing home's Facebook account—complete with audience analysis, production plan, and market fit for the 55+ Facebook-active demographic. Fred felt Claude had absorbed his taste through their long collaboration.

— Fred Graver
39:02

Success is knowing what your audience wants before they know

When asked his core principle, Fred says success is ‘knowing what your audience wants before they themselves know it.’ AI can surface relevant material, but it cannot produce the odd personal quirks that make characters alive—the way Homer Simpson's voice actor ad-libbed a single word that made the character work. That only comes from real humans in a real writers' room, not from models.

— Fred Graver
45:17

A deliberately unforgiving Claude agent keeps him accountable

Fred's daily workflow lives inside Claude: he feeds it a folder of old work and repeatedly tests whether an idea feels like him, has real audiences, and fits real platforms. He built a Claude agent as his ‘morning accountability officer’ and made it deliberately difficult—if he pivots to a new idea at night, the agent grills him the next morning about abandoning yesterday's thread. At the polish stage, he uses the Pixar ‘because/therefore’ framework, runs it through ChatGPT (which he describes as ‘having read every textbook and seen everything but understanding nothing’), then feeds it into NotebookLM to hear it as a podcast.

— Fred Graver

Glossary

Compound Writing
A reusable AI workflow plugin that codifies a domain expert's best practices into a tool other creators can use.
Thousand true fans
Kevin Kelly's theory that a creator needs only about 1,000 paying fans to sustain themselves full-time.
Because/therefore framework
A story structure technique used by Pixar and others, also known as the Ken Adams framework, that builds narrative through causal chains.
Flow
The psychological state of complete absorption in creative work, losing track of time and self-consciousness.
