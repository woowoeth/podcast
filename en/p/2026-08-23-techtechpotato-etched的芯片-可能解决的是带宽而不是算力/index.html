What Etched's Chip Actually Solves May Be Bandwidth, Not Compute

Etched uses 0.45-volt ultra-low-voltage transistors plus cross-chip direct SRAM links to weld the transformer into hardware, but the host suspects the real problem being solved is bandwidth, not compute.

AI chipstransformer-specific chipscompute efficiencyfundingbandwidth bottleneck
Two technologies that had only been announced by name, with no explanation of how they work, get broken down clearly for the first time — but chip power consumption, bandwidth, the compiler and customer scale all remain unanswered. Information density is medium-to-high.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:04

The founder himself admits this chip may be a waste

Two years ago Etched went public with the plan to build a chip just for transformers. At the time the transformer was already the dominant architecture for language models, but still a very specific workload type. The public pitch was that a chip built only for transformers could run fast and cheap, at the cost of becoming useless if the technology path shifted. The CEO himself admits that if the market turns, everything they have done may be for nothing. This is a bet whose failure risk was acknowledged publicly from day one.

2:05

A 23-year-old dropout plus a chip veteran is what raises big money

CEO Gavin Uberti is a 23-year-old dropout from Harvard's computer science department. Co-founder Robert Wacken also came out of Harvard, but on the tech-transfer side, helping the company get off the ground and raise money. CTO Mark Ross has Cisco and Cypress Semi on his résumé; his LinkedIn says he led the team that built the market's first multiport Ethernet switch, and he was also involved in Soho-related technology. The combination of a young founder and a seasoned chip veteran is part of why the company could raise large rounds.

3:05

The reporter's visit showed blinking lights, not silicon

When the company first appeared, it claimed an eight-chip chassis could challenge Nvidia, claiming 500,000 tokens per second — many times the numbers Nvidia was advertising at the time. Around the same time, EE Times reporter Sally visited the headquarters in person and saw a rack of servers blinking and buzzing, but never actually saw the silicon itself. It was more than a year later that the company truly came out of stealth mode and the chip taped out; what Sally saw was very likely a test chip.

5:07

Low voltage fits more compute, at the cost of frequency

A typical high-end desktop CPU runs at about 1.2 to 1.3 volts, and the most efficient desktop laptop processors sit close to 1 volt. Etched's low-voltage inference chip runs at roughly 450 millivolts, or 0.45 volts, already approaching the threshold voltage at which a transistor can just barely switch. The lower the voltage, the more compute units you can pack in, at the cost of frequency. The only high-compute industry currently using these ultra-low-voltage transistors at scale is Bitcoin mining, because mining algorithms need very little data in and out — whereas machine learning is precisely a workload of moving data around, which is the next problem Etched has to solve.

8:13

A cache shouldn't belong only to its own chip

Inside the Etched chip package there is HBM, and inside the chip there is a large block of SRAM scratch pad shared by all the compute units on the die. The second technology, cluster scale memory, opens that SRAM cache over a high-speed interconnect so other chips can access it directly — something like RDMA across chips, reading and writing another chip's cache and scratch pad directly rather than only using it on-die. High-speed interconnect needs voltage, so the power saved by low-voltage compute may be diverted to feed this cross-chip communication.

9:15

A full chassis of black cables reveals where the power budget goes

In the photos of Etched's server hardware, the first thing that stands out is the dense mass of black cables, noticeably more than in Nvidia or AMD systems. Some of the cabling is for liquid cooling, but a large part is believed to be for high-bandwidth interconnect like cluster scale memory. From this the host infers that what Etched is really solving may not be compute but the bandwidth of chip-to-chip communication — the power headroom saved by low-voltage compute is being spent on piling up interconnect bandwidth.

10:18

Hard-coded doesn't mean fixed; it's still programmable

Neither of the two technology names — low-voltage inference and cluster scale memory — mentions the transformer, which suggests the real binding happens in the physical layout of the compute units: this is a chip hard-coded for transformers, but still programmable, able to run different versions of the transformer, unlike another specialized chip design reported a few weeks ago that is far more rigid. The host hasn't yet had the chance to sit down with the team and check how transcendental functions are computed, how memory addresses are managed, and how data movement in and out of the scratch pad is tracked — if all of that is left to the compiler, then you have to hope their compiler is genuinely good.

12:19

Before selling a million, prove you can sell a thousand

Etched says it has drawn interest from a number of hyperscalers, but the host quotes Jim Keller: to sell a million chips, you first have to prove you can sell 100,000; to sell 100,000, prove you can sell 10,000; to sell 10,000, prove you can sell 1,000 — the question startups should always be asked is, where are your hundred-chip, thousand-chip, ten-thousand-chip customers. Co-founder Robert Wacken said at a fundraising event that Etched's core internal creed is ‘ship’ — getting the chip out a year earlier than other startups founded at the same time — and that they intend to keep up that rapid iteration cadence for the next generation.

Glossary

tape out
The final step in chip design, when the design is finalized and sent to the fab for actual manufacturing.
RDMA
A technology that bypasses the other device's CPU to read and write another machine's memory or cache directly.
threshold voltage
The minimum voltage needed for a transistor to just barely turn on (conduct).
cluster scale memory
Etched's proposed technology that lets multiple chips share their respective SRAM caches over a high-speed interconnect.
