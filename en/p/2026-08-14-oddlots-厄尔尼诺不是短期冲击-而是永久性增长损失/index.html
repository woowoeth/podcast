El Niño Is Not a Short-Term Shock but a Permanent Loss of Growth

El Niño is not just a short-term weather shock; research shows it permanently depresses the economic growth trajectory, a single strong El Niño can cause trillions of dollars in losses within five years, and we have not adapted.

El Niño is not warming, it is heat changing location

El Niño is a phenomenon of ocean-atmosphere interaction in the tropical Pacific, part of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO). Normally, trade winds blow from east to west, piling warm surface water in the western Pacific to form the warm pool. When the trade winds weaken, the warm water spreads eastward, reconfiguring the distribution of global weather energy. It is like the difference between a room with a single heater in one corner (La Niña/neutral) and one where an entire wall is a radiator (El Niño): the heat exposure of the whole atmosphere is completely different.

This one could be the strongest since 1950

All forecast models point to at least the strongest El Niño since 1950, possibly exceeding historical strong events such as 1877-78, 1882-83, 1997-98 and 2015-16. The Australian Bureau of Meteorology's Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI) forecast reaches 3.3, which is unprecedented. We are already in an El Niño alert state, and every forecast shows this event will be warmer than the 2016 event.

— Justin Mankin
15:30

Peruvian farmers five hundred years ago could already predict El Niño

Modern scientific understanding of El Niño began in the 20th century, but archaeological evidence shows that five hundred years ago Peruvian potato farmers could predict El Niño based on the visibility of the Pleiades. El Niño changes high-cloud distribution over the eastern Pacific, affecting observations of the Pleiades, and farmers adjusted planting dates accordingly. This shows the value of folk meteorological knowledge and also shows that humanity's history of adapting to El Niño is in fact very long.

22:39

The peak has not arrived, yet the South Asian monsoon is already delayed

El Niño usually peaks between December and February, but its effects have already begun. Since entering alert status in June, the South Asian monsoon has been delayed, directly affecting food security for billions of people. The delayed monsoon forces farmers to irrigate, pushing up diesel demand, which in turn affects prices and politics. There is a non-trivial probability that 2026 becomes the hottest year on record, and 2027 is almost locked in for record heat.

— Justin Mankin
31:46

The loss five years later is 5.7 trillion, not 36 billion

A 2023 study published in Science shows that El Niño is not a simple level effect (recovering after the shock) but systematically depresses the economic growth rate, causing losses to accumulate indefinitely over time. Taking the 1997-98 El Niño as an example, five years later the global loss was about 5.7 trillion dollars, far exceeding the 36 billion dollars estimated at the time. This permanent impact stems from simultaneous disasters worldwide, multiple supply chain disruptions, and reduced efficiency of capital allocation.

— Justin Mankin
41:03

Exposed so many times, humanity still has not learned to adapt

Although humanity has long been exposed to El Niño, research shows we have not adapted effectively. The evidence is that El Niño's economic losses remain enormous, indicating we have not learned from feedback. Adaptation strategies include early warning systems, emergency management, insurance products and so on, but coordination at the global level is lacking. El Niño can serve as a testing ground for adaptation strategies, providing a reference for responding to global warming.

— Justin Mankin
49:12

El Niño is a randomized controlled trial run by nature

Although El Niño occurs periodically, the timing and strength of its occurrence are random, like a randomly assigned treatment. Using this natural experiment, one can compare differences in economic growth between El Niño years and non-El Niño years to identify its causal impact. This is similar to a medical randomized controlled trial: despite the periodicity, the randomness is sufficient to isolate the effect.

Glossary

ENSO / El Niño-Southern Oscillation
A periodic phenomenon of ocean-atmosphere interaction in the tropical Pacific, including El Niño and La Niña.
Teleconnection
The physical link between climate phenomena such as El Niño and weather and climate in distant regions.
RONI / Relative Oceanic Niño Index
A sea surface temperature anomaly index based on a rolling baseline, used to measure El Niño strength.
Level effect
A shock causes output level to temporarily deviate, then return to its original trend.
