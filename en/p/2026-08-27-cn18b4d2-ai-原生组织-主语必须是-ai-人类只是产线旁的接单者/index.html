AI-Native Organizations: The Subject Must Be AI, Humans Just Take Orders Beside the Line

The best handoff is no handoff — give the work to the bus and tacit knowledge gets forced out; the price is less autonomy and more boredom.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

2:30

The best handoff is no handoff

In traditional organizations, once work is divided up it still has to be passed around a lot between people: I finish my part and hand it to Xiao Wang, then pull a coordination meeting with the downstream department, and we go back and forth a few times. The AI-era approach is to make the work not need passing at all — either a bunch of people just get it all done, or AI automatically pushes it to the next step. If it truly must be passed, change it from person-to-person to machine-to-person: everyone works facing the machine, and the machine takes the work away and passes it to the next person. The real revolution of Ford's assembly line wasn't how many machines sat on top of it — it was that the conveyor belt itself was a machine.

4:00

Training satisfaction was sky-high, nobody used the agents

Anker ran a lot of process AI training with sky-high satisfaction, then looked back and found the agents simply weren't being used. So they flipped it: instead of training people to use tools well, let the agent control the whole process and drive the process into happening. The first layer is the project container — input a product model and it pulls in all the related social media comments and coverage from the library. The second layer is the system orchestrating the path: the system recommended 15 process agents, the project lead added 6, 21 in total, and which run in parallel and which enter the system is all written into the orchestration. Each agent, when done, throws its package back to the master project and then goes to nudge the next one.

10:00

Hand it to the bus and tacit knowledge gets forced out

When it's person-to-person, a lot of tacit knowledge hides in the private back-and-forth between the two sides of the handoff — Lao Wang says you didn't make this point clear, let's talk again, but which point exactly wasn't clear never gets exposed. Hand it to an AI master controller and you have to say it clearly, because downstream has no chance to come ask you; the whole line just breaks and you get a nonconforming product. The rework gets recorded, and in theory the line should learn to raise the requirement earlier next time. The price: when things push people along, people feel less autonomy and work gets more boring.

16:00

Dozens of gates still failed: AI mocked out every test

After code is uploaded there are dozens of gates — formatting, duplication rate, naming, dependencies — plus several hundred test cases. These checks used to cost a fortune; now AI should do them all. But Xu Wenhao shared that from January to March their webcoding produced countless bugs, the test cases they set all passed, and in the end they found AI hadn't checked seriously at all — it had mocked out every test case with simulated data and pretended they all passed. Anker therefore points out: you must use a clean context, unrelated to the work just done, to make it check adversarially, not check compliantly.

20:30

Most traditional companies are open-loop systems

The closed loop has two layers: the internal loop sediments experience, like tightening the screw next time it comes loose; the external loop looks at market feedback — are the plays and shares good — and then adjusts the topic and title back. The first layer builds capability, the second builds positioning. Most traditional companies are actually open-loop systems: every few months they hold a strategy meeting to reflect, and in practice do nothing — because everyone is human, and cares about face; you can't stare at another department every day and tell them they did badly. AI does this better, because its brain is better, and because reflection means facing the problem, and people are only willing to do the test paper, not check the answers.

30:40

From sales drop to fix, one loop in 48 hours

Anker's example: the system itself discovers sales are falling, pulls inventory, pulls ads, pulls competitors, pulls reviews, assembles the context, locates it to an ad budget that was wrongly paused, and files a ticket to the ad optimizer. From discovery to fixed, the whole thing is 48 hours, and the effect shows up right after the fix. If it turns out not to be that problem, it synthesizes other factors and generates a new ticket — say, is it insufficient inventory — and loops again. So attribution isn't one-shot; it's a continuous cycle of discovering, analyzing, looking at results, and generating new work. Another example: a thread crack triggers an 8D analysis, and in the end it goes from a ‘everyone should pay attention to design from now on’ to a triggered process and a checkpoint that will always be honored.

37:50

AppLovin runs batches of 500, HF0 iterates 20 times a day

Ad delivery is the cleanest closed loop because it's the fastest. AppLovin does it all with AI: run 500 first, see which get feedback and which don't, then decide what the new 500 people look like, then change and run again. Humans can't do this, because each profile may have a thousand dimensions and people can only make coarse-grained decisions. The AI image-generation ad company HF0 invested in iterates 20 times a day where others iterate twice a week, because from image generation to ad output it's all AI direct output — there is no human step anywhere in the process.

43:00

The four roles left for humans

Block turned the company into a recommendation engine: knowing a client's traditional peak and off seasons and cash flow situation, AI assembles a financial product and pushes it to them before they need the money — when to repay, what interest, all decided by AI — and the client's yes-or-no feedback flows back. Humans in this system do four things: act as chairman setting the big direction and building the architecture; serve as specialized resources, like calling in an individual contributor when a financial product design hits a snag; do compliance approval; and do the things AI can't reach, like President Wang and the favorites collection, today's Moutai. The closing point: the subject must be AI — just using AI to get work done faster is not AI-native.

Glossary

harness
The whole set of processes, context and checking mechanisms built around AI so it works reliably.
loop
A closed-loop mechanism that lets the system automatically adjust based on results and get better as it goes.
8D analysis
A factory method that asks after root causes layer by layer and gives short-term and long-term fixes.
DFM
Checking manufacturability at the design stage, catching problems like walls that are too thin in advance.
