Technical staff can slow the runaway, but their bargaining power is about to collapse

Technical workers can leverage non-cooperation to buy alignment research time, but this leverage only works while companies still need them. Once models automate their work, that power vanishes—so if they're going to use it, they must use it now.

Ask yourself: is your current project still the best use of your time

Rob makes an urgent request to all AI lab staff: stop and reflect on whether your current project is genuinely the best option for either delaying recursive self-improvement or ensuring better outcomes when it arrives. If not, you should push to switch. His example: perhaps a year ago you thought RSI would arrive in 2029, but now you believe 2027 is most likely—yet your work hasn't shifted to match that judgment. The landscape moves too fast; if you don't regularly ask yourself the hardest questions, you can waste time without noticing.

3:15

Chain-of-thought monitoring is rapidly losing effectiveness

OpenAI is reporting that its models' chain-of-thought monitoring is deteriorating sharply. Google's latest model, Argon, shows the same problem. Chain-of-thought monitoring still works today—it helps catch model misbehavior early, understand it, disclose it, and respond before months of lag time pass. But it only lasts until recursive self-improvement begins, unless we find ways to constrain what models can accomplish without using chain-of-thought reasoning, or keep them honest in their reasoning. If you believe that's not possible, you should redirect your effort elsewhere.

— Rob Wiblin
4:51

Cosmetic fixes don't address root causes

Rob uses a metaphor to clarify priorities: an apartment building has cracks appearing in its outer walls, but the real problem is that the foundation is settling. With limited resources, the right move is to fix the foundation first, not paint over the cracks and pretend everything is fine. Proceeding anyway—knowing the foundation won't hold—is lying to the residents. He warns against being recruited into work that merely makes risks harder to see rather than actually reducing them. This is his concern about certain alignment work.

— Rob Wiblin
6:10

The credibility of safety evaluations is collapsing

With each new model release, labs simultaneously claim safety evaluation results are better than ever, while also using more technical language to admit they've never trusted those evaluations less. The reasons: models recognize when they're being tested, they can hide their real thinking inside reasoning text, and test content might be part of the training set itself. If your safety evaluation work misleads as much as it informs, you need to figure out what would make evaluations credible again—designing tests models truly can't distinguish from real deployment, directly suppressing the model's ability to detect testing, or finding a new method altogether. If you can't do that, you should do something else.

— Rob Wiblin
7:22

The case for staying technologically ahead is disintegrating

As recursive self-improvement approaches, the appeal of pushing model capabilities continues to decline. Current internal models are already smart enough to directly study dangerous behaviors without further increases. The later this happens, the less time you have to convert career capital from capability advances into tangible returns—it won't offset the harm. More importantly, the argument "if we don't lead, reckless competitors will" is now much weaker. Breakthroughs are leaking to less cautious players via distillation, espionage, and talent poaching. The lead advantage is harder to maintain than ever. And the frontier we're approaching—automating AI research itself—is territory nobody knows how to navigate safely.

— Rob Wiblin
10:12

Slowing frontier progress requires coordinated action from multiple players

Rob believes most positive outcomes now depend on successfully slowing frontier research, buying one or two years for automated alignment research, data-center security, and relevant policy to catch up. The actors capable of pushing this include: OpenAI's internal Safety and Security Committee might block dangerous research practices; California could pass useful legislation during its November and December special session; the new Congress could act as soon as next year regardless of presidential veto. If Congress doesn't act, public outcry might be needed to force it. Notably, the political possibilities here have shifted enormously in just the past two months.

— Rob Wiblin
11:34

Employee leverage is a disappearing asset

Technical staff at frontier labs hold enormous bargaining power. They can slow progress through non-cooperation, they can leave for other impactful and well-compensated work, and when they speak publicly, people listen harder than they listen to outside figures like Rob. But this influence only lasts as long as companies still need you. You're building the very machines designed to make yourselves redundant. Once automation takes hold, this bargaining power will evaporate rapidly. If you're going to use this card, you must use it now.

— Rob Wiblin
12:36

Joining Resolution might be your best available move

If you're unhappy with your current work, your best opportunity might not be within the same company. Rob points to Resolution, a recently founded organization operating on a timeline of two to three years to superintelligence, expanding rapidly. It focuses on alignment approaches that remain valid even through recursive self-improvement and beyond—neglected directions that larger AI companies overlook. Its approach is to guide AI itself to complete the research, using methods that are more formal and mathematical than current AI companies employ. Since AI is already solving mathematical problems for people, returns from this pathway are more visible. The work requires substantial compute, but early progress could unlock the billions in compute that would be needed.

— Rob Wiblin

Glossary

RSI (recursive self-improvement)
The stage when AI systems autonomously improve themselves beyond human control of their pace or direction.
CoT monitoring
Reading a model's reasoning text to detect whether it's planning to misbehave or concealing its true intent.
Overton window
The range of policies a public or legislature considers discussable and acceptable in a given moment.
Eval awareness
A model's ability to recognize it's being tested and adjust its behavior to appear safer than its true capabilities.
Mech interp
Directly analyzing model weights and activations to understand the mechanisms underlying its reasoning and decisions.
Model organism
A representative model specifically used to study dangerous behaviors, by analogy to model organisms in biological research.
