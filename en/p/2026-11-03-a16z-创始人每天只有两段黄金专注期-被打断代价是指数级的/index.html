On workout days, take caffeine immediately after light exposure, not a half-hour later

When scheduling a workout, Huberman drinks caffeine-containing yerba mate immediately after sun exposure—not waiting even a minute, rather than following a fixed timetable. This means he ties caffeine intake to whether he's exercising that day, not his wake time. On rest days, he postpones caffeine later.

Staying injury-free for 35 years meant never chasing others' training routines

Huberman credits his health at 50 not to mastering specific training methods but to a 35-year discipline: never switching to another person's routine on impulse just because they challenged him to try it—the easiest path to injury. He does only movements his body feels good doing, like rowing machine and Airdyne sprints instead of cycling.

— Andrew Huberman
11:18

Two hard sets yield results with one-third the volume of conventional training

His strength structure is fixed: one light warm-up set, one medium set, then only two sets pushed to the edge of form breakdown, resting three to four minutes between. Per bodypart, just two or three movements. A multi-year soccer athlete testing his method reported it was one-third her previous volume but yielded results she found almost unbelievable.

— Andrew Huberman
21:35

Blue light sensitivity varies enormously; your skepticism might actually be justified

On pre-sleep blue light, Huberman notes enormous individual variation: some people spike cortisol and lose sleep from barely-visible light even through closed eyelids; others are completely insensitive, for reasons still unclear and independent of eye color. He mentions Marc Andreessen publicly saying he quit alcohol but dismisses blue-light protection entirely—Huberman acknowledges individual variation is real.

— Andrew Huberman
26:43

One supplement cuts infection risk by more than half in flu exposure studies

He cites a study exposing people to flu virus: those taking placebo infected at over 70%, while those taking N-acetylcysteine (NAC, a glutathione precursor) infected at just over 20%. He takes one dose daily himself, escalating to three when he feels illness coming or sees others sick.

— Andrew Huberman
30:55

Your social media phone needs to be locked away, not just silenced

Huberman admits social media is his weakest point: he keeps X and Instagram on a separate phone locked in a ‘super-prison-grade’ safe, accessible only at set times. He says this embarrasses him—ideally humans should self-regulate. He cites research: even with phone in a backpack, powered off, or under a chair, the brain burns extra cognitive resources to maintain equivalent focus. Phone completely absent, those resources release.

— Andrew Huberman
37:18

Peak cognitive windows contain exponentially more value than makeup work done later

For him, the two best cognitive windows are roughly 9:30 am to 12:30 pm and 4:35 pm to 7 pm—but the window varies per person, and he advises everyone to find theirs. The core warning: if work during that peak window is diverted to company-unrelated matters, the loss isn't just those three or four hours. Output during that window is exponentially higher than output from makeup work done later, and it also compresses personal time.

— Andrew Huberman
46:30

Startups are endurance races that demand weekly reset to avoid burnout

Huberman relays neuroscientist Bob Knight's early advice: first determine your daily maximum sustainable work output, then find a restorative method to reset once a week—Knight fishes, Huberman hikes and socializes. He notes that people who run on high stimulation continuously tend to be the ones who disappear last; sustaining intensity matters as much as the intensity itself.

— Andrew Huberman
