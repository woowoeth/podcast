Nvidia's financial guarantees only take effect after data centers are built and powered on

Nvidia has provided over $100 billion in guarantees and compute commitments to CoreWeave and other companies, but its new interim CEO made clear these only take effect after projects are complete and equipment deployed. Construction and financing phase risks remain with borrowers and lenders.

13:11

Nvidia's guarantees only take effect after equipment deployment

The interim CEO was explicit in the earnings call: Nvidia's commitments to compute operators only take effect after they've raised capital, purchased equipment, and deployed services. Without deployment, guarantees can shrink or disappear. Maggie notes this means construction delays directly erase the guarantee—a new repricing risk for creditors, precisely during the highest-risk phase. Once a project is live, Nvidia's guarantees become more substantial and risk stabilizes.

— Maggie
19:14

Grid connection is the real bottleneck, not construction

Eason points out many Neo Cloud companies have multiple gigawatts of data centers queued for grid connection, with only a fraction actually powered on. Once live, these assets become productive cash-generating machines. But getting there is brutal: grid approval cycles stretch 3–5 years (PJM), 2–4 years (Texas); equipment shortages mean GE generators already booked out to 2032. Projects expected to go live in six months routinely slip to twelve-plus months, creating real refinancing risk for bondholders.

— Eason
24:19

CoreWeave's quarterly capex exceeds cash flow by $7.5 billion

Maggie cites CoreWeave's quarterly equipment purchases and data center construction spending at approximately $9.4 billion, against operating cash inflow of about $2 billion—a $7.5 billion gap. Capital spending isn't slowing; it's expanding. The gap keeps widening, forcing more external financing, while rising rates push borrowing costs higher. This is a self-reinforcing fundamental pressure.

— Maggie
29:20

CoreWeave's bond yields reveal the true market confidence in AI

In October, CoreWeave's unsecured bonds fell to roughly 75–78 cents on the dollar, with yields exceeding 16%. The market wasn't pricing cash flow risk; it was pricing timing risk—when would cash materialize, and who gets paid first. After Huang spoke, earnings were published, Huang resigned, and the Fed released its financial stability report, CoreWeave stock swung wildly each time, with two-digit daily moves common over the month. It became the barometer for AI infrastructure confidence across the board.

— Maggie
40:24

Meta's data center debt stays off its balance sheet through SPVs and guarantees

Meta's Louisiana data center is held by an SPV jointly owned with Blue Owl (Meta's stake: 20%), which issued $20+ billion in bonds staying off Meta's balance sheet. But Meta still takes two liabilities: a payment guarantee and a residual value guarantee—if the lease isn't renewed, Meta covers the gap between asset value and remaining debt. This structure got the bonds a rating just below Meta's own, but rates far lower than single-revenue-stream operators like CoreWeave.

— Maggie
55:33

Silicon Valley prices long-term demand; Wall Street prices near-term cash flow

Maggie points to real disagreement between coasts: banks face regulatory constraints and limited AI usage, so bankers more easily doubt demand. But Anthropic's disclosures show Fortune 500 spending on AI still under 1% of total spend at most companies—vast undrilled demand remains. Equities price the eventual pull-through; bonds price the gap between now and that realization. These aren't contradictory signals; they're answering different questions.

— Maggie
1:14:45

AI debt is still modest in size, but off-balance commitments are staggering

Even if all planned AI infrastructure financing hits the market, Maggie estimates it reaches roughly 8% of the $12 trillion corporate bond market—not enough to trigger systemic sub-prime-style crisis. But the podcast's postscript adds Fed and ECB data: through the first ten months of 2026, compute-related issuance exceeded $680 billion; seven major tech companies' off-balance commitments exceed $3 trillion, and another count shows eight US tech firms with off-balance pledges above $4 trillion—exceeding their total on-balance liabilities. This is the variable to keep watching.

Glossary

Neo Cloud
Emerging compute service providers focused purely on GPU rental, without cloud software stacks; examples include CoreWeave and Nebius.
SPV
Special Purpose Vehicle; a legal entity created for a single project to issue debt while keeping liabilities off the parent company's consolidated balance sheet.
vendor financing
Arrangements where a supplier (such as Nvidia) provides guarantees or financial support to cover a customer's equipment purchases.
residual value guarantee
A commitment by the lessee to cover the difference between asset value and remaining debt if the lease terminates without renewal.
Air Pocket
A pricing gap in emerging credit markets where new asset types lack comparables, causing yields to jump suddenly within a band.
