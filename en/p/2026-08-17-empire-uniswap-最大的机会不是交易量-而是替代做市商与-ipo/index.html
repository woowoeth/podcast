Uniswap's Ambition Isn't DEX, It's Replacing Market Makers and the IPO

Uniswap's long-term goal isn't to be a DEX but to become the global liquidity network; the negative cost of capital for long-tail assets plus v4 hooks let it replace traditional market makers and even the IPO step.

The word "DEX" costs Uniswap partners

Hayden argues the word "DEX" makes Uniswap look like it competes with centralized exchanges, which blocks integration. He stresses that Uniswap is fundamentally a liquidity network, a platform more like Ethereum, and should be seen as a "liquidity market-making system." When talking to TradFi and fintech companies, this framing decides whether they treat Uniswap as a rival or as infrastructure. The positioning question isn't PR packaging, it's the definition of the business boundary.

— Hayden Adams
17:00

Payment companies that want stablecoins can't route around Uniswap

Uniswap holds 60-70% of stablecoin swap market share on EVM chains, and roughly 40-50% counting non-EVM chains. That share directly brings payment-network partnerships: traditional payment companies considering accepting stablecoins face the question of which chain to pick and who provides the liquidity, and Uniswap positions itself as the liquidity source for those networks. The share numbers aren't a boast, they're proof to TradFi that this liquidity is irreplaceable.

— Hayden Adams
34:39

For long-tail issuers, the cost of liquidity is negative

Hayden offers a counterintuitive point: for long-tail asset issuers, the cost of capital is negative. Without an AMM, they have to pay professional market makers, or sign exploitative options agreements; with an AMM, they can use assets already on their balance sheet to create liquidity and generate yield. That means the AMM doesn't just replace market makers — it turns "paying someone to make a market" into "earning with your own assets," completely changing the issuer's cost structure.

— Hayden Adams
39:44

The AMM's real battleground is mainstream assets, not the long tail

Hayden splits assets into three categories: low-volatility stablecoin pairs, where AMMs are already strong; long-tail assets, where there's little competition; and the real battlefield, high-volatility mainstream assets. Critics argue that under a power-law distribution, AMMs are weakest precisely on the most mainstream assets and will be eroded by active market-making systems. He first gives the traditional response, then a new view that evolved over six months — this taxonomy is the key to understanding Uniswap's roadmap.

— Hayden Adams
46:02

Stocks should pair with SPY, not open a pool alone

Hayden's new answer is to change the base trading pair and reduce the delta between the AMM and active market makers. DeFi tokens are highly correlated with ETH, making ETH a good base pair; stocks can pair with SPY or QQQ, commodities with commodity ETFs. This hub-and-spoke model lets correlated assets share one liquidity hub instead of each fighting professional market makers on its own.

— Hayden Adams
54:22

Listing and market making are merging into one step

Hayden points out that projects like Aztec issue tokens directly through Uniswap's Dutch auction mechanism, completing fundraising and building a deep liquidity pool at the same time, skipping the fees traditional IPOs pay to market makers, with liquidity that is permanent. He argues this provides competition to the IPO market and could eventually be integrated into the traditional IPO system. For crypto projects, that means "listing" and "market making" may merge into one step.

— Hayden Adams
1:11:37

Burn is already $100M annualized, and comments still ask when it starts

After the fee switch was implemented, Hayden says recent V4 and Robinhood chain data put annualized UNI burn at about $100 million, with a long-term average of $50-60 million. He notes a particular irony: the most common comment before the proposal passed was "when does the fee switch turn on," and after it passed the most common comment was still the same. Communication and narrative lag behind the mechanism change.

Glossary

AMM / Automated Market Maker
Uses algorithmic pricing instead of an order book; users trade by supplying assets to a pool.
Hooks
Customizable plugins in Uniswap v4 that let you execute custom logic during a liquidity pool's lifecycle, compressing AMM development from years to days.
Hub-and-spoke
A model where core assets like ETH or SPY act as the hub and correlated assets trade in pairs around them, improving price discovery efficiency.
RWA / Real World Assets
Tokenizing real-world assets such as stocks and bonds on-chain, e.g. non-US users trading tokenized US equities.
Dutch auction
Price declines from high to low until a sale is made; the auction issuance mechanism on Uniswap uses it to price tokens and build pools.
