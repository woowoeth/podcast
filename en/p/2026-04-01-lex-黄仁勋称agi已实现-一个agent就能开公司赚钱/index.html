Jensen Huang says AGI is here: one agent can build a company and make money

Jensen Huang says a "billion-dollar company" doesn't have to last—an agent can build an app that goes viral overnight, gets bought by billions for 50 cents each, and dies months later: still counts.

Two and a half hours, wide-ranging; but Jensen Huang's views on AGI definition, CUDA's moat, and power grid efficiency are worth hearing out to the second half.

2:02

Extreme co-design stems from Amdahl's law's hard ceiling

Jensen Huang explains why NVIDIA moved from chip optimization to co-designing entire machine racks and whole data centers: when a problem spreads across tens of thousands of computers, network, scheduling, and communication become new bottlenecks. He cites Amdahl's law—if compute is 50% of the total work, speeding up compute a million times only speeds up the whole system twice. So NVIDIA can't optimize just the GPU; it must simultaneously address CPU, memory, network, switches, power delivery, and cooling. Otherwise growth merely follows Moore's law's slowdown.

— Jensen Huang
14:20

CUDA in GeForce was a nearly fatal gamble

To give CUDA enough developer install base, NVIDIA decided to pack it into every consumer GeForce card, even if customers never used it. This increased card costs by 50%, and the company had only 35% gross margin at the time—it nearly swallowed all profit. Jensen Huang recalls the company's market cap dropped from $60–70 billion to around $1.5 billion; the decision took ten years to truly pay off, eventually becoming the compute foundation of the deep learning revolution. He says "NVIDIA is a house built on GeForce": researchers first encountered CUDA through personal gaming PCs they assembled.

— Jensen Huang
22:46

Agentic scaling expands compute power by spawning more AI

Jensen Huang outlines four scaling laws: pre-training (more data makes it smarter), post-training (synthetic data takes over; training becomes compute-constrained rather than data-constrained), test-time scaling (inference is really "thinking," consuming more compute than mere retrieval—the industry previously underestimated this), and the latest agentic scaling—one agent working spawns many sub-agents that form teams. He notes "scaling NVIDIA requires hiring people, easier than scaling myself"; this loop also feeds new agent-generated data back into pre-training and post-training, creating a self-reinforcing flywheel.

— Jensen Huang
47:54

Power grids waste capacity ninety-nine percent of the time

Jensen Huang points out that power grids are designed with reserve margins for extreme cases (peak days in winter and summer), yet run at only about 60% of peak capacity 99% of the time—vast idle power just waiting for rare extremes. Data centers could sign "degradation" agreements with grids instead: when the grid tightens, automatically move compute loads away, slow processing speed, or accept slightly longer response latency, rather than demanding "six nines" (99.9999%) uptime guarantees like cloud vendors do now. The problem, he says, lies in the contract chain—end customers demand zero downtime, pushing that all the way up to power companies, and CEOs often don't know what they actually signed.

— Jensen Huang
1:01:41

China's tech boom stems from open source and class ties

Jensen Huang notes that roughly half the world's AI researchers are ethnically Chinese, many still based in mainland China. He attributes China's tech boom to converging factors: tech industry rise coincided with the mobile cloud era, where the barrier was mainly software not hardware; provinces compete with each other, spawning many companies in the same category with fierce internal competition; more critically, the culture holds "family first, friends second, company third," so engineers who are alumni and brothers through friendship networks already share technical information within circles—open source is nearly a natural choice, not a strategic one.

— Jensen Huang
1:15:31

CUDA's moat is forty thousand people, not the technology

Jensen Huang says NVIDIA's most important asset isn't any specific technology, but CUDA's install base. Even if a competitor called CUDA or TUDA had appeared 20 years ago, it wouldn't matter—because CUDA's success came not from three people but from 43,000 employees and millions of developers investing over years, porting code. Developers choose CUDA because they can 100% trust NVIDIA will keep maintaining it, keep optimizing to version 13.2, and because it's embedded in virtually every cloud vendor's products and every computer company's offerings. That trust, plus breadth, plus execution speed—that's the truly hard-to-replicate moat.

— Jensen Huang
1:56:01

We have achieved AGI, by Jensen Huang's definition

Lex defines AGI as "doing your job"—founding and running a company worth over a billion dollars. Jensen Huang answers "I think we've achieved AGI," but immediately loosens the premise: that "billion-dollar company" doesn't have to last. An agent might easily create an app that goes viral overnight, gets bought by billions for 50 cents each, then quietly dies months later—these kinds of companies are everywhere in the internet era, no more complex than what today's OpenClaw can generate. He notes deliberately that this claim will excite many: just spin out an agent and make big money.

— Jensen Huang
1:59:04

Radiologist numbers grew after AI, undercutting the job displacement narrative

Jensen Huang counters the "AI will kill this job" panic with radiology: computer vision hit superhuman level back in 2019–2020; all radiology platforms today are AI-driven; yet radiologist numbers grew, even facing shortages. The reason: the "purpose" of work differs from the "tasks/tools" used to do it. AI makes reading scans faster, so hospitals treat more patients and earn more, thus need more radiologists. He argues the same logic applies to programmers: the specific task of writing code gets automated, but the purpose—solving problems, team collaboration, judgment calls—doesn't vanish.

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

Amdahl's law
System performance gain is capped by the unaccelerated portion of the work.
Extreme co-design
Joint optimization of an entire system stack—chips, network, power, cooling—rather than any single component.
Install base
The deployed user base actually using an architecture or platform; the installed footprint.
Agentic scaling law
Scaling output by having AI autonomously generate and dispatch many sub-agents.
HBM
High-bandwidth stacked memory that delivers extreme bandwidth to GPUs; now standard for data center memory.
