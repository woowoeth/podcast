1:08

From $48 million IPO to $46 billion quarterly profit: the full-stack strategy

Nvidia went public 27 years ago at $48 million; it was then bringing in $30 million annual revenue, with IPO pricing at $12 a share and a valuation of roughly $300 million. During roadshow pitches, investors' only question was ‘When will you go out of business?’ Last quarter brought $46 billion in net profit on $70 billion revenue. Huang attributes this leap to one sustained commitment: a ‘full-stack’ strategy of building everything in-house—from chip design to networking to software libraries. This allows the company to redesign its entire infrastructure annually rather than cobble together other vendors' pieces.

— Jensen Huang
5:23

Gaming graphics cards accidentally hatched the AI deep-learning era

Huang highlights an underappreciated fact: there are hundreds of millions of active GeForce users globally, and some later became AI researchers. It was because Jeff Hinton asked Ilya Sutskever and Alex Krizhevsky to buy GTX 580 cards that CUDA's utility for deep learning was discovered. He uses this history to show that Nvidia has been a full-stack company for 33 years: from GPU architecture to DirectX protocols to system memory virtualization (which later became PCI Express), every layer has been redesigned in-house.

— Jensen Huang
11:51

Three AI inflection points in two years drove compute demand up exponentially

Huang summarizes AI's evolution over the past two years as three inflection points. First came generative AI, with ChatGPT packaging GPT-3 as a product, but generated content tends to hallucinate. Next came reasoning models like o1, using retrieval-augmented generation (grounding) to make outputs more reliable. But the result: token consumption roughly 100x higher than the first generation, models 10x larger, compute consumption around 1,000x more—and with usage exploding, the total could reach roughly 1,000,000x. The third inflection point is now: the agentic stage. AI can call tools, read documentation, make plans. That's why he says Nvidia's internal compute demand is skyrocketing.

— Jensen Huang
15:58

An agentic tool surpassed Linux's 30-year download volume in three weeks

Huang uses a concrete comparison to illustrate how fast the agentic stage is growing: Linux took roughly 30 years to reach the adoption scale it has now. The agentic coding tool that Nvidia's teams internally call ‘Claude Code’ (transcribed as OpenClaw) hit that same scale in just three weeks—becoming the largest-downloaded open-source software in history, with growth curves nearly vertical on a semi-log chart. He frames the shift in prompt paradigm: from queries like ‘what is, when is, who is’ to task commands like ‘create, do, build, write.’ This means AI is no longer just answering questions; it's continuously consuming massive amounts of tokens executing tasks in the background.

— Jensen Huang
19:03

Data centers are money-printing token factories, not mere data storage

Data centers are no longer storage systems; they're token-printing factories. Huang redefines the data center as an ‘AI factory’: factories produce tokens, tokens convert to revenue, more compute means more tokens, which means higher revenue. This is no longer speculation but ‘established fact.’ He cites SemiAnalysis's large-scale benchmarks, where Nvidia is called the ‘inference king,’ leading alternatives by an order of magnitude in both tokens per watt and tokens per dollar. Since the factory is always constrained by power—even a 100 megawatt or 1 gigawatt plant has a fixed limit—choosing the wrong architecture directly hurts next year's revenue. That's why he says computational architecture decisions now ‘require CEO sign-off for the first time.’

— Jensen Huang
23:17

Enterprise software will shift from renting tools to renting expert AI agents

Huang predicts that future software will always come with agentic capability: every software company will use both open-source models (downloaded and fine-tuned in-house) and closed-source models, just as companies hire both employees and outside contractors and expert consultants. The key shift: today's multi-trillion-dollar IT industry is fundamentally a ‘tool rental service.’ In the future, enterprises won't just rent tools; they'll rent ‘experts who know how to use those tools’—that is, an industry that currently consumes almost no tokens will become a massive token consumer. That's the core of his answer to ‘where does the money come from.’

— Jensen Huang
31:31

Nvidia chose $30 billion for OpenAI over $100 billion—IPO timing changed the game

In response to earlier rumors of a ‘$100 billion investment in OpenAI,’ Huang clarifies that Nvidia has locked in a deal for $30 billion—not the speculated figure—because OpenAI is expected to go public before year-end, and this may be the last chance to invest in such a ‘consequential’ company this way. He also discloses that over the past year-plus, Nvidia has helped OpenAI expand compute capacity from Azure and OCI to AWS, and is now scaling up AWS capacity substantially. Additionally, a brand-new AI lab—he mentions one called MSL—has brought ‘new GPU demand beyond Meta,’ making overall demand ‘go from already very high to even higher.’

— Jensen Huang
34:43

Nvidia bets on four open-source models to dominate physical AI

Huang believes that for AI to truly leave the lab and enter the largest physical industries, it must understand causality in the physical world—push a bottle and it falls, occlusion doesn't mean disappearance. He lists four directions where Nvidia claims to be at the frontier: the physics AI model Cosmos (most downloaded), the autonomous driving model Alpamayo, the humanoid robot model Groot N2 (most-downloaded humanoid robot model), and the digital biology project La Protina. All these models are open-source, designed to let new and existing industries deploy across Nvidia's full software-hardware stack into factories, robots, and edge devices. He expects physical AI to remain a constant topic for the next two to ten years.

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

AI factory
A data center redesigned to continuously produce monetizable tokens rather than merely store data.
tokens per watt
A metric measuring both the energy efficiency of a computing system and its revenue-generating capacity.
agentic AI
AI that autonomously calls tools, reads documentation, and executes tasks sequentially rather than merely answering questions.
grounding
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) that anchors model outputs to verified data sources, reducing hallucination.
Claude Code
Anthropic's agentic programming tool for autonomous software development.
