Delaying hepatitis B vaccination two months raises chronic infection 19%

A simulation study published in Annals of Internal Medicine found that even a two-month postponement of newborn hepatitis B vaccination raises the rate of chronic infection by age 20 by 19%. If adoption of ‘when or whether to vaccinate’ language results in only 50% completion, chronic infection rates jump 83% and U.S. cases balloon from under 250,000 to 1.8 million.

Potassium salt substituted globally could prevent approximately 3 million deaths

Replacing chloride with potassium chloride in salt lowers absolute sodium intake while improving the sodium-to-potassium ratio, simultaneously suppressing blood pressure through two mechanisms. Modeling shows that global replacement of all added salt with potassium salt prevents roughly 3 million deaths yearly and avoids approximately 70 million disability-adjusted life years. Researchers tested the conclusion against thousands of parameter variations; the finding remains robust.

Mathematicians suspect OpenAI trained its model on their research queries

On September 8, OpenAI announced a breakthrough on the Navier-Stokes equation—one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems—claiming to have found a solution that ‘blows up’. Two mathematicians, Buckminster and Alpoga, who had previously used OpenAI tools to research the same problem, publicly accused the company of training its model on their research queries, suspecting it had reverse-engineered their thinking. OpenAI denied this. Outside observers can never verify the truth; they can only choose to believe or disbelieve.

— Brian
1:20:18

One batch of 722 papers overwhelms the mathematics community's verification capacity

On October 6, OpenAI simultaneously published 722 mathematics papers on GitHub spanning proofs and counterexamples, some with verifiable Lean code and some without. Papers often run hundreds of pages and show no consideration for human readability—the goal is ‘to produce results’, not ‘to teach’. Even professional mathematicians found them unreadable, and the entire discipline erupted in shock and argument.

— Brian
1:39:02

The frontier of mathematics may now be comprehensible only to AI

Theoretical physicist Matt Strassler wrote that this development means the age of a ‘Newton’ or ‘Einstein’ is finished—no human can keep pace with this speed. Guest Brian argues that mathematics derives value from the proof process itself, not the result. If the process becomes unverifiable and subsequent researchers continue stacking work on top of unvalidated AI results, the entire discipline risks becoming a ‘house of cards’.

— Brian
1:54:41

Ancient Athens filled most public offices through lottery, not election

Though ancient Athens is the earliest recorded democracy and did allow free adult male citizens to vote, most officials were appointed by sortition—random lottery—typically for one-year terms. The design explicitly prevented power from concentrating in wealthy hands. If someone accumulated too much influence, citizens could vote to exile that person for ten years. Ironically, only about 10% of the total population could participate in voting.

Glossary

Lean
Programming language that automatically verifies whether mathematical proofs are logically sound.
sortition
Selection of officials by random lottery rather than election; used to prevent power concentration in ancient Athens.
Navier-Stokes equation
Partial differential equation describing the motion of viscous fluids; one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems in mathematics.
mathpocalypse
Term describing the sudden release of vast quantities of AI-generated mathematical proofs that disrupt the entire discipline.
action bias
Cognitive bias whereby people blame bad outcomes caused by action more readily than those caused by inaction.
