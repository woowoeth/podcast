Analyst Downgrades Nvidia: The Real Problem Is Jensen Huang's Personal Network

Bernstein analyst believes Nvidia's recent performance and demand are fine, but Jensen Huang's departure means he can't recommend buying because too many supply chain and financing decisions depended on his personal relationships and trust.

Downgrade Triggered by Governance Scandal, Not Business Weakness

Rasgan initially thought Jensen Huang and Alice Wang's relationship could be managed—a gray area the board wouldn't want disrupted. The dealbreaker was lying to the board: a "death sentence" in corporate governance, mirroring what happened to other CEOs Rasgan covered. The team downgraded the day after Jensen's firing was confirmed.

Hours Debating the Decision, Twenty Minutes Writing the Report

The team spent hours wrestling with whether to downgrade, reluctant because Rasgan liked Jensen and Nvidia's narrative. The case for buying: stock already cheap, near-term business unaffected. But Rasgan concluded more bad news would follow and he couldn't defend the buy rating to clients. He cut the target price in half.

9:16

China's Real Exposure Hides in Taiwan Customer Channels

Direct China sales account for only ~8% of revenue (including HK), and data center sales to China are negligible. The real exposure is the Taiwan customer channel at ~20%+ of revenue. However, the actual proportion smuggled to China likely falls far short of the 20% figure market participants believe.

— Stacey Rasgan
16:37

New CEO Must Inherit Jensen Huang's Judgment, Not Operations

Nvidia's Rubin roadmap is already set, with Rubin Ultra bridging to Feynman. The critical challenge is the next-generation architecture decision: HBM allocation and memory design. Jensen made the CUDA bet when his stock was worth 25-50 cents (on a 40-for-1 split basis); a new CEO's willingness to place such bets is unknown.

— Stacey Rasgan
18:39

China Hawks May Not Have Foreseen This Outcome

Rasgan suspects the scandal was initiated by China hawks to prevent Jensen from publicly advocating for chip sales to China. But he agrees with Jensen's original position: selling older chips like H200/H20 makes China "addicted" to US technology. Restricting them actually forces China toward self-sufficiency—precisely what's happening now.

— Stacey Rasgan
25:47

Nvidia's Moat Depends on Market Size, Not Competition

OpenAI and Meta are signing deals with Broadcom and AMD; Google sells TPU chips; AMD uses AI to migrate CUDA software. The real question isn't whether Nvidia loses market share (it will) but whether the overall market grows large enough to sustain everyone. Nvidia forecast above 70% revenue growth next year.

— Stacey Rasgan
28:53

Nvidia's Federal Reserve Role Rests on Jensen's Relationships

Nvidia earned the nickname "AI's central bank" by offering customers financing, guarantees, and direct investment. The interim CEO, Cologne (from finance), reportedly doesn't fully embrace Jensen's scale of capital deployment. The company's multi-billion-dollar outflows support capacity locks—memory, wafers, packaging—often built on Jensen's personal relationships. How aggressively his successor pursues such bets remains unknowable.

— Tracy Alloway / Stacey Rasgan

Glossary

CUDA

Vendor financing
Nvidia providing funds or guarantees to customers to encourage chip purchases; called the 'Federal Reserve of AI'.
