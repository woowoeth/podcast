AI's true target isn't new jobs—it's the clean work outsourced to India a decade ago

Work with clear boundaries, verifiable results, and single tasks was already shipped to India; now AI takes over. What actually resists AI is messy work that runs on relationships and political deal-making.

1:05

Spain's building capacity collapsed after the crisis

Before 2008, Spain completed up to 800,000 housing units yearly, with elastic land supply. After the bubble burst, the public linked easy construction approval to corruption and rising prices; the central government clamped down on local land approvals. Now, with tens of thousands of immigrants arriving yearly, Spain builds under 100,000 units a year. Notably, there is no YIMBY movement in Spain—not even Garicano himself publicly advocates for more building.

— Luis Garicano
5:07

Spain is governed by and for the elderly

Spain has the world's 5th-lowest fertility rate and 5th-highest life expectancy. Since 2008, essentially all GDP growth has flowed to pensions, not productive investment. High-speed rail and motorways are well-developed; the investments the country actually needs are scarce. Garicano's conclusion is direct: Spain is currently ruled by and for its retired, elderly population.

— Luis Garicano
17:31

The Basque Country's fiscal privilege rests on invented history

The Basque region was granted autonomous tax collection and separate negotiation with the central government—a federal-style fiscal arrangement—because of past terrorism. By timing negotiations just before votes that could topple the government, the Basque Country has frozen its remittance to Madrid. As pensions rise nationwide, the Basque Country pays nothing extra; it has become a net beneficiary. Catalonia demands the same deal, but its size means the same arrangement would break the national budget. Unsolvable.

— Luis Garicano
19:31

Parties promote the trapped, not the talented

Garicano's time in politics revealed that the real mechanism linking voters to parties is internal discipline. Parties advance not the smartest policy designers, but people who have nowhere else to go—those who will never rebel because leaving the party means losing everything. The same logic explains how the EU Commission picks its president: member states choose candidates who don't threaten them, not the most capable person.

— Luis Garicano
22:38

The EU veto locked defense and tax to unanimity

The EU uses majority voting on the single market but retains unanimity on core sovereignty: tax, defense, foreign policy. Each country wants final say, so they buy tanks, jets, and ammunition separately—artillery shells come in incompatible calibers. Weeks of negotiation just to settle 55mm versus 55.5mm. Garicano is clear: if he could press a button to swap unanimity for a two-thirds supermajority, he would press it.

— Luis Garicano
29:59

Rigid labor law's real cost is the price of error

Europe's strict firing protections don't just raise hiring costs—companies can cut salaries to offset that. The actual killer is making mistakes expensive. Facebook realized the metaverse was wrong and disbanded the team in a week, then hired an AI team. Ericsson or Nokia would spend months in union talks before laying anyone off; by then, the company might be dead. The company can't afford the pivot.

— Luis Garicano
36:06

AI will first devour the work outsourced to India a decade ago

Garicano and his coauthor's core claim: AI will empty not messy work, but the kind of work that was mass-outsourced to India—clean boundaries, single tasks, verifiable output. This work was always easy for companies to define precisely and ship out; now AI takes over. Reinforcement learning trains directly on verifiable outcomes. Messy work that requires managing relationships and political maneuvering? That stays.

— Luis Garicano
42:15

Productivity gains evaporated into the hallway

A Chinese IT executive told Garicano that every employee's individual efficiency is rising, but the whole IT department's output hasn't budged. The productivity gains, she said, are being extracted ‘in the hallway’. A study of doctors using AI transcription tools showed doctors see more patients, but some use the freed time for yoga class—leisure gains exist, but get converted into ‘do a bit more’ rather than ‘rest a bit more’.

— Luis Garicano

Glossary

flexicurity
A labor-market model combining high unemployment insurance with employers' freedom to hire and fire at will.
cupo
The fixed annual share of tax revenue that the Basque Country remits to Spain's central government.
strong bundle / weak bundle
Two categories of work from Messy Jobs: strong bundles are tasks so integrated that AI cannot execute them separately; weak bundles consist of discrete, single tasks AI can handle independently.
Jevons paradox
The phenomenon where efficiency gains increase overall resource consumption (e.g., working hours) rather than reduce it.
second-mover strategy
Adopting proven AI systems quickly while maintaining replaceable open-source alternatives, rather than chasing frontier models.
