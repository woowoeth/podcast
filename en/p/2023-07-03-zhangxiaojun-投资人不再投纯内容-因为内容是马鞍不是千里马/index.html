Investors have stopped funding pure content, because content is the saddle, not the thoroughbred

Content companies succeed far more often than most businesses, but their ceiling is set by the nature of the industry: a hundred flowers blooming means low concentration. So investors treat content as a whip and go looking for a horse they can ride.

6:40

The ability to make hits is innate talent, not methodology

Lao Fan's core judgment: the ability to make hits is an innate attribute, unrelated to methodology. He gives the example of Mimeng — back then a Southern Metropolis Daily leader told him he had an editor who hadn't yet written a WeChat public account, but if she did, she would definitely blow up. He added her on WeChat, they tried to arrange a meeting, never managed it, and then Mimeng became famous in three days. He also says Churchill's and Kennedy's best speeches were not logically strong; they relied on inflammatory appeal; using logic to make hits is a dead end. But a good content entrepreneur needs both emotional resonance and logical ability — people like Steve Jobs are extremely rare.

10:05

Hearing is inherently inferior to sight; podcasts are undervalued but can't get big

Lao Fan uses ‘supernatural powers cannot overcome karma’ to explain the innate weight of media: if you had to lose either hearing or sight, losing sight is more painful, so what you see has always weighed more than what you hear — TV largest, newspapers next, radio weakest. He maps newspapers to WeChat public accounts, Douyin and Kuaishou to TV stations, Xiaohongshu to city papers plus fashion magazines, while radio is a weak but stable category. Podcasts are best for input of ideas, because you have to listen continuously for half an hour to an hour, making it easy to influence others; the rise of political factions abroad and stock-investing shows both relied on radio. The conclusion: podcasts are undervalued, but they can never become the most mainstream category.

— Fan Weifeng
16:31

The podcast ecosystem has no true top tier

Lao Fan, kicking over the table, says: among all podcasts today, including everything on Xiaoyuzhou, no true top tier has appeared. He defines ‘hot’ — you've never heard his show, yet you know he's a star. He cites Wang Yibo and Dilraba; he hasn't seen any of their work, but that doesn't stop him from knowing they're stars; the true top-tier are Zhao Benshan, Michael Jackson, Elvis — the whole country has seen their work, you couldn't avoid knowing them if you tried. For a podcast to break out of its circle and be known by everyone, the next step up is going on the Spring Festival Gala. He also says he now records podcasts completely uninhibited, and his rule for himself is ‘I may not be famous, but I will tell the truth’.

— Fan Weifeng
20:10

Content is the whip; business is the thoroughbred

Lao Fan explains why content cannot be the horse: content is a hundred flowers blooming — the good is the hundred flowers, the bad is that market concentration is not high. No matter how much you like a singer, you can't get everyone to like them, but you can get everyone to use WeChat and drink Coca-Cola. So investors prefer to fund companies with high ceilings and high concentration. He calculates that Gaozhang's portfolio, by users, has roughly 300 million in traffic; in the 2016 new-media wave it was one of the few that actually got returns and mentioned carry. But he also says the ceiling for content companies isn't that low: with China's one billion netizens, gaining the deep trust of one percent is ten million people; at a thousand yuan per person per year, that's ten billion.

— Fan Weifeng
31:53

Decide what to invest in first, then reverse-search for the person

Lao Fan reviews investing in Fantuan Dai Laoban: first figure out clearly that you want to invest in someone writing middle-class finance in a WeChat public account, with buy-side thinking and fund-manager thinking, then work backwards to find the person. At the meeting the other party said, ‘Even if you hadn't come looking for me, I would have come looking for you.’ His method for judging people is to look at very little work — to see whether someone can survive in short video, three clips are enough. He looked at several paragraphs in one of Dai Laoban's articles that used several memes; his colleagues didn't know these memes, but still thought it was well written and interesting; those who knew the memes smiled knowingly, those who didn't still found informational value and emotional resonance — that is a top-tier master. Xingqiu Yanjiusuo was also a reverse search: he had colleagues find every account with ‘geography’ in the name, and finally someone searched ‘planet’ and found it, with fewer than ten thousand followers at the time; the founder didn't even have a BP, and Lao Fan helped him talk through the BP.

— Fan Weifeng
38:01

Doing hard and right things is poisonous chicken soup

Lao Fan directly disses the phrase ‘hard and right’, saying it's very misleading: you should do the right thing; whether it's hard doesn't matter; if it's hard I'll still do it, if it's easy even better. The vast majority of right things are not hard, because you're going with the wind. He cites what Lei Jun said for many years, and buying a house in Beijing or Shenzhen fifteen years ago — that was an incomparably simple and right thing. He also uses weightlifting versus skiing and tennis to show that difficulty and ceiling are not the same thing: a weightlifter, even at world-class level, earns less than a second-rate skier or tennis player, because weightlifting has no derivative product categories; no one sculpts themselves in the image of a weightlifter.

— Fan Weifeng
46:00

Society has already built the monetization chain for content companies

Lao Fan divides content companies into three layers. The first layer is individual self-media: as long as you have continuous hit-making ability and don't build any company organization, revenue of one to two hundred million is no problem; he cites Liu Shen Leilei and Mantou Dashi. The second layer, reaching five hundred million or a billion or more, requires building a company, supply chain, clients, government relations; representatives are Xingqiu Yanjiusuo and the like. The third layer, above a billion, either has the attribute of a small platform on a big platform, with independent channels and a large private domain, or becomes an excellent consumer brand, like Boluo Banwa, which went from media completely to a consumer brand, and the consumer side fully opened up. He stresses that a content company will definitely make money as long as it keeps producing hits, because society has already built the monetization steps for you; if you don't want to deal with clients, just put up a price and the deal closes; try that with a consumer company — absolutely impossible.

— Fan Weifeng
51:10

Traffic-type companies: hits but not called IP

Lao Fan distinguishes people who play IP from people who play traffic: people who play IP are more famous, people who succeed at traffic are very rich. Traffic-type companies also produce hits and have continuous hit-making ability, but after the hit you have 100k-plus or a million-plus, and you still haven't heard of them, because they don't make hits under a media name. Pull up the reading list of WeChat public accounts, and you haven't heard of a single one of the top few dozen articles, because they spread across society, not among the cultural elite. He judges traffic-type companies on two points: whether they have continuous hit-production capability, and whether they have excellent traffic buying and selling capability — it's more like a trader, wholesaler, futures trader; how much per click, how much per downloaded user, sold to game companies and health-product companies.

— Fan Weifeng
1:03:11

Young people should not take over the baton from the middle-aged and elderly

Lao Fan's advice to young people: do new things, don't join the games of middle-aged and elderly comrades, don't take over the baton from them. He gives examples: a few years ago you should have been studying web3, not studying antiques, jewelry, jade, emeralds, walnuts; when WeChat public accounts came out, do public accounts; when Douyin came out, do Douyin; when podcasts came out, do podcasts; when MR comes out, do MR. When new things come out, believe first, because the cost of trying is extremely, extremely low, and once it works you can leap across classes in one go. He also says if you already have a job, you can do it as a side hustle first, paddle a bit — big companies won't miss you; a person in a big company effectively works maybe two hours a day at most, and most of the time is spent paddling and attending meetings that don't need attending.

— Fan Weifeng
