When Does the AI Bubble Pop? Watch Credit Default Swaps and Token Prices

The AI bubble will eventually burst, but current valuations are far below dot-com levels — Nvidia trades at just 15x earnings. The real risk sits in credit markets, where credit default swaps have climbed to historic highs, signalling a possible sharp selloff ahead.

4:03

Token prices fell 50% while usage rose 2.5x

Dan Niles points out that judging whether the AI bubble has topped requires watching two core variables: token output and unit price. Since the end of May, competition from open-source models has pushed token unit prices down roughly 50%, but output over the same period grew 2.5x. As long as volume growth offsets price declines, industry revenue is still expanding. At the same time, revenue growth at the three big cloud providers (AWS, Azure, GCP) accelerated from 35% in March to 43% in June, with operating margins up about 2 percentage points — a sign the industry's fundamentals remain healthy.

7:03

4x leverage, not bad positioning, blew up the fund

The Situational Awareness fund did not blow up because its positions were pointed the wrong way; it blew up because it used 4x leverage. If the underlying falls 25%, 4x leverage means a 100% loss, triggering liquidation. This echoes the lesson of Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM) in 1998. After hearing about the fund's problems on July 29, Dan Niles judged that a short-term bottom was near, because the fundamentals (growth in token usage) had not deteriorated. He warns that leverage magnifies risk in both directions, and when long and short positions both move against you at once, it can trigger catastrophic selling.

— Dan Niles
9:12

Hyperscaler cash flow turns negative for the first time

Even as hyperscaler revenue accelerates and margins improve, the market worries about deteriorating free cash flow. Google, for example, saw cash flow turn negative for the first time since its IPO, because of massive AI capex. Dan Niles sees a parallel to the dot-com era: the technology revolution was real, but stocks could still fall 78% (the Nasdaq from 2000 to 2002). He expects strong industry growth over the next year, but once a hyperscaler cuts capex because of debt problems, it could set off a chain reaction.

— Dan Niles
14:19

Data center politics is the biggest near-term threat

Dan Niles points out that the biggest short-term risk to AI growth is not demand but political resistance. A Gallup poll shows 71% of people oppose a data center in their own backyard, higher than the 53% who oppose a nuclear power plant. The two parties rarely agree, but they agree on opposing data centers, especially in red states like Texas. Hyperscalers have failed to effectively promote the jobs and tax revenue data centers bring, while anti-business forces have successfully cast them as a negative. He expects political risk to weigh on the market through the November midterms.

— Dan Niles
21:28

Chinese semiconductors will break the no-more-cycles myth

Dan Niles argues that the view that semiconductors are no longer cyclical is dangerous, and China may be the key to breaking that myth. He draws an analogy to Japan in the 1980s and Korea in the 1990s entering the DRAM market with government support and ultimately displacing US dominance. Today China's CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies) and YMTC (Yangtze Memory Technologies) are expanding fast: CXMT plans to raise monthly capacity from 300,000 wafers to 500,000, and YMTC aims to overtake Samsung and SK Hynix in NAND. For China, semiconductor self-sufficiency matters more than aircraft carriers.

— Dan Niles
27:18

Agentic AI extends the cycle by at least a year

Dan Niles sees agentic AI as a brand-new phase, whose start was marked by the launch of the OpenClaw project on January 30 this year. The agentic phase consumes 10-100x more tokens than chat AI, which will keep driving demand growth. He expects the stock market to have at least another year of upside, but warns to watch competition such as Chinese memory chips. He reminds investors to watch the data like a hawk and avoid being either too optimistic or too pessimistic.

— Dan Niles
30:43

Software faces AI disruption; security and gaming are relatively safe

Dan Niles argues AI's impact on software is enormous. OpenAI and Anthropic's combined annualized revenue soared from $29 billion at the start of the year to $105 billion seven months later, and that spending must come out of other software budgets. He likes three relatively safe areas: cybersecurity, recording systems, and video games. Recent moves — Salesforce partnering with Anthropic, Workday being acquired by Silver Lake — suggest software stocks may be near a bottom, but valuations in security are already high.

— Dan Niles
39:23

Credit markets are the AI bubble's early-warning system

Dan Niles stresses that credit markets are the key to judging the AI bubble. He monitors daily the credit default swap (CDS) prices of hyperscalers such as Microsoft, Google and Nvidia, and finds they are already above the North American investment-grade average and at historic highs. Credit investors are more cautious than equity investors, because bond returns are fixed and principal must be protected. Massive AI capex depends on debt financing, and 30-year Treasury yields have risen to their highest since 2007, raising financing costs — which could be the spark that pops the bubble.

— Dan Niles

Glossary

Credit Default Swap (CDS)
A financial derivative used to hedge bond default risk; its price reflects the market's expectation of default probability.
Hyperscaler
A giant that owns large-scale data centers and provides cloud services, such as AWS, Azure and Google Cloud.
Token
The basic unit of text an AI model processes; token usage is a key measure of AI demand.
Agentic AI
AI systems that can autonomously carry out multi-step tasks, consuming far more tokens than chat AI.
