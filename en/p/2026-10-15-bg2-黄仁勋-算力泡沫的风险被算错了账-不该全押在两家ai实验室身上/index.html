Jensen Huang: Compute Bubble Risks Are Miscalculated, Don't Bet Everything on Two AI Labs

He breaks NVIDIA's revenue into three equal parts—classical computing, frontier labs, and vast numbers of startups each taking a third—and the real bottleneck for next year's 43 gigawatts of new compute is land, power, and facilities, not the revenue curves of two labs.

8:16

Thinking consumes more compute than memory

Huang explains that the thousand-billionfold surge in compute demand stems from ‘reasoning’ being far more expensive than ‘retrieval’. Early ChatGPT mostly regurgitated what pretraining had memorized. Today's models first reflect, enumerate multiple options, simulate outcomes, and prune to find the best path—that's where compute is consumed. Better models breed broader adoption; usage and workloads compound together, and that cycle is the root logic driving next year's 43-gigawatt demand.

— Jensen Huang
34:06

Alignment has no final solution because goodness is irreconcilable

Alignment's real challenge isn't technical—it's that ‘do good’ can be reasoned as infinite valid acts: some believe serving society means charity, others that it means building strong company, both defensible. Perfect alignment as a safety guarantee is a dead end. His alternative: borrow from parenting, pet-keeping, hiring. Start with zero trust and minimal privileges, like gating a one-year-old's space, then gradually expand as trust is earned. Monitoring runs ‘out-of-band’—the AI never learns it's watched—with escalation or shutdown if drift occurs.

— Jensen Huang
43:19

Regulation already has three legs of governance in place

Against claims of US regulatory inaction on AI, Huang lists three operational mechanisms: Commerce and Casey review every new frontier model pre-release—and have actually delisted Mythos; the White House superintelligence accord (which Huang helped draft) requires internal and external audit and board oversight; and SI Force under Jay Clayton coordinates cross-industry cybersecurity. His position: debate perfect solutions versus implement these three teeth-bearing mechanisms and iterate live.

— Jensen Huang
46:20

The bottleneck is land and power, not silicon

Industry forecasts 43 gigawatts of new AI capacity next year—exceeding all existing global AI compute—plus another 40 the year after. Huang concedes demand is real. The bottleneck is physical: land permitting, power provisioning, construction labor, and installation capacity, none substitutable, all vulnerable to local halts and protest. NVIDIA's hedge is universal coverage—every cloud provider, every sector, full train-to-infer chain—so regional chokepoints divert to others. But Huang admits that if the US lags, other nations happily absorb the data-center build.

— Jensen Huang
1:01:53

Revenue splits three ways, don't mistake frontier labs for the entire market

To counter bubble claims, Huang offers a three-part framework: roughly one-third of NVIDIA's revenue is AI-agnostic classical compute—simulation, molecular dynamics, recommendation, anti-fraud—already growing 30-40% annually on its own; one-quarter to one-third from frontier labs, not just OpenAI and Anthropic but also Meta, Gemini, Grok, and five or six others; the remainder from startups and next-gen cloud, 80% running open models (including NVIDIA's Nemotron) on vertical AI—manufacturing, medical—in no way cannibalizing frontier labs. His conclusion: multiplying GPU units by per-gigawatt revenue and extrapolating to ‘labs must earn $X trillion’ miscounts the entire picture.

— Jensen Huang
1:08:22

Moore's Law is dead, and that's pushing capex skyward

For 30 years Moore's Law halved compute cost yearly—double transistors, wait a year, same price. Moore's Law is over: double transistors today, and you pay double forever. That ‘Moore tax’ stacks atop organic growth in the half-trillion-dollar global IT spend, pushing the industry toward his prior forecast: $3-4 trillion annual infrastructure spend by decade end, $1.5 trillion of it compute alone. In other words, capex isn't runaway spending—it's the per-unit cost of silicon.

— Jensen Huang
1:19:42

Hundred-billion-dollar orders bet blind because validation itself is impossible

Huang explains customer willingness to commit tens of billions to next-gen Vera Rubin Ultra unseen: systems this complex can't be benchmarked like chips—building a test environment takes a data center and years. So trust shifts to manufacturer. As NVIDIA backs 100% of orders with TSMC because TSMC alone enables five-year planning and guarantees on yield and volume, NVIDIA earns the same. Hardware's universal across models, workloads, customers, geographies—so concentrating spend cuts risk, not increases it. That's part of why NVIDIA's 2027 consensus forecast flipped from ‘flat or decline’ to ‘74% growth’ in a year.

— Jensen Huang
1:26:59

Two software and services revenue streams are taking shape alongside chips

Huang discloses two new revenue engines independent of hardware: one, software licensing at ‘thousands of dollars per GPU per year’, for enterprises without IT ops who need NVIDIA long-term for AI factory uptime and security; NVIDIA ships tens of millions of GPUs annually, and that compounds. Two, RevShare: cloud providers dependent on NVIDIA's full-stack platform, power bundles, and financing pay upfront plus 4-10 year revenue splits rather than one-time silicon buys. He notes: paper gains from OpenAI and Anthropic stakes won't repeat. These two lines are the coming cash-flow core.

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

Physical AI
AI that interacts with the physical world, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and sensor networks.
Digital twin
A virtual replica of the physical world, used to train AI and robots before deployment to reality.
Zero trust
Assume no trust by default, grant only the minimum permissions needed to complete tasks, and gradually expand access.
Fungible compute
Hardware that works interchangeably across different models, workloads, customers, and regions.
RevShare
A model where NVIDIA takes a cut of cloud providers' future revenue rather than just selling hardware once.
Navier-Stokes equations
A Millennium Prize Problem describing the mathematics of fluid dynamics.
