Conspiracy theories persist because people refuse to accept that a minor figure could topple a president

A Pulitzer Prize-winning biographer says forensic evidence points strongly to Oswald acting alone, but believes people resist this conclusion because they cannot accept that such a pivotal historical moment could result from the actions of one marginalized individual.

8:28

Dallas that morning was saturated with murderous intent

That morning as Kennedy set out for Dallas, the streets were distributing flyers bearing his image and accusing him of being a ‘traitor.’The Dallas Morning News had published a full-page satirical advertisement framed in the black borders of a funeral notice. Those around him urged him not to go. Jackie felt dread, saying they were entering ‘the territory of madmen.’Yet Kennedy insisted. Texas Democrats were split between warring factions, and he needed this trip to heal the breach and secure his path to reelection. Jackie's presence was part of the strategy—he needed her at his side.

11:40

He predicted he would die at forty-five and nearly did

Kennedy often told people he would live to forty-five; by the event, he was nearly prescient—he died at forty-six. His biographer sees multiple roots to this fatalism: illness recurring from age two or three, three times given last rites; witnessing comrades fall one by one on the battlefield, which convinced him of life's fragility; and his consistent personal courage, which made him indifferent to Secret Service protection. Deeper still: both his brother Joe and sister Kick died in plane crashes, yet it was Joe—not Kennedy himself—who was supposed to ‘not live long.’This borrowed-time sensibility, perhaps, was the real reason he insisted on going to Dallas despite knowing the danger.

— Frederick Logevall
19:59

Three shots in six to seven seconds, with only the third proving fatal

As the motorcade passed below the Texas School Book Depository and turned into Dealey Plaza, three shots rang out over roughly six to seven seconds. The first likely missed. The second struck Kennedy in the back and throat, also hitting Governor Connally. The third, truly fatal, caused severe head trauma. Jackie instinctively crawled toward the car's back. Secret Service agent Clint Hill jumped aboard, and the motorcade sped toward Parkland Hospital four minutes away—but by then everyone close enough to see already knew it was over.

— Frederick Logevall
23:03

Twenty-six seconds of home video became the most dissected footage in history

That day, a Dallas tailor named Abraham Zapruder brought his camera and inadvertently captured roughly twenty-five to twenty-six seconds of color footage. This material was later dissected into 486 frames for frame-by-frame analysis, becoming the most-studied visual record in history. Because this footage exists, the event remained vivid in collective memory decades later—had Zapruder not been there or had he been ill, these images would not exist. The most contested element within the footage is the so-called ‘magic bullet theory’: the bullet's trajectory became the central battleground between conspiracy theories and the official account.

— Frederick Logevall
33:38

Forensic evidence points to Oswald, but whom he may have met remains a mystery

The biographer is unambiguous: forensic evidence points very strongly to Oswald acting alone; all three shots could easily have been fired from the same rifle within six to seven seconds. He rejects the claim that this was ‘impossible.’Yet he simultaneously admits to an open mind on whether anyone may have encouraged Oswald: even if someone had told him ‘you should do this’or ‘if you do this I support you,’by definition that would constitute a conspiracy. Weeks before the assassination, Oswald had been in Mexico City meeting with Cuban and Soviet officials, requesting passage to Cuba. Given prior assassination attempts against Castro, Cuba had motive; the Soviet Union, with warming relations between the two countries, had weaker motive.

— Frederick Logevall
40:12

Conspiracy theories endure because the truth is simply too hard to accept

The biographer identifies several reasons conspiracy theories have endured: the Warren Commission's investigation did contain errors; the case itself left unresolved questions; Oswald maintained until his death that he was a ‘scapegoat.’Yet he especially emphasizes a deeper psychological cause: people want to believe that momentous events must have momentous causes behind them. It's difficult to accept that a marginalized, self-righteous loner could single-handedly topple the most powerful man in the world at that time. He points to both the assassination of Gandhi and Gavrilo Princip—who sparked World War I—as examples of how marginal figures have catalyzed massive historical upheaval.

— Frederick Logevall
42:13

The killer died on camera; the photographer's equipment failure gave him the exclusive

Two days later, as Oswald was being escorted from the police headquarters basement, nightclub owner Jack Ruby shot him dead in public—also televised, further fueling conspiracy theories. People couldn't understand why a nightclub owner was allowed to shoot a suspect at close range. The photographer who captured this moment was Robert H. Jackson. Two days earlier, at Kennedy's assassination site, his film had failed to arrive in time, causing him to miss the entire shooting sequence. This time, his flash malfunctioned temporarily and he fell behind—but this very mishap allowed him to avoid the moment others had captured, and he instead caught the instant when Oswald was shot, securing the photograph that would later enter the history books.

— Frederick Logevall
48:23

Jackie replicated Lincoln's funeral and staged the most-watched ceremony of the era

Jackie herself determined nearly every detail of Kennedy's funeral. She had materials on Lincoln's funeral retrieved from the Library of Congress and replicated key elements: the riderless horse with reversed stirrups, the eternal flame. She insisted on walking behind the coffin rather than riding in what she called ‘that black Cadillac.’The funeral drew a larger television audience than the Kennedy-Nixon debates. About ninety foreign heads of state and dignitaries walked in the procession. The series concludes with Kennedy's undelivered speech—the one he was meant to give at the Dallas Trade Mart, warning against ‘mistaking rhetoric for truth’and calling for ‘knowledge and reason.’That sentiment cuts just as sharply today.

— Frederick Logevall

Glossary

magic bullet theory
A contested explanation of the ballistic evidence: the theory that a single bullet struck both Kennedy and Governor Connally multiple times before exiting.
grassy knoll
A small rise in Dealey Plaza near where Kennedy was shot; conspiracy theorists believed a second shooter was positioned there.
Warren Commission
The official U.S. government body established to investigate Kennedy's assassination, which concluded Oswald acted alone.
Zapruder film
Approximately 25-26 seconds of color footage captured by Dallas resident Abraham Zapruder, showing the assassination; the most analyzed video in history.
