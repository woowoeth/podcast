Liangpei wants users to leave fast—that's exactly how it makes money

Liangpei (良配) sells memberships that can't be renewed; users leave once they match. The faster they go, the fewer tokens the platform burns—exactly opposite to most AI products profiting from retention.

3:00

Traditional matchmakers price by what you can pay, not what service costs

Zeng Xinxun notes that among 150 couples who registered at Shenzhen's Civil Affairs Bureau, fewer than 5 percent succeeded through professional matchmakers. Traditional matchmakers don't price based on the service itself, but first probe your willingness to pay and credit card limits—the same service sells for 20,000, 30,000 or 50,000 yuan depending on how much you can afford. More egregiously, some agencies pressure young women to take loans, using the pitch ‘it's better to find a husband than buy a handbag’ to package dating as an investment.

21:15

Grassroots teams lack resources to compete with well-funded rivals

Zeng ran a campus food delivery platform in university that generated over 10 million yuan in annual revenue at peak, but was completely shut out when Ele.me raised 80 million dollars and rapidly expanded to 200 cities. He concluded this wasn't a product or execution problem, but that grassroots teams lack the resources to race against opponents already backed by capital markets—which is why he spent 8 years before this venture deliberately ‘accumulating equipment’ (technology, team, industry credentials).

— Zeng Xinxun
37:23

Three hours to raise 15 million required eight years of prep

A week after resigning as CTO of Kimi, Zeng met with investors; by chance at a hackathon café, he talked for 15 minutes with someone from Jujin Capital, who immediately called to verify his Kimi background and set up a meeting with Xu Xin the next day. In three hours of negotiation, Xu Xin proactively offered to provide the full 15 million yuan representing 20 percent equity in one payment, with zero protective clauses in the agreement. Zeng says this wasn't luck, but the accumulated technology, team, and industry credentials he'd built over 8 years all materializing at that moment.

— Zeng Xinxun
44:29

Four structural flaws prevent dating apps from helping you pair up

Zeng identifies four categories of problems in existing dating products: platforms mix users with different needs to boost DAU, with profiles limited to 100-150 characters creating severe information shortage; an endless stream of candidates creates the illusion of abundance, making people constantly compare and undervalue the person in front of them; and the platform's business model rewards longer user stays, giving it no incentive to actually help you find someone, putting user and platform interests fundamentally at odds.

— Zeng Xinxun
47:31

Single-match design escapes the user-platform prisoner's dilemma

Liangpei designed the EV1 matching mechanism, which doesn't offer a continuous candidate pool but shows one person at a time. Zeng believes previous products didn't dare try this because match success rates were too low—EV1's risk directly exposes you to the 1-minus-success-rate failure probability; Liangpei only dared build EV1 because AI collected two to three thousand characters of context, raising match accuracy high enough to support it.

— Zeng Xinxun
50:36

Only non-renewable memberships make platforms want you to succeed

Liangpei's core memberships are non-renewable: the 2,000 yuan ‘guaranteed marriage membership’ keeps the payment if users marry within three years, otherwise fully refunded; there's also a 1,000 yuan perpetual membership paid in one lump sum. Zeng explains the design works because the platform burns tokens daily for each user, and the longer users stay the higher the cost, so the platform naturally wants users matched quickly and gone—the opposite of most software that profits from renewal and time spent.

— Zeng Xinxun
53:37

Acquisition cost rose from a few yuan to over 50

In 2018-19 doing campus delivery, acquiring a user cost just a few yuan, but today in the two-sided market Liangpei faces, 50 yuan acquisition cost isn't enough. In the first week after launch, Zeng watched growth curves daily with anxiety, because the earliest users trusted the product most but experienced the smallest sample size and matching pool, until a few articles went viral and user scale picked up.

— Zeng Xinxun
56:39

Betting everything on one path beats hedging between many options

Zeng says at every major inflection point in his life he chose only one path—applied to only Southern University of Science and Technology for college, picked one place when job hunting and went there, and for this venture only does dating, something he experienced personally, rather than the billion-dollar-market spaces investors suggested. He believes that with multiple choices you fall into decision paralysis, efficiency drops lowest, and going all-in actually provides clarity.

— Zeng Xinxun
