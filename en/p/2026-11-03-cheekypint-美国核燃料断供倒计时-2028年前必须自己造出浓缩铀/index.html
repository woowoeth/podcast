America's nuclear fuel cutoff countdown: must build domestic uranium enrichment by 2028

After the Cold War, America abandoned its uranium enrichment capacity and turned to importing Russian nuclear fuel; Congress set a 2028 ban, and General Matter must build domestic capacity by then.

The hardest part isn't the license, it's the fuel

Venture capitalist Scott Nolan spent a decade assuming new nuclear companies' biggest hurdle was getting NRC approval—until he asked them what was really hard and got an unexpected answer: fuel. America's 94 reactors generate 20% of its electricity, yet whether new or established, every company must import enriched uranium from Russia or Europe. The U.S. has no domestic commercial supplier. Congress has already passed legislation: effective January 1, 2028, Russian uranium imports are banned.

— Scott Nolan
9:22

Smaller reactors require higher-enrichment uranium fuel

To make small modular reactors (SMRs) compact enough for factory production, fuel enrichment must jump from today's 3-5% to nearly 20%—HALEU. Lower enrichment requires larger cores to maintain criticality; higher enrichment allows smaller cores and stretches refueling intervals from 18-24 months to 5-10 years. This matters most for reactors deployed in remote locations.

— Scott Nolan
12:13

The AI boom hasn't yet driven nuclear power adoption

Though everyone talks about AI data centers spinning up with natural gas turbines, Scott Nolan says it barely affects their business. For the next few years, the real contracts come from utilities running those 94 existing grid reactors—even if data centers run on gas for now. The real inflection comes later, when baseload demand shifts to ‘safe, clean, reliable’. That's when it matters whether gigawatt-scale reactors or SMRs fill the gap.

— Scott Nolan
15:27

Nuclear didn't get expensive because of Three Mile Island

Conventional wisdom blames Three Mile Island and Chernobyl for stopping nuclear. Nolan says safety concerns only accelerated the decline—the real culprit is that nuclear never achieved economies of scale. Each plant built itself, supply chains never formed, workers migrated between projects. Vogtle's cost overruns are brutal. Interest rates were at historic lows, yet 20% of the overrun came from interest costs. Over a decade build time, interest rates and schedule matter more than spot construction costs.

— Scott Nolan
17:56

Power utilities use cost-plus contracts; no one wants to cut prices

There's an incentive layer too. Utilities mostly operate on cost-plus contracts, outsourcing plant construction, so no one in the ecosystem has real pressure to cut costs. Nolan compares this to old-school space launch: without genuine market competition, everyone collects rent in their corner instead of racing on price.

— Scott Nolan
32:27

Fuel costs can represent half of an SMR's total expense

Nolan uses SpaceX as a parallel: when satellites cost $1 billion, a $100 million launch was a small fraction. But smaller, cheaper satellites? Launch becomes the lever. Same with SMRs. The plant shrinks from a $10-billion megaproject, yet fuel costs rise as a fraction—fuel costs can hit 50% of levelized cost in some SMRs. It's the largest lever General Matter believes it can move.

— Scott Nolan
44:11

Uranium enrichment technology hasn't been redesigned in fifty years

Cost advantage, Nolan says, comes from three sources: scale, simplified processes, and the one most overlooked—dormant technical progress. Globally, Russia, China, and two European companies all use the same underlying enrichment technology. Since the 1950s-60s, no one has redesigned it from scratch. Fifty years of technical improvements have never been applied to enrichment. That's the gap new entrants can exploit.

— Scott Nolan
53:19

Avoid competition first, then talk about scale

Reflecting on Founders Fund's investment philosophy, Nolan says the move is not to charge a trillion-dollar market on day one, but to find a small market with genuine need for a new solution, lock in those customers until they depend on you, then expand. Startup thresholds should be extreme: the mission must be critical, no one else is doing it, and it matches your capabilities. That intersection is tiny—which is why SpaceX, Anduril, and General Matter each emerged about a decade apart.

— Scott Nolan

Glossary

HALEU
Uranium enriched to approximately 19.75% for advanced small modular reactors
SMR
Small modular reactor—a new-generation reactor designed for factory production with a smaller core
LCOE
Levelized cost of electricity—construction and operating costs divided by total electricity output
EPC
Engineering, procurement, and construction—turnkey contracting integrating design, sourcing, and execution
cost-plus
Cost-plus contract—compensation based on actual cost plus fixed profit, lacking inherent pressure to reduce costs
