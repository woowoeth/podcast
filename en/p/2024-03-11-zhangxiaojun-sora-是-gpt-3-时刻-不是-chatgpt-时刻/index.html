Sora Is a GPT-3 Moment, Not a ChatGPT Moment

Sora shows an emergent capability, not a usable product; it pushes the large-model war to a new level, but catching up in a year is too optimistic, because between being able to do it and actually doing it lies a great deal of know how.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:07

Sora is a GPT-3 moment, not a ChatGPT moment

Ji Yichao likens Sora to GPT-3: it is a model that has certain emergent capabilities, not a product. ChatGPT was a product innovation that aligned GPT into a Chat form, letting users converse and generate more input and feedback; Sora, by contrast, generates a video and that is it, with no interaction with the user. So it still has a long way to go before becoming a product, and what emergence shows is only a spark. This distinction determines how to price Sora: it proves that a known model architecture can produce Sora, but that does not mean it can start charging money right away.

— Ji Yichao
23:45

Model scale is roughly 6B, compute one or two thousand H100s

From the sampling, the model parameter count is probably around 6B, based on the 4x and 16x compute scaling examples in the Tech Report, extrapolated from the DiT paper baseline. Training compute may be on the scale of one or two thousand H100s, that is, tens of millions of dollars, not something that requires a billion dollars. But running experiments and doing the final training run are two different things; OpenAI's already-deployed cards are estimated at two hundred thousand plus, so it can run experiments in parallel on far more cards. Data is what everyone is most focused on now, because model size has not risen significantly, and progress generally lies in the data and how it is processed.

— Ji Yichao
28:54

Between being able to do it and actually doing it lies know how

Dai Yusen thinks catching up to Sora in 6 to 12 months is too optimistic. The general direction is known, but how to tune the specific spots is not, and that is exactly what has to be figured out. After GPT-4 came out last year, many companies said they would catch up by year-end; now it is 2024, and the only one that has truly reached GPT-4 level across the board is probably Google's Gemini 1.5, and it has not yet been used in an actual product. Ji Yichao adds that OpenAI's starting point is much higher than that of domestic companies: Sora used DALL·E 3's recaptioning, and the language conditioning part may have used GPT's weights, so even with Sora sitting here, it still takes a long time of investment to approach it.

— Dai Yusen
34:37

Sora pushes the war to a new level, and draws regulation

The impact of Sora's release on the global large-model battle is, first of all, to push this war to a new level. First-tier companies all knew multimodality would see a breakthrough like this, only the timing came earlier, and many original plans have to change. Unlike ChatGPT, it is not an interactive model; as a pure generative model, what happens after generation is still unknown. But the fact that it so intuitively presents such a good video has a huge impact on startups, application companies, the entertainment and film industries, and government departments, and will also draw more worries about AI regulation; departments such as the broadcasting regulator are paying very close attention to Sora's appearance.

— Dai Yusen
37:06

World simulators have three layers: physical, social, long-duration

Ji Yichao divides world simulation into different layers: the first stage is relatively static scenery like aerial empty shots; the second stage is physical causality, such as a cup filled with water falling and shattering, but Sora performs very poorly on this example, the cup does not know how to shatter; the next layer is social causality, such as a shark appearing on a beach being surprising, a dolphin appearing feeling cute, a crocodile appearing making people flee, or a baby punching its father versus a strong man punching a thin person meaning completely different things; further still is the echo between the beginning and the end of a film. He believes what Sora shows now is still an emergent capability, and actually putting it to use will take quite a long time.

— Ji Yichao
46:22

No viable path has been found for a unified model yet

Language is discrete, while images and audio are natural continuous signals, and putting all the different inputs into one feature space is itself a difficulty. Harder still is the training objective: language models predict the next word, diffusion models learn to create or predict noise, and designing a Loss or task that achieves both multimodal understanding and multimodal output at once is very hard and worth researching. There is currently no definitive evidence that training multiple modalities together lets model capabilities achieve a higher breakthrough; the main capability of multimodal models still comes from the language backbone, and the more likely outcome is that progress advances in stitched-together or bolt-on form.

— Ji Yichao
56:19

The bubble is not scary; it brings infrastructure

Dai Yusen says the bubble will bring important infrastructure, and infrastructure lays the foundation for future applications; the 99% that die in the bubble will die, but the 1% that remains may be great companies. After the internet bubble burst, what remained was Amazon and Google, and Yahoo also survived for a long time. Right now we are far from the point where the bubble is fairly crazy, because the internet's real peak came when the first wave of internet-native applications truly landed and truly went public. Why not wait for the bubble to burst before investing? Because not investing at all means missing Amazon and Google, and the industry knowledge gained along the way matters a lot; it is hard to eat only the fifth bun.

— Dai Yusen
1:14:27

AI applications become useful more slowly, but spread faster

Ji Yichao uses mobile internet as an analogy: after the iPhone moment came the App Store moment and the iPhone 4 moment; GPT now has a GPT store, but it cannot be compared to the App Store at all, and may be at the 0708 point in time. Dai Yusen thinks that under the existing paradigm, the point at which AI applications become useful will be slower than mobile internet, because the model needs to reach a certain level of capability before it can emerge from useless into useful; but once it becomes useful, its spread may be far faster than that of mobile internet applications, because the internet and mobile internet were processes where both software and hardware had to spread, whereas for AI, as long as the device does not change and it runs on phones, usefulness could sweep across hundreds of millions of people in one or two years.

— Dai Yusen

Glossary

DiT / Diffusion Transformer
A model architecture that uses the Transformer architecture for diffusion generation; Sora's backbone.
recaptioning
Using GPT-4 to write more detailed text descriptions for training material, enhancing the realism of generation.
native resolution
Training and inference support various resolutions, directly outputting the resolution suited to the device.
cherry picking
Selecting the best from many generation results to show, with the outside world unable to see the failures.
alignment
Making model behavior conform to human intent and values; involves the question of whom to align with.
