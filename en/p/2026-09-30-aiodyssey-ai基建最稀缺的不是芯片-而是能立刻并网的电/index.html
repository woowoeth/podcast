AI Odyssey 2026-09-30

The scarcest resource in AI infrastructure is not chips—it's power that can go online immediately

GPUs can be bought with money and optimization capability will be leveled by open-source; in the long term, the only true scarcity is electricity that can be deployed immediately and land with pre-approved grid interconnection permits. This means New Cloud competition is actually about fundraising and operational capacity, not technology.

6:04

Acquisition or open-source: two opposite bets

Nebius paid $643 million to acquire the Eigen AI team and infrastructure stack; Clarify even sealed the deal with just a technical license—the fact that a technology could be bought this way suggests it never had pricing power to begin with. Crusoe took the opposite route: it contributed its own Managed Inference Tokenizer directly to NVIDIA's Dynamo open-source project, effectively admitting the technology cannot be defended, and instead planted itself at the center of the standard. The two companies are betting opposite propositions: Nebius is betting that optimization capability can be privatized; Crusoe is betting that it will become a public good, with everyone running code Crusoe wrote from then on.

— Ray
9:09

The truly scarce resource is power, not GPUs

GPUs can be purchased with money, and optimization capability won't be scarce long-term—it can be open-sourced or acquired, and within 12 to 24 months gaps between players will narrow. The truly scarce resource is electricity available immediately and power land, plus the capacity to absorb utilization risk. Nebius discloses power reserves in its financial reports more aggressively than GPU inventory; contracted power in Q1 exceeded 3.5 megawatts with guidance of 4 megawatts by year-end—this is the metric investors should use to value the company.

— Ray
14:19

Revenue doubled, but profit fell 36%

Revenue growth only proves that market demand exists and capacity can be sold; it doesn't prove the business is profitable, because revenue growth is a function of capital expenditure, not competitive strength—buy more cards, rent them out, revenue goes up naturally. CoreWeave's adjusted operating profit tells the real story: this quarter $128 million at 5% margin, versus $200 million the year before—over the past year, revenue doubled but operating profit fell 36%.

— Ray
17:31

Lock in long contracts or keep ammunition to bet on spot prices

This year Nebius didn't lock all its capacity into customer contracts; management said datacenter capacity remains tight but deliberately reserved some compute for its token factory, betting that shorter-term contracts would command higher prices—betting it has pricing power. CoreWeave went the opposite way, extending contract terms from four years to about five, locking in supply aggressively. These two paths correspond to two risk configurations: CoreWeave transfers utilization risk to customers and gives up upside; Nebius retains pricing upside but takes the risk of idle GPUs itself.

— Ray
20:37

Selling GPU hours and selling tokens are two different businesses

Switching from GPU hours to tokens is like moving from a landlord model to running an operations business: income shifts from multi-year contracts to real-time usage, earning only when someone uses it today; profit margin risk transfers from customers back to you; the source of gross margin changes too. GPU hours profit from funding-cost and rental spreads—financially driven at heart; tokens profit from utilization spreads and optimization efficiency—operationally driven at heart. Customer switching cost is nearly zero: replacing a supplier is just changing one line of code in the base URL. Token pricing has higher gross margin potential but lower certainty.

— Ray
26:50

The inference platform's moat lives on the customer side

Inference API layers have near-zero switching costs, unit pricing is directly comparable, pushing serverless platforms into a price war with no exit until there's no markup left. The layer that can command a premium is post-training, but Fireworks founder Qiao Lin has said that moat is the customer's moat, not Fireworks' own—by embedding customer data and training workflows on their platform, the moat is rented. It depends on customers growing first, and once customers mature, they have some probability of switching or building in-house.

— Ray
33:12

Power land is harder to replicate than GPUs

Sun Yuchun tweeted that we face a GPU shortage in the short term, power shortage in the medium term, and storage shortage in the long term. By 2026, signs of GPU shortage relief are visible, but power infrastructure is nowhere close. More precisely, we lack power that can be deployed immediately. Power land means land that has already obtained grid interconnection permits, with substations and water access. GPUs can be mass-produced and depreciate within 18 months, but grid interconnection permits cannot be mass-produced—they only become scarcer and more expensive. The podcast also mentioned that tidal power company Panthalassa no longer lays undersea cables; instead, it converts power directly to tokens locally and transmits via Starlink.

— Ray
40:44

Two signals of the hype cycle topping out

Two signals tell you whether AI infrastructure hype is topping out: one is if a company unwinds its long positions early and actively seeks out customers to lock in resources, it signals a shift from optimism to pessimism about future token prices. The second is when a mature company's token revenue from datacenter operations can no longer offset total operating costs—that marks a structural inflection point.

— Ray

Glossary

Token Factory
An inference business model that converts compute into token output and charges real-time per-unit usage.
Power land
Land with grid interconnection permits already obtained, equipped with substations and water access for building datacenters.
Post-training
Secondary training or fine-tuning done on open-source base models using customer proprietary data.
Gateway
A neutral multi-model API endpoint that avoids system-level optimization to preserve neutrality.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization—a profit metric that excludes depreciation and interest but can mask real financial risk.
