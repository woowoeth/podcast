Chaos theory reveals the physical ceiling of intelligence

A meteorologist told the story that explains it: run a weather forecast twice, changing only the precision of initial pressure and temperature data—drop a decimal place. Both runs should produce nearly identical results. Instead, one forecast says sunny and 70°F, the other says storms and 20°F—worlds apart. The reason: many systems are inherently unpredictable. Not because intelligence is lacking, but because simulating part of the universe requires simulating the universe entire. No model, however sophisticated, surmounts this constraint.

When everyone owns best-in-class intelligence, what competitive advantages remain?

His method for analyzing a company: assume all people and all companies own top-tier intelligence, then ask what remains that intelligence cannot buy. The answer is data, attention, emotional attachment, physical proximity, scale, even regulatory moats—these are the ‘complements to intelligence.’ If a company's value comes from consumer laziness and reluctance to think, AI proliferation destroys it. If value comes from these complementary resources, AI proliferation strengthens it.

— Cliff Sosin
9:17

Meta's entire 250-billion-dollar budget flows almost exclusively into intelligence

From a satellite view of Meta: 250 billion in revenue, 250 billion in spending, nearly all flowing to intelligence—data centers are the infrastructure for building intelligence, purchased inference is intelligence, engineering is intelligence, even reviewing video and moderating content count as intelligence-related work. As intelligence costs plummet, this spending theoretically disappears. What Meta truly owns, what intelligence cannot destroy, is massive user data and attention. That is the moat.

— Cliff Sosin
17:29

Jevons paradox applies only to one segment of the demand curve

He rejects the claim that lower prices mean infinite demand growth. Every market traces a U-shaped curve: the product is too weak, nobody buys it; it improves and stays expensive, few buy it; price drops and demand explodes Jevons-style, then continues falling and demand actually shrinks, eventually to zero. He cites fiber-optic communications: transmission costs collapsed, markets boomed then imploded. Cogent Communications today carries approximately 30-40% of global IP traffic but has a market cap measured in only billions of dollars.

— Cliff Sosin
22:37

Breeding mice takes a year: automated labs still hit biological clocks

Automated wet labs and robot-driven research cycles do accelerate learning, but they crash into the physical world's speed limit. If an experiment requires mice to breed and mice need a year to mature and die, the experiment takes a year—no model changes that. Even if AI picks experiments that succeed at only 1% but that is ten times better than human researchers, that is impressive. But lab scale and physical time remain hard constraints, not ‘the model suddenly grasped something humans never understood.’

— Cliff Sosin
30:57

Universal comparison agents force every industry toward winner-take-most

Take garbage collection. Today people lazily keep their inefficient providers rather than switching. Once everyone owns an AI agent that auto-compares prices and negotiates for them, the market overnight shifts to the lowest-cost provider. Further: the agent can persuade an entire neighborhood to consolidate to one provider; economies of scale let that provider cut prices further; one player wins everything, but margins compress to nothing.

— Cliff Sosin
42:24

Advising portfolio companies: vibe-code hackathon first, read-only access second

His staged rollout advice: First, run a ‘vibe coding’ hackathon where everyone—board to CEO—participates. The goal is not usable software but resetting everyone's expectation of what AI can do. Next, the CEO requires an AI agent have read-only access to all company systems. Then gradually expand to all staff, then write access, then agents that directly modify systems. This takes years. The real question is: do you have enough time to complete this before newcomers starting from zero catch up?

— Cliff Sosin
52:43

Split stock-picking from position-sizing: AI portfolio management vastly outperforms

A friend ran an experiment: split investing into ‘what to pick’ and ‘how much to allocate.’ Stock-picking remained human. Position-sizing split into two: one branch stayed human, one went to AI. The AI branch vastly outperformed the human's already-mature position-management method. Cliff sees AI entering investing first in second-to-minute cycles, then gradually longer ones, because long-cycle strategies need decades to validate.

— Cliff Sosin

Glossary

Jevons Paradox
The phenomenon where efficiency improvements in resource use lead to increased total consumption rather than decreased consumption.
complements to intelligence
Resources, capabilities, or assets that AI cannot replace and become more valuable as artificial intelligence scales and commoditizes knowledge work.
diamond-shaped demand frontier
A demand distribution where effective demand for intelligence expands as capability improves, then contracts again as price drops, forming a diamond-shaped curve.
vibe coding
Generating usable programs through conversational interaction with AI rather than writing code directly by hand.
freestyle chess
Human-machine collaborative chess competition in which human players use computer assistance, once producing stronger play than pure human or pure AI competitors.
