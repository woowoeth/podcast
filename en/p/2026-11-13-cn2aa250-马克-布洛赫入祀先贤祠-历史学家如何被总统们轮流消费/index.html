Marc Bloch's Induction into the Panthéon: How Historians Become Political Tools for Presidents

Bloch resisted academic authority in life and rejected racial identity labels; in death he became a tool for successive French presidents to construct a "resistance president" image, splitting his family into opposing camps.

5:03

New historiographical method written in WWI trenches

Bloch served in trenches at the Marne and Somme in WWI, lived as a common soldier, and judged shell types by their sound and smoke rather than relying on traditional sources. From this he emphasized that history's subject is fundamentally plural humanity, not one or two elites—a principle central to the Annales School's choice of sources: not reconstructing elite narratives from archives, but grasping the masses.

11:05

Mainstream historiography's rejection gave birth to the Annales School

Mainstream French historiography then was Rankean positivism, whose core doctrine was ‘no archive, no history’; history became a chronicle of great men. Bloch mocked this as ‘the noise of events—you capture only the waves on the surface but miss the deep currents that determine the ocean's flow.’ Unable to succeed in Parisian academia, he seized the chance when Alsace was restored and Strasbourg University recruited young scholars, finding in Lucien Febvre a perfect partner to launch the Annales journal—an academic revolution that was, in a sense, a breakthrough from the margins.

— Xu Zhikai
20:11

Backward research method: from present traces to ancient origins

Bloch advocated doing history backward: starting from habits we recognize today, peeling away like an onion rather than marching chronologically from ancient to modern—the latter easily traps us in lies invented by later generations, since the past is always shaped by those who come after. In The Royal Touch, he used this reverse genealogy to discover that the ritual's rise occurred precisely when royal power was weakest, most desperate to wrest sacred legitimacy from Rome.

— Xu Zhikai
24:13

The day after Dunkirk, he chose to slip back to France

Bloch was among the few French soldiers evacuated to Britain from Dunkirk; another month would have let him join de Gaulle's June 18 broadcast and become a Free French founding hero. Instead, that very day he shed his uniform, put on civilian clothes, and slipped back into France—because his wife, children, and elderly mother remained there. This choice determined the next four years of his life: underground resistance rather than glory abroad.

— Xu Zhikai
31:16

Strange Defeat: anger more than analysis in his account of collapse

Bloch insisted that France's 1940 collapse was not purely military defeat but political collapse and social decay, and in Strange Defeat he condemned politicians, generals, workers, capitalists, and intellectuals alike. Yet American historian Ernest May used quantified data to show the French had more guns and tanks than the Germans on paper and could have won. Xu Zhikai argues that Bloch, having just witnessed the fall of his nation and home, judged in anger; the view may be extreme, but it holds more value than cool historical reasoning.

— Xu Zhikai
40:24

For his family's sake he abandoned a chance to escape to America

In 1941 Bloch received eager responses from Columbia, Chicago, and UC Berkeley and could have escaped to America under the pretense of a visiting fellowship. But he insisted on taking his elderly mother and young adult children; visas never materialized, tickets were unaffordable. By the time his mother fell gravely ill, his wife became seriously sick, and the American invitations lapsed, he no longer reapplied—his family could not go, and so neither could he.

— Xu Zhikai
56:33

The 'resistance president' persona: French presidents' politics with borrowed ghosts

From Sarkozy to Hollande to Macron, no 21st-century French president actually participated in the Resistance, yet all style themselves ‘resistance presidents,’ using the Panthéon to display their toughness and resolve. Xu Zhikai uses a Dark Souls metaphor: historical figures burn their reputations to extend the life of a politician's power; once one dead king is exhausted, quickly find another.

— Xu Zhikai
1:01:38

Macron emphasizes Bloch's Judaism, contradicting his own will

In his will Bloch explicitly wrote ‘any form of religious faith, any so-called racial solidarity, is irrelevant to me; I was first a Frenchman,’ yet Macron has repeatedly emphasized his Jewishness and his experience of persecution in multiple settings. This is described as a form of cross-temporal gaslighting political correctness; moreover, Bloch's granddaughter Suzette has previously made public statements rejecting the instrumentalization of Marc Bloch by Nicolas Sarkozy.

Glossary

Annales School
A French historical school that studies the past through cross-disciplinary, long-term, and holistic perspectives.
Panthéon
France's national mausoleum for the burial and commemoration of distinguished figures.
longue durée
A time scale, proposed by Braudel, that emphasizes deep historical structures rather than single events.
