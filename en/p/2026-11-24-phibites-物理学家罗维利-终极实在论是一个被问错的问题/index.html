Carlo Rovelli: Ultimate Reality Is a Wrongly Posed Question

Quantum mechanics reveals particles lack properties independent of observation; they possess only relational properties. This convinced Rovelli that ‘ultimate reality’ itself is a wrongly formulated problem, not a mystery awaiting solution.

Physicists do not possess reality's ultimate account

Rovelli opens by rejecting a claim common among his peers: that physics can tell us reality's essential nature. Physics does reveal truths about reality—important, sometimes paradigm-shifting ones—but revelation is not the ultimate story. There is no ultimate reality as such; reality is the full complexity visible and comprehensible around us.

— Carlo Rovelli
3:06

No single science holds privileged access to describing reality

Physics, chemistry, psychology all tell us more about reality—just in different languages. Describing something physically and mentally are two perspectives on one thing. Translating between some is easy (chemistry and physics), between others hard (psychology and neuroscience), but Rovelli sees no genuine contradiction.

— Carlo Rovelli
4:07

We are inside reality, not outside observing it

The Enlightenment bequeathed us an idea: reality exists out there, we stand outside using reason to grasp it completely and clearly. This is being abandoned. We inhabit reality; we see only locally from within. Everything else in reality also has its own local perspective on what remains.

— Carlo Rovelli
8:10

Quantum mechanics demolished classical realism's strongest defense

Rovelli credits quantum mechanics for this view. Trying to describe reality as systems possessing independent objective properties fails—Heisenberg uncertainty and superposition show that an isolated system has no properties in itself, only relational properties relative to other things. The deeper reductionism takes us, the less objectivity appears: we describe how things present themselves to us, not what they are.

— Carlo Rovelli
9:11

Relational properties do not equal subjective perception

Pressed on whether he's reducing reality to human subjective experience, Rovelli firmly denies it. He calls himself a strict naturalist: humans are part of nature, nothing special. His analogy: velocity is relational—a train is stationary relative to the ground but moving relative to the sun—yet has nothing to do with consciousness or subjectivity. The moon has different velocities relative to Earth and the sun, not because the sun is a conscious observer.

— Carlo Rovelli
13:16

Wittgenstein and Nagarjuna taught him to discard wrongly framed questions

Asked how he knows his tool won't itself mislead, Rovelli admits he offers not certainty but instruments to avoid error. His major influences are Wittgenstein, who showed certain problems are meaningless, and the Indian philosopher Nagarjuna (龙树), who taught that all things are ‘empty’—dependent on other things. Crucially, Nagarjuna even negates the proposition that emptiness is ultimate truth; that view too is empty, all views provisional.

— Carlo Rovelli
18:20

From heliocentrism to relativity: science advances by conceptual reframing

Responding to the duck-rabbit analogy, Rovelli sees science's most exquisite moments as precisely these gestalt shifts. Copernicus through Newton shattered the ancient divide between chaotic Earth and perfect eternal heaven, revealing Mars and Earth as the same kind of thing. Einstein's special relativity paper contained no equation his predecessors hadn't written; Poincaré and Lorentz had them. Einstein's move was abandoning one assumption: that the simultaneity of two events is time's inherent property. He reorganized how we think about reality—not through new equations but through reconceiving it.

— Carlo Rovelli

Glossary

relational properties
properties that exist not intrinsically but only relative to other systems
emptiness
Nagarjuna's concept that all things exist interdependently and lack independent self-nature
duck-rabbit / gestalt shift
a perceptual phenomenon where the same visual stimulus presents as entirely different images depending on perspective; used metaphorically for how scientific paradigm shifts reframe our conceptual categories
