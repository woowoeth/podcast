The Foundation Model War Is Over; Vertical Copilots Are the Old Guard's Opportunity

Foundation models are an emergence built by young people leveraging Microsoft's compute, but the real money in vertical copilots belongs to veteran entrepreneurs—two-thirds of vertical opportunities have already been killed by ChatGPT itself, and what remains is a combined battle of data, social, and business model.

4:04

ChatGPT trains the next version on the previous one, so improvement isn't linear

Wang Huainan points out that ChatGPT's leap from 3.5 to 4.0 is not version iteration, but using the full intelligence of the previous version to train the next, so each improvement is not linear. He quotes Lu Qi: there are only two moments in life when information explodes beyond coping—once when the browser appeared in '98, and now. This nonlinear pace of progress rapidly turns what seemed like an opportunity two months ago into something taken away—for example, wanting to build smart Excel reports, ChatGPT did it itself; wanting to do medical records, a Microsoft-invested company is already doing it.

— Wang Huainan
7:25

The foundation belongs to the young, vertical copilots to the old guard

Wang Huainan offers a polarized judgment: the foundation large language model is an emergence built by young people under Sam's leadership leveraging Microsoft's compute and Bing's data—belonging to new thinking, reckless innovation; but the vertical copilot opportunities growing on top most likely belong to veteran entrepreneurs. Because this is complex, requiring connection, judgment, and the ability to assemble teams—fledgling tech-savvy young people can't handle it. He observes that the first wave of activated people are Wang Huiwen, Wang Xiaochuan, Zhou Hongyi, and himself—all old guards over 35, while the internet once excluded those over 35.

— Wang Huainan
11:08

Two-thirds of vertical industries will be killed by ChatGPT itself

Wang Huainan judges that not every vertical industry copilot has a chance—more than half, even two-thirds, of industries will be killed by ChatGPT itself. The remaining industries, because of their complexity, require industry knowledge, data, and cognition, and belong only to those who have deeply studied the industry for a long time. He gives the example of lawyer AI: tools can always be replaced; you find 100 lawyers to train a lawyer AI, someone else finds 200 lawyers to train lawyer AI2; only community is irreplaceable, because it involves human interaction. So from tool to community to generating a business model—these three layers must be done in one go.

— Wang Huainan
13:10

Amazon will release a large and small model by end of May, anticipating the future

Wang Huainan reveals undisclosed news: Amazon will release two models by the end of May, a large language model and a small language model. He thinks Amazon is interesting—it anticipated the future of this matter: I'll compete for a ticket to the general large model, but simultaneously do a small language model, preemptively entering the possibility of vertical copilots. Amazon has dozens of teams operating in parallel, most working on the large language model, but very strong people working on the small language model, including Chinese people in the team. He judges this day will definitely come within a month, and what Amazon competes for will certainly be related to e-commerce and transactions.

— Wang Huainan
25:57

The foundation model war is over; Google should concede

Wang Huainan clearly judges: the foundation model war is over. Last time we talked, he said to observe a bit more; today he feels it's already lost, Google should concede, but won't concede immediately. He believes emergence can only emerge once, because leading the world, the best search engine to this day is still only Google; large models are fiercer than the search engine era, one dominant player, because it gets all the world's knowledge in the first instant. There is only one God, there cannot be multiple Gods. The only opportunity is in verticals; if there are 100 vertical opportunities, 60 are already off the table, and the remaining 30% must be found.

— Wang Huainan
27:30

Vertical moats are not in algorithms or compute, but data plus social plus business model

Wang Huainan believes the moat in verticals is not in algorithms, not even in compute—compute is a moat, like nuclear war, but more critical is data. In the coming year, companies with data and capability will fervently build independent vertical copilots themselves; those with data but no capability will be fervently acquired. But data is not entirely a moat either; social on top of data is the moat, and third is business model. He cites Bilibili as an example: it has data and social, but hasn't made money; content creators haven't made money on Bilibili, and if this problem isn't solved, excellent content and data creators will flee.

— Wang Huainan
32:40

The first shot of large models is search, second e-commerce, third entertainment

Wang Huainan judges the large model war's first shot is search, second e-commerce, third entertainment. E-commerce is a search scenario, but the pain is that no one browses anymore, so an intelligent copilot is needed to restore browsing—for example, a girl asking what cosmetics to use for her first day at work, not a copilot after knowing what to buy, but one that accompanies shopping when you don't know what to buy. In entertainment, putting ChatGPT and Midjourney together, everyone becomes a producer of their own entertainment content, the cost of creating excellent content rapidly approaches zero, entertainment will be bloodbath, everyone is a film director.

— Wang Huainan
37:50

The internet once excluded 35-year-olds; this time it's all over 35 rejuvenated

Wang Huainan observes something strange: the internet was once the domain of the young, excluding 35-year-olds, but this time everyone interested in this matter is over 35, from Zhou Hongyi to Wang Xiaochuan, Wang Huiwen, to himself. He believes the error rate of young people's judgments may be very high, while veterans have experience, feel, and the ability to handle complex situations—exactly what this matter needs. He suggests listing China's top 100 platforms by traffic today and seeing if attacks are effective; for example, if Xiaohongshu doesn't cut its own throat, it will have its throat cut by others in these three waves.

— Wang Huainan
