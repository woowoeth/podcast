Tesla admits: Hardware 3 can never achieve unsupervised self-driving

Hardware 3's memory bandwidth is only one-eighth of Hardware 4's; for the first time, the company officially confirmed this generation of hardware cannot achieve unsupervised FSD—owners must pay to replace it with new hardware to join the robotaxi fleet.

The information density here is substantial: Hardware 3 formally discontinued, Optimus output publicly stated as ‘impossible to predict’, AI5 chip taped out early but won't go into cars yet, and TerraFab responsibilities split between Tesla and SpaceX—all useful new information for judgment. The earnings call itself has significant numeric noise, though, so it's worth listening selectively.

8:00

2026 capital spending surge will fund generational transitions

Elon previewed in opening remarks that 2026 capital expenditure will increase significantly, funding battery power systems, AI software training, chip design and manufacturing capacity in preparation for substantially higher future revenue. He emphasized Tesla is not alone—nearly all major tech companies are simultaneously increasing capital investment, suggesting industry consensus rather than Tesla's unilateral gamble. The company later detailed this preview with specific numbers: 2026 capex expected to exceed $250 billion, with negative free cash flow in the second half.

— Elon
13:32

B3 goes public only when production-ready, not to let rivals copy

Elon acknowledged Tesla deliberately delayed B3's public launch because competitors analyze frame-by-frame and copy every reproducible technology, so the company prefers to wait until near-production to show it. B3 design is ‘nearly ready for demonstration’, functions operational, only cosmetic details need refinement—expected mid-year. Production launch around late July to August; a second Optimus factory at Giga Texas expected to begin production next summer.

— Elon
26:20

Ten thousand unique parts mean Optimus output is impossible to forecast

When asked Optimus's output target at year-end launch, Elon flatly said ‘impossible to predict’: wholly new product, entirely new line, over 10,000 unique parts—all must succeed for production ramp to work. Speed depends on the slowest, most unreliable part. He also corrected a misconception: Model S/X production lines can't be torn down and rebuilt overnight. The company must start with base components and gradually disassemble, then finally tackle the assembly line. The full conversion is expected to take months; completing it in four months would be unprecedented in the industry.

— Elon
30:55

Hardware 3 is finished; owners must pay to upgrade

Elon explicitly confirmed for the first time: Hardware 3's memory bandwidth is one-eighth of Hardware 4's, and bandwidth is the critical bottleneck for AI, especially autoregressive transformers. So Hardware 3 can never achieve unsupervised FSD. For HW3 owners who bought FSD, Tesla offers ‘discounted new-car trades’ or computer and camera upgrades; but replacing each one at service centers is too slow, so the company will build small ‘micro factories’ in major cities for batch processing. As a bridge, HW3 will get a distilled V14 before end of June—supporting direct launch from parked state and other core functions—but the ceiling remains supervised driving.

— Elon
34:10

AI5 taped out early but stays out of cars for now

AI5 finished taping out ahead of schedule; Elon credits the team working every weekend for six consecutive months, including all holidays, with no major mistakes forcing delay. On AI5's deployment, he was explicit: it goes into Optimus and data centers first, not cars immediately—AI4 already achieves unsupervised self-driving far safer than humans, so no urgent need to switch vehicles to AI5. Meanwhile, Tesla plans to upgrade to ‘AI4.1’ (memory from 16GB to 32GB, 64GB total per SoC), with ~10% higher compute and bandwidth, expected mid-next-year production—pace depends on when Samsung completes process modifications.

— Elon
43:48

TerraFab splits into Tesla and SpaceX division of labor

Asked about TerraFab's internal task split, Elon revealed the confirmed arrangement: Tesla builds a research fab around $3 billion at Giga Texas, starting with thousands of wafers monthly to validate new physics and processes; the scaled-up full-size TerraFab initially falls to SpaceX. Any related transactions between the two firms require separate Tesla and SpaceX board approval, going through conflict-of-interest review to protect both shareholders—meaning the process will be complex and slow. The process will adopt Intel's not-yet-mature 14A node, with the bet that 14A will be mature enough by the time TerraFab scales.

— Elon
48:54

FSD subscriptions are growing twice as fast as car sales

Analyst Pierre noted a counterintuitive pattern: this quarter added roughly 180,000 paying FSD users, but North America sold only about 100,000 cars—meaning FSD subscriber growth is two times faster than car sales, suggesting substantial Hardware 4 owners retroactively subscribing. Vaibhav confirmed this direction and added subscriber churn is declining and average driving miles per user are climbing, both signals of improving product experience. He cited personal experience: used to self-park; now doesn't have to park at all.

— Vaibhav
1:01:13

Robotaxi delays stem from overcaution, not actual safety shortfalls

Asked about Robotaxi expansion's safety metrics, Elon acknowledged the main bottleneck is not safety but ‘convenience’—the system is tuned for extreme safety caution, becoming oversafe: won't cross railroad tracks, gets stuck at lights that never turn green. He gave a concrete example: an entire row of Tesla Robotaxis in Austin blocked on a left-turn lane because a Waymo ahead hit a bus—since buses never move, the fleet sits idle. There have also been cases where cars loop in ‘dead cycles’ during construction. These stuck-state problems are the team's top priority now, not direct safety failures.

— Elon
