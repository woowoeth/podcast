Musk: Money will lose meaning, future currency is watts and tonnage

Musk argues that digital superintelligence could grow Earth's economy by at most 30 percent, but once robots can manipulate matter and enter space, economic scale could reach a million times Earth's current size, at which point traditional currency becomes completely obsolete.

High information density: the opening covers AI safety and political reflection, the middle presents hard judgments on economics and currency, the later sections touch controversial positions on race and immigration—worth hearing complete.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

2:04

He chose to guide the future he could not prevent

Musk acknowledges that AI carries some probability of existential threat, but he believes he cannot stop this process, so he has simplified his choice to two roles: ‘participant’ or ‘observer’, choosing the former in hopes of steering direction toward better outcomes. This logic explains his continued escalation in AI—buying Cursor, rebuilding xAI—which stems not from optimism but from active engagement after recognizing helplessness to prevent it.

— Elon Musk
7:10

The more he tried to stop AI, the more he accelerated it

Musk uses ‘reality is not just a pleasure maximizer but also an irony maximizer’ to explain an unexpected outcome: creating OpenAI partly to counterbalance AI risk instead led OpenAI to accelerate AI development; Anthropic, as a splinter from OpenAI, amplified that acceleration further. He admits this is a chain of unintended consequences.

— Elon Musk
9:11

Peer oversight from competitors works faster than government regulation

Musk believes government regulators build too slowly and set rules inefficiently, suggesting instead that the few frontier model companies—including his own and Anthropic—meet weekly with mutual access to test for vulnerabilities before releasing new models, such as checking whether models could be misused for nuclear weapons. He sees peer oversight among competitors as more practical than waiting for regulators and says he has already proposed this to the industry.

— Elon Musk
15:12

National debt cannot be solved by cutting spending, only by AI and robots

From his year in government, Musk concluded that neither the president, Congress, nor the judiciary genuinely wants to cut spending; interest on national debt now exceeds defense spending. He therefore judges that the only path to solving national debt is not internal cost-cutting but rather relying on AI and robots to dramatically increase output, explaining his decision to leave government and return to AI as the more rational choice.

— Elon Musk
16:12

The ability to manipulate matter amplifies intelligence's value exponentially

Musk uses dolphins and whales as analogy: they are intelligent but lack hands and cannot manipulate complex objects, so their intelligence's value is severely discounted. Once robots can manipulate atoms and shape objects, intelligence converts to actual output—building labs, running experiments, understanding the universe—and intelligence's value grows exponentially. This is the core logic behind his judgment that physical-world AI is one to two orders of magnitude more important than LLMs.

— Elon Musk
17:13

Digital superintelligence moves the economy 30%; robots plus space move it a millionfold

Musk provides two rough estimates: digital superintelligence could probably lift Earth's goods and services output by 20 to 30 percent, adding roughly thirty trillion dollars annually; but once equipped with robots and able to build mass drivers on the Moon and enter space, economic scale could reach a millionfold that of Earth's current economy. This magnitude gap underlies his entire thesis on currency.

— Elon Musk
18:13

Money becomes meaningless; future currency is watts and tonnage

Musk's core thesis: conventional currency will become unimportant in the future, replaced by ‘currency’ measured in physical units—such as watts (power) and tonnage (mass/energy). His chain of logic: once economic scale far exceeds current Earth volume, satisfying any conceivable human need becomes effortless, the resource scarcity that money allocates away from disappears, and money as a tool for distributing scarce resources becomes obsolete.

— Elon Musk
20:16

Wealth is not consumption power but resource allocation authority

Responding to concerns about inequality leading to social discontent, Musk offers an economic correction: people conflate resource distribution with consumption. Individual consumption has limits—increasing net worth from one billion to one hundred billion doesn't multiply happiness or possessions tenfold—the real issue is allocation efficiency. He uses this to explain his accumulation of trillions in assets: not for consumption but to retain sufficient voting power to continue allocating capital to Mars, AI, robots, and other pursuits, while acknowledging these assets will ultimately be diluted by heavy taxation and generational transfer, leaving descendants far less wealthy.

— Elon Musk
34:26

Humans may not retain control of AI

Musk concedes that if digital intelligence reaches a scale a million times that of biological intelligence, it is hard to imagine humans still maintaining control; he even says he has had moments thinking Larry Page's past position might be right—when Page once called him a ‘species bigot’ for insisting that ‘humans must always be in control’. This is the moment in the episode when Musk's position is most visibly flexible.

— Elon Musk
