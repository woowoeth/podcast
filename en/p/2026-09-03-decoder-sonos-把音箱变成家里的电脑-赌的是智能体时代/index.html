Sonos turns the speaker into the computer in your home, betting on the agent era

Sonos no longer sells individual speakers but a system: a home compute network of 1.7 billion devices, connecting to outside AI through MCP, and letting multiple assistants coexist on one platform via custom agents.

4:05

The company thought it was 100% a hardware company

Tom Conrad's earliest observation after taking over was that Sonos behaved like a 100% hardware company, when in fact it was 100% hardware plus 100% software — a slip on either side has enormous impact. He pulled the company back to one core proposition: Sonos does not sell individual products, it sells one thing, the Sonos system. Every product has to work together as a whole, or it isn't done well. That judgment directly explains why the Pinewood set-top box was killed and why the org was restructured.

— Tom Conrad
7:11

The unintended consequence of a product execution machine

Former CEO Patrick Spence turned the company into a "product execution machine," emphasising a steady cadence of a few new hardware products every year, and he hit that goal. But Tom argues that incentivising the org around that frame had an unintended consequence: the company became extremely focused on individual products, and even software work was organised around individual NPIs — "what can we do in software to make Move different from other speakers?" Horizontal software experiences that benefit the whole platform struggled to get prioritised. That debt accumulated, and the company thought the fix was redoing the app — which is what set off the 2024 crisis.

— Tom Conrad
17:32

Three weeks to go from business units to functions

Tom restructured the company within three weeks of taking over. The old structure was divided by business unit: home, pro, home theatre each running their own shop. His judgment was that the company isn't big enough to carry the inefficiency that structure creates — duplication across silos is nearly inevitable. More importantly, if the product is essentially a system, then a portable speaker team far from the home theatre team and far from the plugged-in speaker team is nearly guaranteed to work against the progress you want. Under a functional model, the product team is represented by three lines — hardware, software, global supply chain operations — and can flexibly assemble cross-functional matrix teams by priority, so people can move without changing bosses.

— Tom Conrad
23:50

Killing the set-top box was a strategy call, not a product call

Sonos had a set-top box project called Pinewood that would aggregate all streaming services. Tom killed it. His reasoning had two layers: first, the company was spread too thin and couldn't be excellent in every direction, and he wanted to return to where it could differentiate most — the integrity of the system and the audio experience; second, he suspected the product's motivation came from an internal imagining of a business opportunity rather than customer demand. He says that when a company has a business model first and a product second, it lands in big trouble. He acknowledges an obligation to keep evaluating new business opportunities, but it has to start from genuinely seeing customer demand in the market.

— Tom Conrad
33:45

Naming the software platform Sonos 27

For 20 years Sonos named every hardware product — Roam, Arc, Beam, Ace, Era — but never named the software platform. Now that platform is called Sonos 27. It has several layers: the ecosystem platform that aggregates a large number of heterogeneous services in the home; TruePlay tuning for each space; Sonos Fabric, which lets products discover each other on the local network and form a system greater than the sum of its parts; and the control and interaction layer including app, voice, web, plus the newly added MCP dimension. The naming logic: the company name already contains OS, so launching a separate brand would be self-harm, so the operating system is also called Sonos, with a year suffix to express that the platform evolves every year.

— Tom Conrad
44:20

MCP puts Sonos inside every AI assistant

Sonos 27 MCP will put Sonos in the connectable services directory of environments like Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT and Grok. Users at work can just ask Claude or Gemini to play music without opening the Sonos app. Tom says the community already had a pile of hacked MCPs and CLIs connecting to Sonos, so official support is going with the flow. He also mentions working with Amazon to bring Alexa Plus to the platform, but with nothing to announce yet. As for Google — the relationship is still strong, YouTube Music is one of the most-listened music services on the platform, but Gemini is not currently on Sonos devices.

— Tom Conrad
46:24

Custom agents let multiple AIs coexist on one platform

Sonos demonstrated Custom Agents: users can pick the reasoning engine from Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini or Grok, name the agent, give it a voice, set a personality, and have them all exist on the Sonos platform at once. Tom's example is the opinionated record store clerk John Cusack plays in High Fidelity, there specifically to shape your music taste and make recommendations; another agent using Anthropic reasoning handles cooking suggestions; a third cares only about live show opportunities. These agents can hand off to each other and can access Sonos context. This is bringing the multi-tenant concept to a voice assistant platform.

— Tom Conrad
1:07:10

The headphones failed because they weren't wired into the system

Sonos Ace launched two years ago, and Tom admits it's a good headphone — good noise cancellation, comfortable, good-looking — but it wasn't thoughtfully integrated into the Sonos ecosystem, and at launch it could only switch to some soundbars. He says this is the clearest example of the company missing an opportunity at the time: it forgot the product is part of the Sonos system, not a standalone item. Ace Ultra therefore moved to an entirely new hardware platform, Headphone Engine 2, and was virtualised inside Sonos OS 27: any speaker in the home can run the full audio stack on the headphone's behalf, because the headphone itself lacks the compute and running the full stack would drain the battery. A user walks up to a playing speaker and presses a button on the headphones, and the music jumps from the speaker into the headphones — no Bluetooth, no phone.

— Tom Conrad

Glossary

MCP / Model Context Protocol
A standard protocol that lets AI assistants connect to external services and tools; Sonos uses it to plug into ChatGPT, Claude and others.
Sonos Fabric
The technology layer that lets Sonos products discover each other on the local network and form a unified system.
TruePlay
Sonos's system for automatically tuning audio to a room's acoustics.
NPI / New Product Introduction
The cadence at which a hardware company ships new products each year.
Custom Agents
Users can pick different reasoning engines and give agents a name, voice and personality, letting multiple assistants coexist on the Sonos platform.
