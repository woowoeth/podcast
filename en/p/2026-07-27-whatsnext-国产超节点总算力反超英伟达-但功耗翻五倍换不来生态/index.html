Domestic supernodes now beat Nvidia on total compute, but 5x the power buys no ecosystem

Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 trades roughly 560 kW of power for 1.7x the total compute of NVL72, but what decides the fate of domestic supernodes is not specs — it's production capacity and software ecosystem.

This episode takes apart the supernode on three levels — technology, business and production capacity — to help you judge where the real bottleneck sits in the domestic compute supply chain.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

5:28

Supernodes became the default exhibit at the show

More than 10 supernode products were shown at this year's WAIC. Huawei, Alibaba and Baidu, chipmakers including Biren, Moore Threads, MetaX and Enflame, and even server vendors like ZTE and Sugon all put supernodes in the most prominent spot on their booths. Brokerages are calling 2026 the first year of the domestic supernode. Behind this homogenized competition is strong FOMO: without a supernode, you're embarrassed to show up. Supernodes have evolved from 8-card machines into rack-scale forms of dozens to over a hundred cards, and the core purpose is to solve the communication wall in model training and inference.

— Yaxian
11:51

Single chips can't catch up, so make it up at the system level

Domestic AI chips lag clearly behind overseas ones at the single-chip level, and that gap is hard to close quickly, so performance has to come from the system level. Kimi's K3 model has 2.8T total parameters and 896 experts, selecting 16 experts per token, and the official recommendation is a supernode of 64 or more accelerators to solve capacity, bandwidth and latency problems. At the same time, interconnect technology can already make supernodes a product, and vendors are willing to sell a whole rack rather than single cards, because the value is completely different. Together these factors make supernodes the mandatory path for domestic compute to catch up.

— Zhang Haijun
15:56

The Scale-up ecosystem is fragmented

Almost every supernode scale-up protocol in the world is proprietary: Nvidia has NVLink, Huawei has UB, and Alibaba and domestic chip companies each have their own. Overseas, UALink was initiated by AMD, Marvell, Broadcom and others, but AMD is the main driver, Marvell makes switch chips yet lacks the motivation, and Broadcom pulled out to do its own protocol, leaving the open protocol loose. Huawei's UB can unify scale-up and scale-out, while Nvidia's NVLink only handles in-rack interconnect, with IB between racks. Fragmented protocols mean ecosystem lock-in, and domestic substitution is not just a hardware problem.

— Zhang Haijun
21:35

Benchmark comparison: NVL72 versus Huawei 384

NVIDIA NVL72 consists of 18 compute trays and 9 NVSwitch trays, 72 B200s in total, with whole-machine power above 120 kW, and early GB200 shipments were mostly air-cooled, not all liquid-cooled. Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 is China's first supernode, 16 racks with 384 910Cs in total. Single-chip BF16 compute is only 30% of B200's, but total system compute is 1.7x that of NVL72. The price is whole-machine power of about 560 kW, close to 5x Nvidia's. This is buying total compute by piling on hardware, and it also explains why China has to pursue system-level innovation.

— Zhang Haijun
27:33

System vendors refuse to just assemble

Traditional server OEMs like ZTE and H3C also rolled out supernode designs this time. ZTE used its networking expertise to develop its own switch chip, with a protocol called Olink that it says is compatible with several domestic chips, though no details were released. Lightelligence is betting on optical interconnect, positioning itself as a supplier of optical communication and optical switching components. Right now the only supernodes actually in mass production and use in China are Huawei's and Sugon's: Huawei's customers are spread across carriers and cloud vendors, while Sugon is tied to Hygon and focused on xinchuang. Other vendors' claims of "zero adaptation" are still a long way from large-scale commercial use.

— Yaxian
32:54

Can domestic supernodes beat Nvidia

Zhang Haijun argues that domestic supernodes have already surpassed Nvidia on system specs: CM384's total compute is 1.7x NVL72's. But his definition of "surpass" is: if Nvidia opened up the market and cloud vendors, under equal conditions, would rather choose a domestic supernode — that's when you've won. Right now that's hard, because the CUDA ecosystem is too deep, domestic engineers are used to CUDA, DeepSeek can even find bugs in Nvidia chips, and switching to domestic hardware and software means relearning; Nvidia is also more stable. But Nvidia's next-generation product delay gives domestic players an opening, and the software ecosystem has improved noticeably over the past year, so the gap is narrowing. From 0 to 1 China is weak; from 1 to 100 China is stronger.

— Zhang Haijun
37:02

Production capacity is the real bottleneck

Many chipmakers does not mean large capacity; the real choke point is AI chip production capacity. The price of an 8-card B300 machine rose from 3.7-3.8 million yuan in last year's Q3 to 12-13 million yuan this year, showing that overseas goods can't get in and are scarce. Big internet companies are buying more domestic chips this year than last, and one important reason is that domestic capacity is relatively reliable. Because each company's software ecosystem is incompatible, big players can't adapt to all chips at once, so if a second-tier vendor hasn't landed a big order from a major player by next year, it will be very hard afterward. The industry landscape may reshuffle like autonomous driving, leaving only five or six players. Huawei and Cambricon are stable, and big-company in-house efforts like T-Head and Enflame also have a shot.

— Zhang Haijun
43:19

Supernodes pull up power, liquid cooling and switch chips

Supernode power has surged, and Nvidia has been pushing 800V high-voltage DC power since last year; liquid cooling has gone from optional to standard, with GB200 later switching to liquid cooling and GB300 fully liquid-cooled, and Google data shows liquid cooling demand value in 2027 will be 4x the previous level. On the network side, China previously relied mainly on Broadcom switch chips, and now domestic switch chips can rival Broadcom. Broadcom still dominates scale-out, while scale-up is split among proprietary protocols. These supporting technology upgrades will likely release commercial opportunities earlier than supernodes themselves.

— Zhang Haijun

Glossary

Scale-up
Combining multiple chips into one giant GPU to raise single-task compute.
Scale-out
Expanding cluster scale by adding more racks or nodes.
UALink
An open accelerator interconnect protocol initiated by AMD, Broadcom and others, whose participants are currently loosely organized.
CloudMatrix
Huawei's multi-rack interconnect supernode line, such as the 384-chip design.
