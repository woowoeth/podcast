ADHD is trapped between two unhelpful narratives

Edinburgh University researcher Andrew Ivan Brown, who has ADHD himself, identifies a trap: one side frames it as a fixed brain disorder—something you either have or don't—while the other dismisses it as medicalization of normal behavior that everyone experiences. Whichever side you stand on, neither helps the people who actually have ADHD. What this episode challenges is the shared framework behind both narratives.

— Jen
3:40

Barkley models executive function as talent-to-disability bell curve

Barkley demonstrates that executive function (self-control, planning, delayed gratification) distributes normally like height or weight: most people cluster in the middle, the far right extreme are ‘gifted’self-regulators, and when dysfunction causes enough environmental friction, that's where the ADHD line gets drawn. He acknowledges the line's position is somewhat arbitrary, but stresses ‘arbitrary doesn't mean meaningless’—where damage appears, diagnosis is warranted.

— Dr. Barkley
5:23

Extreme self-control praised as talent actually resembles anorexia

Jen points out that calling one end ‘talent’and the other ‘disability’sounds scientific, but the curve's far right—extreme self-control, rigid rule-following, compulsive traits—mirrors the core of some of the deadliest mental illnesses, including anorexia nervosa. Research shows restrictive anorexia patients score highest on ‘overcontrol,’exactly the traits Barkley calls ‘talent’at the curve's far end. Disability scholar Ne'eman adds: on a bell curve, ‘normal’statistically means only ‘most common,’not ‘healthy,’yet it gets swapped for a moral standard everyone should pursue.

— Jen
7:42

The logic of correcting left-handedness now applies to ADHD

Jen invokes ‘species-typical functioning’: if 95% sit still for six hours, stillness becomes ‘species-typical,’and restlessness becomes disorder—but that proves neither that six hours benefits anyone nor that the 5% who can't are ‘broken.’Left-handedness is the historical lesson: once forced into right-handed writing as abnormal, later recognized as simply a variant. She acknowledges attention differences do cause real suffering in unfriendly environments, but that doesn't justify the current medical model.

— Jen
9:34

ADHD children face peer rejection at 4 times the rate

ADHD children are rejected by peers at four times the rate of other kids, with 50–60% experiencing chronic, sustained rejection rather than occasional friction—uninvited to birthday parties, eating lunch alone, sometimes even hinted by parents and teachers to ‘stay away.’Research links peer rejection and bullying to higher depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation—none of which are core ADHD symptoms, but products of years of environmental rejection.

— Jen
11:37

Training peers to accept ADHD children rewrites the rejection cycle

Jen shows rejection is solvable: peer-mediated programs explicitly train typical peers to notice, accept, and support ADHD classmates in games and class participation, and research shows both groups' social interaction and joint participation improve afterward; structured group work that gives each child a clear role also increases the odds ADHD kids get picked as partners. When you deliberately teach neurotypical children how to relate to ADHD peers, the long-running rejection story can be rewritten.

— Jen
13:33

58% of Irish teachers hold negative views of ADHD

In the link between ADHD and academic failure, social class and IQ often predict dropout risk better than ADHD itself. A survey of 500+ Irish teachers found 58% hold negative attitudes, often viewing ADHD as ‘a trendy label’or ‘an excuse for bad behavior’—even when these teachers can accurately describe ADHD's core symptoms. Such attitudes correlate with less support, more conflict, and worse academic and social outcomes; the show quotes one parent whose child's teacher ‘caused her trauma through misunderstanding.’

— Jen
16:26

Gaming addiction and risky behavior look like escape from rejection

Barkley attributes gaming addiction (he says one-sixth to one-fifth of ADHD teens have it), risky behavior, substance abuse, and eating disorders directly to ADHD. But Jen points to 2022 research showing ADHD kids use online gaming as escape from the daily frustration of low self-worth and social anxiety; Barkley's own research also links ADHD eating disorders to ‘earlier impulsivity, peer rejection, harsh parenting, and concurrent depression and anxiety’—the path to early death runs through layers of amplification in family, peers, and school, not one-step causation.

— Jen

Glossary

Species-typical functioning
The assumption that one biologically ‘correct’way to run a brain exists, and deviation from it is disorder.
Executive function
Self-control, planning, delayed gratification—the cognitive abilities that regulate your own behavior.
Misrecognition
Andrew Ivan Brown's concept: the deeper harm experienced by ADHD communities, beyond stigma itself.
Peer-mediated programs
Classroom interventions that train typical peers to actively accept and engage ADHD children in activities.
