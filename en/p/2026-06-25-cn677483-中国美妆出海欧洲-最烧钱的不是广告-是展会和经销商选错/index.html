Chinese beauty brands in Europe: the money drain is trade shows and distributor misfits, not ads

Trade show booths cost hundreds of thousands yet yield few contracts; European distributor relationships take 10-20 years to build, and picking the wrong partner can waste an entire year.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

5:00

Channels themselves define your brand identity

European consumers don't judge brands on price alone; they assess brand identity through channels—whether they perceive you as an Amazon brand or a Space NK brand signals different trust systems. Offline, dermatologists and beauty professionals carry enormous credibility; many also function as influencers. Channels are therefore not just sales routes but part of brand positioning itself. Choosing the wrong channel actively undermines brand perception.

— Permella
11:01

Distributor networks help you save money entering Europe

Europe's distributor system is mature, operating 10+ years at scale: brands supply at wholesale prices while distributors handle local market development and marketing spend; brands only track net sales, not marketing costs. The prerequisite is clarity: which key countries to enter, which channels to access, which distributors fit. Limited budget doesn't exclude Europe—solid upfront strategy does.

— Permella
18:04

Maintain restraint even when pursuing rapid growth

Every channel wants inventory, but brands must move strategically according to positioning and plan. Skip channels that don't fit, even if they could drive short-term volume. Hold prices firm rather than discounting; this enables both long-term business and steady growth. Beyond Chinese scale, European overall market volume can be 2-3x China's, but the tempo is measured, focused on year-over-year sustained growth rather than explosive spikes.

— Permella
25:06

Calculate market size first, then decide your strategy

Before entering Europe, run comprehensive assessment: identify priority markets, define consumer and channel strategy for each, forecast year-one and three-to-five-year targets. Clarity itself saves substantial cost. Reference major conglomerate financial reports: Europe's total cosmetics market size matches or even reaches 2x China's—a potential many brands underestimate.

— Permella
28:07

UK channels form a spectrum from luxury to mass market

Harrods and Selfridges occupy the apex; they fit brands with unit prices above 1,000 RMB. Space NK is the UK's Sephora equivalent, with roughly 100 stores, receptive to new brands and conducting annual product reviews. Below that are pharmacy chains Boots (roughly 1,800 UK stores, also handling NHS prescription counters) and the younger, value-focused Superdrug; online: Look Fantastic and Cult Beauty. Once you enter a mismatched mass-market channel, moving upscale becomes very difficult.

— Permella
41:14

C-Beauty hasn't yet told a coherent story

K-Beauty in European minds equals 'understands skincare'—built through K-pop culture over time, then reinforced by emphasis on actives and value (e.g., trending PDRN stem cells). China's edge is supply-chain speed and R&D efficiency—domestic brands launching three to five products yearly is considered strong; domestic can hit that monthly. Yet consumer perception of C-Beauty remains fragmented and nascent; it needs sustained, consistent messaging to build mind-share like K-Beauty did.

— Permella
46:19

Trade shows and wrong distributors are the most expensive pitfalls

Attending mainstream shows like Cosmoprof costs hundreds of thousands, yet contract yield and useful contacts remain low—and show efficiency is declining. Another trap: signing distributors without due diligence. One mismatch, trial-and-error, contract unwinding: six months to a year gone. If online PR budgets still target domestic platforms like Little Red Book, overseas reach is limited—hire local-experienced agencies instead. Lock in sales channel strategy before spending.

— Permella
54:22

Differentiation lies in scalp care and telling cultural stories

Europe faces hard water and widespread hair loss; China's advanced scalp and head-care technology and services are mature—local salons already adopt Chinese head-washing equipment, an untapped gap market. Primer bases layered with multi-function, high-value positioning also have potential. L'Oréal's founder once told Li Jiaqi directly: tell China's cultural story clearly, because culture closes the trust gap with French consumers. Yet foreign perception of Chinese culture remains scattered symbols—pandas, Jackie Chan, Bruce Lee—needing sustained output from quality brands to weave into narrative.

— Permella
