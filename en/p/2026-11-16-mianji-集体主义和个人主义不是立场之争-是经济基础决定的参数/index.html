Collectivism and individualism are not ideological stances—they're parameters set by economic foundations

Americans buy their first home at age 36. Even in this most liberal nation, plenty of thirty-somethings still live with their parents. Whether individualism or collectivism dominates depends on material conditions and social safety nets, not ideological stance.

0:00

The Gen-80s' 40 years overlap entirely with China's compressed modernization

Gen-80s nearly witnessed the complete transition from scarcity to surplus, from production-driven to consumption-driven society, from one-child policy to fertility encouragement, from collective to individual. They were the PRC's third baby-boom wave; demographic dividends demanded they work more and save more, while expanding domestic demand asked the same cohort to lever up and consume, and now they must downshift with the economy and handle the mid-life issues left by structural transition.

19:15

Erase ten years' memories and restart younger? She firmly refuses

The thought experiment: erase the last ten years, subtract ten years from your age, start over—would you trade? The answer is an absolute no—not because things are good now, but because she doesn't believe restarting would reach better odds. The mid-life curve turning down isn't purely personal; industry cycles turn every five years, and each inflection can wipe people out—that's not something individuals control. Layered on top: since 2021, balance-sheet recession, macro leverage ratios dropped from peak to single digits, and many who levered up to buy homes then faced shrinking assets.

30:20

The next generation's education became a forced experimental field

Children born in '16–'17, who caught the second-child boom's tail, hit bad luck: expensive school-district homes and fierce peer competition. Yet parents, despite bearish on school-district homes, still buy this year—treating it like consumption. Deeper anxiety: education is a slow variable. Everyone knows AI will change how the next generation learns and works, but schools still live in the last era. No one knows how it'll change or how long to find working methods, so this cohort is forced to test-fail on everyone's behalf.

40:30

2008: 5.59 million graduates to 2026: 12.7 million—more than doubled

2008 graduation hit the financial crisis, with 5.59 million graduates—considered a brutal job year. By 2026, that number is 12.7 million. So long as higher-ed gross enrollment stays up, the next decade will see every year as ‘the hardest employment year’—not unusually hard in any one year, but the base has shifted; it only gets more crowded.

58:37

Collectivism and individualism aren't ideologies—they're products of economic base

Gen-60s and Gen-70s love invoking Mao because his thought shaped their values during formation. The in-laws' generation believes in collectivism because their pensions and state-system stability are real payoff from the collective. Flip side: Americans buy first homes at 36; even in the world's most liberal nation, many rely on living with parents to get through those years. Whether collective or individual wins depends on whether you've actually benefited from that system, not ideological stance.

1:07:45

Gen-10s' socializing: no pleasantries, no inner struggle

A cousin's friend visits, dinnertime comes, the cousin says straight up: ‘You should go, we're eating now.’ The friend replies without hesitation: ‘Okay, I should head out anyway.’ Two kids end a call just as abruptly—one says ‘I'm hanging up’ and hangs. This generation, treated as independent individuals from childhood, respects their own feelings more, and when bullied, solves it directly rather than enduring or working around.

1:11:14

Freedom exists inside the system too—just in a different form

A friend at the provincial-committee level in the system disdains maintaining relationships or climbing—because it gives him freedom: his life has a floor; he can coast in his position without extra effort, just do the job well, the boss can't fire him, he wants the system not the person. The mother-in-law worked in the system her whole life, never said what she didn't mean—her confidence: ‘You can't fire me either; worst case I don't climb.’ Individualism and collectivism aren't sharply bounded in actual people.

1:40:01

Living off parents is no longer shameful—the next generation holds a floor-put option

Jokingly threatened ‘If you don't study hard you won't afford steak later,’ a Gen-10 answers ‘I'll spend your money.’ The negative connotation of living off parents is fading. Adult children getting paid to care for elderly parents full-time is now seen as a solid option, essentially the same as the old way. Actually, it's an option: the floor is covered, want conformity go grind, want difference go be yourself, the cost is lower. That's the next generation's new script.
