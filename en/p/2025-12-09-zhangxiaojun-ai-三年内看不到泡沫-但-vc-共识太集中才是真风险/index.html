AI Won't Show a Bubble Within Three Years, but Concentrated VC Consensus Is the Real Risk

Zhu Xiaohu says bubble talk is secondary-market investors deliberately steering a correction; the real problem is that every primary-market deal has become a Club Deal, so GPs can't make big money and LPs aren't happy either.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

2:01

OpenAI's shift from weekly to daily actives is a strategic turning point

Zhu Xiaohu believes OpenAI has almost stopped mentioning AGI this year, strategically shifting from weekly-active scenarios to daily-active scenarios. Weekly-active scenarios are hard to defend against big-platform attacks, while daily-active defense gets thicker and thicker. Pulse, the browser, group chat—all are about increasing usage frequency, refocusing from weekly to daily actives. He judges ChatGPT will become a new super-app entry point; once group chat is established, social relationships may be rebuilt, with a big impact on Meta.

— Zhu Xiaohu
9:06

The Transformer architecture has basically hit its ceiling

Zhu Xiaohu says LeCun has been talking about scaling hitting a wall, and over the past year it's become obvious: going further is marginal improvement, but the cost is high and the gains are limited. At least on the Transformer architecture, it may be at its end. But he thinks today's technology level is already sufficient to support an application explosion; industry-wide token consumption has risen more than tenfold, proving this wave of AI tech is already good enough in many scenarios.

— Zhu Xiaohu
10:07

The next competition isn't AI competition, it's electricity competition

Zhu Xiaohu says depreciating GPUs over two or three years is simply impossible; many five- or six-year-old cards, even seven- or eight-year-old cards, are still in use—as long as you have cards, you can use them. Token consumption is exploding too fast: more than tenfold this year, and next year it can explode another tenfold; data center construction is far from keeping up. He believes the next competition isn't AI competition, it's data center construction, it's electricity competition; China building a dozen nuclear power plants and many solar plants is absolutely correct.

— Zhu Xiaohu
12:08

US AI valuations are 100 times China's; one end must be wrong

Zhu Xiaohu says US AI valuations are 100 times China's, and one end must be wrong: either its revenue is unsustainable, or China's revenue is undervalued. He thinks there are some divergence points here, and how it evolves later is still unknown. On the GPU flywheel, he says it's a bit like the internet bubble back then, but understandable, because it needs to be off-balance-sheet—an accounting trick by some listed companies to make the financials look better. The core question is still whether anyone is actually using this thing and whether it's enough.

— Zhu Xiaohu
16:16

Without DeepSeek, human AI might be controlled by a few private companies

Zhu Xiaohu says people still underestimate DeepSeek's change to all humanity and to history. He believes that looking back ten years from now, DeepSeek will still be a very important turning point in the entire AI competition; otherwise China's open-source ecosystem today wouldn't be so resolutely open. Without DeepSeek, it's very likely human AI would be controlled by the AI models of a few private companies, which would be dangerous for all humanity.

— Zhu Xiaohu
24:41

In the AI era, leave the big platforms by three streets

Zhu Xiaohu says in the mobile internet era people said leave the big platforms by one street; in the AI era you may need to leave by three streets. Specifically: first, do vertical applications; second, do private deployment, for example taking Qwen's 30B small model for private deployment; third, do sales. Big platforms generally also look for startups to help them with last-mile implementation. He has invested in several such vertical-scenario agents, growing from tens of millions last year to over a hundred million this year, and two or three hundred million next year.

— Zhu Xiaohu
38:30

VC consensus is too concentrated; GPs can't make big money and LPs aren't happy

Zhu Xiaohu says at a VC meeting in Hong Kong last week, many LPs complained that in this cycle GP consensus is too concentrated: every project is a Club Deal, several funds invest together, each stake is very small, so how do you make money? GPs can't make big money, and LPs are very unhappy. Moreover, investing in different VCs seems about the same, portfolios are similar, and there's no risk diversification for LPs. He thinks an obvious problem this cycle is that consensus is too concentrated, because aesthetics have become too uniform.

— Zhu Xiaohu
40:15

Large model companies are worse than Cisco; even commercialization isn't easy

Zhu Xiaohu says these large model companies are very similar to Cisco in the last cycle, even worse than Cisco. Cisco's commercialization at least wasn't a problem back then; it's just that other technologies quickly caught up, so gross margins were low. Today it may really be that even commercialization isn't easy. Big platforms weren't very willing to do Cisco's businesses, but today big platforms are desperately all-in on foundation models. He believes if commercialization can never be achieved, then the consensus is a bubble.

— Zhu Xiaohu

Glossary

Club Deal
Multiple funds jointly investing in the same project, with each holding a very small stake.
Dark Fiber
Fiber laid but unused during the 2000 internet bubble, one of the bubble indicators.
SOTA
State of the Art, referring to the currently best-performing model.
Token
The basic unit of text processed by large models; consumption is often used as an indicator of AI application activity.
