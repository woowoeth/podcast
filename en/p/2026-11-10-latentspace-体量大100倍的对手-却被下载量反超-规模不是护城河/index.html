Competitor 100x larger, yet overtaken in downloads: scale is not a moat

A founder with no email background overtook downloads through product experience, while Twilio—inventor of DevRel—acquired SendGrid but left it dormant.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:00

Once thought beating SendGrid impossible, now doubling his lead

Zeno Rocha recalls early fears SendGrid was ‘impossible’ to beat. This year, Resend's npm downloads surpassed SendGrid—now more than double. Resend serves 4.3M users, just crossed 120k+ paying customers including Warner Brothers and HBO, plus a Japanese group that purchased ~20k domains in one transaction.

— Zeno Rocha
15:48

Trust users first, police behavior after, not before

Most email providers require signup forms and 2-day manual review, rejecting most applications. Zeno rejects this model: instead of imprisoning users for eighteen years before proving they deserve freedom, he flips it. Default trust, then immediately block on hard signals like bounce rates and complaints—since most issues stem from misuse, not abuse.

— Zeno Rocha
30:31

A hundred times larger, yet unable to win with scale

Twilio was 100x Resend's size at the start, yet couldn't crush it through scale. The conversation landed on a judgment: commodity businesses like email have no scale advantage. Product quality is all that matters. Twilio, which invented DevRel, now appears to coast on past laurels, underinvesting in SendGrid and SMS.

34:50

From leasing AWS SES to owning the entire sending stack

Resend started on AWS SES, differentiating on developer UX. Now building in-house: acquiring and managing IP pools, establishing carrier relationships, switching email delivery from Node.js to Go for speed. The goal is finer observability—previously the platform couldn't see whether a sent email reached the inbox, spam folder, or promotions tab.

— Zeno Rocha
40:26

One MCP forced a complete rebuild of permission systems

Resend's first MCP was a local version written part-time by Yoko, an a16z investor. Growth demanded a remote MCP, but that required OAuth and RBAC first. They redesigned the entire account permissions system from scratch, only then moving to distribution and the official plugin marketplace.

— Zeno Rocha
43:47

Eighty tools didn't confuse the model; data density did

The industry feared many MCP tools would confuse models; Cloudflare even capped theirs at two. Resend went opposite: ~20 at launch, now 80+. Tool quantity itself hasn't caused confusion. The real issue is single calls returning too much data, losing information. Achieving feature parity with the dashboard—billing, team invites, account deletion—is extremely hard.

— Zeno Rocha
1:01:17

AI miscalculated pricing, so he copied the price list to markdown

Zeno asked AI to compare Resend against competitors' cost for 1.2M emails—the numbers came back wrong, and the model claimed Resend ‘pricing is opaque’. The problem: AI won't execute JavaScript calculator widgets on the page. Fix: duplicate the numbers as plain text in a markdown version of the same page. Fixed in days, but now maintaining two content sources with no single source of truth.

— Zeno Rocha
1:21:06

Better a smaller company his daughter actually wants to spend time with

A YC partner told Zeno his design perfectionism was slowing progress—it stung, but was fair. He clarified his priorities: in ten years, he'd rather have $10M revenue and a daughter who wants to play with him than $100M and a distant relationship. He carried this priority back into parenting decisions.

— Zeno Rocha

Glossary

MCP / Model Context Protocol
Protocol enabling AI assistants to directly call application backends and execute operations.
AEO / AI Answer Engine Optimization
Optimizing content so large models recommend your product when answering user questions.
Domain Connect
OAuth-like protocol allowing one-click DNS configuration via registrar dashboards instead of manual record entry.
SES / Amazon Simple Email Service
AWS's foundational email delivery service, used as the base by many email platforms.
llms.txt
Site content summary placed in the root directory for models to read, analogous to robots.txt.
