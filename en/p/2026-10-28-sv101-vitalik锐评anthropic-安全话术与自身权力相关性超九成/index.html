Vitalik's sharp critique of Anthropic: safety rhetoric's link to power expansion exceeds 90%

Vitalik donated billions for AI safety, yet now says AI labs preach safety while their actions align over 90% with expanding their own power.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

0:01

Anthropic's succession is designated by founders and kept from the public

Anthropic's prospectus uses "safety" 248 times—"AI safety" 36 times—and its seven co-founders will control 50.1% of voting rights on ordinary matters through special shares; a long-term trust can elect a board majority. More critically: founders can pre-designate successors for voting power after losing office, requiring internal approval but with names kept secret—the public doesn't know who might decide Anthropic's future.

— Yiwen
9:07

Effective altruism shifted from global health to AI as its core

Afra splits EA's decade-plus history into four phases: the mosquito-net era of quantifiable evidence for global health; around 2015, the long-termist shift toward future generations and existential risk, when SBF and other billionaires poured in money; post-FTX crisis, amid ChatGPT's emergence, an era of trust loss; then today's AI safety as core agenda—making EA a phrase ordinary people now know.

— Afra
18:10

Anthropic's auditors have financial stakes in the company's success

METR, which audits Anthropic independently, is funded mainly by Coefficient Giving, whose founder Dustin Moskovitz is an early Anthropic investor holding major shares. Afra sees no conspiracy—but the real issue is: when a thought movement gets vast funding, it doesn't just find important problems; it decides which problems count as important. That's the AI policy world today: young researchers are being hired away in high-paying AI-safety roles.

— Afra
28:15

Donating later enabled early ruthless profit-chasing in the name of effective altruism

MacAskill told Oxford students that a doctor saves ~140 lives, but earning on Wall Street or in consulting, then donating wisely—buying more nets—can save ten times as many. Afra argues this logic later became moral cover for quant traders (including SBF) to justify ruthless profit-chasing: just claim you'll donate it, and greed becomes charity.

— Afra
59:37

Shiba Inu accidentally created billions in AI safety funding Vitalik couldn't control

Vitalik recalls Shiba Inu's team putting half the token supply into his public wallet. He assumed it would collapse fast, so distributed it to a few organizations—figuring they might extract $20+ million max. Three months later, no crash. Combined cashing out: ~$470 million for India's CryptoRelief, $400–600 million for FLI—far beyond his expectation or control.

— Vitalik
1:10:48

AI safety no longer meets the criteria for effective altruist funding

Vitalik's core test for whether to fund a field: neglectedness—is this problem important but understaffed? Ten years ago, AI safety and longevity research were lonely. Today AI safety has vast funding, roles, and attention. The issue isn't scarcity of resources; it's that current methods may be wrong. And that type of problem has no simple fix.

— Vitalik
1:34:08

Anthropic's safety arguments correlate over 90% with expanding its own power

Vitalik says he understands Anthropic's every argument—accept the premises, the conclusions follow—but watching what it actually does, he sees correlation with expanding power "likely over 90%." His question: why should a company decree "we're safe, so we win, others shouldn't have power"—while attacking open AI as dangerous, yet still pushing the AI race forward?

— Vitalik
1:38:10

Decentralized technologies offer better AI defense than concentrated corporate control

Vitalik proposes d/acc: technology advance is worthwhile, but invest first in tools protecting regular people through decentralization, not concentrating power in a few models or firms. Example: some EA organizations spend 80% of resources on "making AI refuse to help make pathogens"—centralized defense, useless if rivals have their own models. Real protection: detection, filtering, faster vaccine R&D—decentralized means.

— Vitalik

Glossary

Effective altruism
Using evidence and rational analysis to allocate limited money and time to where they bring the most good.
e/acc
Advocates accelerating AI and technology development; opposes slowing it for safety reasons.
d/acc
Proposed by Vitalik: accelerate technology while prioritizing defenses that protect ordinary people over concentrated control.
Neglectedness
An EA standard for assessing donations: whether a field still lacks sufficient attention and resources.
P(doom)
Subjective estimate of the probability that AI or other risks cause human extinction.
Orthogonality thesis
AI's intelligence level and whether its goals align with human interests are two independent dimensions.
