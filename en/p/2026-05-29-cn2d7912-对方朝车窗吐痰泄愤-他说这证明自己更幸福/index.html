17:13

Emotional stability is not a virtue—it's social conditioning

Society never questions whether adults should be emotionally passionate, yet uniquely demands they be stable—a standard that only tightens with age. This is not a virtue but a rule imposed without justification.

1:06:47

Stupidity is worse to endure than malice

Malice used to seem worse than incompetence. Now incompetence hurts more: you can rightfully attack a bad actor, but a well-meaning fool offers no clean response. Criticize and you seem cruel; stay silent and you choke.

1:12:50

Blocking and deleting are addictive thrills

Blocking and deleting feed addiction. The practice calcifies into a reflex until the person cannot tolerate disagreement. Two methods emerge: mock sarcastically to disguise attacks as jokes, or block ruthlessly. Both become compulsive; both harden into arrogance.

— Su Heng
1:14:52

Never block: actively search your own name to hear criticism

Almost never block anyone; actively search your own name to find criticism. The habit—like dumpster-diving after meals—prevents you from becoming insular. Only accept affirmation, and you narrow into yourself.

— Luo Yonghao

1:24:55

Refusing to fight back proves you're the happier person

During a road-rage confrontation, the other driver threatened him; Luo stayed in his car filming. When the man spat on the window in frustration before leaving, Luo invented a rule: before any conflict, determine who is happier, and the happier person yields. By this logic, remaining calm while your opponent loses control proves you're happier—so not fighting back is rational, not fear.

— Luo Yonghao

In an alcohol-fueled collision, the at-fault driver's mother arrived in tears begging mercy; Luo was prepared to settle. But when she spotted his car's temporary license plate, she flipped instantly and counter-attacked: wasn't he driving illegally? This prompted his sole profane outburst at a woman.

— Luo Yonghao
2:18:26

To broaden your mind, you must engage with people you despise

Actively engage with people whose values repel you. Everyone believes they're doing good—no one thinks they're the villain. Understanding why someone acts the way they do reveals your own blind spots. Retreat into agreement-only circles and you harden into something smaller.
