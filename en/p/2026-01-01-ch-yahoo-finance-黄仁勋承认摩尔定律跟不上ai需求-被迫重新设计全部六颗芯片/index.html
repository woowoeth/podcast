Jensen Huang admits Moore's Law cannot keep pace with AI demand, forced to redesign all six chips

20:33

Computing industry's five layers are being simultaneously rewritten

Platform shifts happen every 10–15 years—from mainframes to PCs, PCs to internet, internet to cloud, cloud to mobile. But this time, two platform shifts overlap: applications are built on AI foundations, and the development model itself changes. Code becomes training, CPUs become GPUs, and applications generate output token-by-token, pixel-by-pixel, understanding context in real time rather than running precompiled binaries. Huang estimates roughly $10 trillion in computing industry value is being remade this way, explaining why hundreds of billions in venture funding and trillions in industry R&D are flowing into AI.

— Jensen Huang
26:50

Open source is now only six months behind frontier models

Huang acknowledges that DeepSeek R1 surprised the world and ‘truly triggered this entire movement’. His assessment: open-source models have reached frontier quality, merely running six months behind frontier labs. New models catch up every six months. The reason is straightforward—startups, large companies, researchers, students, nearly every country wants to join the AI revolution. Digital intelligence has no reason to leave anyone behind.

— Jensen Huang
27:53

Nvidia built supercomputers worth billions without becoming a cloud company

Nvidia has built self-operated AI supercomputers called DGX cloud, now worth ‘tens of billions of dollars’. Many ask if this makes Nvidia a cloud provider. Huang's answer: no. These supercomputers train Nvidia's own models. Using this infrastructure, Nvidia produced frontier open-source models—La Proteina for protein generation, weather forecasting, and NemoTron combining Transformer and SSM architectures—and open-sourced all data, models, and training code. ‘Only this way can people truly trust where models come from.’

— Jensen Huang
51:04

Self-driving AI needs two parallel systems: reasoning and backup

Nvidia released Alpamayo, described as ‘the world's first thinking and reasoning autonomous driving AI’—not only outputting steering and brake commands but explaining why and predicting the trajectory. Yet Huang stresses that reasoning alone is insufficient. The car simultaneously runs a completely traceable classical self-driving stack governed by a ‘policy and safety assessor’ that decides: if confident in Alpamayo, use it; if not, switch to the classical stack. ‘We are the only car running both systems simultaneously,’ Huang said. The Mercedes-Benz CLA equipped with this system earned NCAP's rating as the world's safest car.

— Jensen Huang
1:14:10

Model scaling at 10× yearly meets token costs collapsing 10×

Three growth curves explain compute demand explosion. Model parameters scale roughly 10× per year. Since o1 introduced test-time scaling—allowing models to ‘think longer’ before answering—inference token generation grows about 5× annually. Simultaneously, as every lab races toward the next frontier, prior-generation model token generation costs fall roughly 10× yearly. Combined, Huang concludes: ‘Everything is a compute problem’—whoever computes faster reaches the next frontier first.

— Jensen Huang
1:19:27

Breaking tradition: all six chips must be completely redesigned

Huang revealed Nvidia's internal principle: each platform generation should change at most one or two chips. The Vera Rubin generation redesigns all six, because Moore's Law is decelerating. Annual transistor growth reaches only 1.6×, insufficient against 10× model scaling and 5× token scaling. ‘Unless every chip in the entire system undergoes extreme co-design, this industry cannot sustain this pace of progress,’ Huang stated. Extreme co-design means chip, network, and software stack redesign happens simultaneously this generation.

— Jensen Huang
1:23:48

NVFP4 dynamically adjusts precision per layer, not just a format

Huang highlighted NVFP4 tensor cores as this generation's most significant innovation—not simply fitting 4-bit floats into the datapath, but an entire dynamic, self-adaptive processing unit adjusting precision and structure. At Transformer layers where throughput matters more than precision, it sacrifices accuracy; where precision is critical, it maintains full resolution. This tuning must happen in silicon; software cannot keep up. Huang predicts this format and structure ‘will likely become an industry standard’ going forward.

— Jensen Huang
1:29:02

10% network improvement is equivalent to fifty billion dollars

Here is Huang's math: a gigawatt-scale data center costs roughly $50 billion to build. If Spectrum X networking improves throughput by 10%, that 10% equals $50 billion in value—and Spectrum X typically delivers 25% improvement. ‘The network is essentially free,’ Huang concluded, ‘so everyone uses Spectrum X.’ This explains why Nvidia is now ‘the world's largest network company.’

— Jensen Huang

Glossary

NVFP4
A dynamic precision processing unit that adjusts floating-point accuracy and throughput per transformer layer, not merely a numerical format.
KV cache
The GPU memory structure storing context during inference, expanding as conversation length grows.
test time scaling
A technique letting models allocate more computation before answering, ‘thinking longer’ to produce better outputs.
extreme co-design
Simultaneous complete redesign of chips, networks, and software stacks as a single integrated effort.
Alpamayo
Nvidia's end-to-end reasoning autonomous driving AI that explains driving decisions and predicts vehicle trajectory.
Bluefield 4
A data processing chip handling storage, security, and KV cache offloading in the system architecture.
