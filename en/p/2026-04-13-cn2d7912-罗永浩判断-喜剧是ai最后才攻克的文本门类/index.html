Luo Yonghao's judgment: comedy is the last text category AI will conquer

He polled top-tier large language models on text creation and got consistent answers: stand-up comedy, with its one-liner density—text so tightly packed with jokes—can theoretically be conquered by AI, but will inevitably rank last among all text types. Which means comedy writers will outlive other creators by several years in the age of AI.

40:17

Excessive fear kept our ancestors alive

When early humans heard rustling in the grass, those who overestimated danger—treating every sound as a predator—survived; the relaxed ones got eaten. Modern people inherited this hypervigilance; hormonal shifts trigger baseless alarms everywhere. Men especially suffer in silence, feeling emasculated by admitting fear.

1:23:08

Learning to refuse unreasonable requests is safer than fearing conflict

A listener was pestered by a street vendor after an open-mic show. Too afraid to refuse, she paid repeatedly—then the vendor chased after her claiming she hadn't paid. Two comedians had to rescue her. One pulled her aside afterward: ‘Next time, refuse.’ She realized that fearing confrontation more than consequences is what puts you in danger.

1:41:38

You don't have to become outgoing to accept connection

A guest advised a socially anxious listener: don't force yourself to become social; just muster courage to not reject when others invite you or show affection. He shared his own experience: he used to decline 100% of social advances. He changed only that one thing—and everything got easier. You don't have to rewire your whole life; just practice catching what others throw.

2:05:48

AI invents legal citations that don't actually exist

An intellectual property court worker encountered AI-generated briefs that looked airtight at first glance—even lawyers initially found them persuasive. But scrutiny revealed invented case numbers that don't exist, and citations of laws unrelated to the actual case. Most people have no way to catch these fabrications with their own knowledge.

2:08:48

Cross-verify between three top models to catch AI errors

For work that cannot afford mistakes, Luo's solution: subscribe to the three or four best models globally. Take the first result and paste it verbatim into the second, third, and fourth—let each search online for latest facts and catch errors. He uses this constantly and hasn't seen an error survive three independent checks. Caveat: only works with the very top tiers, not the budget models.

— Luo Yonghao
2:10:48

Comedy ranks last in what AI will conquer

When asked about his fear of AI, Luo gave a specific judgment: he's asked top models about text creation and got consistent answers—stand-up comedy, with its densely-packed one-liners, is a problem AI will theoretically solve, but will definitely come last among all text types. So comedians will outlast all other writers. And comedy requires live audience testing; AI can't fake that in the near term.

— Luo Yonghao
2:11:49

Everyone researches AI; he still edits scripts by hand

He confesses he feels no change from the AI wave—everyone around him is debating and diving deep, while he still edits comedy the stone-age way: no phone calls with comics, no video. He has to show up in person and sit face-to-face for forty-fifty minutes to hash out one script. Recently it's been relentless. He jokes he works like a caveman, and sometimes that dumb method brings odd reassurance.

— Luo Yonghao
2:27:59

AI remembered a shopping suggestion from two years ago

A listener shared an uncanny experience: two years earlier she'd asked AI for advice on buying a certain product, then didn't purchase. Months later, shopping for the same category, AI unprompted mentioned that old recommendation was actually superior, now discontinued, and suggested finding it secondhand—better than anything new on the market. She'd completely forgotten the conversation; checking chat history confirmed every word. She found unopened items listed on the secondhand platform.
