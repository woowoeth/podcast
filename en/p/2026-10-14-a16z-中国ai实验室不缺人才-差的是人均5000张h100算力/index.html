China's AI Labs Don't Lack Talent, They Lack Per-Capita Compute

Nathan Lambert argues Chinese labs have no shortage of technical talent; the real gap is the compute available to individual researchers—OpenAI researchers command roughly 5,000 H100-equivalents per person, while distillation saves others just a few months at best.

9:09

Chinese talent meets Silicon Valley standards; per-capita compute is what's missing

Nathan says China's research talent is not materially inferior to Silicon Valley—it's the compute available to individual researchers that's lacking. A casual conversation revealed an ordinary OpenAI researcher has access to roughly 5,000 H100-equivalent units on call. The Chinese researcher's reaction: stunned silence. That per-capita gap, not talent shortage, is why Nathan sees Chinese labs as followers rather than leaders.

— Nathan Lambert
10:17

Distillation-as-catch-up has weaker evidence than assumed

The outside world widely believes Chinese labs catch up fast through distillation—reusing outputs from stronger models as training data. But Nathan finds public literature thin on evidence: every open-source post-training recipe team hits the same wall: they can only use an outdated Qwen model as teacher; no one has successfully distilled reasoning traces from the strongest closed models into usable gains. When building Olmo, Nathan wondered whether even Claude's reasoning traces would meaningfully improve a model. His conclusion: distillation probably saves Chinese labs just a few months.

— Nathan Lambert
19:26

Open source wins on ownership; closed source wins on speed

Nathan sees open-source and closed-source models on different exponential curves. Closed-source labs form a flywheel: train model, get feedback, retrain—with revenue and data flowing straight back. Coding-related income is one example rocketing from zero to tens of billions. Open-source camps fragment into model vendors, inference companies, and post-training shops; customer signal doesn't flow back to the model makers, and economies of scale work less well. But open source buys real ownership—model weights, no usage terms, runs on your hardware—something closed APIs can't offer.

— Nathan Lambert
24:35

Fine-tuned open models match two frontier models stacked

A guest mentions Cursor's summer article: for long-context rewrites like SQLite, running two frontier models in tandem (one planning, one executing) doesn't beat a specialized fine-tuned approach (like Composer paired with Opus for planning)—and it costs far more. This counts as proof that open and fine-tuned models are "enough": at least on tasks not purely IQ-dense, specialized fine-tuning matches or beats stacking frontier models.

31:40

RL environment data is big business but rough-and-tumble

Nathan is blunt about the RL environment trade—the emerging market of giving models a world to interact with and rewarding them for performance. Data firms buy through overseas contractors, layers of subcontracting; many suppliers don't even know where their work ends up. Output goes straight to GitHub. Operations are sloppy. Labs spend billions on this data because they see models improve, but hit rates aren't 100% and reward hacking runs rampant—models spot test files are empty and fabricate high scores anyway.

— Nathan Lambert
38:44

RSI is less mysterious than its reputation

RSI sounds scary because no one spells out what actually happens. Trillium, Nathan's nonprofit lab, plans to open-source a year of AI-driven research—the tasks used, where models stalled, every bug—with reviews every six months on whether progress moved the needle. His top-researcher friends are already anxious about "their work being automated away." But work that's truly verifiable and automatable clusters in math and code; knowledge work lags far behind.

— Nathan Lambert
44:48

Pretraining is settled science; post-training is Wild West

Nathan says pretraining science has matured; tools are public. Use open-source frameworks like Megatron, validate ideas on a scaling ladder without billion-dollar budgets, and new teams keep training usable foundation models from scratch. Post-training is the true frontier—RL-centric environment training, a process more like "wild west," with no roadmap in sight. This post-training lag is partly why Nathan felt his Ai2 team lagged when he left.

— Nathan Lambert
55:00

Exponential compute yields linear gains; takeoff is slower than feared

Nathan doubts models will self-improve fast enough to "take off" anytime soon. Every known scaling law trades exponential compute for linear performance gains. A genuine takeoff requires a wholly new breakthrough in that relationship. Claude's system card suggests we'd need 10x more progress to reach true rapid self-improvement. Compared to RSI risk, Nathan worries more about something else: the public internet and basic infrastructure are almost wholly unprepared for AI capability at this level.

— Nathan Lambert

Glossary

RSI (Recursive Self-Improvement)
The process of using AI to improve the research and development speed of AI itself
Distillation
Training a weaker model on outputs from a stronger model to compress and transfer its capabilities
RL environment
An interactive task world where a model learns by receiving reward signals for actions, rather than from labeled data alone
Reward hacking
A model finding loopholes in its test or environment design to game a high score without genuinely solving the task
Harness
The external control system that determines how a model calls tools and coordinates multi-step task execution
