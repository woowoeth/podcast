When everyone can build it, only taste still matters

Everyone can tune models now; picking which one thing is actually worth doing is why PMs matter again.

Models emerge from cooking, not from code

The most counterintuitive part of building product around models, Doshi says, is that you can set targets and tweak training data like you would in traditional product work, yet the result stays partially beyond prediction. She likens it to Snape's potions class: ingredients placed correctly, and magic still erupts unexpectedly. Product work splits in two—half is shaping what you feed the model, half is waiting for training to finish, then finding where it surprised you, and turning that flash into a user-facing experience.

7:06

Google's full stack is the edge, not any single model

When pushed on whether Google lags OpenAI and Anthropic, Doshi reframes: Google isn't abandoning frontier models—Gemini 4 Argonne is chasing frontier performance. But her real point is that Google's true advantage is full-stack: chip to model to endpoint products that reach a billion users (she mentions 14 Google products with over a billion monthly active users). This stack lets Google offer different-sized models for different customers—searchers, engineers, network defenders—along that cost-to-quality curve, rather than chasing a single "strongest model" story.

— Tulsee Doshi
10:10

Chain-of-thought monitoring catches misalignment before harm

On AI safety, Doshi skips the "we are careful" platitude and explains the concrete work: Google deploys monitors on models to read their chain of thought in real time, checking whether behavior drifts from assigned intent. Once misalignment appears, they intervene. She's also clear that safety flows both ways—AI hardens defenses. Google partnered with the acquired Wiz on a "Scan for Good" project that helped a hospital system find and patch a security hole, and cases like that shape how she judges "this is moving in the right direction."

— Tulsee Doshi
20:19

When everyone can build, taste becomes the bottleneck

Once AI makes building cheap, Doshi argues, PM value actually rises. With the barrier gone, the scarce skill is picking which of the hundred buildable things is the one worth building. She emphasizes companies like Google, running dozens of product lines in parallel, still need someone to pull order from noise—and once building outpaces coordination, chaos itself becomes the cost. "Taking signal from the noise, aligning everyone on one direction"—that's why PMs are valuable again.

— Tulsee Doshi
23:23

Model intuition is the gatekeeping hire for product research PMs

These days, hiring for research PM roles increasingly prizes something hard to name: vibes about models. Does this person play these models every day in real scenarios? Can they pressure-test models themselves? Will they dive into data and write their own evals? Doshi's entry advice is specific: don't start big. Begin with five prompts. Even 20 prompts across two models surface clear signals. Get crisp on what "good" looks like at small scale before thinking about infrastructure.

— Tulsee Doshi
26:34

The six-level skin-tone standard wasn't designed for global AI

In six years leading responsible AI, one concrete change Doshi pushed through: discovering that Google's early skin-tone evaluation used the Fitzpatrick scale—six levels, originally built for medicine—which inherently biased toward lighter skin tones and couldn't represent the world's actual skin distribution. The team found Harvard professor Monk's 10-level scale, grounded in years of anthropological fieldwork, and swapped it in. That shift now shapes how skin tone renders in Pixel cameras and image generation across products.

— Tulsee Doshi
33:40

Build one notch ahead of where models can perform today

Doshi offers a "60-20-20" frame for how far ahead to build: 60% of features run stably now, forming a real, usable prototype; 20% are rough but ship-ready, and you see the model filling the gap soon; the last 20% are "I see a glimmer of light—this would be beautiful if it works." It's more actionable than "ship three months early," since even with models she struggles to predict what's possible in ninety days.

— Tulsee Doshi
45:56

Burnout is organizational chaos, not hours logged

When anxiety about "we can do more, so we must do more" circulates, Doshi diagnoses the real culprit: not work time itself but two things—churn and chaos inside the org, and doing work that doesn't feel meaningful or rewarding. If you deploy AI right, she expects, it first clears the drudgery, freeing time for the creative, higher-meaning parts. Used correctly, AI tools could ease burnout instead of sharpening it. The catch is using them right: against drag, not as an excuse to do more of everything.

— Tulsee Doshi

Glossary

harness
The system prompt, tools, skills, and context wrapping a model, forming its actual operating environment.
Pareto curve of cost to quality
The cost-quality trade-off curve across models; used to match model size to use case.
chain of thought monitoring
Real-time access to a model's reasoning process to detect whether its behavior drifts from intent or stays aligned.
flash teams
Temporary teams assembled to complete a specific task efficiently, then dissolved.
Monk skin tone scale
A 10-level skin-tone scale created by Harvard professor Monk from anthropological research; better represents global skin diversity than earlier 6-level scales.
Fitzpatrick scale
A 6-level skin-tone classification originally designed for medical use; misapplied as an AI evaluation standard.
