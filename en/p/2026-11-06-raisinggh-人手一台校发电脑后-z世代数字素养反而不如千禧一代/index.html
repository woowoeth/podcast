Gen Z's digital literacy has fallen behind millennials' despite universal school laptops

Introduce a tool before mastering the underlying skill and you guarantee the skill won't be learned—precisely what happened when 88% of American students received school laptops.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

7:17

Teaching the skill must come before introducing the tool

This principle emerged from the 1980s calculator debate: when should children use calculators? Answer: 'when you no longer need one'—once students understand arithmetic, a calculator is an aid; introduce it before they do and it permanently blocks skill formation. Children learn only to operate the machine until someone approves. This is what's happening at scale in schools distributing laptops today.

— Horvath
10:18

Teachers mid-career confuse engagement with learning

Those most confident in technology aren't veteran educators (20+ years, who've seen before-and-after and mostly reject it) or newcomers (who prioritize relationship-building). They're mid-career teachers—5 to 15 years in—trained by technology themselves. They judge tech 'effective' because students stay engaged. But engagement and learning are different metrics: swiping TikTok keeps you engaged without teaching you anything.

— Horvath
24:50

Learning happens through biological synchrony between teacher and student

Horvath redefines empathy not as emotion or personality but as a 'transpersonal emergent property': when two people synchronise, their heart rates and breathing align, their brain images converge. In sync with a teacher, students don't listen passively—they think alongside the teacher. Comprehension and motivation spike. Screens have no biology; they can never synchronise and actively sever the empathy already flowing between people.

— Horvath
36:04

Technology amplifies rather than closes the equity gap

Neil Postman's 1995 air-conditioning metaphor holds: tools never bridge equity gaps; they enlarge them. Horvath ran a counterfactual on New York data—assume minority students use tech all day and white students never use it—and the equity gap didn't shrink; it expanded 26%. The variable that works is 'every student has a teacher who genuinely cares.' Tech can't solve that.

— Horvath
39:10

Growing up with tools actually undermines digital literacy

Horvath often asks at AI education forums: 'How old are you and who taught you to use AI?' The answer is typically 30-something—a generation that never took a class on it yet outperforms kids who grew up onscreen. Tests show Gen Z and Alpha score high only on 'operating tools,' falling behind across every dimension that defines real literacy: creative use, critical use, safe communication. Those abilities depend on knowledge you bring to the tool, not what the tool teaches.

— Horvath
44:22

When SAT embedded powerful calculators, students learned to trick them

Until 2013, only four of America's top 50 universities allowed calculators at the college level—schools insisted students master arithmetic first. Last year, SAT eliminated its no-calculator math section entirely, embedding a calculator so powerful it can 'crack' every problem. Now tutoring doesn't teach math; it teaches kids how to coax the machine into solving it for them.

— Horvath
50:34

Test scores rose because the tests themselves were quietly redefined

NAEP and tests that maintain their measures show reading at historic lows, math barely back to 1992 levels. Even working memory—'remember seven digits'—supposedly a fixed constant, has fallen from seven to 5.5. But SAT scores rose after last year's redesign because reading was redefined as skimming 75-word excerpts (one question per 54 words) and writing redefined as spotting spelling errors. Rename the test and scores improve.

— Horvath
58:57

Countries that rejected EdTech longest are now seeing the steepest declines

China, Japan, South Korea and Singapore were the globe's lightest users of education technology before COVID—despite digital-dependent economies, they'd never scaled tech into classrooms. The pandemic forced adoption. Since then, even Singapore, whose test scores only ever climbed, has seen performance fall noticeably.

— Horvath

Glossary

transpersonal emergent property
Empathy redefined as biological synchrony between two people—their heart rates, breathing and brain activity align—rather than as individual personality or emotion.
NAEP
The National Assessment of Educational Progress, a U.S. standardized test that uses the same scale every two years since 1992 to track American student achievement.
formative vs summative assessment
Formative assessment allows repeated feedback and revision; summative assessment is a one-time score with no opportunity to improve.
ISIL literacy test
A digital literacy assessment measuring across four dimensions: tool operation, creative use, critical use and safe communication.
