Zhipu's R&D spending is twice its revenue; how long can free open-source models sustain growth?

Zhipu releases all its latest models free and open-source, but R&D spending is already twice revenue—the real question, hanging unanswered at the piece's close, is how long this business of burning cash for market attention can last.

The complete arc from Tsinghua lab to Hong Kong IPO is rarely documented; the wild swings in valuation and the burn-cash-to-grow strategy offer real insight into how Chinese AI companies model their business.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

1:02

Paper-to-product culture turned knowledge graphs into revenue

Zhipu traces back to Tsinghua's Knowledge Engineering Group, founded in 1996 and focused on knowledge graphs and information extraction. The lab's internal motto was P2P—Paper to Product—reflecting the conviction that AI required industry engagement. In 2006, KEG spun out AMiner, an academic search engine. Starting in 2015, it built commercial partnerships with Microsoft, Elsevier, Google, ExxonMobil, and IBM, generating revenue that convinced the team to incorporate AMiner as a standalone company.

5:13

GPT-3's scale forced Tsinghua to bet on building its own large models

GPT-3's release in 2020, with 175 billion parameters and generation quality far exceeding Google's BERT and other mainstream models at the time, made Zhang Peng realize that classical NLP's complex syntactic pipeline was unnecessary. His advisor Zhang Bo agreed the large language model represented a new paradigm but warned that models ‘spout utter nonsense’and don't truly understand what they say. Zhang Peng pushed ahead anyway, rallying the team to train their own billions-parameter Chinese-English bilingual model. With OpenAI's details unpublished, they had to discover everything themselves.

8:21

GLM proved that blending BERT-style fill-in-the-blank with GPT-style generation works at scale

In the WuDao project of 2021, Tsinghua proposed GLM (General Language Model), a pretraining framework that used bidirectional context to fill blanks like BERT, but generated token-by-token like GPT rather than predicting entire masked spans at once. Beyond 100 billion parameters, the model began showing signs of intent understanding—for example, on arithmetic questions about a newborn's head length, it divided rather than simply multiplying 50 by 1/4, suggesting it grasped the problem's underlying logic rather than pattern-matching formulas.

— Zhang Peng
11:27

GLM-130B went open-source because monetization looked impossible at the time

In 2022, Zhipu trained GLM-130B, a 130-billion-parameter dense model (avoiding the sparse mixture-of-experts used in WuDao 2.0) chosen so it could run on a single A100 server. Training compute cost roughly 4 million yuan, with total end-to-end cost around 10 million yuan. After successful training, the team chose to open-source it—Zhang Peng's reasoning was blunt: even closed-source, it wouldn't make money anyway, so trade it for reputation. GLM-130B became the only Chinese model to appear on Stanford's November 2022 large model benchmark report.

— Zhang Peng
16:47

Consumer chat lost to Baidu and ByteDance; Zhipu pivoted entirely to enterprise

Zhipu's consumer chatbot, Zhipu Qingyan, launched in August 2023 as one of China's earliest but couldn't compete against Baidu's Wenxin and ByteDance-backed Doubao. Doubao eventually reached 324 million monthly active users; Qingyan peaked below 11 million. Paid subscriptions saw no consumer appetite. Zhipu turned to model-as-a-service for enterprises—Zhang Peng's logic: enterprise customers grow suspicious of prices too low; the enterprise market's price wars are far less vicious than consumer.

— Zhang Peng
19:49

DeepSeek proved MoE architecture could reach GPT-4 caliber; Zhipu adapted

The industry had doubted whether mixture-of-experts architecture could deliver GPT-4-level performance until DeepSeek's R1 validated the path. Zhipu studied DeepSeek's model architecture and built its own variant, releasing its first mixture-of-experts flagship model, GLM-4.5, on July 28, 2025—and open-sourced it for the first time. Expecting modest response, it topped Hugging Face's leaderboard within 48 hours; even U.S. companies began integrating it into their products. Zhang Peng realized new model launches no longer required heavy-handed marketing campaigns.

— Zhang Peng
21:51

Stock soared tenfold then crashed back; R&D burn now exceeds revenue by twofold

Zhipu went public on Hong Kong's exchange in January 2026; the stock price soon surged roughly tenfold, reaching a peak market cap around $170 billion in June, surpassing Xiaomi and BYD and equivalent to more than half Alibaba's market value. It later fell back to around $50 billion, with the company issuing new stock to institutional investors in July to shore up cash. First-half revenue grew 400% year-over-year to $140 million; annualized revenue reached roughly $1.6 billion. The critical problem: R&D spending now exceeds revenue by more than twofold—whether the free open-source model strategy can sustain this burn rate remains an open question.

Glossary

GLM (General Language Model)
Tsinghua's pretraining framework that fuses BERT-style fill-in-the-blank with GPT-style token-by-token generation.
MoE (Mixture-of-Experts)
A sparse model architecture that activates only part of its parameters at inference, more compute-efficient than dense models.
Model-as-a-Service
A business model delivering large model capabilities to enterprise customers via cloud API or private deployment.
KEG (Knowledge Engineering Group)
Tsinghua's lab that spawned Zhipu, focused on knowledge graphs and information extraction research.
P2P (Paper to Product)
KEG's internal motto encouraging research findings to become commercial products.
