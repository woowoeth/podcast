Feishu and DingTalk Exit; the Star of AI Office Is the Personal Agent

Feishu folded into Doubao, DingTalk making way for Qwen Office — the historical mission of enterprise IM is over; the core of AI office shifts from organizational collaboration to personal productivity, and coding capability becomes the Agent's foundation.

Three people who lived through it cut in from org restructuring, the token cost ledger and product form, explaining the underlying logic of ByteDance, Tencent and Alibaba all pivoting to office Agents within half a year.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

9:19

Feishu wasn't killed, two sales teams just merged

Eric breaks it down from the inside: the commercialization team merging into Volcano Engine is essentially just two sales teams merging, because they face the same wave of customers and the same budget, and used to fight each other for orders — merging raises efficiency. The product team wasn't swallowed by Doubao; a Doubao enterprise edition never existed, it's just a Doubao bot added inside Feishu, and the product team was always seconded from Feishu, so now it's only the reporting line returning to Xie Xin. The R&D line hasn't changed at all. From Zhang Yiming's angle, this is using org adjustment to solve internal friction, not Feishu being ‘eliminated’.

— Eric
15:42

One crayfish compressed big-company planning from years to months

Minghao gives two timelines: first, the demonstration effect of leading vendors — products like OpenAI and Claude Code brought revenue explosions; second, OpenCloud (crayfish) going viral in China during Spring Festival, which made giant-company management clear for the first time what an ‘Agent should look like’, forming a consensus without hesitation. Plans originally made on a half-year or one-year cycle are now compressed to monthly — the Spring Festival plan made in last year's Q4 was obsolete by this March. Across the whole AI office battlefield, from project kickoff to product launch, there was less than two quarters.

— Minghao
28:30

Tencent had no to-B card, and made a winner anyway

Minghao argues the core is that Tencent had no to-B card, so it was the most anxious, which let an edge team (backgrounds in Yingyongbao, the browser, Tencent Meeting) build Workbody with a tiny team. It is essentially a personal productivity tool, grabbing local data, with no enterprise data moat, hence no barrier — but it happens to match the ‘individuals pay for AI’ trend. The credible DAU figure is a bit over a million, from Yao Shunyu's own statement in a LatePost report. More importantly, in the coding battlefield domestic vendors are being beaten down by overseas products, while the office scenario (non-programmer population) is incremental, and Workbody seized that gap.

— Minghao
32:42

Feishu dismisses personal local data as trash others don't want

Eric argues Feishu holds enterprise data, and its intelligent Q&A and ‘open the sky eye’ features can already turn enterprise documents into AI context — Zhang Yiming doesn't need an assistant, he can just search inside Feishu and get the essence of everyone's documents. So Feishu looks down on personal-version local-data tools, seeing them as ‘picking up trash others don't want’. But Minghao counters that this is exactly big-company arrogance: Feishu being folded into Doubao shows Zhang Yiming realized he couldn't bet everything on Doubao, and the boundary between personal productivity and enterprise office is blurring — Feishu bet on the wrong direction.

— Eric
49:54

It's not who beat whom — Feishu and DingTalk exit together

Minghao proposes that Feishu and DingTalk fought for five or six years, and in the end it wasn't that one beat the other, but that both exited the stage of history one after the other — Feishu entered the Doubao system, and above DingTalk grew Qwen Office. Enterprise IM in the narrow sense has completed its historical mission, because human-machine collaboration in the AI era doesn't need IM as its foundation; but the full enterprise digital AI stack is a new topic, and each company's state differs. Previously neither big company's to-B facade produced independent commercial results, and both ended up as pieces of the group's big AI story.

— Minghao
1:13:26

Doubao's three monetization paths all fail; the user profile is what's valuable

Yanan takes stock of Doubao's C-end monetization attempts: product-jump GMV is only tens of thousands of orders, brand advertising (GEO) caps out at tens of millions and hurts user experience, and C-end paid penetration is low to the point of being ‘outrageous’. All three paths are dead ends, so it can only turn to to-B. But Doubao has a hidden value: it shares an account system with Douyin, and its user profiles are far higher quality than Douyin's, which can improve ad recommendation precision — ByteDance's domestic and overseas ad revenue last year was nearly 90 billion USD, and even a 1% lift is 1 billion USD. That is the below-the-waterline part of Doubao as an asset.

— Yanan
1:25:57

When nobody knows the answer, AB testing just wastes time

The discussion notes that ByteDance's proud AB testing and middle-platform mechanisms crumble before AI: multi-team coordination and AB testing waste time, and AI changes too fast — by the time coordination finishes, the market window has passed. Workbody was built fast by a single-person group, then stacked up by continuously adding people later; ByteDance, by contrast, has many teams cobbling together one product. When nobody knows the right answer, many attempts are worse than doing one or two with conviction. Even product naming carries path dependency — single-character brands like ‘Cui’ are hard for Chinese users to read accurately.

1:31:10

Office Agents just hide coding behind the interface

Minghao concludes: general office Agents just hide coding behind the interface — generating websites, handling spreadsheets, editing files are all tool-calling capabilities underneath, and coding is the core constituent of an Agent. Over the past half year and the next half year, coding is the absolute consensus; whether it's model training, inference or post-training, the self-evolution process is built on code capability. This also explains why leading vendors all treat code capability as the most core breakthrough point; another camp advocates the world-model route, but in the short term coding is still the infrastructure.

— Minghao

Glossary

Agent
An AI program that can autonomously perceive, decide and execute tasks; an office Agent, for example, can draft documents and handle spreadsheets for you.
ARR
Annual recurring revenue — a SaaS company's subscription revenue calculated on an annualized basis, measuring the health of a to-B business model.
CAPEX
Capital expenditure — spending on long-term assets such as compute and servers, the main money-burning item for large-model companies.
GEO
Generative engine optimization — influencing product recommendations or brand descriptions in large-model answers through paid or technical means.
