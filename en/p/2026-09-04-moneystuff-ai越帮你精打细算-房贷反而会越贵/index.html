AI That Helps You Refinance Will Make Mortgages More Expensive

Cheap US mortgages rely in part on most borrowers being too lazy to refinance when rates fall. Once AI agents remind everyone to refinance promptly, the option gets repriced and mortgage rates rise.

5:08

AI reminders to refinance make mortgages more expensive

US homeowners typically stay put when rates fall: Matt met someone on a plane who was about to sell and upsize despite a good mortgage rate, and Matt talked him out of it. Morgan Stanley research suggests that if AI agents remind you to refinance when rates drop by 200 basis points, future refinancing becomes much easier. Sounds like saving you money, but more frequent refinancing means the prepayment option people used to "neglect to exercise" actually gets exercised, lenders reprice for that, and everyone's mortgage rate rises by 10 to 20 basis points.

— Matt Levine
6:09

The smarter homeowners are, the fewer people can buy homes

Fabrice Touré — former head of mortgage derivatives at Goldman Sachs, now a finance academic — writes bluntly: policies that make refinancing more frequent also raise equilibrium mortgage rates and reduce the likelihood that many borrowers get credit. The reason is that the US 30-year fixed-rate, prepayable mortgage embeds an expensive option, but most borrowers exercise it poorly, which is why lenders are willing to charge lower rates. Once institutions or AI make everyone more rational, the option gets repriced, rates rise, fewer people are covered by the low-rate subsidy, and more families cannot afford a home.

— Matt Levine
10:13

Models priced on past behavior will get blindsided

Investors will feel the shock first. Morgan Stanley warns that historical prepayment behavior will understate future prepayments; any model trained on past data misprices "borrowers' greater willingness to exercise the prepayment option." As a result, some mortgage-linked assets look cheap but actually depend on borrowers staying lazy. When AI cuts refinancing friction from two weeks to ten minutes, those assets need a wholesale repricing. Matt generalizes: credit card points, high-yield savings accounts, life insurance policies — the financial system runs largely on customers not using every option to the max.

— Matt Levine
13:16

AI makes the billable-hour model obsolete

The squeeze on professional services shows up first in law firms: clients assume AI can do the work of a squad of junior associates and demand lower rates. Firms bill by the hour on the surface, but the real profit engine is partners selling junior associates' hours at a high rate and paying them a lower wage. If AI handles much of the grunt work, "charging for junior associate hours" no longer works. Senior lawyers' judgment still has value, but it needs a new pricing model. The bigger problem: when juniors no longer spend hundreds of hours on basic work, where will the next generation of partners develop their instincts?

— Matt Levine
17:24

Top partners can fly solo with AI

Since a senior lawyer's value no longer depends on headcount, the very best will do the math first: rather than stay in a big platform to support a partnership committee and train a horde of juniors, they will leave and open a boutique with just themselves and six AI agents. Chris Kircher, a former Quinn Emanuel partner, has already resigned to start an AI-native firm built on that bet. If enough senior people leave, large firms cannot retain them even knowing apprenticeship matters — the profit-generating capacity walks out the door, the money-losing training system stays, and big firms struggle to survive.

— Matt Levine
21:29

Amazon's auction hides a phantom bidder

The FTC and 22 state attorneys general sued Amazon, arguing its ad auction is not the second-price auction it advertises. In a normal second-price auction, the highest bidder pays the second-highest bid. Amazon adds a "soft reserve" — the platform's own valuation of the ad slot, like a hidden bidder. If your bid is above the soft reserve, the soft reserve becomes the second price; if your bid is below the soft reserve, the winner pays its own maximum bid. Amazon's response: the ad auction is a real-time black box; no one can know the soft reserve in advance, and advertisers just decide based on return on ad spend.

— Matt Levine
25:33

Bidding at the chandelier is legal

Finance bans exchanges from secretly placing their own bids, but art auction houses have a legal performance. The seller sets a confidential reserve price; when live bids are too low, the auctioneer calls out bids at empty air, pushing the price up to the reserve — the term is "chandelier bid," because he often looks up at the chandelier. New York requires auction catalogs to disclose that the auctioneer may bid on the seller's behalf up to the reserve and will not indicate which bids come from the seller. Amazon's soft reserve is also the platform bidding, but it does not use such a fine-print disclosure to explain the rules — that is what the FTC is pouncing on.

— Matt Levine
30:36

Switching ETFs dodges dividend taxes

Foreign funds (e.g., Cayman structures) pay withholding tax on US stock dividends, which drags on the thin margins of basis trades. Some traders exploit two nearly identical S&P 500 ETFs with ex-dividend dates about a week apart: BlackRock's IVV and Vanguard's VOO. The maneuver: sell IVV before its ex-dividend date and switch into VOO; then switch back when VOO is about to pay. After the ex-date, the fund price drops by roughly the dividend amount, so the investor avoids the taxable dividend and earns back the same amount by selling high and buying low. This is an institutional tax play; retail investors copying it would incur large capital gains taxes — not a TikTok money tip.

Glossary

second-price auction
The highest bidder wins but pays only the second-highest bid.
soft reserve price
A hidden minimum price set by the platform or seller in an auction, effectively adding a phantom bidder.
chandelier bid
An auctioneer bids on the seller's behalf against empty air to push the price toward the reserve; the name comes from often looking at the chandelier.
basis trade
Simultaneously buying the cash asset and selling the futures contract to capture the spread; essentially providing funding.
apprenticeship model
The professional services model where juniors do lots of low-level work and gradually develop into senior partners.
withholding tax
A tax deducted at source on payments such as dividends, often levied on foreign investors.
