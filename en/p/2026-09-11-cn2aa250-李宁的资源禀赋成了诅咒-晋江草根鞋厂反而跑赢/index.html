Li Ning's resource endowment became a curse; Jinjiang's grassroots shoe factories ran ahead instead

Li Honggu said Li Ning is a company made of countless mismatches: the founder had no ambition for business, held resources no one could match, and those resources became a curse. Jinjiang's shoe factories were started by Minnan farmers pooling funds household by household, with no baggage, decisions made only around the market, and over the long run they walked more steadily.

Jinjiang is not the Fujian version of the Wenzhou model

Fei Xiaotong summarized China's rural development into four models: Sunan had the government leading farmers to run factories, Wenzhou relied on individual households, the Pearl River Delta relied on foreign capital plus labor for processing and assembly. Jinjiang appeared latest and is most easily underestimated. It was not individual households, but township enterprises run by several families, relatives and friends pooling funds, with a nature of private shareholding: in 1979 when Lin Tuqin started Jinjiang's first shoe factory he called together 14 village households to buy shares, these people were both shareholders and employees; in 1982 when they wanted to start anew, they also split from the original factory into five factories. Funds relied on the Minnan-specific biao hui, in a financial gray area, and many companies that later grew big raised money this way early on. The local government also proposed the "five allows", allowing dividends, share-based fundraising, hiring workers, and sales commissions.

Under the fake medicine case, the government chose to remove pests while protecting flowers

Jinjiang had two early quality crises. One was "week shoes" and "street-crossing shoes": farmers didn't understand that shoes needed professional glue, and used ordinary glue to stick soles and uppers, which came apart after seven days; one consumer said they came apart just crossing the street. The second was the 1985 fake medicine case: white sugar was ground into powder and condensed into blocks, stuffed with white fungus to pass off as granules, flowing through gray channels to hospitals, and reported nationwide by People's Daily. Jinjiang goods became notorious, over a thousand ground promotion salespeople were rejected, and some hotels directly threw their luggage out. At the time, Fujian Provincial Party Committee First Secretary Xiang Nan was very market-oriented, expanded the Xiamen Special Economic Zone area, borrowed international loans to build Xiamen Airport, and called Chendai's workshops "a flower of township enterprises", but was criticized for being too leftist, made five or six self-criticisms within half a year and then stepped down. Before leaving he instructed those handling the fake medicine case: remove pests while protecting flowers, don't throw the baby out with the bathwater.

37:27

Li Ning's resource endowment became a curse instead

Jinjiang shoe factories were all Nike's disciples, but the first to develop awareness of building a domestic brand was Li Ning. In 1990 Li Ning created the brand, collecting sacred fire under the snow mountains of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and passing it nationwide, with huge impact; the registration nodes of Jinjiang brands almost all came after this—Anta applied in 1990 and registered successfully in 1991, Peak was originally Fengdeng and changed its name from 1990 to 1991, 361 Degrees was previously called Bieke. But the two paths were completely different: Li Ning carried a national halo from its founding, and when performance doubled repeatedly in the mid-1990s it set a goal to spend ten to twenty years making a Chinese brand sit at the number one position in the world; he himself had no ambition for business, hired professional managers to run it, and ended up falling into crisis and internal strife again and again. Jinjiang brands were grassroots, with no historical baggage, and all decisions revolved around the market.

— Huang Ziyi
44:28

The family that gave away 51% of the benefits hit every node

In 1988, Ding Shizhong was not yet 18, dropped out of middle school, persuaded his father to take 10,000 yuan, purchased 600 to 700 pairs of shoes, took a two-day two-night hard seat to Beijing, and became one of the first Beijing drifters. Around the same time, Li Ning was wearing Li Ning-brand clothes passing the Asian Games sacred fire in Beijing. He wanted to sell Jinjiang shoes into Xidan and Wangfujing, but those were powerful state-owned department stores, and a seventeen or eighteen-year-old Fujian kid simply couldn't get in; he spent one or two years grinding before getting a counter. Another easily overlooked point is his father Ding Hemu: the only male in the family, the family was actually weak in the village—Minnan clans fought over land and factory buildings relying on having many sons and fists, and Ding Hemu did not participate. He taught his son to give others more benefits in doing things, "give others 51% of the benefits, take only 49% yourself".

— Huang Ziyi
54:32

Inventory enough for the whole nation to wear for three years

Around 2000, Anta signed Kong Linghui and advertised on CCTV, becoming famous overnight, and combined with the Sydney Olympics, the men's football team entering the World Cup, and the successful Olympic bid, the whole industry frantically signed spokespeople and opened stores, with nearly 1000 stores opened in a single year at the peak. Peak founder Xu Jingnan's metaphor was "riding an elevator": a person standing still in an elevator grows very fast without doing anything, and everyone thought that after the Beijing Olympics they would ride an even faster elevator. But two or three years after the Olympics the inventory crisis broke out: one manager described it as, even if Jinjiang shoe factories stopped producing, the shoes in inventory would be enough for the whole Chinese population to wear for three years. The root cause was the distribution model of feudalism plus futures system—ordering half a year in advance, the brand side too far from the market, and distributors unwilling to share real sales data. Anta's response was to strengthen centralization, with Ding Shizhong personally inspecting stores like an emperor on a campaign, traveling to over 400 to 500 market points in China in the worst one or two years.

The book lacks the coordinate system of Nike and Adidas

Li Honggu's biggest criticism of this book is: it lacks an implicit thread—the campaigns of Nike and Adidas in the Chinese market actually determined the ups and downs of Chinese brands to a greater extent, they are the real coordinates. Huang Ziyi agrees, but explains that there is too little detailed material on Adidas and Nike's playbook, because multinational companies have to go through procedures and discipline to accept interviews. Li Honggu also points out that Li Ning's problems cannot be explained by the professional manager system: Li Ning is "a company made of countless mismatches", he is not happy doing business, his resource endowment far exceeds everyone else's, yet forms a huge gap with his vision and strategy, and resources became a curse instead. And today after Jinjiang brands obtained capital and acquired international brands, new problems have appeared: how to tell a story of an international brand with oneself, "capital has become their curse".

— Li Honggu
1:22:53

Anta multi-brand: independent front end, shared middle and back office

Anta doing FILA was learned. Li Ning was first surpassed by Nike in 2003 and lost market number one, and was the earliest to try multi-brand; Li Ning's first professional manager Chen Yihong also did KAPPA very well. Anta acquired FILA, and at first also put traditional Anta products with FILA labels to sell, and only when they didn't sell did they painfully start over: in organizational structure and processes, keep the FILA team absolutely independent from the Anta team, eliminate mutual influence, then poach a world-class team for design, move toward fashion and trends, and also do DTC direct retail. After it worked, Anta's multi-brand mechanism is: retail end and product design and R&D end each independent, sharing Anta's strong middle and back office, including human resources, OEM factory system, and Jinjiang's industrial cluster. What Ding Shizhong now repeatedly says is: I don't want to be China's Nike, what I want to do is world's Anta.

— Huang Ziyi
1:28:57

When per capita GDP passes $10,000, vertical categories take off

In 2021 when Huang Ziyi interviewed Anta, an Anta vice president explained why they acquired Amer Sports, Descente, and Kolon: based on their research in European and American markets, when a country's per capita GDP reaches $10,000, niche sports tracks begin to emerge—ice and snow, outdoor, hiking; when it reaches $20,000, such niche sports begin to flourish on a large scale. At the time of the interview China had just broken $10,000, now Beijing and Shanghai have exceeded $20,000, and the national average is about $15,000, so these brands taking off may be a natural process. Where Anta is stronger than others is that it may, through relatively advanced professional manager awareness, be the first to do market research and seize these tracks.

Glossary

Three idles
Jinjiang local term for overseas remittances, vacant overseas Chinese houses, and surplus labor, which became the three original conditions for starting factories.
Biao hui
Mutual aid lending in the Minnan acquaintance society, in a financial gray area, and the startup funding source for early factories.
Five allows
Policy proposed by Chendai and Jinjiang local governments: allow dividends, allow share-based fundraising, allow hiring workers, allow sales commissions.
Halfway-up-the-mountain theory
When an enterprise reaches medium scale, if it can complete modern enterprise transformation it can go further up, if not it may fall into the abyss.
DTC
Brands opening their own stores to directly reach consumers, bypassing layers of distributors.
Flyknit
Nike's technology of weaving an entire shoe upper from a single yarn, making sneakers light and breathable, overturning the leather upper paradigm.
