Li Ning's resource endowment became a curse, while grassroots shoe factories in Jinjiang ran ahead

Li Honggu said Li Ning is a company made of countless mismatches: the founder had no ambition for business, held resources no one could match, and those resources became a curse. Jinjiang's shoe factories were started by Minnan farmers pooling funds household by household, carrying no baggage, making decisions only around the market, and over the long run they walked more steadily.

Jinjiang is not the Fujian version of the Wenzhou model

Fei Xiaotong summed up China's rural development into four models: Sunan had government leading farmers to start factories, Wenzhou relied on individual households, the Pearl River Delta relied on foreign capital plus labor doing processing and assembly. Jinjiang appeared latest and is also the easiest to underestimate. It was not individual households but township enterprises started by several families and relatives pooling funds household by household, with a nature of private shareholding: when Lin Tuqin started Jinjiang's first shoe factory in 1979, he gathered 14 village households to buy shares, and these people were both shareholders and employees; in 1982, when they wanted to start something new, they also split from the original factory into five factories. The money relied on the bidding associations unique to Minnan, in a gray zone of finance, and many companies that later grew big raised money this way early on. The local government also proposed the "five permissions," permitting dividends, share-based fundraising, hiring workers, and sales commissions.

Under the fake-medicine case, the government chose to remove the bugs while protecting the flowers

Jinjiang had two quality crises early on. One was "week shoes" and "street-crossing shoes": farmers did not understand that shoes needed specialized glue, and used ordinary glue to stick the soles and uppers, so they came apart after seven days, and one consumer said they came apart just from crossing the street. The other was the 1985 fake-medicine case: white sugar was ground into powder, congealed into blocks, and stuffed with white fungus to pass as granules, flowing through gray channels to hospitals, and reported nationwide by People's Daily. Jinjiang goods therefore stank to high heaven, over a thousand ground-promotion salespeople were turned away, and some hotels directly threw their luggage out. At the time, Fujian's first provincial party secretary, Xiang Nan, was very market-oriented, expanded the Xiamen special economic zone's area, and borrowed international loans to build Xiamen airport, calling Chendai's workshops "a flower of township enterprises," but for this he was criticized as too far to the left, and after making five or six self-criticisms within half a year he stepped down. Before leaving, he instructed the person handling the fake-medicine case: remove the bugs while protecting the flowers, and when pouring out the bathwater, do not throw out the baby too.

— Huang Ziyi
37:27

Li Ning's resource endowment instead became a curse

Jinjiang's shoe factories were all Nike's disciples, but the first to develop the awareness of building a domestic brand was Li Ning. Li Ning founded the brand in 1990, collecting sacred flame beneath the snow mountains of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and passing it nationwide, with enormous impact; the registration milestones of Jinjiang brands almost all came after that — Anta applied in 1990 and registered successfully in 1991, Peak was originally named Fengdeng and changed its name in 1990 to 1991, and 361 Degrees was previously called Bieke. But the two paths were completely different: Li Ning carried a national halo from the very founding, and when performance doubled repeatedly in the mid-1990s he set the goal of spending ten or twenty years to put a Chinese brand in the world's number one brand position; he himself had no ambition for business and hired professional managers to run it, and as a result fell into crisis and internal friction again and again. Jinjiang brands were grassroots, had no historical baggage, and all decisions revolved around the market.

— Huang Ziyi
44:28

The family that gave away 51% of the benefits hit every node

In 1988, Ding Shizhong was not yet 18, had dropped out of middle school, and persuaded his father to take 10,000 yuan, buy 600 to 700 pairs of shoes, and ride a hard seat for two days and two nights to Beijing, becoming one of the first Beijing drifters. Around the same time, Li Ning was wearing Li Ning-brand clothes and carrying the Asian Games sacred flame in Beijing. He wanted to sell Jinjiang shoes into Xidan and Wangfujing, but those were powerful state-run department stores, and a 17- or 18-year-old kid from Fujian simply could not get in; it took him one or two years of grinding before he got a counter. Another easily overlooked point is his father, Ding Hemu: the only male in the family, the family was instead weak in the village — Minnan clans fought over land and factory buildings by having many sons and by fists, and Ding Hemu did not participate. He taught his son to give others more benefit in doing things, "give others 51% of the benefit, take only 49% yourself."

— Huang Ziyi
54:32

Inventory was enough for the whole nation to wear for three years

Around 2000, Anta signed Kong Linghui and advertised on CCTV, becoming famous overnight, and on top of the Sydney Olympics, the men's football team entering the World Cup, and the successful Olympic bid, the whole industry frantically signed spokespeople and laid out stores, opening close to 1,000 stores in the peak year. Peak founder Xu Jingnan's metaphor was "riding an elevator": a person standing still in the elevator grows very fast without doing anything, and everyone thought that after the Beijing Olympics they would ride an even faster elevator. But two or three years after the Olympics the inventory crisis erupted: one manager described it as, even if Jinjiang's shoe factories did not produce, the shoes in inventory were enough for the whole Chinese people to wear for three years. The root cause was the dealership model of enfeoffment plus futures — ordering half a year in advance, the brand side too far from the market, and dealers unwilling to share real sales data. Anta's response was to strengthen centralization, with Ding Shizhong personally touring stores like an emperor on campaign, covering 400 to 500-plus market points in China in the worst one or two years.

— Huang Ziyi

The book lacks the coordinate system of Nike and Adidas

Li Honggu's biggest criticism of this book is that it lacks an implicit thread — the campaigns of Nike and Adidas in the Chinese market actually determined the rises and falls of Chinese brands to a greater extent, and they are the real coordinates. Huang Ziyi agrees, but explains that there is too little detailed material on Adidas and Nike's playbook, because multinational companies have to go through procedures and have discipline when accepting interviews. Li Honggu also argues that Li Ning's problems cannot be explained by the professional-manager system: Li Ning is "a company made of countless mismatches," he was not happy doing business, his resource endowment far exceeded everyone else's, yet it formed a huge gap with his vision and strategy, and the resources instead became a curse. And today, after Jinjiang brands obtained capital and acquired international brands, new problems have appeared: how to tell a story of an international brand with oneself, "capital became their curse."

— Li Honggu
1:22:53

Anta's multi-brand: independent front end, shared middle and back office

Anta learned to do FILA. Li Ning was first overtaken by Nike in 2003 and lost the market's number one spot, and was the earliest to try multi-brand; Li Ning's first professional manager, Chen Yihong, also did KAPPA very well. When Anta acquired FILA, at first it likewise put traditional Anta products under the FILA label to sell, and only after they did not sell did it learn the bitter lesson and start over: in organizational structure and process, keep the FILA team absolutely independent from the Anta team, eliminate mutual influence, then poach a world-class team to do design, move toward fashion and trends, and at the same time do DTC direct retail. After it worked, Anta's multi-brand mechanism was: the retail end and the product design and R&D end each independent, sharing Anta's powerful middle and back office, including human resources, the contract-manufacturing system, and Jinjiang's industrial cluster. What Ding Shizhong now repeatedly says is: I do not want to make China's Nike, what I want to make is the world's Anta.

— Huang Ziyi
1:28:57

Once per-capita GDP passes $10,000, verticals take off

When Huang Ziyi interviewed Anta in 2021, an Anta vice president explained why it wanted to acquire Amer Sports, Descente, and Kolon: according to their research in European and American markets, when a country's per-capita GDP reaches $10,000, niche sports tracks begin to emerge — ice and snow, outdoor, hiking; when it reaches $20,000, such niche sports begin to flourish on a large scale. At the time of the interview, China had just broken through $10,000, and now Beijing and Shanghai have already exceeded $20,000, with the whole country at about $15,000, so these brands taking off may be a natural process. Where Anta is stronger than others is that it may, through relatively advanced professional-manager awareness, be the first to do market research and seize these tracks.

— Huang Ziyi

Glossary

Three idles
The local Jinjiang term for overseas remittances, vacant overseas Chinese houses, and surplus labor, which became the three original conditions for starting factories.
Bidding association
Mutual-aid lending in Minnan's acquaintance society, in a gray zone of finance, and the startup funding source for early factories.
Five permissions
The policy proposed by the Chendai and Jinjiang local governments: permit dividends, permit share-based fundraising, permit hiring workers, and permit sales commissions.
Halfway-up-the-mountain theory
When an enterprise reaches medium scale, if it can complete the modern-enterprise transformation it can go further up; if not, it may fall into the abyss.
DTC
The brand itself opens stores and directly faces consumers, bypassing layer upon layer of dealers.
Flyknit
Weaving an entire shoe upper from a single yarn, making sneakers light and breathable, overturning the leather-upper paradigm.
