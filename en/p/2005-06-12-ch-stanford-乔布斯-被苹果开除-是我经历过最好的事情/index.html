Steve Jobs: being fired by Apple was the best thing that ever happened to me

After being publicly fired from his own company at 30, Steve Jobs entered the most creative five years of his life, which spawned NeXT and Pixar, and eventually he returned to Apple with that technology.

10:14

Calligraphy chosen after dropping out became the design foundation of the Mac

After dropping out, Jobs no longer had to take required courses, so out of curiosity he audited a calligraphy class at Reed College, learning about serif fonts and letter spacing—things with no apparent practical use at the time. Ten years later, when designing the first Macintosh, he used all this knowledge; the Mac became the first computer with beautiful typography. He notes that because Windows copied the Mac, if he hadn't dropped out and audited that class, all personal computers probably wouldn't have multiple fonts and variable spacing.

— Steve Jobs
12:15

Life's dots connect only in retrospect, never in advance

Reflecting on this period, Jobs said it was impossible to connect these dots in advance while at college, but looking back ten years later it became strikingly clear. His conclusion: you can't connect the dots looking forward, only backward, so you must trust that the dots will connect someday in the future—trust intuition, destiny, life, karma, whatever you call it. This belief gives you courage to follow your heart, even if it leads you off the conventional path.

— Steve Jobs
13:16

Fired publicly from his own company at thirty

In a decade, Apple grew from two people in a garage to a company with $2 billion in revenue and more than 4,000 employees. A year after releasing the first Macintosh, just as Jobs turned 30, a vision disagreement emerged between him and the manager brought in to run the company; the board sided with the other man, and Jobs was publicly pushed out. He considered fleeing Silicon Valley and even sought out David Packard and Bob Noyce to apologize for his failure.

— Steve Jobs
14:18

Losing his company freed him for his most creative five years

Jobs says the weight of success was replaced by the lightness of being a beginner again, which led him into the most creative period of his life: over the next five years he founded NeXT and then Pixar, which later created the world's first feature-length computer-animated film, Toy Story. Later, Apple acquired NeXT, and Jobs returned with it; NeXT's technology became central to Apple's revival.

— Steve Jobs
16:26

Every morning he asks: what if this were your last day

At 17, he read a line—if you live each day as if it were your last, someday you'll be right—which influenced him for 33 years. Every morning he looks in the mirror and asks himself the same question; if the answer is no for too many days in a row, he knows change is needed. He believes reminding himself he's going to die is the best way to avoid the illusion ‘I still have something to lose’, because you already own nothing.

— Steve Jobs
17:28

The misdiagnosis that gave him three to six months to live

About a year ago, Jobs had a scan at 7:30 in the morning that clearly showed a tumor on his pancreas. The doctor told him it was almost certainly an incurable form of cancer with an expected lifespan of three to six months, and advised him to go home and get his affairs in order—essentially to prepare to face death. That evening, during a biopsy, the doctor wept while looking at the cells under the microscope, because it was actually a rare form of pancreatic cancer that could be cured by surgery. He had the surgery, and everything has been normal since.

— Steve Jobs
19:28

Death is life's best invention, clearing the old to make room for the new

After this misdiagnosis, Jobs says these words now carry more weight than when he once saw death merely as a useful intellectual concept. He believes death is very likely life's best invention—life's renewal mechanism—that clears out the old and makes room for the new. He reminds graduates that time is limited, don't waste it living someone else's life, don't be trapped by dogma, don't let others' opinions drown out your inner voice.

— Steve Jobs
20:29

The final issue's back cover message: stay hungry stay foolish

Jobs speaks of the Whole Earth Catalog, which influenced his generation in his youth. Founded by Stuart Brand using a typewriter, scissors, and a Polaroid camera, it was ‘a paper Google’, 35 years before Google existed. The back cover of the magazine's final issue featured a photo of a rural road at dawn, with ‘stay hungry stay foolish’ printed below, as their parting words. Jobs says he's always held this phrase as an expectation for himself, and now offers it to the graduating students.

— Steve Jobs
