AI will replace the phone, but the winner may not be a phone maker

Zhu Mingming says AI will definitely replace the phone, but glasses are not a winner-take-all business — at most ten players can sit at the table; what he is betting on is the two or three years when the big companies can't see it, don't understand it, don't care about it, and it's too late.

Four thousand yuan in the account, payday in two weeks

At the worst moment of his first startup, Zhu Mingming called everyone together and gave them two choices: keep going, and he would carve out his own shares for them, but he said plainly that they might be worthless paper; or take the computers on the table back as wages, so he wouldn't owe anyone. He said he wouldn't come in that Saturday or Sunday, and wouldn't lock the door, because he couldn't bear to watch people carry the computers away. When he came back on Monday, not one person had left. Less than two weeks later, Alibaba invested, and he never missed a paycheck.

— Zhu Mingming
19:13

Alibaba OS lost on closedness, not on technology

There are two ways to build an operating system. Alibaba chose the relatively closed one, controlling everything from hardware to software directly and polishing it as a whole, a bit like Apple's approach. In the end, that path proved slower than Android's open route, and Alibaba didn't have that kind of pull at the time. Zhu Mingming calls it a decent start and a not-so-great outcome; the system is now mainly used in cars. By then he had already been reassigned to work on AI.

— Zhu Mingming
31:41

AI and AR were never two separate paths

Outsiders thought Rocket suddenly switched from AI to AR in 2019, but Zhu Mingming says that's completely wrong: internally, both legs were always running. As early as 2013 he posted that he wanted to build a product like Google Glass, and when he left Alibaba he told Jack Ma that AI and AR would change the world and would become one thing. But at the time an AR product couldn't be productized, so they first shipped AI products to land, giving AR enough time to develop, and then switch when the time was right. The switch came a year earlier than planned.

— Zhu Mingming
59:45

The hardest part of the pivot wasn't the direction, it was the people

In the 2019 restructuring, more than half — about 60% — of the staff left within two weeks, and one of the two buildings was basically emptied. Zhu Mingming says the direction decision itself wasn't painful, because he had figured it out long ago; the pain was that many of them he had personally interviewed and hired, they were excellent, and they had done nothing wrong. He set himself two preconditions: first, he had to be able to give N+1 severance, so no one leaving would feel wronged; second, the account had to keep 10 months of cash to validate the new product. So this wasn't a strategic decision, it was a decision calculated from the books, dragged out until the cash-planning deadline.

— Zhu Mingming
1:05:50

Five days later, this company wouldn't exist

The restructuring happened in October 2019, and the first product came out in January 2020. The glasses' first dealer conference was on January 15, 2020. Five days later, on January 20, the pandemic broke out, and no suppliers or channels were allowed to move. Precisely because that launch event happened, after the pandemic arrived they found that the glasses, combined with infrared sensing, could measure body temperature non-contact and at scale, and suddenly sold to 80 countries. Zhu Mingming says, if we had delayed just 5 days, Rocket wouldn't exist.

— Zhu Mingming
1:19:29

Of Jack Ma's four noes, glasses hit none

Jack Ma summed up four opportunities for startups competing with giants: can't see it, can't understand it, don't care about it, too late. Zhu Mingming says that with AR glasses, from day one big companies will definitely see it and definitely understand it; the only question is whether they care about it now. So the window left for Rocket is to use the time when the giants don't care to get to the point within two to three years where it's too late for them. He admits equal competition is impossible — you can never compete on equal terms with a company like Tencent; being able to compete on par is already good.

— Zhu Mingming
1:33:01

Hardware's steps can't be crossed by effort alone

Zhu Mingming says hardware growth is never linear or continuous; at a certain point it enters a plateau, and no amount of effort helps, because that's the boundary of the entire industry, nothing to do with your effort. At that stage big companies slowly catch up. So after you finally jump one step, the next jump might be a year away, and that year is the time others use to catch up. Only software, experience, and ecosystem can grow linearly or even exponentially — that's Rocket's real know-how: battery life more than an hour longer than Meta, translation results within 0.5 seconds, all achieved by squeezing power consumption at the OS level, not by application work.

— Zhu Mingming
1:57:13

Production can't keep up because last year was too conservative

Zhu Mingming says this year's biggest difficulty is production — whether the industry can meet capacity is not easy. He reflects that this came from not being bold enough in financing: in the middle of last year he judged the macro environment might not be so good, so he changed the supply chain to build-to-order, adding capacity only as orders came in. It seemed reasonable, but this year when traffic came, they couldn't handle it. If funds were ample, he would keep at least two to three months of inventory, and at worst treat inventory as a cost. He says at Alibaba he could spend several billion a year at most, so his concept of capital is actually more solid.

— Zhu Mingming

Glossary

Always on
A device that is worn all the time and available at any moment — Zhu Mingming sees this as the precondition for an AI carrier to work.
System Cost
The steps that must be taken along the GUI operation path before reaching the target function.
PMF
The point where a product matches market demand; Zhu Mingming says this road is very long.
Dare to be a follower
Waiting for mature supply chains and solutions before following up — Zhu Mingming sees this as the optimal strategy for hardware giants.
