Meta's 4+N/2 Severance: With a Green Card It's Happiness, Without One It's a Countdown

In the same Meta layoff, people with green cards take their six-figure severance to a new job, while those without one have four months — right through the holiday window — to find their next job or leave the United States.

The severance formula is 4+N/2, not 4+4

What laid-off people got was ‘4 plus 4 plus N divided by 2’: two months of salary plus two months of severance, four months of exit pay in total; then an extra N/2 based on tenure — two-plus years gets one more month, one year gets half a month. On top of that, six months of Cobra health insurance, and shares that were due to vest on November 15 were still granted. The guest says this money is actually easy to calculate, because US salaries are basically transparent — base salary and bonus can be looked up, only the stock portion varies by person. Using the Level.FYI median, he estimates an E5 gets around $150,000, an E6 under $200,000, and after California's 30-40% tax, around $120,000 is left.

10:13

If a product manager gets laid off, you weren't watching your own business

The guest says he was cold-blooded when talking with friends: as a product manager or data scientist, getting laid off at a company like Meta or Google shouldn't happen, because you have first-hand information and should be able to judge whether your business is a company priority. He feels it's a shame when engineers get laid off, because engineers often focus only on the task at hand and lack a broad judgment about the business. When he joined Meta, he deliberately chose recommendation systems and short video, reasoning that these four products — Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger — plus Reality Lab are ones the company will definitely not give up; what can be given up is peripheral business.

16:57

Without a green card, it's a four-month countdown

Laid-off people will stay on payroll until January 15 next year, meaning they can tell immigration they were laid off on January 15, after which they have 60 days to find a job, with a deadline of March 15. The guest says the difficulty is that the market is cold and it happens to hit the holidays, and next year's hiring won't open up until late January or February. Conversely, for people with green cards, being laid off is a kind of happiness for many, especially capable people — he knows two people laid off this week who started work the following Monday, essentially getting a windfall.

— Ni Youzhang
20:53

Silicon Valley has no 35-year line, because it lacks people

The guest says he ultimately plans to return to China, for family reasons: he is an only child, his parents are in China, and he doesn't want to spend his sixties and seventies alone in a small apartment in a foreign country. But he also admits China has a 35-year age line, while Silicon Valley generally doesn't, for two reasons: a shortage of tech talent, and age discrimination not being allowed in workplace culture — not marrying at 30, buying a house after 40, all normal. Last year he interviewed at Tencent and ByteDance, got offers but chose not to go. He positions himself as a ‘professional wage earner’, not the risk-taking entrepreneurial type.

— Ni Youzhang
24:46

At the same level, US pay is about double China's

He aligns by level: Tencent P12, ByteDance 3.2 to 4.1, Meta is 6, Google is 6 to 7. The US line pays roughly $550,000 to $650,000 a year, or about 4.2 million RMB at $600,000; the same level in China is 2.4 to 2.5 million RMB. But the cost of living works in reverse: a meal in China is 300-400 RMB, $40 a day, $1,200 a month; in the US a meal is $60 to $150, and you first pay 40% tax, then 10% sales tax, then a 15% tip, meaning only 35% of the money actually goes to the food. He also says basic healthcare is faster in China; in the US seeing a doctor for a stomach ulcer takes three months.

— Ni Youzhang
29:25

The root cause of the layoffs is the end of the mobile internet dividend

The guest relays three outside judgments: Zuckerberg will keep increasing metaverse investment next year, costs won't come down and revenue won't grow; Zuckerberg holds company control, so investors have no way to change his behavior; the company made wrong estimates of its own P&L and balance sheet and over-hired. But he pushes the root cause one step further back: thank Steve Jobs — today it's because the phone dividend has run out, mobile internet growth is over, and every company valued on growth has hit a bottleneck. He says this dividend began with the iPhone and 3G, 4G, 5G, lasted about 12 to 14 years, and officially ended in 2021.

— Ni Youzhang
30:23

This is far less brutal than 2008

The guest went to the US in 2006 and lived through the 2008 financial crisis. He says compared with that, this is ‘no contest’ — 2008 was worse: he was in school then, and one reason he delayed graduation by several years was that there were no jobs; when he graduated in 2010, the 65,000 H1B quota wasn't even filled. Now, H1B in April next year will certainly still be snapped up in one draw, which shows the economic crisis is far from here, and right now it's only big companies laying off. He also mentions that an extra buffer for Chinese companies is going overseas, but apart from gaming, most Chinese companies have not done overseas expansion well.

— Ni Youzhang
32:22

He interviewed for fewer than a hundred rounds

The guest shares the intensity of his interview preparation while not knowing whether he'd be laid off: he found a dozen headhunters, applied to thirty or forty companies himself, practiced interviews with others for at least 60 hours, and actually went to roughly fewer than 100 interviews. Among them, he interviewed with Microsoft 11 or 12 times, with different departments at least seven or eight times, with Google 7 times, and with Meta at least 6 times. He says it adds up to just under 100 hours.

Glossary

Level.FYI
A US tech company pay and level comparison site where employees anonymously upload salary data.
Cobra
The legal arrangement in the US to continue company health insurance at your own expense after leaving a job.
H1B
A US work visa for foreign professional employees; after losing a job, a new employer must be found within a deadline.
P&L
Profit and Loss, a financial statement reflecting a company's revenue and cost structure.
IC
Individual Contributor, a career track that does not go into management and focuses only on professional work.
