News Central 2026-02-01

Musk predicts AI will surpass any single person this year, all humanity by 2031

Musk connects solar power, fully reusable rockets, and humanoid robots into one argument: electricity, not chips, is AI's real constraint—and the cheapest AI data center will end up in space.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

20:31

Only cheap pervasive AI and ubiquitous robots can end global poverty

Musk argues the only way to give everyone a high standard of living is through AI and robots, provided AI becomes nearly free and robots are everywhere. His prediction: robot numbers will exceed human population, goods and services will be so abundant you cannot think of what to ask robots for. This is central to his ‘sustainable abundance’ narrative and the basis for later discussion of humanoid robot commercialization timelines.

— Elon Musk
21:35

Affluence cannot be universal if any work requires humans

Musk highlights a counterintuitive tension: if ‘mandatory human work’ still exists, only some people can do it—making universal affluence impossible, only narrow affluence possible. Universal affluence becomes achievable only when billions of humanoid robots are common and everyone owns one, handling childcare, pet care, elder care—then affluence becomes truly universal, not a luxury for the few.

— Elon Musk
27:39

Electricity, not chips, is the real constraint on AI expansion

Musk says AI chip output is growing exponentially, but electricity comes online only 3–4% per year. He predicts that soon—possibly even later this year—everywhere except China will see ‘chip supply exceeding electricity to run those chips’. Meanwhile, China is building 100 gigawatts of nuclear power and producing 1,500 gigawatts of solar annually, with over 1,000 gigawatts deployed—translating to roughly 250 gigawatts sustained power, about half U.S. average consumption.

— Elon Musk
30:44

A small piece of U.S. desert can power America

Musk does the math: 100 by 100 miles (roughly 160 by 160 kilometers) of solar panels would power all of America—say, a small corner of Utah, Nevada, or New Mexico. Europe needs only a small portion of Spain or Sicily. But he notes the U.S. imposes steep tariffs on solar equipment because almost all solar cells come from China, artificially raising the cost of solar deployment.

— Elon Musk
33:50

Consumers will buy humanoid robots by year-end next year

Tesla's Optimus robot currently handles simple factory tasks; Musk expects more complex tasks by year-end, though still in industrial settings. His specific timeline: before next year ends, the company will be confident selling humanoid robots to the public, provided reliability and safety are high enough and the capability range is broad—basically it does whatever you ask.

— Elon Musk
38:54

Complete Starship reusability will slash launch costs by a factor of 100

SpaceX's big goal this year is Starship full reusability—no rocket has achieved this before. Falcon 9 achieved partial reusability (booster landing over 500 times) but the upper stage burns up, costing as much as a mid-size commercial jet. Once Starship reaches full reusability, launch costs will drop below $100 per pound—cheaper than airfreight.

— Elon Musk
39:54

Solar panels in space produce five times more power

Space has no day-night cycle, no seasons, no weather, no atmosphere dimming light. The same panel generates five times the energy in space. With shadow zones at just 3 Kelvin, cooling systems run extremely efficiently. Musk's conclusion: building solar-powered AI data centers in space is ‘unquestionably’ the choice, and within two to three years this will be the cheapest place to build AI.

— Elon Musk
40:54

AI will exceed individual human intelligence by next year at latest

When asked how confident he is in predictions about AI and space over the next decade or two, Musk says he does not know what happens in ten years, but he is confident AI progress is fast enough that before year-end possibly, definitely by next year at latest, AI will appear smarter than any single human. He further predicts that by roughly 2030 or 2031, AI will exceed the collective intelligence of all humanity.

— Elon Musk
