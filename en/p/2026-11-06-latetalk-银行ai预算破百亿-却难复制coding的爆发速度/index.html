Bank AI budgets exceed 100 billion dollars, but can't match coding's explosive growth

Banks and pharmaceutical companies have raised AI budgets to their highest-ever priority, but Zhang Lu believes enterprises must traverse two stages—automation and optimization—to truly transform workflows, making year-one explosive growth like coding impossible.

4:02

Embedded in workflows, Claude has become enterprise infrastructure

Zhang Lu says the prospectus figure that struck her most wasn't revenue growth but customer composition: roughly 11,000 to 12,000 enterprise customers spending over $100,000 annually, with about 2,000 spending over $1 million yearly. This reveals Claude isn't merely a trial tool but deeply embedded in workflows—functioning like infrastructure. The result: far faster upsell velocity and opportunity.

— Zhang Lu
10:03

Enterprises cut closed-source adoption from 57 to 14 percent in six months

A report from two weeks prior showed large enterprises' adoption of cutting-edge, closed-source models collapsed from 57% to 14% in half a year, shifting toward open-source deployment. AT&T publicly committed 60-70% of internal AI budgets to open-source; Nvidia's Jensen Huang also publicly backs the open-source ecosystem. But Zhang Lu doesn't expect this to squeeze Anthropic and OpenAI from the market—AI is permeating the entire economy, expanding the pie itself. They'll simply have more competitors dividing it.

— Zhang Lu
20:09

FTX collapse forced liquidation of SBF's Anthropic stake for debt

Anthropic's early financing was rocky. In May 2023, Spark Capital made a non-consensus bet at Anthropic's ~$4.5 billion valuation when markets weren't sure whether they could support a second frontier model company. More dramatically: FTX founder SBF had invested in Anthropic too, but after FTX's bankruptcy, his shares were largely auctioned off for debt repayment. Zhang Lu noted that had FTX not collapsed, those investment returns could have covered much of what SBF needed to pay back.

— Zhang Lu
35:15

US bank's 2026 AI budget exceeds 100 billion dollars annually

Zhang Lu disclosed that a major US bank's Chief AI Officer told her the bank's AI budget this year has already reached over 100 billion dollars—shocking even her. She observes a pattern: once one company in an industry commits heavily to AI, the rest race to catch up fast. Companies fear falling behind AI-savvy competitors more than being disrupted by startups.

— Zhang Lu
36:44

Enterprise AI transformation requires automation first, then optimization—years not quarters

Zhang Lu divides enterprise AI into two stages: Business Automation (embedding AI in existing workflows for cost cuts and efficiency gains), then Business Optimization (lifting revenues, showing in financial statements). Most enterprises don't want to scrap systems and rebuild; they want agents to keep working with their current software. She estimates stage one alone taking 3-5 years to roll out broadly, with stage two taking longer. Don't expect AI to transform the entire economy in one or two years.

— Zhang Lu
38:15

A 500 million monthly bill taught enterprises to ration AI use

Zhang Lu relayed that a consulting firm heard an unnamed large enterprise's single-month Claude bill suddenly spike to nearly 500 million dollars, shocking their CFO. This unsustainable burn quickly triggered cost controls: major tech companies shifted from early encouragement to burn tokens to now strictly rationing usage. This partly explains why enterprises favor open-source models—closed-source pricing is prohibitive.

— Zhang Lu
56:27

Pharma's real bottleneck is raising clinical success rates, not discovering targets

Regarding Anthropic and OpenAI's bet on AI for Science (drug discovery, mathematical proofs), Zhang Lu thinks near-term revenue contribution is unlikely, though reasoning ability will improve. She pushes back on AI pharma narratives: even if AI expands target discovery from 2-3 targets to hundreds, those don't directly become drugs—clinical trials consume the cash. She's bullish on using AI to identify, from hundreds of targets, the few with highest clinical success rates, lifting success above 50-60%—that's genuinely valuable to the industry.

— Zhang Lu
1:08:33

Physical AI failures mean deaths, not hallucinations—regulation must come first

Zhang Lu recently spent considerable time thinking about AI regulation—her UN AI for Good board just held a 2+ hour session on it. Language model hallucinations were once funny; Physical AI failures risk human lives and catastrophic accidents. CTOs she meets are anxious, insisting that regulation and technical safeguards must be embedded before scaling physical AI—a prerequisite no high-regulation industry like healthcare can bypass.

— Zhang Lu

Glossary

Physical AI
AI systems requiring operation of physical equipment and interaction with the physical world.
Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
A metric measuring year-over-year spending growth from existing customers.
Business Automation / Business Optimization
Two sequential stages of enterprise AI deployment—first reducing costs and improving efficiency, then lifting revenue.
AI for Science
Using AI to assist scientific research including mathematical proofs and drug-target discovery.
