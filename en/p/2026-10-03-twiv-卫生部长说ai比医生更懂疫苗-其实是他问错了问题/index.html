3:01

AI beats doctors on vaccines—because the question itself is wrong

Kennedy claimed at MAHA summit that AI can provide patients with a more reliable ‘second opinion’ than any US doctor and can free them from ‘medical tyranny’. Griffin countered by sharing his own experience: when he asked Claude about viruses, the AI often gave wrong answers, apologized when corrected, and repeatedly told him ‘don't rely on me for answers’. The core issue isn't whether AI is smart—it's that Kennedy's question is fundamentally flawed. Vaccines' purpose is preventing severe disease and death, not stopping transmission; when you ask AI from that false premise, you get answers that sound reasonable but mean nothing.

— Daniel Griffin
8:01

Global cholera deaths have surged to their worst in 26 years

WHO data shows 2025 cholera deaths climbed 30% year-over-year to 7,870—the most since 1999. Cases concentrated in seven countries: Angola, Bangladesh, DRC, Nigeria, South Sudan, Sudan, and Yemen—all places the US has withdrawn aid from and all facing conflict or instability. Griffin presented this as concrete evidence that ‘misinformation and withdrawn aid kill people’.

— Daniel Griffin
11:01

Florida's dengue outbreak is now homegrown

Three Florida counties declared dengue emergency states; 392 resident cases and one death reported in Tampa. CDC data shows 1,524 local-transmission cases far outnumber the 484 imported cases, meaning mosquitoes have established a self-sustaining transmission cycle. Griffin attributes this to climate change expanding the mosquito range; Florida's year-round heat allows continuous transmission, and once case numbers are high enough, the cycle sustains itself—travelers then carry the virus beyond the state.

— Daniel Griffin
19:05

Congenital TORCH infections triple autism risk

A Swedish cohort study tracking 3.6 million births across national registries found that children with congenital TORCH infections had intellectual disability risk 7.22 times higher and autism risk 3.10 times higher than uninfected peers. Commenters note US CMV epidemiology differs from Sweden—suggesting the real impact could be larger—which is why Congress is now reviewing the CMV End Act to require universal newborn screening for congenital CMV.

— Daniel Griffin
22:05

US blocks progress on UN pandemic preparedness accord

A UN high-level meeting failed to reach consensus on pandemic declaration language. The US representative cited ‘unresolved key issues’; the EU, while stating commitment to overall goals, said the text misaligned with its interests and contained ‘red lines’, pushing the vote to December. Both hosts see the blockage stemming from the Trump administration's earlier rescission of Obama-era pandemic preparedness documents and declining US commitment to multilateral health security.

25:07

Ebola case fatality rate nears 50% near regional borders

DRC's Ebola outbreak now shows 8,116 confirmed cases and 3,924 deaths—a 50% fatality rate. Cases concentrate in the east, bordering South Sudan and Uganda, and near Rwanda and Burundi. Griffin expressed concern about spillover: Uganda has handled past outbreaks well, but South Sudan's fragility is deeply worrying.

— Daniel Griffin
30:09

Dropping vaccine mandates could unleash Florida's worst measles outbreak in 50 years

A PNAS modeling study assessed Florida's proposed elimination of mandatory school vaccines. Current state measles coverage has already fallen below the threshold needed to stop sustained transmission; lifting mandates could trigger the nation's largest measles outbreak in half a century, with hundreds of predicted childhood deaths over five years. Children are the primary virus spreaders; adult boosters and non-pharmaceutical measures like masks and isolation can blunt but not offset the impact of a vaccine coverage collapse. This echoes the current Pennsylvania crisis: that state now has 943 measles cases—the nation's largest and fastest-growing—with a fifth death newly reported.

— Daniel Griffin
34:09

Novavax triggers half the side effects of mRNA COVID vaccines

The PICOBOO study compared 586 adults across 1,049 doses of nine approved COVID vaccines in a Bayesian adaptive randomized controlled trial. Local reactogenicity ranged from Novavax's 9–47% to Moderna's 90%; systemic reactogenicity ranged from Novavax's 69% to Pfizer-BioNTech's 85%. The protein-based Novavax caused roughly half the discomfort of mRNA vaccines, and serious adverse events overall remained rare. Griffin often recommends Novavax to patients who complain about needing several days in bed after mRNA doses.

— Daniel Griffin

Glossary

TORCH infections
Collective term for five congenital infections—toxoplasmosis, syphilis, rubella, CMV, and HSV.
MTCT (mother-to-child transmission)
Disease transmission from mother to fetus or newborn.
Germinal centers
Lymph node regions where B cells mature and produce high-affinity antibodies.
Reactogenicity
Local or systemic adverse reactions following vaccination.
ACIP (Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices)
The official US committee that sets national vaccine schedule recommendations.
