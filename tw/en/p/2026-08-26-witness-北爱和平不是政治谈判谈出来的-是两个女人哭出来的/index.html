Northern Ireland's peace wasn't negotiated into being — two grieving women started it

A 1976 car crash in Belfast killed three young children. Their aunt and a woman who witnessed it founded the Peace People; violence in Northern Ireland fell by 70%, and the two won the Nobel Peace Prize.

0:50

Seven years of British troops hadn't stopped it; neighborhoods became urban guerrilla war

The narration sets the scene: Northern Ireland was already seven years into sectarian violence. On one side were the mainly Protestant "unionists", who wanted Northern Ireland to remain part of the United Kingdom; on the other, the mainly Catholic "nationalists", who wanted it to join the Republic of Ireland. Britain had sent troops in as early as 1969, and the situation kept getting worse. Jerry Graham, who lived in West Belfast, says the community had degenerated to the level of urban guerrilla warfare, where an ordinary trip to and from work could put you in the middle of a gun battle or a riot.

2:30

In Belfast, the news was how you checked whether someone you knew had died

Jerry remembers that afternoon, around three o'clock, hearing three or four shots from inside the house; they sounded very close. For years the family had listened to the news to find out whether anyone they knew had been caught up in something. The four o'clock bulletin reported that pedestrians had been caught up in an incident on Finaghy Road North. Jerry says he sometimes gets a premonition, and he turned to his wife and said: "I think that could be Anne." When he went in, he saw someone sitting there with their head in their hands. That, he says, was the beginning of the nightmare.

— Jerry Graham
4:00

The funeral wasn't the end: the mother took her own life four years later

The next day, Mairead went with the children's father to the morgue to identify the bodies. She said in an interview that she had seen two of the wee ones, that the third was gone too, and that there would be a triple funeral on Friday. She publicly condemned everyone carrying out the violence: the people who call themselves Christians cannot manage what God said — to love one another, to forgive and forget. A baby from the incident was in intensive care. The narration adds that the mother, Anne Maguire, was thought never to have recovered from the mental trauma of losing her children, and took her own life four years later.

6:00

Peace started with knocking on doors, not with a negotiating table

Betty Williams, who witnessed the crash, began going door to door collecting signatures condemning the violence — the starting point of the Peace People. Four days after the tragedy, 10,000 women, Protestant and Catholic alike, carried banners from the spot where the children died to the cemetery, the first of dozens of peace marches to come. On BBC radio, Mairead described the cross-community turnout: the women came from the Shankill, came by bus from East Belfast, came from Turf Lodge carrying banners.

— 旁白
7:30

Calling for peace was first read as siding with British unionists

The campaign did not have an easy run. The narration says they had to withstand the suspicion that they were "taking the side of the British unionists". In BBC footage, Betty stands in a room packed with women and says, with real feeling: for seven years, every time there was an explosion we repaired it, every time someone was shot we cried and we buried them, but it cannot go on like this; a future has to be built for everyone, whatever their political views, and "nobody gets a future out of the barrel of a gun".

— Betty Williams
9:00

The achievement wasn't cutting violence 70%, it was putting peace on the agenda

The narration gives the key number: the Peace People brought violence down by 70%. Violence later flared up again, and it was only the political negotiations of 1998 that really settled it. But the program quotes Jerry saying the movement's greatest achievement was "putting peace, in capital letters, on the agenda". In 1976, Mairead and Betty collected the Nobel Peace Prize at Oslo City Hall. In 1998, the Good Friday Agreement passed in referendums on both sides, ending thirty years of conflict in Northern Ireland.

— 旁白
10:00

The founders died one after another; the movement did not stop with them

The program closes by accounting for the people in it: co-founder and journalist Ciaran McKeown died in 2019 at the age of 76; Betty Williams died in 2020, also 76. Jerry Graham is now chair of the Peace People, and together with Mairead Corrigan Maguire continues to campaign for peace around the world. The program trails two episodes to follow: The Good Friday Agreement, and the referendum on the Good Friday Agreement.
