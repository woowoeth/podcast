The phage has to act early, because once σA binds it cannot be evicted

GP27 is the key protein that forms the condensate: its intrinsically disordered region (IDR) binds DNA, while its folded region interacts with the beta subunit of RNA polymerase. GP33 is an inhibitor of the host sigma factor σA; it binds between σA and the beta subunit and blocks the transcription complex from entering a productive transcription state. Competition experiments show that if σA binds first, GP33 cannot displace it — which is why the phage must suppress host σA early.

— Jolene Ramsey
57:54

Smallpox was carried into the Americas by European colonization: the first molecular evidence

A new paper reports the earliest ancient smallpox genomes from South America, recovered from two individuals from the Inca colonial period in northern Chile, dated to roughly 1492-1631. The phylogeny places them within a European medieval lineage, providing the first molecular evidence that smallpox entered the Americas through European colonization.

1:19:55

Smallpox gene loss is a continuing process that had already begun in medieval times

In the CAM208 genome, 49 genes are deleted or inactivated. Of these, 37 are inactivated in both the ancient and modern genomes, 2 are unique to the CAM lineage, and 10 are inactivated in CAM and in modern smallpox but variable in the medieval genomes. This supports the view that gene inactivation is an ongoing process, and that some genes had already begun to be inactivated in the early medieval period.

1:40:45

The marginal notes in Jenner's own book are today's anti-vaccine talking points

Rich shares a scan he found of an original copy of Jenner's An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolae Vaccinae, and discovered that the book once belonged to the Southampton Anti-Vaccination League: its pages carry a large number of handwritten annotations by anti-vaccinationists. For example, where Jenner says cowpox virus can render a person permanently immune to smallpox, the annotation reads ‘nonsense’; where Jenner notes that blacksmiths are often resistant to smallpox inoculation, the annotator attributes it instead to their being physically fit from their labor. These notes are indistinguishable from contemporary anti-vaccine rhetoric.

Reading is not an instinct, and AI makes judgment scarcer still

Brianne shares The Atlantic's article ‘The End of Reading’, which notes that in 2022 fewer than half of adults had read any book at all. The article argues that reading is not a human instinct — it has to borrow other regions of the brain — and that television and the internet have crowded out the time for it. On AI, the article argues that AI-generated prose is fluent but lacks originality, and may erode people's powers of discernment and judgment, which are precisely the capacities that will be needed more in the future.

Glossary

liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS)
The process by which molecules inside a cell aggregate into membraneless organelles; implicated in transcriptional regulation.
intrinsically disordered region (IDR)
A region of a protein with no fixed three-dimensional structure but important function, often involved in phase separation.
biomolecular condensate
A membraneless organelle formed by phase separation that concentrates particular proteins and nucleic acids.
RNA polymerase holoenzyme
The form of the core enzyme bound to a sigma factor, able to recognize promoters and initiate transcription.
vaccinia virus factory
The region inside a cell where the virus replicates and assembles, induced by viral proteins.
synonymous substitution rate
The rate of nucleotide mutations that do not change the amino acid; used as a read-out of evolutionary time.
