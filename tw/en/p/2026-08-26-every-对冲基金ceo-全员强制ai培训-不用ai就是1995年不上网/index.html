Not Using AI Is Refusing the Internet in 1995: A Hedge Fund Makes Training Mandatory

Walleye's CEO made AI use mandatory for every employee, saying refusing AI is like refusing the internet in 1995. AI is leverage, not cheating, and most employees at future companies will be AI bots.

AI transformationHedge fundsMandatory trainingData strategyQuant trading
A hedge fund CEO managing nearly $10 billion is personally driving an AI transformation across his entire firm, and he lays out the specific mechanics and his own change of mind. Directly applicable if you manage people.

The argument · tap a timestamp to hear it

4:08

If AI disrupts you, the failure is yours, not the technology's

Will says he is an engineer with a PhD in mathematics and has used statistics and AI for a long time. In recent years the tools have gotten stronger and far friendlier to non-technical people. Curiosity led him to notice the efficiency gains, and with that came a sense of responsibility: if AI disrupts him, the fault is his. He started an internal AI project two years ago. This is not a story about the future.

— Will
7:08

The internal AI product started with an analyst trying to replace himself

In March 2023, a TMT analyst built a tool on GPT-3 to make himself more efficient, essentially trying to automate his own analyst job. Will was skeptical at first, but the demo convinced him the direction was right. That project became Current, the firm's internal AI product for fundamental investment analysis. Every team uses it, and usage spikes during earnings season.

— Will
10:10

AI training cannot be voluntary; it has to be mandatory for everyone

After listening to a podcast with Chris Saka, Will decided AI training would be mandatory for every employee, regardless of department or technical background. He emailed the whole firm under the subject line ‘AI at Walleye: A Challenge to All of Us’, saying that not using AI is like refusing the internet in 1995, and that for a hedge fund, ignoring the tool is shameful.

— Will
16:11

Calling it cheating to write an email with AI is an academic's idea

Will insists that using AI to write an email is not cheating; that framing comes from academia. In business, AI makes you more efficient, the same way hiring an assistant does. He encourages employees to manage AI the way they would manage an employee, because everyone will be managing AI bots before long. He rejects dismissing a tool because it is imperfect: embrace it and iterate.

— Will
22:16

Quants have used AI for years; what's new is reading unstructured data

Will notes that quantitative trading has used AI for years. What LLMs added recently is the ability to handle unstructured data, which the firm uses for sentiment analysis. 75% of employees use ChatGPT weekly, and a third use AI coding tools such as Windsurf. The internal product Current has become indispensable to fundamental investing.

— Will
27:22

Every company should get used to having its meetings recorded end to end

Will says the firm records nearly every meeting and call, pipes it into a data lake, and processes it with LLMs to generate insights and predictions. He calls it ‘Borg’, after Star Trek: the goal is to consolidate information across the whole firm into a collective intelligence. He believes every company should get comfortable with having its data recorded.

— Will
33:32

What AI removes is not the thinking, it's the wording

Responding to Dan's worry about AI-assisted writing, Will argues the tool does not eliminate the need to think; it frees up time for thinking. He separates the concept from the language and syntax: AI handles the latter, humans focus on the former. He does warn against outsourcing your brain entirely, and says you have to find the optimal point of human-machine collaboration.

— Will
40:38

At tomorrow's world-class companies, most employees will be machines

Will points to the technological upheaval between the Civil War and World War I and argues the AI shift will move faster. He predicts a world-class company will still need thousands of employees, but most of them will be machines. He considers himself an optimist, while insisting you have to adapt or be left behind.

— Will
